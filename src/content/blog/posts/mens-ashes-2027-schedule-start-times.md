## Men’s Ashes 2027 schedule and start times

England and Australia’s five-Test men’s Ashes series is scheduled for **18 June to 2 August 2027**. It opens at Trent Bridge, visits Lord’s, Edgbaston and Southampton, and finishes at The Oval. The published start is **11:00 local time**, equivalent to **15:30 IST** on each opening day. [ECB schedule](https://www.ecb.co.uk/news/4539854)

## All five Tests

| Test | Scheduled dates in 2027 | Ground and city |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First | 18–22 June | Trent Bridge, Nottingham |
| Second | 30 June–4 July | Lord’s, London |
| Third | 8–12 July | Edgbaston, Birmingham |
| Fourth | 21–25 July | Utilita Bowl, Southampton |
| Fifth | 29 July–2 August | The Oval, London |

The [ICC fixture list](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/england-unveil-schedule-for-blockbuster-2027-home-summer) calls Southampton’s ground the Rose Bowl; the ECB uses Utilita Bowl. Those are names for the same venue, not alternative fourth-Test destinations. Southampton is scheduled to stage its first men’s Ashes Test.

The date ranges allow five days per Test. They do not guarantee five days of play: a match can finish earlier. For a calendar entry, keep the start date and scheduled final day rather than treating the whole series window as continuous cricket.

## Start times for India and Australia

| Location | Scheduled opening-day start |
| --- | --- |
| England | 11:00 BST |
| India | 15:30 IST |
| Sydney and Melbourne | 20:00 AEST |
| Perth | 18:00 AWST |

Britain is on summer time throughout this series. [GOV.UK lists the 2027 clock changes](https://www.gov.uk/when-do-the-clocks-change) as 28 March and 31 October. Therefore 11:00 in England is 10:00 UTC, not 11:00 UTC. Adding India’s five-and-a-half-hour offset gives 15:30, rather than the 16:30 produced by mistakenly treating the English time as GMT.

The Australian entries use the named cities’ winter offsets. All four listed local starts fall on the same calendar date. These are conversions of the announced start, not guarantees against a delayed toss or a revised later-day session.

## The gaps are uneven

Measured from each scheduled fifth day to the next opening day, the calendar contains seven clear days after Trent Bridge, three after Lord’s, eight after Edgbaston and three after Southampton.

| Between Tests | Dates with neither scheduled Test in progress | Clear days |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| First and second | 23–29 June | 7 |
| Second and third | 5–7 July | 3 |
| Third and fourth | 13–20 July | 8 |
| Fourth and fifth | 26–28 July | 3 |

The longest interval is eight clear days, not nine: 12 July is still a scheduled playing day and 21 July starts the next Test. Across the full 46-day window, the five fixtures occupy 25 potential match days and the four intervals account for the other 21. The arithmetic reconciles as 25 + 7 + 3 + 8 + 3 = 46.

These are gaps between scheduled matches, not verified rest days for individual players. Training and travel can occupy them, and an early finish can extend the actual time between games.

## Why 7 June appears on some tour calendars

Australia is also scheduled to face England Lions at Old Trafford on **7–10 June**, before the Ashes. The ECB explicitly makes this warm-up subject to review if Australia reaches the World Test Championship final. [Warm-up conditions and tour dates](https://www.cricket.com.au/news/4537483/england-2027-ashes-venues-world-test-championship-final-return-the-oval-australia-dates-trent-bridge-lords-edgbaston-southampton-rose-bowl)

A series page can consequently show a 7 June tour opening while the first Ashes Test still begins on 18 June. The Lions fixture is not a sixth Ashes Test. The WTC final is another separate event, scheduled for 9–13 June at The Oval. [Venue confirmation](https://www.kiaoval.com/the-ashes-returns-to-the-kia-oval-as-2027-international-fixtures-are-confirmed/)

If Australia qualifies and the final uses its scheduled five days, 14–17 June supplies four clear days before Trent Bridge. That conditional calculation is not a declaration that Australia has qualified or that the warm-up has already been cancelled.

## Keep the competition visible when planning

The women’s Ashes is a separate multi-format series taking place during the same summer. Its Headingley Test begins on 24 June; that fixture does not belong in the five-match men’s list. [Cricket Australia’s women’s schedule](https://www.cricket.com.au/news/4541972)

Before arranging viewing or travel around a specific day, check the organizer’s latest fixture entry and the ground’s match-day information. Use the named venue, local timezone and Test number together. This schedule is checked through 14 November 2026; it does not establish ticket availability, a confirmed XI or an India broadcast entitlement.
