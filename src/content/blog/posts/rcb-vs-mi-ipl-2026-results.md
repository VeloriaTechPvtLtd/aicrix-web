Royal Challengers Bengaluru beat Mumbai Indians in the two IPL 2026 matches compared here: 12 April and 10 May. The first was an 18-run defence of 240 at Wankhede; the second was a two-wicket chase of 167 that lasted until the final delivery in Raipur. Identical winners did not mean similar contests. [IPL April report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/tata-ipl-2026-match-20-mi-vs-rcb-match-report), [IPL May report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/tata-ipl-2026-match-54-rcb-v-mi-match-report).

## Mumbai: a large total built by several batters

Phil Salt made 78 from 36 balls, Virat Kohli 50 from 38 and Rajat Patidar 53 from 20. Tim David added an unbeaten 34 from 16. Salt and Kohli's opening partnership supplied 120, but Patidar's much faster contribution ensured the innings did not depend only on the openers. Salt was player of the match. [Official report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/tata-ipl-2026-match-20-mi-vs-rcb-match-report).

Those four batters contributed 215 of RCB's 240 runs, or 89.6%. That calculation describes the concentration of the total, not their share of batting balls or a complete measure of individual value.

Sherfane Rutherford kept Mumbai's reply moving with 71 not out from 31 balls, including nine sixes. RCB nevertheless had enough room to defend. Krunal Pandya conceded only 26 in four overs and took a wicket; Suyash Sharma took two for 47. Those spells matter alongside the more conspicuous batting numbers. [Full scorecard](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/149812/mi-vs-rcb-20th-match-indian-premier-league-2026).

Mumbai needed 241, not 240, to win. Their eventual 222 left the official margin at 18 runs: the margin compares the two totals, while the remaining requirement compares the chase with its target. Those are related but different numbers.

## Raipur: Krunal's batting and Bhuvneshwar's two roles

The return fixture began with Bhuvneshwar Kumar taking four for 23. Tilak Varma's 57 and Naman Dhir's 47 helped Mumbai reach 166/7, giving their bowlers a markedly smaller total to defend. [Official report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/tata-ipl-2026-match-54-rcb-v-mi-match-report).

Krunal's 73 from 46 balls then became the central innings of RCB's chase. Corbin Bosch's four for 26 and Jasprit Bumrah's wicketless four overs for 20 prevented an easy finish. RCB were still short when Krunal departed at 149/7 after 18 overs. [May scorecard](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/152097/mi-vs-rcb-54th-match-ipl-2026).

Bhuvneshwar supplied a late six as well as his wickets, finishing seven not out from two balls. Rasikh Salam Dar completed the winning two from the last delivery. Bhuvneshwar, rather than the leading scorer, received player of the match. [Official finish](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/tata-ipl-2026-match-54-rcb-v-mi-match-report), [match result and award](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scores/152097/mi-vs-rcb-54th-match-indian-premier-league-2026).

## What the two games actually compare

The initial required rates were 12.05 runs per over for Mumbai in April and 8.35 for RCB in May. A lower opening requirement did not prevent the second chase becoming closer. Wickets, scoring sequence and bowling performance changed the available routes to victory.

Krunal illustrates the contrast especially well: his restrictive four-over spell mattered in the high-scoring defence, while his batting was decisive in the lower-scoring chase. Selecting one headline statistic would miss half that story.

The responsible conclusion is narrow. RCB won these two completed fixtures through different combinations of contributions. Neither the 2–0 record nor one batter's best innings establishes a future win probability, a venue's permanent par score or a guaranteed fantasy selection.

## How much was left after Krunal fell?

At 149/7 after 18 overs, RCB still required 18 from 12 legal deliveries to reach 167. That was nine runs per six-ball over, slightly above the original 8.35 requirement. The more important change was the loss of seven wickets. A required rate is useful, but it cannot express the same batting resources at the beginning and end of an innings.

Bhuvneshwar’s seven from two balls should also remain separate from the team’s final-over total. Other batters and extras contributed to the finish. The scorecard credits individual runs to the batter and extras to the side; combining them as though one player supplied every late run would distort how this chase was completed.
