India won the inaugural men’s World Twenty20 final by five runs at the Wanderers in Johannesburg on 24 September 2007. India made 157/5; Pakistan were dismissed for 152 in 19.3 overs. Misbah-ul-Haq was the last wicket, caught by Sreesanth off Joginder Sharma while attempting a scoop. Pakistan still needed six to win when the last wicket fell because the target was one more than India’s total. Three scheduled legal deliveries remained, but no batting partnership remained to use them.

## Gambhir supplied the foundation, Rohit the finish

Gautam Gambhir’s 75 from 54 balls was the largest innings of the match. Rohit Sharma finished unbeaten on 30 from 16. Together they supplied 105 of India’s 157 runs, or 66.88% of the team total. That is their combined individual contribution, not a partnership figure. [ICC account of India’s batting](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/a-look-back-at-previous-icc-world-twenty20-final-matches)

Umar Gul took 3/28, dismissing Yuvraj Singh, MS Dhoni and Gambhir. India were 130/5 when Gambhir fell at the end of the eighteenth over. The remaining two overs added 27 runs, taking the target beyond 150. Rohit’s complete 30 should not be treated as 30 scored in that closing segment: he had already begun batting before Gambhir’s dismissal. [Batting and fall-of-wickets ledger](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/3687/ind-vs-pak-final-icc-world-t20-2007)

India’s scoring was therefore unevenly distributed. A long opening contribution and a short unbeaten finish compensated for three middle-order scores of 8, 14 and 6. The total cannot be understood just by describing the final two overs or the biggest individual innings in isolation.

## Wickets constrained Pakistan’s recovery

RP Singh removed Mohammad Hafeez and Kamran Akmal, and Robin Uthappa ran out Imran Nazir after his 33 from 14 balls. Pakistan subsequently fell to 77/6. [ICC rivalry archive, 2007 final section](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/a-look-back-at-previous-icc-world-twenty20-final-matches)

Irfan Pathan’s four overs cost only 16 runs and brought three wickets: Shoaib Malik, Shahid Afridi and Yasir Arafat. RP Singh also took three, conceding 26. Their combined eight overs therefore yielded six wickets for 42 runs, an economy of 5.25. Those figures describe their actual spells, not a claim that every remaining over should have been assigned to them. [Full bowling figures](https://www.mykhel.com/cricket/india-vs-pakistan-scorecard-icc-world-t20-s9920-122506/)

At 104/7 after 16 overs, Pakistan needed 54 from 24 balls: 13.50 per over. Misbah’s recovery brought the chase back within reach, with support from the lower order. The [BCCI retrospective](https://www.bcci.tv/news/article/icc-world-t20-rewind-india-win) records that checkpoint and the eventual six-needed-from-four position. The falling requirement did not restore the wickets already lost; by the last over, only one remained.

## The final over, delivery by delivery

Pakistan began the twentieth over at 145/9, needing 13. Joginder’s first delivery was a wide; his next was a dot ball, followed by a six and the catch that ended the match. That sequence is recorded in [PTI’s contemporary report](https://www.rediff.com/cricket/report/india1/20070924.htm).

| Event | Pakistan score | Runs needed to win | Legal balls remaining |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Start of final over | 145/9 | 13 | 6 |
| Wide | 146/9 | 12 | 6 |
| Dot ball, 19.1 | 146/9 | 12 | 5 |
| Six, 19.2 | 152/9 | 6 | 4 |
| Misbah caught, 19.3 | 152 all out | Chase ended | 3 unused |

The wide changed the score without consuming a legal delivery. The six then made one more six sufficient for victory, but Misbah’s dismissal ended the innings before that possibility could be used. This arithmetic explains the tension without inventing a probability of winning or judging what an alternative shot would have produced.

Misbah finished with 43 from 38 balls. The memorable last catch settled the result, but the narrow margin was built across both innings: Gambhir and Rohit’s batting, Gul’s wickets, India’s economical spells and Pakistan’s lower-order recovery all preceded it. This is a dated final archive, not evidence of either team’s present strength.
