The ten innings include three centuries, three other fifties and one duck. They begin with his unbeaten 84 against Afghanistan at Dharamshala on **13 June 2026**, not with the January New Zealand series.

The distinction is important: a recent-form table must include every eligible batting innings in sequence. It must not jump over an entire series because one annual summary is incomplete. ## Why a match appearance is not always a batting innings

The [full Chennai scorecard](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/148415/ind-vs-afg) records Gill as did-not-bat in the third Afghanistan match at Chennai on **20 June 2026**. It sits between the June and July batting entries but does not occupy a row in a ten-batting-innings table.

Likewise, the New Zealand–India match at Hamilton on **10 November 2026** was abandoned after the toss without a ball. Nobody produced a batting innings. Gill's most recent completed ODI batting entry therefore remained the first-ball duck in Wellington on 7 November when this record was checked on 12 November. [NZC no-result scorecard](https://archive.nzc.nz/Scorecards/1455/1455468.html).

A duck and a did-not-bat are not interchangeable. The Wellington zero was an actual one-ball innings ending in dismissal. The Chennai DNB and Hamilton washout added neither a batting dismissal nor a zero-ball batting score to this table.

Test innings are another exclusion. Gill's August Test performances can appear between ODI entries in an all-format player log, but they do not belong in an ODI form calculation. The selection rule is completed ODI batting innings, not the ten most recent rows displayed by any player page.

## How the summary numbers are calculated

| Measure | Value in this window |
| --- | ---: |
| Batting innings | 10 |
| Runs | 797 |
| Balls faced | 669 |
| Conventional not-out entries | 2 |
| Retired-hurt innings, not dismissed | 1 |
| Dismissals | 7 |
| Batting average | 113.86 |
| Combined strike rate | 119.13 |
| Hundreds | 3 |
| Fifties excluding hundreds | 3 |

The average is **797 ÷ 7 = 113.857…**, rounded to 113.86. Counting the retired-hurt innings as a dismissal would incorrectly produce 99.63. Dividing by ten instead gives **79.70 runs per batting innings**, a different measure.

The strike rate is **797 ÷ 669 × 100 = 119.13**. It is calculated from total runs and total balls, not by averaging the ten displayed innings strike rates.

The median score was **78.5**, halfway between 77 and 80 after the scores are sorted. Mean score, median score and batting average all describe the same ledger from different angles. They should not be swapped simply because one makes the form look stronger.

## The three centuries and the shorter innings

The centuries were 154 against Afghanistan, 110 against West Indies and 223 not out against West Indies. Combined, they supplied **487 runs, or 61.1% of the window's total**. The other seven innings supplied 310.

That concentration explains why the headline average needs the full ledger beside it. Gill scored at least fifty in six of the ten innings, but his latest three batting entries were one, 37 and zero. The aggregate contains both sustained large contributions and a much smaller latest three-innings return of 38 runs.

Neither observation cancels the other. Describing the window only as “averaging 113.86” misses the shorter recent scores. Describing it only as “38 in his last three” hides the much larger contributions immediately before them.

The chronology after the latest century also matters. The 223 not out was followed by one against West Indies, then 37 and zero against New Zealand. A record innings cannot be assumed to carry forward mechanically into the next fixture.

## Comparing recent form without changing the question

This table is most useful when compared with another batter's ODI ledger using the same counting rule. Keep balls faced, not-outs and dates visible, rather than comparing one player's career average with another's last-ten average.

Equal numbers of innings do not necessarily cover equal calendar periods. Gill's ten here span June to November. Another batter's ten may stretch further back. That difference affects how much old cricket enters the comparison, even though both tables have ten rows.

Opposition and venue also matter. This sample includes Afghanistan, England, West Indies and New Zealand across several countries. It is not a ten-match record against one bowling attack, nor a venue-specific forecast.

For fantasy selection, a batting ledger informs one part of the decision. Announced selection, batting position, likely opportunities, contest rules and fielding contributions still need separate checks. The figures above are batting statistics, not a promise of points or a recommendation based on a guaranteed next-match score.
