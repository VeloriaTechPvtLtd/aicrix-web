## RCB and SRH shared the two wins

Royal Challengers Bengaluru and Sunrisers Hyderabad met twice in IPL 2026 and won one match each. RCB completed a six-wicket chase in Bengaluru on 28 March. SRH answered with a 55-run win in Hyderabad on 22 May.

The two results make a useful comparison because RCB chased on both occasions. A score just above 200 was enough for victory in the opener, but 200 was far short in the return match. The target, not the apparent size of a total in isolation, explains the difference.

## Bengaluru belonged to the chase

RCB chose to field and Jacob Duffy removed Abhishek Sharma, Travis Head and Nitish Kumar Reddy in the powerplay. His 3/22 in four overs earned the Player of the Match award.

SRH nevertheless recovered to 201/9. Ishan Kishan made 80 from 38 balls, while Aniket Verma supplied 43 from 18. The recovery gave SRH a competitive total, but it did not erase the value of those early wickets. [IPL opening-match report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/tata-ipl-2026-match-1-rcb-vs-srh-match-report)

Devdutt Padikkal’s 61 from 26 balls and Virat Kohli’s unbeaten 69 from 38 gave RCB both speed and continuity. Rajat Patidar added 31 from 12. RCB reached 203/4 after 94 legal balls, leaving 26 of the scheduled 120 unused. [Full opening-match scorecard](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/149618/srh-vs-rcb-1st-match-indian-premier-league-2026)

That calculation also explains the notation: 15.4 overs means 15 complete overs plus four balls, not 15.4 decimal overs. Using the latter in a run-rate calculation would produce the wrong answer.

## Hyderabad demanded a different chase

SRH chose to bat in the return game and built 255/4 through contributions across the innings. Abhishek’s 56 from 22 balls provided an early burst, Kishan made 79 from 46, Heinrich Klaasen scored 51 from 24, and Nitish finished unbeaten on 29 from 12.

RCB’s reply contained runs but not enough acceleration to reach 256. Venkatesh Iyer scored 44 from 19 and Patidar made 56 from 39. Eshan Malinga’s 2/33 from four overs helped SRH limit the chase to 200/4. Kishan received the Player of the Match award. [IPL return-match report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/tata-ipl-2026-match-67-srh-v-rcb-match-report)

The opening required rate was 10.10 runs per over in Bengaluru, calculated as 202 divided by 20. In Hyderabad it was 12.80, from 256 divided by 20. RCB’s second total therefore cannot be assessed as “almost the same performance” merely because it was only three runs below the first.

## What the two meetings tell us

Kishan scored 159 runs from 84 balls across these two innings, a combined strike rate of 189.29. That is a useful summary of this particular matchup sample, not his full-season record.

The team comparison is equally narrow: one win apiece, with opposite outcomes despite RCB batting second both times. Two matches do not establish that either side always benefits from chasing, or that either venue will produce the same scoring pattern next season.

## Quick answers

**Who won RCB versus SRH in IPL 2026?** Each team won once: RCB on 28 March and SRH on 22 May.

**Did RCB finish on 202 or 203 in the opener?** The scorecard says 203/4. RCB needed 202 to win.

**Was there a third playoff meeting?** No. The season record contains the two league meetings above.

## Compare the time used, not just the totals

RCB’s 203 in 94 legal balls translates to 12.96 runs per over, rounded to two decimal places. Their 200 in 120 legal balls in Hyderabad came at exactly ten per over. The totals differed by three runs, but the second innings used 26 more deliveries. That difference is essential when comparing two chases.

Likewise, the Hyderabad winning margin was 255 minus 200: 55 runs. RCB would have needed 56 additional runs to reach the winning target of 256. Runs short of a tie and runs required to win are not interchangeable. This does not change the official result; it explains why the target is one greater than the defending side’s total.
