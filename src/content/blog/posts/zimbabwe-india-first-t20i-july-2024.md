Zimbabwe beat India by 13 runs in the first T20I at Harare on 6 July 2024. Zimbabwe made 115/9, then dismissed India for 102 in 19.5 overs. Ravi Bishnoi’s 4/13 had kept the first innings small, but India still had to complete a target of 116. [Match scorecard](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/88991/zim-vs-ind-1st-t20i-india-tour-of-zimbabwe-2024), [Contemporary report](https://www.cricket.com.au/news/4052981/zimbabwe-beat-india-first-t20-harare-report-scores-sikandar-raza-world-cup-winners-rested)

## The last wicket still added value

Zimbabwe were 90/9 after 15.3 overs. Clive Madande and Tendai Chatara then carried them to 115/9 without another dismissal. Madande finished on 29 not out from 25 balls; Chatara was unbeaten on zero from nine. [Batting and fall-of-wickets card](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/88991/zim-vs-ind-1st-t20i-india-tour-of-zimbabwe-2024)

Their stand added **25 runs in the final 27 legal balls**. Chatara’s zero does not mean he contributed nothing to preserving the partnership: the innings continued while the last pair remained together. Nor should all 25 partnership runs automatically be assigned to one batter without checking extras and individual scoring.

## India lost wickets before they lost time

India fell to 22/4. Shubman Gill made 31, but his dismissal left them 47/6 after 10.2 overs. Sikandar Raza took 3/25 and Chatara 3/16. [Contemporary report](https://www.cricket.com.au/news/4052981/zimbabwe-beat-india-first-t20-harare-report-scores-sikandar-raza-world-cup-winners-rested)

At 47/6, India needed **69 from 58 balls**. More than nine overs remained, but only four wickets were available. The small initial target had become a more difficult chase without requiring a spectacular Zimbabwe total.

Washington Sundar finished with 27, yet India needed 16 from the final over and could not finish. [Report](https://www.cricket.com.au/news/4052981/zimbabwe-beat-india-first-t20-harare-report-scores-sikandar-raza-world-cup-winners-rested)

The final margin is 115 − 102 = 13 runs. India’s innings ended with one legal ball unused; that unused ball does not alter the margin. The match illustrates why target size and wickets remaining must be read together.
