England won the inaugural Women's World T20 final at Lord's on 21 June 2009, beating New Zealand by six wickets. New Zealand were dismissed for 85, and England reached 86/4 in 17 overs with Claire Taylor unbeaten on 39. Katherine Brunt was Player of the Match after taking 3/6. [NZC historical scorecard](https://archive.blackcaps.co.nz/Scorecards/180/180955.html)

The final was not decided by a spectacular last-over rescue. England created a small target through sustained bowling pressure and then avoided the kind of collapse that could have brought New Zealand back into the contest. Its significance lies in that combination, as well as its place at the beginning of the women's tournament's history.

## Brunt removed more than just runs from the equation

Brunt's four overs included two maidens. Her wickets were Aimee Watkins, Lucy Doolan and Rachel Priest; New Zealand were 23/4 after Priest's dismissal. Amy Satterthwaite eventually top-scored with 19 from 42 deliveries. [NZC bowling and batting ledger](https://archive.blackcaps.co.nz/Scorecards/180/180955.html)

Six runs from four overs gives an economy rate of 1.50. Her three wickets accompanied that restriction; England’s other bowlers and fielders accounted for the remaining seven.

## A low total is an outcome, not a universal pitch rating

England had elected to field. The ICC's contemporary report described an attack that maintained pressure throughout and backed it with effective fielding. It also recorded New Zealand captain Watkins's view that her side had been outplayed. [ICC contemporary report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/england-eves-are-inaugural-icc-world-twenty20-champions)

A retrospective should resist turning 85 into a permanent statement about Lord's. This was one team total in a specific match. It does not establish the ground's normal scoring level across seasons, formats or different opponents.

## Claire Taylor and Sarah Taylor had different roles in the reply

Claire Taylor made 39 not out from 32 balls, while Sarah Taylor scored 23. Charlotte Edwards was dismissed for nine. The ICC's retrospective identifies the two Taylors as central to keeping the chase on course after the captain's early departure. [ICC retrospective](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/32-balls-claire-taylor-faced-on-her-way-to-39-the-top-score-in-the-first-final-in-2009)

Using full names is particularly important here. A reference to Taylor's score without an initial or first name can accidentally combine two separate batting contributions. The unbeaten 39 belongs to Claire; Sarah's contribution was 23.

Claire also benefited from a missed chance before she had scored, according to the contemporary report. [ICC match account](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/england-eves-are-inaugural-icc-world-twenty20-champions)

## The match award and tournament award were different

Brunt received the final's award, while Claire Taylor was Player of the Tournament after scoring 199 runs across the competition. [ICC award confirmation](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/england-eves-are-inaugural-icc-world-twenty20-champions)

England's victory is therefore best remembered as a team performance with clearly identifiable contributions: early wickets, continued restriction and a chase that retained its direction. The inaugural title was secured not by making the finish dramatic, but by doing enough earlier to avoid needing a dramatic finish at all.
