India beat Australia by three wickets at the Gabba on 19 January 2021, winning the four-Test series 2–1. The Brisbane match began on 15 January. Set 328, India finished at 329/7, with Rishabh Pant unbeaten on 89. The target, the final score and the runs still needed on the last morning are three different figures. [ICC final-day report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/rishabh-pant-leads-brilliant-india-to-historic-win-at-the-gabba)

## All four innings

| Innings | Score | Overs | Leading scorer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Australia first | 369 all out | 115.2 | Marnus Labuschagne 108 |
| India first | 336 all out | 111.4 | Shardul Thakur 67 |
| Australia second | 294 all out | 75.5 | Steve Smith 55 |
| India second | 329/7 | 97 | Shubman Gill 91 |

Australia led by 33 after the first innings. Adding their second-innings 294 produced an overall lead of 327, so India needed 328 to win. Australia were bowled out rather than declaring. [Complete scorecard](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/31648/aus-vs-ind-4th-test-india-tour-of-australia-2020-21)

## The recovery before the chase

India’s eventual pursuit depended on a substantial first-innings repair. At 186/6 they were still 183 behind Australia’s 369. Washington Sundar and Thakur added 123 for the seventh wicket; Sundar finished with 62 and Thakur with 67. Josh Hazlewood took 5/57, but India reached 336 and restricted the deficit to 33. [ICC day-three report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/thakur-sundar-rescue-india-with-record-stand-on-day-three)

The stand ended at 309/7, still 60 behind. The remaining wickets added another 27. That sequence matters: the partnership did not put India ahead, but it reduced the amount Australia could carry into their next innings. Treating the later chase as Pant’s isolated rescue leaves out this earlier work.

## Siraj and Thakur finished Australia’s second innings

Mohammed Siraj took 5/73 and Thakur 4/61 as Australia were dismissed for 294. Smith’s 55 was the highest individual score. India then faced only 11 legal deliveries before rain ended day four, reaching 4/0 with Rohit Sharma on four and Gill yet to score. [ICC day-four report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/india-set-328-to-win-after-siraj-five-for)

India therefore began the last day needing 324 more, not a new target of 324. The original target remained 328. They ultimately added 325 on day five because the winning boundary took the completed innings to 329.

## Gill and Pujara gave the chase a foundation

After Rohit fell for seven, Gill and Cheteshwar Pujara added 114. Gill made 91 from 146 balls; Pujara’s 56 occupied 211. Their contrasting contributions were not competing definitions of a good innings: one supplied faster progress while the other spent much longer at the crease. India reached lunch at 83/1 and tea at 183/3. [BCCI final-day report](https://www.bcci.tv/news/article/aus-vs-ind-4th-test-india-breach-fortress-gabba)

At tea the task was still substantial: 145 required with seven wickets available. Pujara subsequently fell to Pat Cummins with India on 228, leaving 100 to win. Cummins also removed Mayank Agarwal at 265/5. His final 4/55 shows that Australia continued taking important wickets even while India moved toward the target. [Fall-of-wicket ledger](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/31648/aus-vs-ind-4th-test-india-tour-of-australia-2020-21)

## Pant and Sundar changed the closing equation

Pant and Sundar added 53 from 55 balls. That took India from 265/5 to 318/6: a requirement of 63 became ten. Sundar’s 22 ended when Nathan Lyon bowled him; Thakur followed for two, leaving Pant to finish alongside Navdeep Saini. Pant’s boundary sealed the win with three overs remaining. [Cricket Australia’s contemporary report](https://www.cricket.com.au/news/3310762)

The three-wicket margin describes the wickets India still had when their two-innings aggregate passed Australia’s, not the difference between 329 and 328. A draw would have been enough for India to retain the Border-Gavaskar Trophy at 1–1; this victory instead secured the series outright. [ICC day-four context](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/india-set-328-to-win-after-siraj-five-for)

Brisbane’s result was built in stages: reducing the first-innings deficit, taking all ten wickets again, surviving the early part of the chase and accelerating late without losing the remaining wickets. Each stage belongs to the same Test, rather than being a separate record or a promise about future Gabba matches.
