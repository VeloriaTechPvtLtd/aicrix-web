Australia beat England by three wickets at Lancaster Park, Christchurch, on 7 February 1982. England made 151/5 in sixty overs; Australia reached 152/7 after 59. The allocation matters: this was a sixty-over final, and Australia finished with six legal balls remaining. [Archived scorecard](https://i.imgci.com/link_to_database/ARCHIVE/WORLD_CUPS/WWC82/AUS-WOMEN_ENG-WOMEN_WWC82_WODI-FINAL_07FEB1982.html)

## England recovered, then lost wickets near the finish

England moved from 63/3 to 150/4, an 87-run fourth-wicket partnership. Southgate made 53 and Rachael Heyhoe-Flint 29. Marie Lutschini conceded 17 in twelve overs and took one wicket; Lyn Fullston returned 2/20. England ended only one run beyond the fourth-wicket checkpoint. [Innings ledger](https://i.imgci.com/link_to_database/ARCHIVE/WORLD_CUPS/WWC82/AUS-WOMEN_ENG-WOMEN_WWC82_WODI-FINAL_07FEB1982.html)

The wicket totals establish the partnership's size, but not its duration. Without delivery timings, they cannot show exactly when England's scoring slowed. The reliable comparison is the recovery from three wickets down and the limited advance after that stand ended.

## Several contributions completed Australia's chase

Australia fell to 28/3. Karen Read scored 32, Sharon Tredrea 25 and Jacobs 37; Lutschini finished unbeaten on 24. At 145/7, Australia still needed seven. Three dismissals were run-outs, so bowling figures alone do not account for the lost wickets. [Chase ledger](https://i.imgci.com/link_to_database/ARCHIVE/WORLD_CUPS/WWC82/AUS-WOMEN_ENG-WOMEN_WWC82_WODI-FINAL_07FEB1982.html)

The final seven runs completed work shared across the order. No batter made fifty, yet successive contributions carried Australia past the target. The three-wicket margin describes wickets remaining; it does not mean the finish came on the last ball.

## A final after a long league campaign

Unlike the 1973 and 1978 league deciders, 1982 had a designated final. Five teams played a triple-league competition, with the top two advancing. Australia won eleven of their twelve preliminary matches before retaining the trophy against England. [ICC tournament history](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/womens-world-cup-a-trip-down-memory-lane)

That dominant qualifying record did not remove the need to recover on the final day. Australia won through several completed batting tasks rather than one uninterrupted opening partnership.
