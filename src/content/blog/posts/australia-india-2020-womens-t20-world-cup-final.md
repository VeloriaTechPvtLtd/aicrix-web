Australia beat India by 85 runs in the Women's T20 World Cup final at the Melbourne Cricket Ground on 8 March 2020. Australia scored 184/4 before bowling India out for 99. Alyssa Healy made 75 from 39 balls, Beth Mooney finished unbeaten on 78 from 54, and Megan Schutt took 4/18. [Cricket Australia match report](https://www.cricket.com.au/news/3306109)

The match is remembered for its opening batting display, but Australia's victory had two separate parts. The first was building a demanding target without sacrificing the innings to early risk. The second was preventing India's strongest batting options from constructing a viable response.

## Healy and Mooney built the advantage together

Australia's opening partnership reached 115. Healy supplied the faster individual innings, including seven fours and five sixes, while Mooney remained to the end. India finally removed Healy in the twelfth over, but Australia were already well positioned to build a substantial total. [ICC match report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/australia-lift-fifth-t20-world-cup-title)

Their innings should not be compared solely by asking who scored more. Mooney's total was three runs higher; Healy used 15 fewer deliveries. Those differences describe complementary contributions rather than proving that one was intrinsically more valuable.

Healy's seven fours and five sixes accounted for 58 of her 75 runs. That calculation makes the boundary contribution visible without needing to invent a description of every delivery. It also illustrates why a partnership can accelerate sharply even when the two players are scoring at different speeds.

## The first six overs explain more than the final margin

Australia reached 49 without loss at the end of their powerplay. India were 32/4 at the same stage of the chase. Schutt removed Shafali Verma, Jess Jonassen dismissed Jemimah Rodrigues and Harmanpreet Kaur, and Sophie Molineux took Smriti Mandhana's wicket. [Cricket Australia powerplay comparison](https://www.cricket.com.au/news/3306109)

The difference was therefore not just 17 runs. It was also four wickets. A team chasing a large target generally needs both scoring progress and enough remaining batting resources to keep attacking. Looking only at the runs can conceal how much the second requirement has changed.

## The concussion replacement was not a tactical substitute

Taniya Bhatia had to leave her batting innings after being struck on the helmet. Richa Ghosh entered as her concussion replacement and scored 18. Deepti Sharma made 33, but India could not rebuild into a successful chase. [ICC account of India's innings](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/australia-lift-fifth-t20-world-cup-title)

## Keep the occasion separate from the records

The ICC recorded a crowd of 86,174, a women's cricket attendance record at the time, as Australia secured their fifth Women's T20 World Cup title. [ICC final report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/australia-lift-fifth-t20-world-cup-title)

The result itself needs no embellishment. Australia's total exceeded India's by 85, while the winning target had been 185. The difference between a target and a margin is small numerically but important when checking a summary.

For a balanced retrospective, retain all three layers: the opening partnership that built the target, the early wickets that disrupted the reply and the occasion witnessed by the crowd. None requires pretending that the result erased India's earlier tournament performances or that one match predicts the next meeting.
