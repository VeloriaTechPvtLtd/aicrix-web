MS Dhoni scored 161 runs from 73 balls for Chennai Super Kings in IPL 2024, with a strike rate of 220.55 and a highest score of 37 not out. He appeared in 14 matches but batted in 11. Eight of those innings were unbeaten, leaving three dismissals and a batting average of 53.67. The [season summary](https://www.mykhel.com/cricket/players/ms-dhoni-ipl-p3243/) agrees with the scorecard totals below.

Those numbers describe a highly compressed batting season. His longest innings lasted 16 balls, and six of his 11 innings lasted four balls or fewer. Reading the average without that opportunity pattern gives a very different impression from reading the complete record.

## How much batting opportunity did he have

Dhoni faced an average of 6.64 balls per batting innings, calculated as 73 divided by 11. The median was only four balls: arrange the 11 innings by length and the middle observation is his four-ball innings against Mumbai. The mean is higher because a handful of longer stays pull it upward.

His positions also varied. He batted at No. 5 twice, No. 6 twice, No. 7 twice, No. 8 four times and No. 9 once. That is observed usage, not evidence that one position suited him better. Two innings at the same position can begin with very different scores, required rates and deliveries remaining.

The difference is visible without speculation. At Mumbai he came in during the last over and finished with 20 not out from four balls, including three successive sixes. In Lucknow he arrived late in the innings and made 28 not out from nine. The [Mumbai report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/tata-ipl-2024-match-29-mi-vs-csk-match-report) and [Lucknow report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/tata-ipl-2024-match-34-lsg-vs-csk-match-report) establish those particular entry situations; they do not turn the entire season into a separately measured death-over dataset.

## Scoring speed is not the same as innings size

His 14 fours and 13 sixes contributed 134 runs, or 83.23% of his season total. The other 27 runs came without a boundary credited to him. That calculation describes where his runs came from, not the percentage of deliveries on which he attempted a boundary.

The six innings lasting four balls or fewer produced 31 runs from 13 balls. The other five produced 130 from 60. This is a descriptive split by completed innings length, not a fair experiment comparing batting roles: getting out can itself shorten an innings. The one-ball duck at Dharamsala belongs in the short group just as surely as the four-ball 20 at Mumbai.

Likewise, 53.67 is runs per dismissal, not runs per appearance or per batting innings. Dividing 161 by 11 gives 14.64 runs per batting innings; dividing by all 14 appearances gives 11.50. Neither replaces the conventional average. They answer different questions about the same season.

## What this season can and cannot tell you

The unbeaten 37 against Delhi was his highest score, but CSK lost that match by 20 runs. His 20 not out against Mumbai came in a 20-run victory. The matching number in the latter result does not isolate one player’s causal contribution: the rest of the batting and bowling still determined the match.

At Bengaluru, Dhoni’s last innings of the season was 25 from 13 balls before he was caught off Yash Dayal. CSK’s qualification calculation was distinct from the match-winning target. That context matters more than labelling every late innings a successful or unsuccessful finish. [Final-match report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/tata-ipl-2024-match-68-rcb-vs-csk-match-report).

This record supports a precise conclusion: in 2024, Dhoni scored quickly across a small number of balls, often in very short innings. It does not establish current form, predict his next opportunity or settle an all-time finisher ranking. For that separate comparison, use a consistent phase definition and comparable samples rather than this season’s headline strike rate alone.
