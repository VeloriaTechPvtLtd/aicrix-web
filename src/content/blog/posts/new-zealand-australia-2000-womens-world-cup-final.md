New Zealand defeated Australia by four runs at Lincoln on 23 December 2000. New Zealand made 184; Australia were dismissed for 180. Belinda Clark’s 91 from 102 balls earned her the player-of-the-match award despite Australia’s defeat. [Full scorecard](https://i.imgci.com/link_to_database/ARCHIVE/WORLD_CUPS/WWC2000/SCORECARDS/AUS-WOMEN_NZ-WOMEN_WWC2000_WODI-FINAL_23DEC2000.html)

## Clark’s departure left a different chase

Australia’s seventh wicket fell at 150 when Clark was dismissed. The winning target was 185, leaving 35 to find with three wickets standing. Australia subsequently added 30 before losing their last wicket.

This makes the four-run result easier to understand. A score of 184 would only have drawn level; Australia needed 185 to win. The final score was therefore five short of victory but four below New Zealand’s total.

Clark supplied approximately 50.6% of Australia’s runs, calculated as 91 ÷ 180 × 100. That comparison includes extras in the team total. It shows the scale of her contribution without treating her innings as the entire chase.

## Restriction mattered alongside wickets

Clare Nicholson took the final wicket, Charmaine Mason caught by Rebecca Rolls. Katrina Keenan’s ten overs had cost only 19 runs and brought two wickets. The scorecard records Australia’s innings ending at 49.1 overs, but also notes a five-ball 12th over. Its over notation should therefore not be converted mechanically into an exact innings delivery count. [Scorecard and scoring notes](https://i.imgci.com/link_to_database/ARCHIVE/WORLD_CUPS/WWC2000/SCORECARDS/AUS-WOMEN_NZ-WOMEN_WWC2000_WODI-FINAL_23DEC2000.html)

Keenan’s economy rate was 1.90 runs per over. In a four-run result, that restriction is important even though the last dismissal supplies the most memorable ending.

## A reversal, not a repeat

Australia had beaten New Zealand by six wickets in the tournament opener. The final reversed that result and marked the end of Debbie Hockley’s international career. [ICC tournament history](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/womens-world-cup-a-trip-down-memory-lane)

The useful contrast is between an earlier meeting and the match that decided the title. The opener established neither a guaranteed winner nor a fixed margin for the final.
