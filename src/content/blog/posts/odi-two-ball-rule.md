In men’s ODIs, the fielding team chooses one of its two already-used balls after over 34. It does not receive another new ball. Women’s ODIs retain alternate-ball use without that scheduled choice. Before deciding which rule applies, also check whether the entire match was shortened before the first innings began.

## Choose the right branch

| Situation under the October 2026 ICC conditions | Ball allocation |
| --- | --- |
| Men’s ODI without the pre-start exception below | Two new balls, alternating ends through over 34; choose one used ball for the remaining overs |
| Women’s ODI without that exception | Two new balls used in alternate overs, one from each end |
| Either format reduced to 25 overs or fewer per side before the first innings starts | One new ball for each innings |

The governing provisions are [men’s clauses 4.3.1–4.3.3](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/bxkzkcyvfn6tvi70zh0w.pdf) and [women’s clauses 4.3.1–4.3.2](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/qunpw1kfh3ibxlz58jlw.pdf). In the men’s game, the unselected ball joins the replacement supply rather than remaining the regular ball at its original end.

## Hypothetical allocation in a full innings

Call the original balls A and B. Assume a complete 50-over innings with no replacements. After 34 overs, each has served 17 overs. If a men’s fielding side selects A, the final 16 overs all use A: its allocation becomes 33 overs, while B remains at 17. The check is 33 + 17 = 50.

For a women’s innings under the ordinary alternating arrangement, A and B instead finish with 25 overs apiece. These are allocation examples, not claims that every ball wears identically. They do not predict reverse swing or guarantee a bowling advantage.

A men’s innings ending after 40 overs would give the selected ball six additional overs after the choice, not 16: 17 + 6 = 23. The other remains at 17. “Last 16 overs” describes a full 50-over innings, not a fixed entitlement in every match. [ICC’s explanation of the selection rule](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/several-changes-made-to-icc-playing-conditions).

## Rain timing changes the answer

**Hypothetical example:** rain delays the start, and officials reduce the match to 25 overs per side before anyone begins the first innings. Each innings uses one new ball under the exception. At 26 overs per side, that specific exception does not apply.

Now change the scenario: a match starts with a longer allocation, then rain reduces the chase to 25 overs. The number 25 alone does not meet the before-first-innings condition. Do not infer a new one-ball arrangement merely from the revised chase length. [Women’s conditions, clause 4.3.2](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/qunpw1kfh3ibxlz58jlw.pdf).

## A replacement is a different decision

A lost or normally worn ball that umpires judge unfit can be replaced with one of comparable wear. That umpire-controlled procedure is separate from the scheduled men’s selection; it is not a right to restart with a fresh ball. Both documents cover it in clause 4.4.

## When did the men’s change start

The ICC announced the change on 27 June 2025, with the ODI conditions commencing on 2 July 2025 in the Sri Lanka–Bangladesh series. October 2026 identifies the current conditions edition, not the original introduction date. Use the applicable competition and version when comparing older matches; do not automatically extend ICC ODI arrangements to every domestic one-day event. [ICC announcement](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/several-changes-made-to-icc-playing-conditions).
