India beat Pakistan by **64 runs at Edgbaston on 14 June 2026** in the Women's T20 World Cup. India made 170/6 after choosing to bat, then dismissed Pakistan for 106 in seventeen overs. This was the Group A match in Birmingham, not the women's Asia Cup meeting or a men's fixture. [BCCI match record](https://www.bcci.tv/matches/228862/More/scorecard).

Deepti Sharma's five wickets decided the chase, but the match was not one-way from the start. Pakistan scored faster in the opening six overs, and India had needed a substantial batting recovery.

## Mandhana and Kaur repaired the early damage

India lost Shafali Verma in the first over and Jemimah Rodrigues in the fourth. Smriti Mandhana and Harmanpreet Kaur added 91 for the third wicket, moving the score from 18/2 to 109/3. Mandhana made 68, while Kaur contributed 36. [ICC match report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/media-releases/sharma-stars-as-india-beat-pakistan-in-front-of-record-group-stage-crowd).

Richa Ghosh then supplied 34 from seventeen balls, a strike rate of 200. India were 123/5 when Kaur fell after 16.2 overs, yet added another 47 from the remaining 22 legal balls. That closing segment went at **12.82 runs per six balls**, calculated as 47 × 6 ÷ 22. [Full batting scorecard](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/121813/indw-vs-pakw-6th-match-group-a-icc-womens-t20-world-cup-2026).

The recovery and late acceleration were different tasks. The third-wicket partnership stopped the early losses from defining the innings; the finish turned a stable base into a demanding target.

## Pakistan won the first phase but not the chase

India ended the powerplay on 38/2. Pakistan reached 52/1, fourteen runs ahead at the same stage and with one fewer wicket lost. That promising opening did not survive the middle overs. [Powerplay and wicket ledger](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/121813/indw-vs-pakw-6th-match-group-a-icc-womens-t20-world-cup-2026).

From there, Pakistan added only 54 while losing their remaining nine wickets in eleven overs. Their actual scoring rate dropped from **8.67 per over** in the first six to **4.91** afterwards. These are phase rates for the innings, not tournament net-run-rate calculations.

Muneeba Ali top-scored with 41 but was run out by Deepti. Fatima Sana and Natalia Pervaiz followed, leaving Pakistan 79/6 in the thirteenth over. [ICC chase account](https://www.icc-cricket.com/media-releases/sharma-stars-as-india-beat-pakistan-in-front-of-record-group-stage-crowd).

## Deepti and Charani combined wickets with control

Deepti finished with 5/10 and Sree Charani with 3/21. Together they took eight wickets for 31 runs across eight overs, a combined economy of **3.88** after rounding. [ICC final bowling figures](https://www.icc-cricket.com/media-releases/sharma-stars-as-india-beat-pakistan-in-front-of-record-group-stage-crowd).

Deepti's involvement in Muneeba's run-out was an additional fielding contribution, not a sixth wicket in her bowling figures. Her five-for and Charani's three-for account for eight bowler-credited dismissals; they should not be inflated by adding a run-out to either spell.

For an analyst, the contrast between the opening phase and the final total is the central lesson. A quick powerplay established opportunity, but Pakistan still needed batters to stay in and score against the later bowling combination.

## A result with a large audience

The ICC's later audience report said this fixture generated **more than 77 million viewing hours worldwide**, making it the most-watched Women's T20 World Cup match in the competition's history at that point. Viewing hours measure cumulative consumption, not 77 million separate spectators or people at the ground. [4 November audience report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/media-releases/record-global-audience-and-93-8m-impact-cap-landmark-women-s-t20-world-cup).

The historical result remains straightforward: India's batting recovery set 171 to win, and its bowlers turned a competitive opening to the chase into a 64-run victory.
