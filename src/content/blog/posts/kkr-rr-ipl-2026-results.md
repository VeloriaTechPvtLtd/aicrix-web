Kolkata Knight Riders beat Rajasthan Royals by four wickets at Eden Gardens on 19 April 2026. Rajasthan made 155/9; Kolkata reached 161/6 in 19.4 overs, with Rinku Singh finishing unbeaten on 53 and Anukul Roy on 29. The winning six carried the score beyond the target of 156.

This was their only completed meeting in IPL 2026. ## Rajasthan lost their opening advantage

Rajasthan chose to bat. Vaibhav Sooryavanshi scored 46 and Yashasvi Jaiswal 39, but the innings changed after their opening stand. Varun Chakaravarthy took 3/14, Sunil Narine 2/26 and Kartik Tyagi three wickets. These are the principal batting and bowling contributions in the [official IPL report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/tata-ipl-2026-match-28-kkr-vs-rr-match-report).

The important distinction is between a fast opening and a strong completed total. Rajasthan had runs on the board without losing a wicket, but could not preserve that combination. Once the partnership was broken, repeated dismissals prevented the remaining overs from becoming an uninterrupted acceleration phase.

The scorecard records the first wicket at 81 after 8.4 overs. Rajasthan added only 74 more across the remaining 68 legal balls. That later segment ran at approximately 6.53 runs per over, compared with 9.35 across the first 52 balls. These are descriptive rates, not proof that the surface changed or that one tactical choice alone caused the slowdown.

## Kolkata rebuilt from six wickets down

KKR also encountered trouble: both openers were dismissed for ducks, and the sixth wicket fell at 85 after 13.3 overs. Rinku and Anukul then put on an unbeaten 76. At 85/6, Kolkata needed 71 from 39 legal balls, approximately 10.92 per over. Their final partnership scored at 12.32 per over across 37 balls. It was not simply a case of staying together while an easy equation took care of itself: the pair had to score faster than the overall target's initial requirement.

Rinku's unbeaten 53 was his whole innings, not his contribution to that partnership. Likewise, adding his final score to Anukul's would wrongly give the stand as 82. Partnership totals count only runs scored while those two batters were together, including extras. The scoreboard difference, 161 minus 85, gives the correct 76.

## Rinku finished the chase in the final over

Kolkata entered the final over needing nine. Rinku's two boundaries brought the scores level, and his subsequent six completed the win with two deliveries unused, as the official report describes. The four-wicket margin records the wickets remaining; it does not mean the chase was comfortable throughout.

The season result was therefore KKR one win, RR none, from one meeting. The useful lesson from the match is narrower than an all-time rivalry claim: Kolkata first reduced the value of Rajasthan's opening stand, then overcame its own loss of six wickets through a high-scoring final partnership. Neither achievement supplies a reliable probability for a future fixture with different players or conditions.

## Read the result without confusing the measures

The target, finishing total and victory margin answer different questions. Kolkata needed 156 to win, but the boundary that ended the game took the recorded score to 161. That does not turn the result into a six-run win: when the chasing side succeeds, the four wickets still available determine the wicket margin. The two balls remaining describe time left, not runs saved. Keeping those fields separate gives a clearer account than comparing 161 and 155 alone.

The opening stand and the finishing stand also occurred under different constraints. Rajasthan were building a first-innings total with all ten wickets initially available. Kolkata’s final pair were pursuing a known target with only four wickets remaining. Their scoring rates help describe those passages; they are not interchangeable tests of which pair was better.
