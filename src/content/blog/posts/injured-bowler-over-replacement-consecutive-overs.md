---
title: "Who can finish an injured bowler’s over"
excerpt: "Work out who may complete an interrupted over, why the previous bowler is excluded and how the replacement affects the next over."
status: draft
---

If a bowler cannot finish an over, the bowler who delivered the previous over cannot simply take over. The restriction concerns adjacent overs in the innings, including shared overs, rather than how rested a player feels.

Under [MCC 2026 Law 17.6–17.8](https://www.lords.org/getattachment/e8b12929-1e84-4927-af4e-2a5875b53589/Laws-of-Cricket-2017-Code-4th-Edition-%282026%29.pdf), a bowler normally finishes the over they start. Incapacity or suspension permits another bowler to finish from the same end, but no player may bowl consecutive overs or parts of consecutive overs. Changing ends does not remove that restriction.

The practical check therefore has two directions: look backwards before choosing the replacement, then forwards before choosing the next bowler.

## Work through the adjacent overs

Consider this fictional Test innings. A bowls all six valid deliveries of over 20. B then bowls two valid deliveries of over 21 before becoming unable to continue. C is available, has not bowled over 20 and has no separate eligibility restriction. The innings continues; there are no miscounts or other interruptions.

| Decision | Answer in this example | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Can A finish over 21? | No | A already bowled over 20 |
| Can C finish over 21? | Yes | C did not bowl the preceding over and is otherwise eligible |
| Can C bowl over 22? | No | C has now bowled part of over 21 |
| Can A bowl over 22? | Yes, if otherwise eligible | Over 21 separates A’s two overs |

A workable sequence is therefore A; B plus C; A. It is not A; B plus A; C. The second sequence places A in two adjacent overs even though A would bowl only four deliveries in the second of them.

The replacement also changes the captain’s next decision. Selecting C to finish over 21 uses C in that over. A plan to have C deliver over 22 must be revised; the small size of C’s contribution does not create an exception.

## One delivery is enough to affect eligibility

Change the fictional incident: B completes five valid balls of over 21, and C finishes with just one. C still participated in over 21, so C cannot deliver over 22. The comparison is about participation in adjacent overs, not whether the replacement delivered a full six.

The ledger reads A: six balls in over 20; B: five in over 21; C: one in over 21. Six plus five plus one equals twelve valid deliveries across two team overs. Recording twelve balls alone loses the information needed to decide who can bowl next.

For that task, keep the innings identifier, team-over number and every bowler used in each over. An aggregate bowling analysis cannot by itself show the order in which those deliveries occurred.

## An interruption does not create a fresh over

The [ICC men’s Test conditions effective October 2026, clauses 17.7–17.8](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/a8114zro5tkioifz0jqg.pdf), require an unfinished over to be completed when play resumes unless the innings has ended. Incapacity or suspension provides the replacement route. An interval is not a tactical licence to swap a capable bowler midway through an over.

Suppose B’s two-ball portion is followed by rain. On resumption in the same innings, four valid balls remain in that over. If B can continue and is not suspended, B finishes it. If an eligible C must complete it, the preceding-over check still applies. Moving the restart to the next morning does not change over 21 into a new innings.

These examples assume the officials have already established that B cannot continue where a replacement is required. A spectator should not diagnose that condition from a player stretching, walking slowly or receiving brief attention.

## Do not confuse eligibility with a bowling allowance

Two questions can produce different answers: does this player have overs left, and may this player bowl this particular over? A spare allowance does not overcome the adjacent-over restriction.

Limited-overs quota accounting and changes of bowling arm or over/round mode are separate questions. Neither resets the identity recorded in the over sequence.

The answer to an interrupted-over question starts with the scorebook: which innings, which over, who bowled immediately before it, and who has already contributed to the current one? Then apply the competition’s eligibility rules before naming the replacement.
