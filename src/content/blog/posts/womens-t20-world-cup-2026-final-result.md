## Australia won by seven wickets at Lord’s

Australia won the 2026 Women’s T20 World Cup by beating England by seven wickets at Lord’s on 5 July. England made 150/4 from 20 overs; Australia reached 153/3 in 17.1 overs, with 17 legal balls remaining.

Beth Mooney’s 64 and Phoebe Litchfield’s 48 anchored the chase. Their century partnership gave Australia room to finish without needing a last-over rescue. The victory secured Australia’s seventh title in the competition. ## England recovered without taking control

England’s fourth wicket fell at 70 in the 11th over. Sciver-Brunt and Kemp then added an unbeaten 80 from 55 balls, taking the hosts to 150/4.

The partnership changed the shape of the innings. England did not continue losing wickets, and Kemp’s faster scoring helped lift the finish. Yet a recovery from early trouble is different from a total that necessarily puts the chasing side under severe pressure. [ICC innings account](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/womens-t20-worldcup-2026/news/england-australia-final-womens-t20-world-cup-2026)

An original calculation illustrates the change in pace. At 70/4 after 10.5 overs, England had used 65 legal balls and scored at 6.46 runs per over. The last 80 runs came from 55 balls at 8.73 per over. Together, those phases produce the final innings rate of 7.50.

The calculation uses legal balls, not the sum of every batter’s recorded balls faced. Those measures can differ when no-balls are involved. It also does not assign all partnership runs to the two batters: extras belong to the team total.

## Australia built a cushion early

England dismissed Georgia Voll at 17/1, but Mooney and Litchfield added 100 for the second wicket. Australia reached 62/1 after the powerplay and 117/2 when Litchfield was bowled at the end of the 13th over.

At the six-over mark, the target of 151 left Australia needing 89 from 84 legal balls. At 117/2 after 13 overs, the requirement was 34 from 42. These are calculations from the recorded scores, not claims that the remaining runs were guaranteed.

Mooney was dismissed at 140/3 after 16 overs. That left 11 required from 24 balls, and the chase ended at 153/3 in the 18th over. [Scorecard and fall of wickets](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/122033/engw-vs-ausw-final-icc-womens-t20-world-cup-2026)

The important feature is the sequence. Australia’s advantage was not simply that its highest scorer made six more runs than England’s. The second-wicket partnership had already reduced the required rate while leaving wickets available.

## Why the winning margin is seven wickets

Australia lost three wickets, leaving seven of the ten available wickets unused. That is the basis of the seven-wicket margin.

The innings notation 17.1 means 103 legal balls: 17 multiplied by six, plus one. Subtracting 103 from the 120-ball allocation gives 17 balls remaining. It does not mean 17.1 decimal overs.

The target was 151, although Australia finished on 153. A chasing side can pass the target on the winning delivery, so the final total and the target should not be silently made identical.

## What this final does and does not establish

The final shows how a stable partnership and an early scoring cushion can simplify a chase. It does not establish a permanent “chasing advantage” at Lord’s, or a guaranteed future winner between these teams.

For the completed match, the answer is straightforward: Australia’s 153/3 beat England’s 150/4. For player evaluation, retain the innings context and distinguish this final from Mooney’s full tournament, England’s preceding matches and later fixtures.

## Keep partnership runs separate from individual scores

Mooney and Litchfield’s second-wicket stand was worth 100, but their complete innings totalled 112. That is not a scorecard inconsistency. Mooney had scored before Litchfield arrived and remained after she departed, while any extras during the partnership were team runs. Adding both full innings is therefore not a valid way to reconstruct that stand.

The same distinction applies to England. Sciver-Brunt’s unbeaten 58 and Kemp’s unbeaten 44 total 102, but their fifth-wicket partnership added 80. Sciver-Brunt had already been batting before the fourth wicket fell. Read the fall-of-wicket positions and partnership ledger together rather than treating a batter’s entire innings as though it all occurred with one partner.
