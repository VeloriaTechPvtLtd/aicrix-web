## Identify the mode before checking a score

A second-innings fantasy score is not simply a smaller version of a full-match total. It answers a different question: which contributions belong to the specified part of the match? Checking that boundary first can explain a discrepancy before you recalculate any batting or bowling bonuses.

In the T20 section of [Dream11’s public guide](https://www.dream11.com/features/how-to-play), checked on 14 November 2026, the documented second-innings format uses five players without role or squad minimums. Only second-innings contributions determine its ranking, and announced-lineup points are excluded.

That rule description is not proof that the mode is currently offered for every match. Do not read an old description of prizes or cash returns as evidence about the current product. This article explains the public scoring reference, not account-specific availability.

## Keep two contribution columns

Consider an invented audit worksheet for two players. These are supplied illustrative base-point entries, not values calculated from Dream11’s scoring table, not predictions and not a complete five-player roster.

| Player | First innings entries | Second innings entries | Whole match arithmetic |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| A | 70 | 16 | 86 |
| B | 12 | 40 | 52 |

A has 70 + 16 = 86, while B has 12 + 40 = 52. But an innings-filtered comparison uses 16 and 40, reversing their order. Both additions are correct; they answer different questions.

To reproduce such a comparison, attach an innings identifier to each event before adding it. Keep the raw contribution ledger separate from the final display. If the ledger combines both innings, subtracting one headline batting score will not reliably repair it: the earlier entries might also contain bowling, fielding or adjustments.

## Read the deadline and exception together

The official guide places the normal lock at the end of the interval, before the second innings’ first ball. Its separate early-start protocol says an early restart with more than two overs bowled leads to abandonment; at two overs or fewer, the contest and declaration proceed as scheduled.

Do not turn that exception into a general permission to edit after play starts. Read the actual mode and displayed deadline rather than reuse a clock time from a different fixture.

For example, a clock time copied from a different fixture cannot establish this match’s lock. Keep the fixture identity beside the displayed deadline rather than turning one remembered time into a daily rule.

## A short reconciliation checklist

Save the match identity, mode label, rules-check date and the time of the points display. Then ask whether you are comparing the same innings, the same player and the same stage of calculation. Separate raw event entries from any later transformation rather than mixing them in one unexplained total.

If you report a discrepancy, supply those details and the specific differing entries. “The full scorecard total differs from this innings-only display” is a narrower, testable observation than “the scoring is wrong.” It leaves room to locate a filtering mistake without accusing a provider of a defect that has not been reproduced.

The useful habit is to establish scope before arithmetic. A carefully added total still answers the wrong question if it includes contributions outside the selected window.
