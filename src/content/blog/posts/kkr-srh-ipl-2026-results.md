## Kolkata brought an explosive start and a substantial recovery

KKR chose to field on 2 April. Travis Head made 46 from 21 balls and Abhishek Sharma 48 from 21, helping SRH reach 84/1 in the powerplay. Heinrich Klaasen’s 52 from 35 and Nitish Kumar Reddy’s 39 from 24 helped extend the innings after early wickets. [IPL match report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/tata-ipl-2026-match-6-kkr-vs-srh-match-report)

The fall-of-wickets ledger shows SRH at **118/4 after 9.2 overs**, then 226/8 at the finish. That means they added **108 runs in the remaining 64 legal balls**, losing four more wickets. The recovery matters as much as the opening burst: a score at the halfway stage is not the eventual total. [April scorecard](https://www.business-standard.com/cricket/ipl/kkr-vs-srh-6th-t20-indian-premier-league-tournament-2026-scorecard-95842)

KKR’s chase contained a 28 from seven balls by Finn Allen and 52 from 29 by Angkrish Raghuvanshi. But the side was dismissed with four overs unused. Jaydev Unadkat finished with 3/21, while the scorecard records two run-outs among the ten dismissals. A rapid individual start therefore did not translate into a completed chase.

## Hyderabad produced the opposite middle over pattern

A month later, SRH batted first again, this time after winning the toss. Head’s 61 from 28 and Ishan Kishan’s 42 from 29 gave the innings early momentum. KKR then reduced the total to 165, with Varun Chakaravarthy taking 3/36 and Sunil Narine 2/31. [IPL May report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/tata-ipl-2026-match-45-srh-vs-kkr-match-report)

The precise scorecard checkpoint is **105/2 after nine overs**, when Head was dismissed. From there SRH added **60 runs in ten overs and lost the remaining eight wickets**. That is a scoring rate of six per over across that segment, compared with approximately 10.13 for the final segment identified in the April innings.

Those are descriptive segments with different starting wickets and lengths, not like-for-like experiments proving one cause. They do show why two strong starts can lead to very different totals. ## KKR protected wickets in the return chase

KKR reached 169/3 in 18.2 overs. Raghuvanshi made 59 from 47, Ajinkya Rahane 43 from 36 and Rinku Singh an unbeaten 22 from 11. The target was 166 and the recorded final total was 169.

The notation **18.2 overs means 110 legal balls**, not 18.2 decimal overs. From a 120-ball allocation, that leaves ten balls, matching the reported margin. The seven-wicket result and the ten-ball remainder describe different things: wickets still available and unused deliveries.

Raghuvanshi’s two scores also illustrate why a strike rate needs context. His quicker April fifty came in a losing chase of 227; his slower May fifty helped complete a chase of 166. Neither innings should be labelled successful or unsuccessful solely by comparing its strike rate with the other.

## What this two match archive can establish

It establishes a 1–1 season split and records how each result happened. It does not establish that the away team has a durable advantage in this matchup, that a venue always behaves the same way, or that a particular player will repeat the performance.

For a future fixture, retain the dated scorecards as background and check the new teams, roles and conditions separately. For these completed matches, keep the two rows together: reversing the team order should not create a second near-identical archive.
