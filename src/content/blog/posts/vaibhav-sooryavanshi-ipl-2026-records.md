Vaibhav Sooryavanshi finished IPL 2026 with **72 sixes**. The often-cited figure of 65 describes an earlier stage of Rajasthan Royals’ playoff campaign, not the completed season. The IPL’s season compilation explicitly identifies all 72 sixes. [Official compilation listing](https://www.iplt20.com/videos/s-all-72-sixes-by-vaibhav-sooryavanshi-in-tata-ipl-2026-oi97ga)

## How 65 became 72

In the Eliminator on 27 May, he made 97 from 29 balls against Sunrisers Hyderabad, including 12 sixes. That took his running season tally to 65. Rajasthan won by 47 runs and advanced. [Official Eliminator report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/tata-ipl-2026-eliminator-srh-v-rr-match-report)

Two days later, he made 96 from 47 balls against Gujarat Titans in Qualifier 2, with seven sixes. Rajasthan lost by seven wickets, ending their campaign. [Official Qualifier 2 report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/tata-ipl-2026-qualifier-2-gt-vs-rr-match-report)

The reconciliation is **65 + 7 = 72**. The earlier report was a snapshot taken before another innings, not a competing final tally.

## Boundary contribution and the previous benchmark

The official boundary counts yield:

- Sixes: 72 × 6 = **432 runs**, or 55.7% of 776.
- Fours: 63 × 4 = **252 runs**.
- All boundaries: **684 runs**, or 88.1% of his total.

Those are derived contributions, not additional records. Dividing 327 balls by 72 gives one six per 4.54 balls across the season; it does not imply evenly spaced sixes in individual innings.

Chris Gayle’s earlier IPL single-season benchmark was 59 sixes in 2012. The final margin was therefore 13, not the six visible immediately after the Eliminator. [Historical comparison](https://www.cricbuzz.com/cricket-news/138972/the-statistical-madness-of-vaibhav-sooryavanshi-in-ipl-2026)

The scope matters: this is an IPL single-season sixes record, not a career tally or an all-format claim.
