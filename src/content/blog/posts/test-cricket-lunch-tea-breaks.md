You return to a Test broadcast at the advertised restart time, but the players are still in the pavilion. Or you expect lunch and find the last batting pair still defending. The useful question is not simply how long the break lasts. First establish whether the interval has started, whether its timing changed, and which match conditions apply.

## The normal durations

In the checked men's Test conditions, lunch lasts 40 minutes and tea 20 minutes, measured between the calls of Time and Play. An approved alternative split can retain a combined 60 minutes. The ordinary innings interval is ten minutes, subject to overlap provisions. [ICC conditions, 11.2](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/a8114zro5tkioifz0jqg.pdf).

| Information to check | Why a viewer needs it |
| --- | --- |
| Match-local time | Avoid mixing a broadcaster's local clock with the venue's clock |
| Originally advertised interval | Establish the baseline rather than relying on memory |
| Actual call of Time | Distinguish a delayed start from an unusually long break |
| Official revised restart | Use the latest announcement, not an old fixture card |
| Reason for departure | Keep a meal interval separate from rain, injury or an innings change |

Treat this as a viewing checklist, not an automated umpiring decision. A score widget saying “break” is less informative than a timed official update.

## Why lunch can start early

The MCC framework takes lunch immediately when an innings ends within ten minutes of its agreed time, including the innings interval within lunch. Tea has a corresponding thirty-minute threshold. If an innings interval is already underway at that tea threshold, the ten-minute interval can finish and play resume instead. [MCC 2026 Laws, 11.5–11.6](https://www.lords.org/getattachment/e8b12929-1e84-4927-af4e-2a5875b53589/Laws-of-Cricket-2017-Code-4th-Edition-%282026%29.pdf).

This is why adding every displayed break duration together is a poor reconstruction method. Record the sequence before calculating the restart. Did an innings end? Were players already off? Was a new meal time announced? Without those observations, a confident countdown can be less helpful than “restart awaiting confirmation.”

For an actual fixture, use its applicable conditions and official announcement. The MCC comparison explains the structure; it is not a substitute for the competition document.

## Why nine wickets down can delay the meal

MCC Law 11.7 extends play when nine wickets are down three minutes before the interval, or the ninth falls in the specified window through the over in progress at the interval. The stopping point becomes the end of the over underway thirty minutes after the original time, unless the innings finishes or players must leave sooner. A retirement is not treated as a wicket for this provision. [MCC 2026 Law 11.7](https://www.lords.org/getattachment/e8b12929-1e84-4927-af4e-2a5875b53589/Laws-of-Cricket-2017-Code-4th-Edition-%282026%29.pdf).

The viewer's first check is therefore the wicket count and timing, not whether the batters look ready to leave. Avoid describing the extension as a punishment, or claiming a captain simply refused lunch, without evidence of what officials applied.

## A separate route when the match is nearly finished

ICC clause 12.8.2 permits a fifteen-minute extension, with at least four overs, at either captain's request if umpires believe a result is achievable. The nine-wicket provision can follow this extension, but the reverse order is not permitted. Check the complete clause for continuation and time-adjustment details. [ICC conditions, 12.8.2–12.8.3](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/a8114zro5tkioifz0jqg.pdf).

Do not label every late lunch “the nine-down rule.” A broadcast explanation should identify whether it concerns finishing an innings or finishing the match. Those are different objectives even when the pictures look similar.

## How to make a useful live update

A clear update answers three questions: what happened, what is known about the restart, and where that information came from. For example:

“Players have left the field. The official update identifies this as lunch; the revised restart has not yet been announced.”

This illustrative wording deliberately contains no invented clock time. Once a confirmed time is available, replace the uncertainty with that time and its zone. If your original post contained the old schedule, correct it rather than leaving two conflicting countdowns visible.

For friends watching from different countries, share the same reference instant or include both venue time and the audience's time zone. If a match crosses a date boundary for the audience, include the date as well. A useful notification is something another person can act on without reconstructing your location.

## What not to infer from a break

A delay alone is not evidence of misconduct, a tactical advantage or a change in win probability. Nor does it establish that a fantasy contest has reopened. These are separate claims needing their own evidence.

For a retrospective match article, keep the original schedule, observed stoppage and revised schedule separate in your notes. A screenshot captured later can display the revised time while an earlier report retains the original one. That difference is worth investigating before calling either source wrong.

The simplest habit is also the most reliable: follow the official restart notice, and use the rulebook to explain it rather than to invent a live update.
