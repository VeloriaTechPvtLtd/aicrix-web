Royal Challengers Bengaluru and Gujarat Titans each won a chase in their two April 2026 IPL meetings. RCB reached 206/5 in Bengaluru on 24 April; GT replied with a four-wicket win in Ahmedabad on 30 April, finishing at 158/6. The outcomes look similar at a glance, but the targets and late-innings pressures were quite different. [IPL Bengaluru report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/tata-ipl-2026-match-34-rcb-vs-gt-match-report), [IPL Ahmedabad report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/tata-ipl-2026-match-42-gt-v-rcb-match-report)

These are the April league matches, not the later playoff meetings. Choose the date as well as the team names when opening a scorecard.

## Bengaluru required a sustained scoring effort

Sai Sudharsan’s 100 from 58 balls gave GT a substantial base on 24 April. RCB then received 81 from 44 balls from Virat Kohli and 55 from 27 from Devdutt Padikkal. Those two innings supplied 136 of the 206 runs in the chase, or approximately 66.0% of the final team total. That calculation includes the team’s extras in the denominator, rather than pretending every run came from a batter. [IPL match report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/tata-ipl-2026-match-34-rcb-vs-gt-match-report), [full batting card](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/151889/rcb-vs-gt-34th-match-indian-premier-league-2026)

The chase still required a finish. At 173/5 after 15.3 overs, RCB needed 33 runs from 27 balls to reach the 206 target. The required rate at that point was about 7.33 per six-ball over.

That position illustrates why the first-innings total does not tell the whole chase story. A target above 200 can become comparatively manageable if earlier batting has already reduced the rate requirement. Wickets remain important, but they interact with the number of runs and balls left.

Krunal Pandya and Tim David completed the task without another wicket falling. The final score came after 18.5 overs, so the unbeaten pair added the remaining 33 runs over 20 legal deliveries. The team did not need to reach the end of the twentieth over. [RCB innings and fall of wickets](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/151889/rcb-vs-gt-34th-match-indian-premier-league-2026)

## Ahmedabad offered time but still cost wickets

Six days later, GT’s bowlers dismissed RCB for 155. Arshad Khan took 3/22, Rashid Khan 2/19 and Jason Holder 2/29. Holder also took three catches and was named Player of the Match. This was not simply a repeat of the Bengaluru batting contest at a lower venue total. GT had removed all ten wickets and reduced the chase target to 156. [IPL Ahmedabad report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/tata-ipl-2026-match-42-gt-v-rcb-match-report)

Shubman Gill made 43 from 18 balls and Jos Buttler 39 from 19. Together, those contributions were 82 runs from 37 balls faced. They are a combined batting contribution, not an opening partnership: Sai Sudharsan opened alongside Gill, and Buttler batted at number three. [GT batting order and figures](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/151965/gt-vs-rcb-42nd-match-indian-premier-league-2026)

GT reached 111/5 after 10.3 overs. From there it needed 45 from 57 balls, a required rate of approximately 4.74. Losing five wickets was meaningful, but the remaining rate requirement was far lower than the original target might suggest in isolation.

Rahul Tewatia finished unbeaten on 27 from 17 balls as GT reached 158/6 in 15.5 overs. The winning total exceeded the 156 target; a successful chase need not finish on exactly the target when the winning scoring shot takes it beyond that number. [Ahmedabad scorecard](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/151965/gt-vs-rcb-42nd-match-indian-premier-league-2026)

## Comparing equivalent chase situations

| Match | Five wickets down | Runs still needed | Legal balls left | Required runs per six-ball over |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Bengaluru | 173/5 after 15.3 | 33 | 27 | 7.33 |
| Ahmedabad | 111/5 after 10.3 | 45 | 57 | 4.74 |

These are calculations from the two scorecards, not win probabilities. Although both teams had five wickets left at the selected moments, GT had 30 more deliveries available and needed only 12 more runs than RCB did in the comparison. Treating “five wickets down” as an identical level of danger would discard that context.

The exercise also shows the limits of two-match analysis. Both chases succeeded, but that does not prove chasing is always preferable at either venue. Team composition, target, conditions and the phase in which wickets fall all matter.
