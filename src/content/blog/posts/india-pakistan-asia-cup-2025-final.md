**India beat Pakistan by five wickets in the men's Asia Cup final at Dubai International Cricket Stadium on 28 September 2025.** Pakistan were dismissed for 146; India reached 150/5 with two balls remaining. Tilak Varma finished unbeaten on 69 and was Player of the Match. [Completed match record](https://cricketdatabase.com/matches/2025-09-28-pakistan-v-india).

The result was not a straightforward chase of a small total. Pakistan built a substantial opening stand before their batting fell away; India then lost three early wickets. The final turned on which middle order could rebuild without leaving too much to do at the end.

India won the toss and fielded. The winning target was 147, not 146; the final score exceeded it because Rinku Singh struck a boundary to finish. Pakistan's innings ended after 115 legal deliveries and India's after 118. These were two completed innings, not rain-reduced allocations. ## Pakistan lost their platform after the opening stand

Farhan and Fakhar Zaman put on 84 for the first wicket. Farhan made 57 and Fakhar 46, but Pakistan could not turn their start into a strong finish. At halfway they were 87/1; Kuldeep Yadav eventually returned 4/30. [Mumbai Indians match report](https://www.mumbaiindians.com/news/asia-cup-2025-final-india-vs-pakistan-report-mumbai-indians-cricket).

The sharper collapse began from 113/1. Saim Ayub's dismissal made it 113/2, and Pakistan finished at 146: nine wickets fell while only 33 runs were added. Varun Chakaravarthy dismissed both openers; Axar Patel took two wickets and Jasprit Bumrah two, alongside Kuldeep's four. [Dismissals and fall of wickets](https://cricketdatabase.com/matches/2025-09-28-pakistan-v-india).

This was more than a late slowdown. Losing established batters repeatedly forced new players to begin their innings, while the team also lost the chance to use its last five scheduled deliveries. The score alone hides how much stronger Pakistan's position had looked earlier.

## Two partnerships repaired India's chase

Faheem Ashraf removed both Indian openers, with Shaheen Afridi taking Suryakumar Yadav between those dismissals. India were 20/3 after four overs. Tilak and Sanju Samson added 57 before Samson fell for 24; Tilak and Shivam Dube then added 60 before Dube's dismissal for 33. [Chase and partnership ledger](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/130179/asia-cup-2025-final).

| Recovery stand | Team score movement | Runs added |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Tilak and Samson, fourth wicket | 20/3 to 77/4 | 57 |
| Tilak and Dube, fifth wicket | 77/4 to 137/5 | 60 |

The stands solved different problems. The first kept the chase alive after the top-order losses. The second carried it into the last over with ten needed. Together they added 117, but that is partnership scoring, not the sum of the three batters' complete innings: Tilak had already started before Samson joined him.

At ten overs, India were only 58/3, needing 89 from the remaining 60 balls. That required 8.90 per over, compared with 7.35 at the start. Survival had preserved the chase, but acceleration was still necessary. [Halfway checkpoint](https://www.mumbaiindians.com/news/asia-cup-2025-final-india-vs-pakistan-report-mumbai-indians-cricket).

## Tilak stayed to complete the job

Tilak's 69 included three fours and four sixes. Samson and Dube's supporting scores mattered because the chase could not be completed by protecting wickets indefinitely. Mumbai Indians' contemporary report records all three contributions rather than presenting the win as a one-batter innings. [Match report](https://www.mumbaiindians.com/news/asia-cup-2025-final-india-vs-pakistan-report-mumbai-indians-cricket).

After Dube fell at 137 on the last ball of the nineteenth over, Tilak and Rinku added 13 from four deliveries to finish. Rinku faced just one ball and scored four. A short appearance can therefore decide a result without representing a large batting workload. [Closing partnership](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/130179/asia-cup-2025-final).

The most useful contrast is the treatment of a setback. Pakistan's early platform did not survive the middle-order collapse. India's early collapse was followed by two substantial stands and a completed chase. This dated final should not be mistaken for either team's current form or a prediction for their next meeting.

## Read the final over against the target

At 137/5, India needed ten runs to reach 147. The closing partnership scored thirteen because the winning boundary took the total beyond the target. These statements are compatible: runs required describe the position before the over, while runs added describe what actually happened. The extra three runs do not create a larger wicket margin.

The five-wicket result records how many wickets India had in hand when the chase ended. The two balls remaining describe unused deliveries. Neither number measures how comfortable the chase had been at 20/3. Keeping those details together gives a more accurate account than calling the win easy simply because five wickets remained.
