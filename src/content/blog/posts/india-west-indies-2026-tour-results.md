**India won the ODI series against West Indies 2–1 in September and October 2026. The five-match T20I series finished 2–2, with the final game producing no result.** The tour ran from 27 September to 17 October: three ODIs first, followed by five T20Is.

The two series need separate scorelines. Adding every win together would describe a mixed-format tour tally, not the result of either individual competition.

India had secured the ODI series after the second match, before West Indies won the third. All three ODIs were won by the chasing team, but the winning side and size of the target changed.

The first match ended after 41.4 overs of India’s chase. At Guwahati, India reached 406/2 in 43.3 overs. West Indies completed the New Chandigarh chase in 48.2 overs. Those are cricket over notations: the final digit counts legal balls, not a decimal fraction.

## Why the Guwahati chase stands out

West Indies’ 405/7 set India a target of 406. India reached exactly that target with **39 legal balls remaining**, calculated as 300 minus the 261 balls in 43.3 overs. Shubman Gill scored 223 from 133 balls and was Player of the Match. [BCCI second-ODI record](https://www.bcci.tv/matches/18dbf036-8619-4610-abbc-2ea9b4725320/ind/match-details).

Gill’s 223 supplied approximately **54.9% of India’s 406**, using the team total including extras as the denominator. His personal strike rate from those inputs was approximately **167.67** runs per hundred balls.

These figures describe that innings. They do not, by themselves, establish a venue’s normal scoring level or an all-time chase record. The three ODI first-innings totals were 295, 405 and 351, so even this short tour did not present an identical batting task each time.

The sequence was India, West Indies, India, West Indies, then no result. Neither side won consecutive completed T20Is.

At Ranchi, West Indies chased a target of 250 and finished on 252/4 in 18.3 overs, with nine legal balls unused. Shai Hope’s 102 from 45 balls earned the match award. [BCCI Ranchi record](https://www.bcci.tv/matches/237258/More/match-details).

India’s two totals of 223 came in different circumstances. At Indore, 223 was enough to defend against 209/7. At Hyderabad, the same total fell short of West Indies’ 247/3. An isolated batting total cannot tell you the match result without the opposing innings.

## The final game was not a tie

Bengaluru ended with India on 118/5 after 7.4 overs. The official result is **no result**, not a tied match, a West Indies chase win or a cancelled fixture that never began. [BCCI final-match record](https://www.bcci.tv/matches/b567f7d4-7455-48b3-8ec2-f76ce4788e70/india-vs-west-indies/match-details).

That distinction preserves both the event and the series arithmetic. Cricket was played, but there was no match winner. The four earlier T20Is supplied two wins each, so the series remained 2–2.

For historical comparisons, keep this incomplete innings out of a sample advertised as five completed 20-over first innings. It contained only **46 legal balls**; treating 118 as a full twenty-over total would distort the comparison.

## Read the format before the statistic

The ODIs and T20Is had different innings limits, sequences and series outcomes. Compare ODI totals with ODI totals, and T20I totals with T20I totals.

For the final answer to “who won?”, use **India 2–1 in ODIs; T20Is drawn 2–2 with one no-result**. That is more informative than a combined tour win count and identifies exactly what happened at the end.
