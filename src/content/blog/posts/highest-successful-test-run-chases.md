West Indies’ 418/7 against Australia in Antigua in 2003 leads the successful men’s Test fourth-innings totals in the record ledger checked on 13 November 2026. Four winning innings reach 400. But the table needs two score columns: the target and the total actually recorded at the finish are not always identical.

India finished on 406/4 in Port of Spain in 1976 while chasing 403. Australia’s 404/3 at Headingley in 1948 completed a target of 404. India therefore sits higher when the comparison uses final scores, but Australia sits higher when it uses targets successfully pursued.

## The four successful fourth innings of at least 400

The [ACS fourth-innings ledger](https://stats.acscricket.com/Records/Test/Overall/Team/Highest_Fourth_Innings_Totals.html) lists every total of at least 300 and marks the match outcome separately. Filtering for wins and then for totals of 400 or more gives the following four entries. Dates identify when each Test began, not the day its chase finished.

| Winning side | Opponent and venue | Match began | Target | Final score | Victory margin |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| West Indies | Australia, Antigua | 9 May 2003 | 418 | 418/7 | 3 wickets |
| South Africa | Australia, Perth | 17 December 2008 | 414 | 414/4 | 6 wickets |
| India | West Indies, Port of Spain | 7 April 1976 | 403 | 406/4 | 6 wickets |
| Australia | England, Headingley | 22 July 1948 | 404 | 404/3 | 7 wickets |

The next winning total in that ledger is West Indies’ 395/7 against Bangladesh in 2021, below the inclusive 400 threshold. A score of exactly 400 would qualify; no such winning entry appears between these records and 395/7.

## Reconstruct the target from the first three innings

For these four matches, the target is the opponent’s two innings combined, minus the chasing side’s first innings, plus one. This is not the same calculation as reading the fourth-innings total backwards and assuming that number was required.

| Chase | Opponent first innings | Chaser first innings | Opponent second innings | Target calculation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [West Indies 2003](https://www.cricketsky.com/australia-vs-west-indies-4th-test-west-indies-vs-australia-2003-live-score-54589/) | 240 | 240 | 417 | 240 + 417 − 240 + 1 = 418 |
| [South Africa 2008](https://www.cricketsky.com/australia-vs-south-africa-1st-test-australia-vs-south-africa-2008-live-score-55092/) | 375 | 281 | 319 | 375 + 319 − 281 + 1 = 414 |
| [India 1976](https://www.cricketsky.com/india-vs-west-indies-3rd-test-west-indies-vs-india-1976-live-score-55554/) | 359 | 228 | 271 | 359 + 271 − 228 + 1 = 403 |
| [Australia 1948](https://www.cricketsky.com/australia-vs-england-4th-test-england-vs-australia-1948-live-score-54606/) | 496 | 458 | 365 | 496 + 365 − 458 + 1 = 404 |

India’s first-innings deficit was 131 runs. Adding West Indies’ second-innings 271 made the aggregate gap 402, so India needed 403 to win. Its final 406 was three above that requirement. Australia, by contrast, began its chase after a first-innings deficit of 38 and England’s additional 365: 403 to tie, 404 to win.

That changes the order without changing either match record. Ranked by successful target, the four are 418, 414, 404 and 403. Ranked by final total, they are 418, 414, 406 and 404. A useful record table declares which measure determines its order rather than presenting the two as interchangeable.

## What held the chases together

The [ICC’s historical retrospective](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/the-five-highest-successful-run-chases-in-test-history), published on 7 February 2021, describes different routes to these totals. Ramnaresh Sarwan and Shivnarine Chanderpaul made centuries in Antigua before Omari Banks and Vasbert Drakes completed the win. At Perth, AB de Villiers finished unbeaten on 106 after contributions including a Graeme Smith hundred.

In the two earlier games, Sunil Gavaskar and Gundappa Viswanath made centuries for India, while Arthur Morris and Don Bradman shared a 301-run stand for Australia. The common feature is sustained scoring across a long innings, not one universal recipe for chasing. These are historical achievements, not evidence that a present target is attainable because it resembles an old one.

## A large fourth innings is not necessarily a successful chase

England’s 654/5 against South Africa in 1938–39 was a draw. New Zealand’s 451 against England in 2001–02 was a defeat. South Africa’s 450/7 against India in 2013–14 was another draw. All belong in the ACS list of large fourth innings; none belongs in this list of victories.

The outcome filter must therefore come before the score ranking. Nor does this four-match table establish a success rate for targets above 400: that would require all relevant attempts, including defeats and draws, rather than just the winning innings.

## Compare like with like

Use final totals when the question is which winning fourth innings scored the most runs. Use targets when the question is which requirement a side successfully chased. Keep both columns when the wording is simply “highest run chase”.

A memorable chase need not reach this numerical boundary. Match narratives explain particular achievements; the threshold table answers a narrower record question.
