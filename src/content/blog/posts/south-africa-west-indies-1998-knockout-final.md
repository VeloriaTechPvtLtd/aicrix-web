South Africa beat West Indies by four wickets in Dhaka on 1 November 1998 to win the first edition of the competition later known as the Champions Trophy. Jacques Kallis’s bowling and Hansie Cronje’s unbeaten innings shaped different halves of the final.

## Wallace’s hundred, then Kallis’s five wickets

Philo Wallace scored 103 from 102 balls. His dismissal left West Indies at 180 for four; the innings ended at 245. Kallis took five for 30, while Cronje accounted for Wallace with Mark Boucher completing the stumping. [Contemporary scorecard](https://img.cricinfo.com/link_to_database/ARCHIVE/1998-99/OD_TOURNEYS/WIC/SCORECARDS/RSA_WI_WIC_ODI-FINAL_01NOV1998.html)

The change from 180 for four to 245 all out was 65 runs for the last six wickets. That is the numerical basis for describing South Africa as having contained the later innings, without needing an unsupported claim about the pitch or dressing-room mood.

## South Africa had to rebuild too

A target of 246 was not a straightforward chase. South Africa moved from 118 for two to 137 for five before Cronje and Dale Benkenstein added 74. When Benkenstein fell at 211, Derek Crookes joined Cronje for the last 37 runs.

Cronje finished on 61 not out and Crookes on 24 not out. South Africa reached 248 for six in 47 overs: three overs remained, and the winning total exceeded the target because a chase need not stop on the exact target score.

## A knockout title, not a league-table finish

The ICC’s history describes the 1998 event as a knockout tournament staged entirely in Dhaka. South Africa defeated England and Sri Lanka before meeting West Indies in the final. Kallis was player of the tournament as well as player of this match. [ICC tournament history](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/icc-champions-trophy-history)

The final was therefore more than a successful chase attached to a famous bowling figure. Kallis helped restrict what South Africa needed; Cronje’s successive stands completed the task after three middle-order wickets had reopened the contest.
