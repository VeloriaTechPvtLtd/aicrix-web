**West Indies beat England by four wickets in the men’s World T20 final at Eden Gardens on 3 April 2016.** England made 155/9; West Indies reached 161/6 after 19.4 overs. Marlon Samuels finished on 85 not out, and Carlos Brathwaite hit four consecutive sixes in the final over. [Match scorecard](https://www.mykhel.com/cricket/west-indies-vs-england-scorecard-icc-world-t20-s9925-900959/) · [ICC’s account of the finish](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/postpe-greatest-moments-the-finalists).

The target was 156. The winning boundary took West Indies beyond it, so 161 was the final score rather than the amount they originally needed. The match ended with two legal balls unused, not after the full twentieth over.

## England recovered before losing three wickets in four balls

England lost Jason Roy, Alex Hales and Eoin Morgan early, reaching 23/3. Joe Root and Jos Buttler then added 61 before Buttler departed for 36. Root eventually made 54 from 36 balls, England’s highest score. [Full batting and wicket ledger](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/15804/final).

The next major change came across the four legal deliveries from 13.4 to 14.1. Dwayne Bravo dismissed Ben Stokes and Moeen Ali, before Brathwaite removed Root. The score moved from 110/4 before that sequence to 111/7. The [ICC’s contemporary report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/sensational-samuels-brathwaite-hand-west-indies-second-title) identifies this cluster as the interruption to England’s attempted acceleration.

David Willey contributed 21 from 14 balls, while Chris Jordan and Adil Rashid finished unbeaten. England added 44 after Root’s dismissal, using the remaining 35 legal balls. That is a team rate of 7.54 runs per six balls: a meaningful recovery, but not an uninterrupted late assault.

## Samuels kept the chase alive

England also struck early. Root dismissed Johnson Charles and Chris Gayle in his only over, and Willey trapped Lendl Simmons for nought. West Indies were 11/3 after 2.3 overs. Samuels and Bravo’s fourth-wicket stand then added 75, taking them to 86/4 after fourteen overs. [Chase ledger](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/15804/final).

That stand rebuilt the innings but did not remove the scoring pressure. It occupied 69 legal balls, leaving a requirement of 70 from 36. Willey then removed Russell and Sammy in the sixteenth over, making the next partnership essential rather than optional.

## Why three sixes were not enough

The final over began with Brathwaite facing Stokes and 19 required. The ICC’s retrospective confirms that Brathwaite struck four consecutive sixes. [ICC](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/postpe-greatest-moments-the-finalists).

| Final over point | West Indies score | Remaining winning requirement |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Start | 137/6 | 19 from 6 balls |
| First six | 143/6 | 13 from 5 |
| Second six | 149/6 | 7 from 4 |
| Third six | 155/6 | 1 from 3 |
| Fourth six | 161/6 | Match won |

Three sixes added 18 and only levelled England’s 155. The fourth completed the chase. Brathwaite scored 24 in those four deliveries, having made ten from his first six balls; those are two segments of the same innings, not separate appearances.

Samuels and Brathwaite’s unbeaten partnership was 54 from 25 legal balls, from 107/6 at 15.3 to 161/6 at 19.4. Its rate was 12.96 per six balls. Samuels’s survival and Brathwaite’s finish describe different jobs within that stand: the four sixes explain the ending, but not the entire recovery from 11/3.
