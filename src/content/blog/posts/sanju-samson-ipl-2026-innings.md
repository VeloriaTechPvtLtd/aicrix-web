## The two Delhi innings and the Mumbai hundred

Samson's first hundred came after scores of 6, 7 and 9. On 11 April, he made 115 not out from 56 balls as CSK reached 212/2 and beat Delhi Capitals by 23 runs. His innings included 15 fours and four sixes. The official report also records his 45 from 19 balls in the opening Powerplay, so this was not simply a late-innings surge. [IPL match report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/tata-ipl-2026-match-18-csk-vs-dc-match-report).

The second century was 101 not out from 54 balls against Mumbai at Wankhede on 23 April. It combined ten fours and six sixes. The [Cricbuzz player scorecard](https://www.cricbuzz.com/player-match-performance/match/151878/player/8271/batting?inningsId=1) distinguishes the unbeaten innings from a score of 101 with a dismissal; that status is essential to the season average.

The return meeting with Delhi produced 87 not out from 52 balls on 5 May. This time the task was a chase. CSK reached 159/2 in 17.3 overs after Delhi made 155/7, and Samson shared an unbeaten 114-run stand with Kartik Sharma. [Official IPL report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/tata-ipl-2026-match-48-dc-v-csk-match-report).

Those are three different match situations. Combining them is appropriate for a season total, but it would be misleading to treat an unbeaten successful chase as if the batter had necessarily exhausted his scoring opportunity.

## How concentrated were the runs

The three biggest innings were also the three unbeaten innings:

115 + 101 + 87 = 303 runs.

That is **63.5% of the season total** from three of 14 innings. They used 162 balls, a combined strike rate of 187.04. The remaining innings supplied 174 from 126 balls, a combined rate of 138.10.

These are descriptive splits calculated from the ledger, not two estimates of Samson's future ability. Selecting only the large scores would overstate his typical return; deleting them would understate the very performances that made the season valuable. Showing both the contribution and the remainder avoids either distortion.

His median innings score was 15.5: the middle two values in the ordered 14-score list were 11 and 20. That describes the middle of the observed score distribution. It does not replace batting average, predict a next score or account for unbeaten innings in the same way as average.

## Average and strike rate answer different questions

The correct season calculations are:

- Batting average: 477 runs ÷ 11 dismissals = 43.36.

- Runs per innings: 477 ÷ 14 = 34.07.

- Strike rate: 477 ÷ 288 × 100 = 165.63, rounded to two decimal places.

A table can therefore show 34.07 without its runs total being wrong, but calling that number his batting average would be wrong. Similarly, the arithmetic produces 165.625 before rounding; displays of 165.62 or 165.6 need not imply a different runs/balls ledger. Compare the underlying counts before declaring a statistical disagreement.

Across the season, 53 fours and 24 sixes accounted for 356 runs, or 74.6% of his total. That is a boundary-run share, not the percentage of deliveries hit for boundaries. [Cricbuzz season totals](https://www.cricbuzz.com/player-match-performance/match/151878/player/8271/batting?inningsId=1).

## The finish to the campaign

After the unbeaten 87 against Delhi, his final four innings were 28, 20, 27 and 0. Together they produced 75 runs from 48 balls, a combined strike rate of 156.25. That rate should be read alongside the modest total and four dismissals, rather than used as a standalone statement that his late-season form was strong or weak.
