## How this rolling table should be updated

Count batting innings, not every match on a schedule. A DNB or abandonment before batting must not become a zero. Once another T20I batting innings is completed, add it at the top and remove the oldest included innings; then recalculate runs, balls and dismissals together.

The present numbers describe this specific ten-innings sample. They do not predict a fantasy score, guarantee a fifty or settle a selection debate.

## Reading the ten-innings boundary

The next older T20I entry in the dated ledger is 61 from 28 balls against Pakistan at Nisshin on 3 October. It is outside this window, even though it is close in time to the Lucknow innings. Adding it would create an eleven-innings sample. The boundary is therefore determined by batting appearances, not by choosing the most productive performances.

For this sample, average and runs per innings are both 34.20 because there are no not-outs. That equality is specific to these ten rows. It would disappear if an unbeaten innings entered the window. The aggregate scoring rate gives every ball equal weight; it does not give the two-ball Hamilton innings the same weight as the 38-ball Wellington innings.
