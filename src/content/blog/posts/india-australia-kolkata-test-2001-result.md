**India won the Kolkata Test against Australia by 171 runs on 15 March 2001 after following on.** The second Test at Eden Gardens ran from 11 to 15 March and levelled the three-match series at 1–1. VVS Laxman was Player of the Match. [Contemporary scorecard](https://img.cricinfo.com/link_to_database/ARCHIVE/2000-01/AUS_IN_IND/SCORECARDS/AUS_IND_T2_11-15MAR2001.html).

The comeback required two different achievements. Laxman and Rahul Dravid first gave India enough runs to declare; the bowlers then had to dismiss Australia before the match ended. The batting partnership made victory possible, but did not itself secure it.

## The four innings in playing order

| Innings | Score | Overs | Position after the innings |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Australia first | 445 all out | 131.5 | India began their reply |
| India first | 171 all out | 58.1 | Australia led by 274 and enforced the follow-on |
| India second | 657/7 declared | 178 | Australia needed 384 to win |
| Australia second | 212 all out | 68.3 | India won by 171 runs |

The follow-on changed the usual alternating order: India batted twice consecutively. The [complete innings ledger](https://img.cricinfo.com/link_to_database/ARCHIVE/2000-01/AUS_IN_IND/SCORECARDS/AUS_IND_T2_11-15MAR2001.html) keeps those two Indian innings separate. Adding 171 and 657 gives 828; subtracting Australia's first-innings 445 leaves a lead of 383. One more run made the winning target 384.

## Australia's lead survived Harbhajan's hat trick

Harbhajan Singh dismissed Ricky Ponting, Adam Gilchrist and Shane Warne on consecutive deliveries in Australia's first innings. Yet Australia recovered from 269/8 to 445, with Steve Waugh making 110. India then fell for 171 despite Laxman's 59. [Scorecard and wicket sequence](https://img.cricinfo.com/link_to_database/ARCHIVE/2000-01/AUS_IN_IND/SCORECARDS/AUS_IND_T2_11-15MAR2001.html).

The last two Australian wickets added 176. That recovery is easy to lose when recalling only the hat-trick: a spectacular bowling passage had not prevented Australia from building a commanding total. India's eventual second innings therefore had a substantial deficit to erase before any winning target could exist.

## Laxman and Dravid changed the match across three days

Laxman moved to number three for India's second innings after batting lower in the first. He finished with 281 and Dravid with 180; their fifth-wicket partnership was 376. BCCI's retrospective records that they batted through the entire fourth day, 14 March. [BCCI annual report, printed page 18](https://documents.bcci.tv/documents/bcci/documents/1638361467_BCCI%20Annual%20Report%202011-12.pdf).

The partnership began at 232/4, when India were still 42 behind overall, and ended at 608/5 on the final morning. By its conclusion India were 334 ahead. The stand had first removed the remaining deficit and then created the opportunity to set Australia a target. [Fall of wickets](https://img.cricinfo.com/link_to_database/ARCHIVE/2000-01/AUS_IN_IND/SCORECARDS/AUS_IND_T2_11-15MAR2001.html).

Day four itself was only part of that stand. India moved from 254/4 overnight to 589/4 at stumps, adding 335 without losing a wicket. Those are team scores, not the partnership's starting and finishing scores; the complete 376-run stand began on day three and continued into day five. [Daily close-of-play record](https://img.cricinfo.com/link_to_database/ARCHIVE/2000-01/AUS_IN_IND/SCORECARDS/AUS_IND_T2_11-15MAR2001.html).

That distinction matters when remembering the match. Laxman's 281, Dravid's 180, the partnership's 376 and the fourth day's 335 describe four different quantities. Summing the two individual innings does not reproduce the stand because runs were also scored outside their time together.

## The declaration still left a bowling job

India declared at 657/7, setting 384 with 75 overs available. Australia reached 166/3 before the innings broke open. Steve Waugh and Ponting fell at that score, and Australia slipped to 174/8. [Contemporary final-day report](https://img.cricinfo.com/link_to_database/ARCHIVE/CRICKET_NEWS/2001/MAR/081140_AUSIND_15MAR2001.html).

Harbhajan finished with 6/73 in the chase, adding to his first-innings 7/123 for match figures of 13/196. Sachin Tendulkar took three second-innings wickets, dismissing Gilchrist, Matthew Hayden and Warne. [Bowling and dismissal ledger](https://img.cricinfo.com/link_to_database/ARCHIVE/2000-01/AUS_IN_IND/SCORECARDS/AUS_IND_T2_11-15MAR2001.html).

Australia's last two wickets resisted, but the innings ended at 212. The margin follows the overall lead, not the target: 383 minus 212 is 171. Australia would have required another 172 to win, which is a different number.

The result levelled the series; it did not complete it. BCCI's retrospective records that India subsequently won the deciding Test in Chennai. Kolkata stands as a complete match in its own right: a deficit overcome, a declaration made and ten final-innings wickets taken.
