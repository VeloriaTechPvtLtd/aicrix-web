Virat Kohli leads the checked IPL career outfield-catch record with **126 catches**. Ravindra Jadeja follows with 110 and Suresh Raina with 109. Five fielders meet the inclusive 100-catch threshold in the 31 May 2026 season-end table. [Dated CricTracker table](https://www.crictracker.com/most-catches-by-a-fielder-in-ipl-history/), [CricPrism’s explicitly keeper-excluded table](https://www.cricprism.com/cricket-records/ipl/most-catches).

The category is important. This is not a ranking of every catch by every IPL player, nor of all dismissals credited to wicketkeepers. A catch taken while keeping belongs to a different fielding-role total. Stumpings and run-outs must not be added to this catch column.

## What an outfield record includes

“Outfield” here means catches taken as a fielder rather than as wicketkeeper. It does not mean only catches near the boundary. A catch at slip, in the inner circle or by the bowler off their own delivery can belong in the non-keeper category.

The clean way to classify a catch is the player’s role for that event. A player who has sometimes kept wicket should not automatically lose every catch from a non-keeping appearance. Conversely, a familiar batter’s name does not establish that all their catches were outside the keeping role.

That is why the source’s definition matters as much as its headline. CricPrism explicitly separates catches taken while keeping. CricketViz reproduces the same leading catch totals, but agreement between published lists does not itself establish an independent event-by-event classification audit.

## Why appearances are not shown

The ranking deliberately reproduces only the agreed catch totals. CricTracker labels Kohli’s denominator as 281 innings, while CricketViz shows 282 matches. Those are different labels and cannot be treated as interchangeable exposure measures.

A catches-per-match calculation would need a consistent appearance definition. A catches-per-fielding-innings calculation would need actual fielding innings. Neither denominator can be silently borrowed from another table simply because its number looks plausible.

Even a correctly calculated rate would not be a catch-conversion percentage. Conversion requires the number of catchable chances offered, including missed chances, under an explicit classification method. Total completed catches alone supplies no such denominator.

## How to compare two fielders fairly

Use this table for the narrow historical question: who accumulated the most IPL catches outside wicketkeeping? For a broader performance comparison, keep three separate layers:

- **Volume:** completed catches within the same competition and cutoff.

- **Opportunity:** fielding innings, time on the field and the types of positions occupied.

- **Execution:** chances taken and missed, with a consistent difficulty assessment.

The second and third layers are not measured here. A larger career total therefore should not be rewritten as proof that someone is a better catcher, saved more runs, or offers a guaranteed fantasy advantage.

A player can also lead a particular season without leading a career table. The separate IPL 2026 catch-leaders article includes wicketkeeper catches and answers a different question. Combining its all-catches definition with this keeper-excluded career record would produce an inconsistent comparison.

## Updating this record

Retain the season-end date whenever the figures are reused. A later refresh should inspect all five qualifying rows and the next players below 100, not merely change the leader’s number. If two totals become equal, they should share rank unless the page explicitly adopts and explains another sorting rule.

For historical lookup, these five totals and the 99-catch boundary are the useful answer. They are not a live fielding feed or a forecast of future opportunities.
