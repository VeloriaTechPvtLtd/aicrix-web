## West Indies won the final by 36 runs

West Indies beat Sri Lanka by 36 runs in the men’s World Twenty20 final at R Premadasa Stadium, Colombo, on 7 October 2012. Marlon Samuels’s 78 from 56 balls helped rescue the first innings; Sri Lanka were then dismissed for 101 while chasing 138. [ICC’s contemporary report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/samuels-special-the-spur-for-epic-west-indies-victory-x2808).

West Indies had chosen to bat. Their 137/6 occupied the full 20 overs, whereas Sri Lanka’s innings ended at 18.4 because all ten wickets had fallen. It was not a reduced-overs match. Samuels was Player of the Match. [Match scorecard](https://sports.ndtv.com/cricket/live-overview/sl-vs-wi-final-colombo-slwi0710201292010).

## A recovery after 32 runs in ten overs

West Indies were 14/2 after the powerplay and 32/2 halfway through their innings. Gayle had made three from 16 balls. Samuels and Dwayne Bravo’s third-wicket stand added 59, before further wickets interrupted the recovery. Sammy ultimately contributed an unbeaten 26. [ICC report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/samuels-special-the-spur-for-epic-west-indies-victory-x2808) and [batting ledger](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/11180/sl-vs-wi-final-icc-world-t20-2012).

The halfway checkpoint makes the recovery measurable: West Indies added 105 in their final ten overs, compared with 32 in the first ten. The corresponding team rates were 10.50 and 3.20 runs per over. Those equal-length segments show how misleading it would be to judge the completed innings from its opening half alone.

That acceleration did not erase Sri Lanka’s successes. Ajantha Mendis continued taking wickets, including Pollard and Russell on consecutive balls. Samuels was eventually caught off Akila Dananjaya, a different bowler from either Ajantha or Jeevan Mendis. Keeping those identities separate matters when reading an abbreviated scorecard.

## Sri Lanka’s bowling figures tell two different stories

Ajantha Mendis finished with 4/12 and Angelo Mathews with 1/11, both from four overs. Lasith Malinga’s four overs cost 54 without a wicket. Samuels’s attack on Malinga was a central part of the recovery, rather than evidence that every bowler suddenly became easy to score from. [ICC](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/samuels-special-the-spur-for-epic-west-indies-victory-x2808) and [full bowling figures](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/11180/sl-vs-wi-final-icc-world-t20-2012).

The other-bowler figure is 135 total bowling runs conceded minus 54. West Indies also received two leg-byes, which belong in the team total but not against the bowlers. Malinga delivered one-fifth of the overs yet conceded 40% of the runs charged to the attack. This describes one final, not his general ability or a forecast for another opponent.

## A manageable target became a difficult chase

After Dilshan’s early dismissal, Jayawardene and Sangakkara added 42. Sangakkara fell at 48/2 after 9.3 overs. From there Sri Lanka needed 90 from 63 balls, approximately 8.57 per over. They then lost five further wickets while adding only 21 in the next 30 legal balls, reaching 69/7 at 14.3. [Wicket checkpoints](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/11180/sl-vs-wi-final-icc-world-t20-2012).

The decisive contrast was therefore not simply 137 versus 101. Sri Lanka still had eight wickets available at 48/2, but the next five overs depleted that batting resource while increasing the remaining requirement. The score and the wickets need to be read together.

Kulasekara’s 26 from 13 balls supplied late resistance. Narine finished with 3/9 from 3.4 overs and Sammy with 2/6; two Sri Lankan dismissals were run-outs, so not all ten wickets belonged to bowlers. The chase ended eight legal balls before its allocation was exhausted. [Full scorecard](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/11180/sl-vs-wi-final-icc-world-t20-2012).

West Indies’ total had needed defending even after the recovery. Their batting created that opportunity, and their wickets prevented Sri Lanka from using the full chase. The 36-run margin records the result; it does not turn 137 into a universal winning score at Colombo.
