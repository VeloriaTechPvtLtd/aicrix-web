India won the **2002 NatWest Series final by two wickets with three balls remaining** at Lord’s on 13 July. England made 325/5; India reached 326/8 in 49.3 overs. Mohammad Kaif finished unbeaten on 87 and was player of the match. This was the final of the England–India–Sri Lanka triangular ODI series. [Contemporary scorecard](https://img.cricinfo.com/db/ARCHIVE/2002/OD_TOURNEYS/NWS/SCORECARDS/ENG_IND_NWS_ODI-FINAL_13JUL2002.html).

## Final score and leading contributions

| Team | Total | Main batting contributions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| England | 325/5 in 50 overs | Nasser Hussain 115; Marcus Trescothick 109; Andrew Flintoff 40 |
| India | 326/8 in 49.3 overs | Mohammad Kaif 87 not out; Yuvraj Singh 69; Sourav Ganguly 60 |

England’s 325 was the first-innings total; **326 was India’s target**. The win was by wickets remaining, not by one run. Kaif’s match award was also separate from the player-of-the-series award, which went to Trescothick. [Scorecard and awards](https://img.cricinfo.com/db/ARCHIVE/2002/OD_TOURNEYS/NWS/SCORECARDS/ENG_IND_NWS_ODI-FINAL_13JUL2002.html).

## England built around two centuries

After choosing to bat, England moved from 42/1 to 227/2 through a 185-run partnership between Trescothick and Hussain. Flintoff then made 40 from 32 balls. Zaheer Khan took three wickets, but England still completed their allocation with 325, requiring India to score at more than 6.5 runs per over from the outset. [Ralph Dellor’s contemporary report](https://img.cricinfo.com/db/ARCHIVE/2002/OD_TOURNEYS/NWS/SCORECARDS/ENG_IND_NWS_ODI-FINAL_13JUL2002_LIVE_MR.html) and [full bowling ledger](https://img.cricinfo.com/db/ARCHIVE/2002/OD_TOURNEYS/NWS/SCORECARDS/ENG_IND_NWS_ODI-FINAL_13JUL2002.html).

That second-wicket partnership should not be confused with the two centurions’ combined scores of 224. Trescothick had already scored before Hussain arrived, and Hussain continued after his partner was dismissed. A partnership counts only the team’s runs while that pair bats together.

## A rapid opening gave way to five wickets for 40

Ganguly and Virender Sehwag added 106 before Ganguly fell for 60 at 14.3 overs. Sehwag made 45, but India then lost Dinesh Mongia, Rahul Dravid and Sachin Tendulkar. Tendulkar’s dismissal left the chase at 146/5 after 24 overs. India had gone from 106 without loss to five down while adding just 40. [Contemporary innings ledger](https://img.cricinfo.com/db/ARCHIVE/2002/OD_TOURNEYS/NWS/SCORECARDS/ENG_IND_NWS_ODI-FINAL_13JUL2002.html).

The remaining requirement was **180 from 156 legal balls**, with only five wickets available. In his later BCCI interview, Kaif recalled taking encouragement from Yuvraj’s positive approach and concentrating on supporting him. That is a participant’s recollection of their approach, not evidence that victory had become likely. [Kaif’s BCCI interview](https://www.bcci.tv/news/article/batted-for-yuvi-in-lords-chase-kaif).

## The recovery did not finish when Yuvraj departed

Yuvraj and Kaif added 121 for the sixth wicket, taking India to 267/6 when Yuvraj was caught off Paul Collingwood for 69. India still needed 59 from 50 balls. Harbhajan Singh’s 15 helped Kaif add another 47, before Flintoff dismissed Harbhajan and Anil Kumble in the 48th over. [Scorecard](https://img.cricinfo.com/db/ARCHIVE/2002/OD_TOURNEYS/NWS/SCORECARDS/ENG_IND_NWS_ODI-FINAL_13JUL2002.html) and [contemporary report](https://img.cricinfo.com/db/ARCHIVE/2002/OD_TOURNEYS/NWS/SCORECARDS/ENG_IND_NWS_ODI-FINAL_13JUL2002_LIVE_MR.html).

The task changed sharply without the target changing. At 314/6 India needed only 12; two wickets later the score was still 314, but only two wickets remained. Kaif and Zaheer had to finish rather than merely preserve the recovery.

## How the last two overs produced the winning runs

India started the 49th over on 315/8, needing 11. Darren Gough conceded nine: Zaheer took a single, Kaif scored two and one, Zaheer faced a dot and took another single, then Kaif edged the final ball for four. India were 324/8, leaving two from the last over. [Contemporary ball-by-ball record](https://img.cricinfo.com/db/ARCHIVE/2002/OD_TOURNEYS/NWS/SCORECARDS/ENG_IND_NWS_ODI-FINAL_13JUL2002_BBB-COMMS.html).

Zaheer faced two dot balls from Flintoff. On the third delivery he pushed into the off side, and the pair completed a run before taking another after the throw missed the stumps. The two runs took India to 326/8. Kaif had hit the important boundary in the previous over, but **Zaheer faced the winning delivery**. Kaif’s later account describes both his dive to complete the first run and the subsequent overthrow. [BCCI interview](https://www.bcci.tv/news/article/batted-for-yuvi-in-lords-chase-kaif) and [contemporary commentary](https://img.cricinfo.com/db/ARCHIVE/2002/OD_TOURNEYS/NWS/SCORECARDS/ENG_IND_NWS_ODI-FINAL_13JUL2002_BBB-COMMS.html).

Lord’s commemorates the match on its Father Time Wall, including Ganguly’s balcony celebration. The scorebook explains what preceded that image: an opening platform, a five-wicket collapse, a substantial sixth-wicket recovery and a finish involving the lower order. It was not one uninterrupted partnership or a chase completed by Kaif alone.
