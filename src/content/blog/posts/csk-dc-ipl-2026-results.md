Chennai Super Kings won both of their IPL 2026 matches against Delhi Capitals: by 23 runs in Chennai on 11 April and by eight wickets in Delhi on 5 May. Sanju Samson was unbeaten in both innings, but the team assignments were different. CSK first defended a substantial total, then completed a chase with 15 balls remaining.

This is a two-match season record, not an all-time head-to-head table. The [completed IPL results ledger](https://tv9kannada.com/sports/cricket-news/series/ipl/results) lists these as matches 18 and 48, with no additional meeting in the playoffs.

Delhi won both tosses, choosing to field in Chennai and bat at home.

## Chennai: a large total still needed defending

Samson's unbeaten 115 from 56 balls and Ayush Mhatre's 59 powered CSK's innings. Their second-wicket stand added 113 before Mhatre retired out. Shivam Dube then finished with 20 not out. In reply, Delhi slipped from a 61-run opening stand to 76/4 after eight overs. Tristan Stubbs made 60, but Jamie Overton's 4/18 and Anshul Kamboj's three wickets helped close the defence. These details are documented in the [official match report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/tata-ipl-2026-match-18-csk-vs-dc-match-report).

Delhi's early scoring was not the whole problem. At 76/4, the target of 213 required another 137 from 72 balls, or 11.42 per over. Four wickets had already gone, leaving a steep rate to maintain with fewer batting resources. That combination explains more than simply comparing the sides' powerplay runs.

The final 23-run margin also should not be confused with the runs still required to win. A team finishing on 189 against 212 has lost by 23, but would have needed 24 more to reach 213. The [full Chennai scorecard](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/149790/csk-vs-dc-18th-match-indian-premier-league-2026) supplies the innings and wicket checkpoints used here.

## Delhi: recovery first, then an unbroken chase partnership

In the return game, DC were 69/5 after 11 overs. Stubbs and Sameer Rizvi added 65 for the sixth wicket; Rizvi finished unbeaten on 40. Samson then made 87 not out and Kartik Sharma 41 not out, sharing an unbroken 114 as CSK completed the chase. See the [official Delhi match report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/tata-ipl-2026-match-48-dc-v-csk-match-report).

CSK's second wicket fell at 45 after 6.3 overs. That left 111 needed from 81 legal balls, a required rate of 8.22. The final stand produced 114 in 66 balls, approximately 10.36 per over. It therefore exceeded the required pace rather than merely protecting wickets. The [Delhi scorecard](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/152031/dc-vs-csk) records those checkpoints. Here, 17.3 means 105 legal balls, not 17.3 decimal overs.

## Samson's two-match numbers, with the right limits

Across these meetings Samson scored 202 runs from 108 balls, a combined strike rate of 187.04. That is a useful season-rivalry statistic, not his full IPL 2026 record. Because he was not dismissed in either innings, a conventional runs-per-dismissal batting average cannot be calculated for this two-innings sample; calling it an average of 202 would be wrong.

The two totals are not equal measures of batting capacity. CSK used all 120 legal balls to make 212 in Chennai, while the Delhi innings stopped at 159 because the target had been passed. There were still 15 balls available, but awarding hypothetical runs for them would invent a total that never happened. The score and margin need to be read with the innings objective.

Nor do two wins prove a permanent matchup advantage. The archive shows CSK solving two different tasks, with Samson present to the end of each batting innings. It does not establish a winning probability, explain the results through the toss alone or supply today's team news.
