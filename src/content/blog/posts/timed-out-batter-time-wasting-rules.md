## A dismissal is not a five run penalty

Timed out can end an incoming batter’s innings. Batter time-wasting provisions use warnings and penalty runs. Although both concern readiness, they are not interchangeable sanctions: two warnings do not give every incoming batter permission to miss the timed-out deadline.

The [ICC’s 1 October 2026 announcement](https://www.icc-cricket.com/media-releases/new-icc-playing-conditions-bring-key-changes-across-formats) says the updated conditions apply to international series starting on or after that date. Series already underway retain their previous regulations. Identify the match’s governing conditions before applying a current rule to an older incident.

## How long does an incoming batter have

| October 2026 men’s format | Timed-out readiness limit | Source clause |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Test | 2 minutes | 40.1.1 |
| ODI | 2 minutes | 40.1.1 |
| T20I | 1 minute 30 seconds | 40.1.1 |

The period runs from the previous dismissal or retirement, unless Time has been called. The requirement concerns readiness to receive the ball, or readiness of the other batter to receive the next ball. It is not merely a deadline for entering the field. The [Test conditions](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/a8114zro5tkioifz0jqg.pdf) and [T20I conditions](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/bsxcho7ufe2pjban0wjp.pdf) deliberately use different limits.

Under the [ODI conditions](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/bxkzkcyvfn6tvi70zh0w.pdf), clause 31 requires an appeal before an umpire gives a batter out. Clause 40.2 says the bowler receives no credit for a timed-out wicket. It is therefore wrong to add one to a bowler’s figures simply because that bowler was due to deliver the next ball.

## Why older explanations mention three minutes

MCC’s [10 November 2023 statement](https://www.lords.org/lords/news-stories/mcc-statement-on-timed-out-in-men-s-cricket-world) explained Angelo Mathews’ dismissal against Bangladesh on 6 November. It contrasted the three-minute general Law then quoted with the two-minute ODI playing condition that governed that match. Mathews was given out on appeal.

The statement also explains why reaching the wicket is insufficient: the batter must be ready to receive the ball. Keep this historical explanation separate from the current format-specific limits; it does not create an automatic extra allowance for equipment problems.

## How batting time wasting is penalised

For the men’s conditions covered here, the first offence in an innings brings a warning, the second a final warning, and the third and each later offence five penalty runs to the fielding side. The striker must be ready when the umpires and fielding side can begin an over, and when the bowler is ready to start the run-up during it. Clause 41.10 does not establish one universal number of seconds between every delivery.

The warnings accumulate across the batting side: changing striker or introducing a new batter does not provide a fresh personal allowance. Clause 41.10.4 combines warnings for the covered forms of batting time wasting. The umpire informs the other batter and incoming batters. [Men’s ODI conditions, clause 41.10](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/bxkzkcyvfn6tvi70zh0w.pdf).

## A team warning example and the Test exception

Imagine an ODI in which Batter A causes the first time-wasting offence. Batter B later causes the second, and Batter C the third. The sequence is warning, final warning, then five runs to the fielding team, not one free warning for each player. This hypothetical concerns the innings-wide process, not three timed-out dismissals.

Tests add a specific qualification: accrued warnings reset after 80 completed overs and after every further completed block of 80 in that innings. Thus a first offence after the reset begins a new warning sequence. The reset does not undo penalty runs already awarded. [Men’s Test conditions, clause 41.10.3](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/a8114zro5tkioifz0jqg.pdf).

## Keep the fielding stop clock separate

The fielding side’s between-over stop clock is a different process. Its penalty runs go to the batting side, the opposite direction from batter time-wasting penalties. A scorecard explanation should identify which side delayed play and which clause was applied, rather than label every timing incident “timed out”. [Men’s T20I conditions, clauses 41.9.4 and 41.10](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/bsxcho7ufe2pjban0wjp.pdf).
