## A movable pitch is still a turf pitch

A drop-in pitch is not a roll of synthetic matting placed over an outfield. The surface can be living grass and prepared soil contained in a substantial transportable structure. The important difference is where it is prepared and how it is moved, not whether it has real turf.

Cricket Australia’s [October 2023 report from Karen Rolton Oval](https://www.cricket.com.au/news/3711018) describes two prefabricated turf pitches grown at Adelaide Oval and transported into the receiving ground. Each concrete trough measured 25 metres by three metres and weighed 30 tonnes. Those are the dimensions of that installation, not universal specifications for every drop-in pitch.

## What South Africa meant by exchangeable

In a [September 2026 interview](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-sports/south-africa-world-cup-cricket-new-exchangeable-pitches-10897803/), consultants Evan Flint and Hilbert Smit described South Africa’s Exchangeable Pitch System. Tray-grown surfaces remain within the ground and can rotate between a boundary-side location and a prepared position in the playing area. Flint described movement using a hovercraft system rather than the conventional nursery-and-lifting arrangement.

The distinction is logistical: the reported system manages a stock of surfaces at the ground. It does not create a universal second category of ball behaviour. Flint said venues retained their own soil, grass and construction methods. That description does not make every exchangeable surface identical.

## Drop in does not mean grown at the match venue

The [April 2024 account of New York’s World Cup project](https://www.cricket.com.au/news/3961231/adelaide-oval-damian-hough-role-new-york-t20-world-cup-nassau-county-cricket-stadium-eisenhower-park-india-pakistan) provides a different example. Damian Hough described pitches being grown in Florida because New York’s winter conditions did not suit the available growing timetable, before planned transport to the match and practice sites.

That account separates several operations: fabricating trays, adding and consolidating soil, establishing grass, transporting the sections and preparing them for play. A report that trays have arrived does not establish that the playing surface is ready. Nor does the place where a tray was fabricated identify where its turf was grown.

## Track the tray separately from its position

Imagine a fictional ground with two removable surfaces, Tray A and Tray B, and one central playing position, Position 1. The following is a record-keeping example, not a real venue history.

| Date | Surface in Position 1 | Event |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 January | Tray A | Match 1 played |
| 4 January | Tray A | Match 2 played |
| 8 January | Tray B | Tray A moved out; B installed |
| 10 January | Tray B | Match 3 played |

Position 1 has hosted three matches in this example, but Tray B has hosted only one of them. Calling the third match “the third use of this pitch” would confuse the slot with the physical surface. Conversely, moving Tray A does not erase its two-match history.

A useful archive therefore stores both identities and the movement date. If Tray A returns later, its history can follow it without relabelling Tray B’s match. When the actual tray is unknown, retain an unknown value rather than assigning every game at the ground to the same surface.

## Installation and match use are separate claims

Karen Rolton Oval’s 2023 report is also a reminder that one wicket block can contain both drop-in and conventional strips. A stadium-wide label need not identify the surface used for a particular fixture. [Cricket Australia report](https://www.cricket.com.au/news/3711018)

For the South African project, the September interview distinguished installation, domestic testing and readiness for major internationals. The consultants did not then confirm World Cup or Test use. Treat that as a dated project description, not confirmation of a particular 2027 match surface. [September interview](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-sports/south-africa-world-cup-cricket-new-exchangeable-pitches-10897803/)

When reading the next announcement, ask whether it confirms a plan, installation, testing or named-match use. Those are four different milestones. Follow the actual surface through them instead of treating “new pitch system” as a complete match report.
