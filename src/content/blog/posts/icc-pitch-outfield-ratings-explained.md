**An ICC pitch rating is an assessment of how a surface performed in a particular international match. It is not a forecast of the next match’s runs.** The match referee rates the pitch and outfield after the game, and the ICC provides that feedback to the home board. [ICC pitch and outfield overview](https://www.icc-cricket.com/about/cricket/rules-and-regulations/pitch-ratings).

That timing matters. A headline saying a ground was rated “unsatisfactory” may describe a specific Test months earlier. Before using it to understand another fixture, identify the match, date and any subsequent appeal.

### The four pitch labels

The ICC’s revised pitch scale uses **very good, satisfactory, unsatisfactory and unfit**. Its January 2025 explanation records the change from the older six-category system. [ICC explanation of the revised scale](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/icc-pitch-ratings-for-border-gavaskar-series-released).

“Unsatisfactory” and “unfit” are the substandard categories identified by the ICC’s current overview. They are not interchangeable with a commentator’s description of a slow, turning or high-scoring pitch.

For example, all four opening pitches in the 2024–25 Border-Gavaskar Test series were rated very good, while Sydney was rated satisfactory. Those labels apply to the pitches used for those matches, not a permanent grade for every strip or every format at those stadiums. [ICC’s series ratings](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/icc-pitch-ratings-for-border-gavaskar-series-released).

The pitch and outfield are assessed separately. A ground’s outfield concerns a different playing area from the central pitch. Do not silently copy a pitch label into the outfield column, or assume that a satisfactory rating for one establishes the other’s condition.

### Demerit points belong to the venue

The ICC’s 2026 sanction announcements describe the following consequences:

| Pitch assessment or accumulated record | Stated consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Unsatisfactory pitch | One demerit point |
| Unfit pitch | Three demerit points |
| Accumulation reaching six points | Twelve-month suspension from hosting international cricket |
| Accumulation reaching twelve points | Twenty-four-month suspension from hosting international cricket |

Demerit points remain active for a rolling five-year period. These are venue sanctions, not runs deducted from a team, player disciplinary points or World Test Championship points. [ICC June 2026 sanction explanation](https://www.icc-cricket.com/media-releases/king-city-pitch-in-toronto-rated-as-unfit).

A single one-point announcement therefore does not itself establish a ground ban. You need the venue’s applicable accumulated record, including later decisions, before concluding that a threshold has been reached.

Avoid importing sanction thresholds from an earlier version of the framework. A historical report can accurately describe the rules at its publication date without being a reliable statement of the current framework.

### What changed after the King City appeals

King City in Toronto provides a useful example of why the latest decision matters.

The ICC initially assigned one point for the USA–Netherlands pitch used on **8 June 2026**, then three for the Canada–Netherlands pitch used on **16 June**. The latter was rated unfit. In its June announcement, the referee described dangerous variable bounce and the decision to stop the match. [Original unfit-pitch announcement](https://www.icc-cricket.com/media-releases/king-city-pitch-in-toronto-rated-as-unfit).

On **20 August**, the ICC announced that the appeal had removed the one point associated with the 8 June match. The three points for the 16 June pitch remained. The published record at that decision was therefore **three, not four**. The same appeal announcement rescinded Gaddafi Stadium’s point for the 4 June Pakistan–Australia ODI. [ICC appeal outcome](https://www.icc-cricket.com/media-releases/demerit-point-on-gaddafi-pitch-rescinded).

Those are dated outcomes. They should not be rewritten as current lifetime totals without checking subsequent matches and decisions.

The arithmetic is simple, but the version history is essential: one plus three initially made four; removing the first point left three. Summing every old penalty headline would retain a sanction that no longer applied.

### How to read a venue rating record

Use a record that identifies the international match, match date, format and ground. Keep pitch and outfield assessments in different fields. If a decision changes, preserve the original event and record the appeal outcome beside it, rather than treating the appeal as another bad-pitch incident.

Also check the dates covered by the archive. On 12 November 2026, the ICC’s ratings page linked a consolidated file labelled through **30 September 2026**. That label does not establish that a November match had already been rated or included. [ICC ratings archive](https://www.icc-cricket.com/about/cricket/rules-and-regulations/pitch-ratings).

### What this means for match analysis

An official rating can provide useful historical context, especially when the decision explains a safety or surface-quality concern. It cannot supply tomorrow’s confirmed strip, player roles, weather or a numeric par score.

Keep two questions separate: **Was the pitch judged acceptable for the international match that was played?** And **what conditions are expected for the match being analysed now?** The first has a retrospective administrative answer. The second requires current match evidence.

A careful venue report can contain both, without turning an old grade into a guarantee about a batter, bowler or result.
