## Sri Lanka won the final by six wickets

Sri Lanka beat India by six wickets at Shere Bangla National Stadium in Mirpur on 6 April 2014. India finished on 130/4 from 20 overs; Sri Lanka reached 134/4 after 17.5. Kumar Sangakkara was unbeaten on 52 from 35 balls and received the match award. [Full final scorecard](https://sports.ndtv.com/cricket/sl-vs-ind-scorecard-live-cricket-score-icc-world-twenty20-2014-final-slin04062014175250).

The winning target was 131, not Sri Lanka’s eventual 134. The chase used 107 legal deliveries, leaving 13 unused. In cricket notation, 17.5 means seventeen completed overs and five balls, not seventeen and a half overs.

## Kohli supplied most of India’s runs

After Ajinkya Rahane fell for three, Kohli and Rohit Sharma rebuilt. Rohit made 29; Yuvraj Singh contributed 11 from 21 balls and MS Dhoni finished on four from seven. Kohli was run out on the final delivery. [Batting and wicket ledger](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/13118/ind-vs-sl-final-icc-world-twenty20-2014).

Kohli’s 77 represented 59.2% of the team’s 130. The other four batters together scored 47, with six extras completing the total. That concentration explains why his innings was so important, but his personal score alone does not describe India’s finishing rate or the distribution of deliveries late in the innings.

## The last four overs prevented acceleration

India scored only 19 in the final four overs. Dhoni’s post-match assessment credited Sri Lanka’s execution, particularly Malinga’s wide yorkers. The [contemporary PTI report](https://indianexpress.com/article/sports/cricket/sri-lanka-executed-their-plans-brilliantly-in-last-four-overs-says-mahendra-singh-dhoni/) records that explanation.

Subtracting 19 from 130 places India at 111 after 16 overs. The first sixteen therefore produced about 6.94 runs per over; the closing four produced 4.75. Those final 24 legal balls used one-fifth of the available innings while adding only 14.6% of its runs. India retained wickets, but could not turn that resource into a late scoring surge.

Kohli faced only eight deliveries in those last four overs, according to [Sky Sports’ contemporary analysis](https://www.skysports.com/cricket/news/9253246/world-twenty20-final-kumar-sangakkara-signs-off-from-sri-lankan-t20-cricket-in-style). That is a separate measure from his 58 balls across the entire innings. It helps explain why a strong individual total and a subdued team finish can coexist, without requiring an unsupported claim about what a different batting order would have scored.

## Four wickets kept the chase open

Sri Lanka lost Kusal Perera early, followed by Tillakaratne Dilshan and Mahela Jayawardene. Lahiru Thirimanne’s dismissal left them 78/4 after 12.3 overs. They still needed 53 from 45 legal balls, a required rate of approximately 7.07 per over. [Chase scorecard](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/13118/ind-vs-sl-final-icc-world-twenty20-2014).

That was higher than the original requirement of 6.55. India had not simply watched an uninterrupted procession to the target: four dismissals had left Sri Lanka needing a productive partnership. Sangakkara was still there, and Thisara Perera joined him rather than Angelo Mathews.

## The unbeaten stand closed the match

Thisara finished on 23 from 14 deliveries, including three sixes. Sangakkara’s innings included six fours and a six. Their fifth-wicket stand remained unbroken at 56. [Batting ledger](https://sports.ndtv.com/cricket/sl-vs-ind-scorecard-live-cricket-score-icc-world-twenty20-2014-final-slin04062014175250).

The stand’s endpoints were 78/4 and 134/4, and it occupied 32 legal deliveries, from 12.3 to 17.5. Its rate was therefore 10.50 per six balls. The partnership exceeded the 53 runs initially needed because the final total went past the target. It is not the sum of Sangakkara’s full 52 and Thisara’s 23: Sangakkara had already scored before they became partners.

The victory was Sri Lanka’s first men’s T20 world title and the last T20 international for Sangakkara and Jayawardene.
