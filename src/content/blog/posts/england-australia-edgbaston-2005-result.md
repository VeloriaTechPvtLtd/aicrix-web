England beat Australia by **two runs at Edgbaston on 7 August 2005**, levelling the men’s Ashes series at 1–1. Australia were dismissed for 279 while chasing 282, with Brett Lee unbeaten on 43. Andrew Flintoff was player of the match. The game was scheduled for 4–8 August but finished on its fourth day, so the five-day fixture label is not its actual playing duration. [Contemporary scorecard](https://img.cricinfo.com/db/ARCHIVE/2005/AUS_IN_ENG/SCORECARDS/AUS_ENG_T2_04-08AUG2005.html).

## All four innings

| Innings | Total | Overs |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| England first | 407 all out | 79.2 |
| Australia first | 308 all out | 76 |
| England second | 182 all out | 52.1 |
| Australia second | 279 all out | 64.3 |

England’s first-innings advantage was **99 runs**. Their second innings lifted that lead to 281, making Australia’s winning target 282: 407 + 182 − 308 + 1. Australia’s two innings totalled 587 against England’s 589, hence the two-run result. Needing three more to win is different from losing by three. [Full innings ledger](https://img.cricinfo.com/db/ARCHIVE/2005/AUS_IN_ENG/SCORECARDS/AUS_ENG_T2_04-08AUG2005.html).

## How England built and nearly lost their advantage

Australia chose to field after Glenn McGrath was injured during the warm-up. England’s opening pair added 112, and Marcus Trescothick’s 90, Kevin Pietersen’s 71 and Flintoff’s 68 helped produce 407 on the opening day. This was a substantial cushion, but not an uninterrupted march to victory. [Edgbaston’s retrospective](https://www.edgbaston.com/foundation/media-article/england-at-edgbaston-test-match-specials).

Justin Langer’s 82 and Ricky Ponting’s 61 helped Australia reach 308. England then fell to 31/4 and later 75/6 in their second innings. Shane Warne took 6/46, so even after conceding a first-innings lead Australia had a route back into the match. [Contemporary scorecard](https://img.cricinfo.com/db/ARCHIVE/2005/AUS_IN_ENG/SCORECARDS/AUS_ENG_T2_04-08AUG2005.html).

Flintoff’s 73 and Simon Jones’s unbeaten 12 were central to England escaping 131/9. Their last-wicket stand added **51**, taking the innings to 182. Those were partnership runs, not Flintoff’s personal contribution alone. This late resistance matters when comparing the two sides’ final pairs: Australia’s last wicket would add more, but still not enough. [Fall of wickets and batting ledger](https://img.cricinfo.com/db/ARCHIVE/2005/AUS_IN_ENG/SCORECARDS/AUS_ENG_T2_04-08AUG2005.html).

## The final morning began with 107 needed

Flintoff removed Langer and Ponting in the same over during the chase. Australia finished the third day on 175/8, leaving England two wickets from victory. For Australia, the task was still 107 runs. The ECB’s later replay introduction preserves that exact starting position rather than treating the final morning as a nearly completed chase. [Ground retrospective](https://www.edgbaston.com/foundation/media-article/england-at-edgbaston-test-match-specials) and [ECB archive introduction](https://www.ecb.co.uk/news/3234620).

Warne and Lee added 45 for the ninth wicket before Warne was out hit wicket for 42, bringing Michael Kasprowicz in at 220/9. Australia now needed 62. Lee and Kasprowicz added another 59, carrying the total to 279. The final two partnerships therefore supplied 104 runs, all on the fourth morning. [Contemporary ABC/AFP report](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2005-08-07/england-snatches-edgbaston-win---just/2076612).

## What happened to the final wicket

Kasprowicz was given out caught by wicketkeeper Geraint Jones off Steve Harmison for 20. The contemporary report describes a short delivery gloved down the leg side; the official result and scorecards record the catch, rather than a run-out or a wicket taken by Flintoff. Lee remained unbeaten, but with the tenth wicket down his innings could not continue. [ABC/AFP report](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2005-08-07/england-snatches-edgbaston-win---just/2076612) and [scorecard](https://img.cricinfo.com/db/ARCHIVE/2005/AUS_IN_ENG/SCORECARDS/AUS_ENG_T2_04-08AUG2005.html).

England’s last pair had added 51; Australia’s added 59, eight more. That isolated comparison does not determine a Test result: the first nine wickets and both earlier innings still count. Nor should Lee’s entire 43 be assigned to the last-wicket stand, because he was already batting alongside Warne.

The win restored parity after England’s defeat at Lord’s. It did not itself win the Ashes: England eventually took the five-Test series 2–1. Keeping the match result separate from the eventual series score is essential when looking up this particular finish. [ECB retrospective](https://www.ecb.co.uk/news/3234620).
