**Jammu and Kashmir won their first Ranji Trophy title after drawing the 2025–26 final against Karnataka in Hubli on 28 February 2026.** Their **291-run first-innings lead** decided the championship. The match itself was a draw, not a 291-run victory. [Official match result](https://www.bcci.tv/matches/230838/More/match-details); [first-title confirmation](https://newsonair.gov.in/jammu-kashmir-clinches-first-ever-ranji-trophy/).

The five-day final ran from **24 to 28 February 2026** at the **DR Bendre Karnataka State Cricket Association Stadium, Hubli**, also described in scorecards as the KSCA Hubli Cricket Ground. J&K won the toss and batted first.

## Final scorecard summary

| Innings | Score | Overs |
|---|---|---|
| Jammu and Kashmir first innings | 584 all out | 173.1 |
| Karnataka first innings | 293 all out | 93.3 |
| Jammu and Kashmir second innings | 342/4 declared | 113 |

Karnataka did not bat a second time. The BCCI match centre records both the declaration and the result: a drawn match with J&K winning on first innings. The “d” beside 342/4 means declared; it does not mean J&K lost their remaining six wickets. [BCCI match details](https://www.bcci.tv/matches/230838/More/match-details).

## Why J&K won a drawn final

The first-innings comparison was straightforward:

**584 − 293 = 291 runs.**

That was J&K’s lead after each team had completed one innings. Because the final ended without an outright result, this first-innings advantage settled which side took the trophy. The description “match drawn” and the description “J&K champions” therefore refer to different aspects of the same final.

J&K later added 342 in their second innings, increasing their overall lead to:

**291 + 342 = 633 runs.**

The 633-run lead describes the position when the match finished. Neither 633 nor 291 is an outright winning margin. Karnataka were not dismissed twice, and no fourth-innings chase took place. [Contemporary result and lead summary](https://newsonair.gov.in/jammu-kashmir-clinches-first-ever-ranji-trophy/).

## Six substantial innings built J&K’s 584

Shubham Pundir led J&K’s first innings with **121 from 247 balls**, but the total was supported throughout the batting order.

| J&K batter | First-innings runs |
|---|---:|
| Shubham Pundir | 121 |
| Yawer Hassan | 88 |
| Sahil Lotra | 72 |
| Paras Dogra | 70 |
| Kanhaiya Wadhawan | 70 |
| Abdul Samad | 61 |

These six contributions totalled **482 runs**, or **82.5% of the innings total**. The remainder came from other batters and extras. The pattern matters: Karnataka could not bring the innings to a quick end by removing only its highest scorer. [First-innings contributions](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/125085/kar-vs-jk-final-ranji-trophy-elite-2025-26).

Pundir’s century became the central innings behind his Player of the Match award. It was J&K’s first century in a Ranji Trophy final. [Contemporary Pundir report](https://sports.ndtv.com/ranji-trophy/meet-shubham-pundir-jammu-and-kashmir-star-who-won-player-of-the-match-after-historic-ranji-trophy-final-triumph-11150027).

## Mayank’s 160 could not erase the deficit

Mayank Agarwal made **160 from 266 balls** for Karnataka. That was **54.6% of their 293**, more than half the team’s total. Auqib Nabi’s **5/54 in 23 overs** helped keep Karnataka well short of J&K’s first-innings score. [Full scorecard](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/125085/kar-vs-jk-final-ranji-trophy-elite-2025-26).

The comparison between the two innings is more revealing than comparing their highest scores alone. Mayank’s 160 was higher than Pundir’s 121, yet J&K made almost twice Karnataka’s total. The five other fifty-plus contributions in J&K’s innings explain much of that difference.

A leading individual score and a first-innings team advantage answer different questions. Mayank had the larger individual innings; J&K had the much larger completed total.

## Iqbal and Lotra finished with unbeaten centuries

In J&K’s second innings, **Qamran Iqbal made 160 not out** and **Sahil Lotra finished unbeaten on 101**. Their unbroken fifth-wicket partnership reached **197** before the declaration at 342/4. [Final-day account](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/125085/kar-vs-jk-final-ranji-trophy-elite-2025-26).

Those centuries extended an advantage already established in the first innings. They should remain separate from the earlier scorecard: Lotra’s 72 belonged to J&K’s first innings, while his unbeaten 101 came in the second. Similarly, Mayank and Iqbal both reached 160, but for different teams and in different innings.

## Match award and tournament award

**Shubham Pundir was Player of the Match. Auqib Nabi was Player of the Tournament.** The post-match presentation records the two awards separately. [Presentation coverage](https://sports.ndtv.com/cricket/live-commentary/kar-vs-jam-final-hubli-kajk02242026263711).

Nabi finished the **2025–26 Ranji season with 60 wickets in ten matches**, including his five wickets in the final. That is a season total, not his final-match return. The ICC later confirmed both the 60 wickets and his tournament award in its profile of his India selection. [ICC season summary](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/fresh-faces-in-whites-for-india-s-sri-lanka-series).

Pundir’s BCCI match card displays **125 runs from 257 balls** across both innings. His first-innings score was **121 from 247**, followed by **4 from 10** in the second. Adding those two innings gives the match aggregate; it does not turn his first-innings century into 125. [BCCI award card](https://www.bcci.tv/matches/230838/More/match-details); [innings breakdown](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/125085/kar-vs-jk-final-ranji-trophy-elite-2025-26).

## Reading this result accurately

The concise result is: **match drawn; Jammu and Kashmir won the title on first-innings lead**. Keep the season label **2025–26** even though the final was played in calendar year 2026.

The innings totals, declaration, first-innings lead and championship outcome each add a different piece of information. Preserving those distinctions gives a more useful account of this final than attaching an incorrect run-margin victory to J&K’s historic title.
