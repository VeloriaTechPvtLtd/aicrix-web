Chaminda Vaas leads the men's ODI bowling-figures record with **8/19 against Zimbabwe** in Colombo on 8 December 2001. The record ranks wickets first and runs conceded second. That is why Vaas's eight wickets place him ahead of Shahid Afridi's seven for 12 despite Afridi conceding fewer runs. [ICC's leading-record table](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/icc-cricket-world-cup-league-2/news/scotland-quick-breaks-rabada-s-record-with-seven-wicket-haul-on-debut).

The checked tables contain 16 innings reaching at least seven wickets: Vaas's eight-wicket return and 15 seven-wicket returns. The dated snapshot below uses CricPrism's 13 November 2026 update, cross-checked with Crickrida on 14 November. It does not claim to incorporate any later match that the providers have not yet processed.

## Why seven for 57 ranks above six for four

A figures table orders the wicket count before the runs. Shami's 7/57 therefore qualifies for this group while Binny's 6/4 does not. That ordering does not say Shami conceded runs more economically, or that every seven-wicket spell was more valuable than every six-wicket spell.

Economy needs runs conceded and legal balls bowled. Strike rate needs balls and wickets. Bowling average divides runs by wickets. These measures can produce different orders because they answer different questions. A record heading should tell the reader which one is being used.

For example, 7/12 gives a spell average of 12÷7, approximately 1.71 runs per wicket. Vaas's 8/19 gives 19÷8, or 2.38 when rounded. Afridi's lower runs-per-wicket figure does not replace Vaas at the top of a wicket-first innings record.

## Debut record and overall record are different

Charlie Cassell took 7/21 against Oman on his Scotland ODI debut in July 2024. ICC identified it as the best men's ODI debut return and seventh on the overall figures list at the time. Both descriptions can be correct without making him the overall record-holder. [ICC debut report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/icc-cricket-world-cup-league-2/news/scotland-quick-breaks-rabada-s-record-with-seven-wicket-haul-on-debut).

The broader table includes qualifying performances for Scotland and the United States. An ODI record is not a Full-Member-only list. Removing those entries because the teams are less familiar would change the scope rather than improve the ranking.

## A shortened match can still produce a seven wicket spell

Hasaranga's 7/19 came in a rain-reduced, 27-over ODI against Zimbabwe on 11 January 2024. ICC's report was published the following day. The match date in the table is therefore 11 January, not the article's 12 January dateline. [ICC report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/hasaranga-claims-fifth-best-men-s-odi-bowling-figures?sf185575304=1).

The Crickrida ledger records 35 legal balls for his spell, equivalent to 5.5 overs. That notation means 35 legal balls, not 5.5 decimal overs. Nineteen runs across 35 balls gives an economy of 19×6÷35, approximately 3.26 per over. ICC records Zimbabwe's 96 and Sri Lanka's eight-wicket victory. [Bowling ledger](https://crickrida.com/records/men/odi/best-bowling-innings/), [ICC match account](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/hasaranga-claims-fifth-best-men-s-odi-bowling-figures?sf185575304=1).

That example shows why the headline figures should travel with the match context. They identify a remarkable return, but do not by themselves describe the length of the match, the bowler's exposure or every difficulty the batter faced.

## How to use the list

Use the figures table to answer a record question, then open the match source for a particular performance. Do not treat the order as a complete ranking of bowlers, career quality or future wicket-taking chances.

A publication date is also not automatically a data cutoff. This page preserves its checked source snapshot and should be refreshed as a whole if a new qualifying spell appears: add the innings, re-evaluate ranks, inspect the six-wicket boundary and update the stated cutoff together.
