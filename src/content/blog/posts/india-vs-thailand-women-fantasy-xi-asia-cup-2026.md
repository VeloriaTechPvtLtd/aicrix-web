## Dubai night: dew vs spin, batter vs spinner

Dubai under lights is not one-note. New ball can still move four to six overs; then the pitch typically slows into a spin squeeze: sweep, nudge, run. Dew is the second variable — wet ball, slipping fingers, leaking length. A research fork, not a slogan.

- **If India bowl first (more grip):** left-arm and off-spin hold value. Thipatcha Putthawong, Deepti Sharma, Shree Charani, Radha Yadav are the first names you study.
- **If India chase and dew is obvious:** spinner grip in the second innings is the first thing you downgrade. Death pace and batters who use a skiddy ball go up. Do not auto-keep four spinners because “Dubai is slow.”
- **Batter vs spinner:** Mandhana and Deepti (left-hand) versus Putthawong (left-arm orthodox) is a matchup, not a free hit. Right-hand intent (Shafali Verma, Harmanpreet Kaur, Richa Ghosh) is how you break a gripping middle.

Par is not the men’s 140-template. Wait for Friday’s opener, then Sunday’s toss.

## India batting depth vs Thailand bowling — Putthawong is the actual story

India have the deeper batting card. That is not “Thailand have no bowling.”

Jemimah Rodrigues is out with a high-grade hamstring tear. Pratika Rawal replaces her: maiden T20I call-up, an ODI opener, no T20 since the Challenger Trophy in November 2024. She is **not** a like-for-like for Rodrigues at 3/4. Whether she starts is uncertain. Bharti Fulmali is the other middle-order/finisher option. G Kamalini is the second keeper, not a default starter.

Batting core you can reasonably expect: **Mandhana** and **Shafali** at the top, **Harmanpreet Kaur** in the middle, **Richa Ghosh** with the gloves, **Deepti Sharma** as all-round glue. That depth is why an 11-India research XI is lazy. Study *this* bowling attack.

**Thailand’s bowling is the page.** Heading into the Asia Cup, ICC records have **Thipatcha Putthawong** on 169 women’s T20I wickets, one ahead of Deepti on 168. Left-arm orthodox; leading wicket-taker in the format. In 2026 T20Is: 37 wickets at 4.53 economy. That is the bowler most likely to take Indian wickets on a slowing Dubai middle. **Onnicha Kamchomphu** (off-spin) is the other Thai bowler past 150 T20I wickets. **Suleeporn Laomi** and **Sunida Chaturongrattana** fill a spin-heavy attack; **Chanida Sutthiruang** and **Phannita Maya** give them new-ball and all-round overs.

Mandhana and Shafali have to beat Putthawong’s first spell. Harmanpreet and Richa have to win the middle if the openers do not. Fail to rotate, and Thailand keep this in a 120-game, not a 160-game.

Thailand can bat. **Nannapat Koncharoenkai** is their highest T20I run-scorer; **Natthakan Chantham** sits almost level; **Naruemol Chaiwai** was their leading scorer at the T20 World Cup 2026 Global Qualifier. They do not have India’s finishing bench.
