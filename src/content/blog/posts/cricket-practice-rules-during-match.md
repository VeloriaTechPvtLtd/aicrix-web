Players throwing a ball on the outfield during lunch may be doing something permitted. Similar activity beside the match pitch while play is underway can raise a different question. “Practice during a match” is not one unrestricted category: location, timing, participants and the ball being used all matter.

This guide compares the MCC Laws with the ICC men’s Test conditions effective October 2026. For another competition, read its applicable conditions before judging an incident.

## Start with the part of the ground

MCC Law 26 prohibits practice on the match pitch on every day of the match. Practice on the rest of the square requires umpire approval. Outfield practice before play, after close, during lunch or tea, and between innings is permitted provided the umpires are satisfied it will not significantly deteriorate the outfield. [MCC 2026 Laws, 26.1–26.2](https://www.lords.org/getattachment/e8b12929-1e84-4927-af4e-2a5875b53589/Laws-of-Cricket-2017-Code-4th-Edition-%282026%29.pdf).

That makes precise description important. A camera angle may make an adjacent strip look like the match strip. Before describing a breach, establish where the player actually was. “Near the pitch” is not a sufficiently precise location for applying these categories.

## The tighter conditions while play is underway

Under the checked ICC Test conditions, between Play and Time, outfield practice is limited to fielders using the match ball. Bowling practice parallel to the match pitch in the area between square and boundary is prohibited, and the work must not breach ball-condition or time-wasting provisions. A short-run-up delivery to a player on the outfield is not classified as bowling practice, but remains subject to the other restrictions. [ICC Test conditions, 26.2.2](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/a8114zro5tkioifz0jqg.pdf).

Do not reduce this to “short run-ups are always allowed.” An exception to one description does not erase the remaining conditions.

## What about nets on the square?

ICC Test conditions add a specific limitation: approved square practice uses netted practice areas or purpose-prepared bowling strips at the square’s edge. Practice on those strips during the interval or innings change remains subject to the umpires’ assessment of potential surface damage in the conditions. [ICC Test conditions, 26.1.2](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/a8114zro5tkioifz0jqg.pdf).

A useful question is not simply whether a net exists, but whether this is the approved area for the activity. Physical availability is not the same as permission.

## Trial run-ups and penalties

MCC permits a trial run-up if the umpire is satisfied it does not involve fielding-side time-wasting or pitch damage. Its practice provisions prescribe an initial warning for a breach of 26.1 or 26.2; a further breach by any player of the same team during the match attracts five penalty runs to the opposition. Other unfair-play provisions also remain relevant. [MCC Law 26.3–26.4](https://www.lords.org/getattachment/e8b12929-1e84-4927-af4e-2a5875b53589/Laws-of-Cricket-2017-Code-4th-Edition-%282026%29.pdf).

The ICC Test document similarly provides the warning and subsequent team-breach penalty. [ICC Test conditions, 26.4](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/a8114zro5tkioifz0jqg.pdf).

## Three questions a replay cannot answer on its own

Imagine a clip showing someone sending a ball towards a teammate near the boundary. Before reaching a conclusion, ask:

1. **When was it recorded?** The upload time says nothing about whether the activity occurred during a session or an interval.

2. **Which ball and participants were involved?** A wide shot may not establish either.

3. **What happened earlier?** A short clip does not reveal previous warnings or an official explanation.

These are hypothetical evidence checks, not allegations about a player. A responsible caption can say that a practice restriction may be relevant while leaving the outcome unconfirmed. It should not invent a warning, penalty or umpire decision to complete the story.

For club cricket, agree the permitted practice arrangements with the officials before starting. That is easier than trying to resolve an argument after an activity has damaged a surface or interrupted play.

## The takeaway

Record the location, timing, participants, ball and governing document before applying a practice rule. Keep the question “Was this permitted?” separate from “What sanction follows?” Those steps make the explanation clearer and avoid treating every warm-up, throw and trial run-up as the same event.
