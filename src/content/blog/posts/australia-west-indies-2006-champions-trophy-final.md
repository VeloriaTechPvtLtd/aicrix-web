Australia beat West Indies by eight wickets in the 2006 Champions Trophy final in Mumbai. Rain changed the target, so Australia’s winning 116 must be read against the revised conditions rather than compared directly with West Indies’ 138.

## A fast opening did not become a large total

West Indies lost their first wicket at 49, then were dismissed for 138 in 30.4 overs. Nathan Bracken took three for 22, including the wickets of Shivnarine Chanderpaul, Ramnaresh Sarwan and Chris Gayle. Glenn McGrath and Shane Watson took two wickets each. [Contemporary scorecard](https://img.cricinfo.com/db/ARCHIVE/2006-07/OD_TOURNEYS/ICCCT/SCORECARDS/KNOCKOUTS/AUS_WI_ICCCT_ODI-FINAL_05NOV2006.html)

Australia also encountered early trouble: Adam Gilchrist and Ricky Ponting were dismissed with only 13 on the board. Watson and Damien Martyn then remained together.

## What changed after rain?

At the interruption, Australia were 45 for two after ten overs. The revised requirement was 116 from a maximum of 35 overs. They reached that total in 28.1 overs, leaving 41 legal balls unused from the reduced allocation.

The result is recorded under the **D/L method**, the terminology applicable to this match. It should not be relabelled with a modern rain-rule calculation or described as Australia failing to pass the original total.

## Watson and Martyn finished the repair

Watson ended on 57 not out and Martyn on 47 not out. Their unbeaten stand was 103, the difference between 13 for two and 116 for two. Watson was player of the match.

In a later ICC interview, Watson described the unbeaten final innings as an important moment in feeling he belonged in Australia’s powerful side. That is his retrospective assessment, not an explanation invented from the numbers. [Watson’s recollection](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/the-moments-you-dream-of-shane-watson-on-champions-trophy-knockouts)

The final combined two recoveries: Australia’s bowlers stopped a promising West Indies opening, and its third-wicket pair prevented two early dismissals from derailing the revised chase.
