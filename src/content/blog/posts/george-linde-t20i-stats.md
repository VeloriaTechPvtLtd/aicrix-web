These measures should be read together. A low economy and a frequent wicket rate answer different questions, while an all-rounder's batting total depends partly on whether the innings reaches his place in the order.

## The Durban game that combined both career bests

Against Pakistan at Kingsmead on **10 December 2024**, Linde scored **48 from 24 balls** and took **4/21 in four overs**. South Africa made 183/9 and won by 11 runs. [Match scorecard](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/94327/rsa-vs-pak-1st-t20i-pakistan-tour-of-south-africa-2024-25)

His innings included three fours and four sixes. Those seven boundary deliveries supplied 36 runs, three-quarters of his score. His batting strike rate was 200, while his bowling economy was 5.25. Neither number is a fantasy-point total: they measure scoring speed and runs conceded, respectively.

South Africa were 141/8 after 16 overs and finished on 183/9. The final four overs therefore added 42 runs. Linde's contribution should be understood as part of that recovery, rather than by subtracting his individual runs from Pakistan's eventual margin. ## Five appearances did not mean five batting innings

Linde's March 2026 tour of New Zealand shows why appearances alone can mislead.

| Date | Venue city | Batting return |

| 17 March | Hamilton | 33 from 12 balls |
| 20 March | Auckland | 23 from 19 balls |
| 22 March | Wellington | 14 not out from 8 balls |
| 25 March | Christchurch | Did not bat |

He scored **70 from 39 balls** across three innings, with two dismissals. That gives a batting average of 35 and a strike rate of 179.49. Dividing those 70 runs by five appearances gives 14 runs per match, not the batting average. A DNB is an appearance without a batting innings, not an unsuccessful attempt at the crease.
