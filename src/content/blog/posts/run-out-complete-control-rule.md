A fielder does not have to catch or hold the ball before every run-out. The distinction is what breaks the wicket. If the fielder uses a hand or arm, the ball must be held under control in the relevant hand. If the ball itself hits the wicket, a deflection can still be valid. [MCC’s 8 October 2026 clarification](https://www.lords.org/lords/news-stories/mcc-clarifies-law-relating-to-breaking-the-wicket-for-a-run-out) addresses precisely this confusion.

That means a bowler’s touch onto the non-striker’s stumps has not been abolished. Nor is a rebound from a wicketkeeper’s pads automatically invalid. A replay must distinguish those events from a fielder sweeping off the bails while merely fumbling the ball.

## What complete control means

The current definition is about the ability to keep possession or release the ball deliberately, assuming nothing else contacts it. Momentary contact is not enough. The requirement is not a fixed number of seconds, and it should not be confused with the separate control-of-movement test for completing a catch. [ICC men’s T20I conditions, Appendix A and clause 33.3](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/bsxcho7ufe2pjban0wjp.pdf).

## Three actions that can look similar

| Action at the wicket | Relevant question |
| --- | --- |
| A fielder’s right hand or arm removes a bail | Was the ball controlled in the right hand (the hand of that arm)? Holding it only in the other hand is not enough |
| A throw strikes the stumps directly | Did the ball fairly break the wicket? No intervening catch is necessary |
| A bowler deflects a shot onto the stumps | Follow the ball, not whether the bowler ever possessed it |

These are illustrative actions, not verdicts on a particular match. The table isolates the wicket-breaking issue; a fair break does not establish every requirement for a dismissal. [MCC explanation](https://www.lords.org/lords/news-stories/mcc-clarifies-law-relating-to-breaking-the-wicket-for-a-run-out).

## Check the batter as well as the fielder

Under the [2026 Laws, 29, 30 and 38](https://www.lords.org/getattachment/e8b12929-1e84-4927-af4e-2a5875b53589/Laws-of-Cricket-2017-Code-4th-Edition-%282026%29.pdf), the ball must be live and the relevant batter out of their ground, subject to the specified exceptions. With bails in use, simply nudging one is not enough: a bail must be completely removed, or a stump removed from the ground.

A running or diving batter who has already made ground can remain protected when contact is subsequently lost. A freeze-frame showing the bat airborne is therefore not enough by itself. Compare the sequence before the wicket breaks, not only one dramatic image.

Consider two hypothetical replays. In the first, a batter’s bat never touches down behind the crease before a valid direct hit. In the second, it reaches safety during a dive and then bounces up. The final still could look similar, but the preceding action changes the question the umpire must answer.

## Read the correct replay moment

In men’s T20Is, Appendix D 4.1–4.2 specifies the replay timing. For ordinary bails, identify their first complete loss of contact, confirmed by subsequent permanent removal. For LED wickets, the first illuminated frame is used with the same subsequent-removal requirement. A flash alone is not the whole test. [October 2026 conditions](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/bsxcho7ufe2pjban0wjp.pdf).

For a useful explanation, keep four observations separate: how the wicket broke, possession when relevant, the batter’s grounding sequence, and the applicable competition rules. State which observation is missing rather than turning an incomplete clip into certainty. That makes the decision easier to understand without inventing a new rule that every fielder must first hold the ball.
