## Australia won by ten wickets in Perth

Australia beat India by ten wickets in the women’s day-night Test at the WACA Ground, completed on 8 March 2026. The match began on 6 March. India made 198 and 149; Australia replied with 323 before reaching 28 without loss in the final innings.

The small fourth-innings target was the consequence of Australia’s 125-run first-innings lead. It does not mean India’s second innings was worth only 25 runs. This was a two-innings match, and the earlier deficit had to be cleared before India could set a target. [Cricket Australia final-day report](https://www.cricket.com.au/news/4464484)

## The first innings gave Australia room to build

Jemimah Rodrigues made 52 from 84 balls, while Kashvee Gautam finished unbeaten on 34. India’s total of 198 included five extras. Annabel Sutherland took 4/46 and debutant Lucy Hamilton 3/31.

Those two bowlers accounted for seven of the ten wickets. That is useful context for the result, but it is not the same as saying they alone conceded the entire innings total or bowled every difficult spell. A team innings combines multiple bowlers, fielding events and extras.

Australia’s reply was also not a smooth opening partnership. They were 58/3 before Ellyse Perry and Sutherland added 128 for the fourth wicket. Perry made 76; Sutherland made 129 from 171 balls. Their stand took the total to 186 before Perry’s dismissal, changing the innings from a recovery into the foundation of a lead. ## Sutherland contributed in both disciplines

Sutherland’s innings was larger than Australia’s eventual first-innings advantage: 129 individual runs compared with a 125-run team lead. That comparison illustrates the scale of her contribution, but it is not a claim that removing one player’s innings would leave the rest of the match unchanged.

Her bowling figures across India’s two innings were 4/46 and 2/15. Added together, that is six wickets for 61 runs. Hamilton’s 3/31 and 3/32 make six for 63. These are match aggregates, not single-innings six-wicket hauls. The distinction matters when comparing this performance with best-innings records.

## India resisted but could not set a substantial target

India lost six wickets for 82 in the second innings. Pratika Rawal’s 63 from 137 balls helped extend the resistance, including a 50-run seventh-wicket partnership with Sneh Rana. That stand carried India from 82/6 to 132/7 and, importantly, beyond the 125-run deficit.

Australia still needed another innings to win. Ashleigh Gardner and Alana King took two wickets each on the final morning, and India’s innings ended at 149. Rawal was the last batter dismissed. ## Why the target was 25 and the winning score was 28

The arithmetic is:

- India’s combined innings: 198 + 149 = 347.

- Australia’s first innings: 323.

- India’s remaining lead: 347 − 323 = 24.

- Australia therefore needed 25 to win.

Georgia Voll finished on 16 and Phoebe Litchfield on 11; one extra brought the final total to 28/0. A winning stroke can take the total beyond the minimum target. The result remains a ten-wicket win because Australia lost no wickets in the successful final innings. It is not a three-run victory based on 28 minus 25.

## Keep the match separate from a player profile

This was Alyssa Healy’s final international appearance, but she did not bat in the chase. Her farewell is an important part of the occasion, not a reason to attribute the winning opening partnership to her. Australia used Voll and Litchfield to finish the match.
