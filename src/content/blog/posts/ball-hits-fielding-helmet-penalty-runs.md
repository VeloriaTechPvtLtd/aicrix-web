A live ball hitting a fielding helmet placed behind the wicketkeeper can add five penalty runs to the batting total. Those five are not necessarily the entire award: qualifying running runs can also count. But neither “helmet means five” nor “five replaces everything else” is a safe universal shortcut.

The baseline here is the [MCC 2026 Laws](https://www.lords.org/getattachment/e8b12929-1e84-4927-af4e-2a5875b53589/Laws-of-Cricket-2017-Code-4th-Edition-%282026%29.pdf), corroborated for the specified examples by the [ICC men’s Test conditions effective October 2026](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/a8114zro5tkioifz0jqg.pdf). Other competitions require their own conditions.

## Identify which helmet and where it is

Law 28.3 concerns unused fielding helmets placed behind the wicketkeeper, aligned with both wickets. A live-ball impact makes the ball immediately dead. Subject to exclusions, the batting side receives five penalty runs plus completed runs and a run underway if the batters had crossed at impact. Any applicable wide or no-ball must also be signalled. [MCC Law 28.3](https://www.lords.org/getattachment/e8b12929-1e84-4927-af4e-2a5875b53589/Laws-of-Cricket-2017-Code-4th-Edition-%282026%29.pdf)

The permitted storage position therefore does not make the helmet immune from a penalty. Nor does the rule require a batter to have deliberately aimed at it. The first questions are about equipment, placement and whether the ball remained live, not the attractiveness of the shot.

## Count at helmet contact not at the throw

Take three invented scenes, each starting at 80/2. Assume a normal delivery struck with the bat, a helmet in the specified position, no dismissal, no short run and no other penalty or scoring restriction.

| Position when the ball hits the helmet | Addition | New team score |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No completed run and no crossing on the first | 0 + 5 | 85/2 |
| One run complete, no crossing on the second | 1 + 5 | 86/2 |
| One run complete, already crossed on the second | 1 + 1 + 5 | 87/2 |

In the final row, crossing after a fielder releases a return throw can still matter if it occurs before helmet contact. The relevant cutoff here is impact. Do not substitute the throw-time cutoff used for an overthrow boundary.

There is also no extra four merely because the ball rolls over the rope afterwards: it was already dead at the helmet. These are applications of the helmet rule, not an overthrow-boundary calculation. [ICC clause 28.3](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/a8114zro5tkioifz0jqg.pdf)

## The exceptions are part of the rule

The helmet award is withheld when the specified disallowed-leg-bye or hit-the-ball-twice provisions apply. In the leg-bye case, the umpire assesses whether the striker tried to play the ball or avoid being hit. A lawful defensive second strike also cannot manufacture a five-run helmet bonus. [ICC clauses 23.2–23.3, 28.3.3 and 34](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/a8114zro5tkioifz0jqg.pdf)

For example, assume an ordinary delivery hits the striker’s pad, with neither a shot attempt nor an attempt to evade it, and then reaches the stored helmet. With no other penalty, an initial 80/2 remains 80/2. Treating this as an automatic 85/2 would ignore the stated exclusion.

MCC additionally lists its restriction on the striker’s runner in Law 28.3.3. Other run-disallowance provisions can also exclude this penalty; the examples above deliberately contain none. Do not merge the general Laws and international conditions into a single universal exception list. [MCC Laws 18.6 and 28.3](https://www.lords.org/getattachment/e8b12929-1e84-4927-af4e-2a5875b53589/Laws-of-Cricket-2017-Code-4th-Edition-%282026%29.pdf)

## Worn equipment is a different question

A helmet being worn is not the stored helmet described in 28.3. The current catch provisions permit relevant contact with worn protective equipment, subject to the other catch requirements. Deliberately using equipment to field the ball belongs to the separate illegal-fielding provision. [ICC clauses 28.2 and 33.2](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/a8114zro5tkioifz0jqg.pdf)

A batter’s helmet is likewise not fielding-side equipment. A photograph showing ball-to-helmet contact is therefore insufficient: identify who owns it and whether it was worn, stored or used in an action.

Keep the five-run award separate from the ball-count decision and from any claim about a fair catch. That avoids turning one recognizable object into the wrong rule for three different situations.
