## Janette Brittin leads the career list

Janette Brittin is the leading run-scorer in women’s Test cricket with 1,935 runs. Charlotte Edwards follows with 1,676 and Rachael Heyhoe-Flint with 1,594. The career table retrieved on 14 November 2026 contains 11 batters with at least 1,000 Test runs. [ACS career records](https://stats.acscricket.com/Records/Womens_Test/Overall/Batting/Most_Career_Runs.html)

This is a cumulative runs list. It answers who has scored the most over a Test career, rather than who has the highest batting average, the largest single innings or the most runs across all international formats.

That boundary matters. A headline about the “1,000-run club” should include Rolton even though she sits outside the top ten. Conversely, a well-known player is not included merely because she has passed 1,000 runs in ODIs or T20Is.

## Brittin has a 259 run lead

Brittin’s advantage over Edwards is 259 runs, calculated as 1,935 minus 1,676. Her career total spans 44 batting innings in 27 Tests.

The ICC’s Hall of Fame profile independently records her 1,935 Test runs, five centuries and 27 appearances. The profile was published in November 2021; the current career ledger is what establishes the ordering at this article’s retrieval date. [ICC Hall of Fame profile](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/janette-brittin-mahela-jayawardena-and-shaun-pollock-inducted-into-icc-cricket-hall-of-fame)

The distinction between appearances and innings is important in Test cricket. A match can provide two opportunities to bat, but it need not do so. A declaration, an innings victory or an unused batting position can leave a player with fewer than two innings for that match.

## Perry became the Australian leader in March 2026

Ellyse Perry entered the March 2026 Test against India with 930 runs. Her innings of 76 took her to 1,006, passing Karen Rolton’s Australian record of 1,002. Cricket Australia reported the milestone on 7 March, during the match. Perry reached 1,000 in her 15th Test and 24th innings. [Cricket Australia’s milestone report](https://www.cricket.com.au/news/4463980/ellyse-perry-australia-women-all-time-leading-test-run-scorer-waca-india-overtakes-karen-rolton-misses-century-day-two)

The arithmetic separates two achievements. She needed 70 runs to reach 1,000 and 73 to move beyond Rolton. Her final 76 put her four runs ahead of the former Australian record.

In the retrieved global table, Perry remains one run behind Maclagan. A national record and a world ranking therefore describe different comparisons: leading Australia does not mean leading the world.

## Read opportunity alongside the total

Brittin’s 44 innings and Perry’s 24 should remain visible next to their career runs. The total ranks accumulated output; it does not adjust for how many Tests a country scheduled or how often a player got to bat.

Nor should an innings-based comparison be called a batting average. Cricket’s batting average divides runs by dismissals, not simply by matches or innings. An average table can consequently have a different order from a total-runs table.

These distinctions are especially useful when comparing eras. Keep the format, matches and batting innings attached to a career total before making a broader judgment about a player. This list alone cannot establish who would perform best in a current match.

## Common record questions

**Who has the most runs in women’s Tests?** Janette Brittin, with 1,935 in the retrieved career ledger.

**Who leads Australia?** Ellyse Perry on 1,006, ahead of Karen Rolton’s 1,002.

**Who is India’s representative in the 1,000-run group?** Sandhya Agarwal, with 1,110.

**Why are there 11 names rather than ten?** The qualification is at least 1,000 runs. All 11 meet it.

**Is the list frozen permanently?** No. It is a dated career-record lookup and should be refreshed after subsequent women’s Tests or an official statistical correction.
