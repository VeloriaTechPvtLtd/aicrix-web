**Before judging a fantasy team, check that the review contains the team you actually selected.** A screenshot can preserve a lineup, but cropped names, missing match details and unclear captain markers can leave essential information unresolved. A polished verdict does not repair those gaps.

The first useful output is a readable transcription that you can correct. Only then should a reviewer assess the selection against the right fixture and game rules.

## Identify the game before the players

Keep the competition, both teams, fixture date and selected game mode with the image. If those fields are outside the crop, add them separately. Two teams can meet several times, and a screenshot from their earlier meeting is not automatically relevant to the next one.

Mode matters independently of cricket format. The [official Dream11 reference](https://www.dream11.com/features/how-to-play) describes a five-player second-innings format separately from its other scoring sections. An image containing five players therefore does not, by itself, prove that six players are missing. Check the mode before applying a team-size test.

Record when the image was captured and whether it shows an editable selection, a saved team or a completed result. Those are different objects. An image of an editing screen alone cannot establish that the platform accepted the selection.

## Ask for a transcription before a verdict

An image-review service should let you inspect the names it understood, their teams, listed roles and any C or VC markers. Treat an unreadable field as unresolved rather than silently filling it with the most familiar player.

If the image is unclear, manually entering the selection can be a useful alternative. It still needs the same identity checks, and it should not silently replace unresolved fields with guesses.

Check each player against the pool for the identified fixture. Initials and surnames are not unique identifiers. A familiar name from another squad, season or competition should not be substituted merely because it looks plausible.

## A worked example of an unresolved image

Consider a fictional eleven-player game. The screenshot contains eleven player cards. Nine cards have readable names and markers; two have unresolved fields:

| Visible information | What is unresolved | What would resolve it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A Patel, team label cropped | Which player the card identifies | Full name and team from the original selection |
| J Smith, name clear but badge blurred | Whether the player has the C or VC marker | A clearer badge or manual confirmation |

Assume the fictional fixture’s player pool contains both Arun Patel and Ajay Patel. Choosing Arun because he is better known would change an unknown field into an unsupported assertion. The correct initial report is nine fully resolved cards, one ambiguous identity and one unreadable multiplier marker.

Notice that those categories overlap: J Smith’s identity is known, while the marker is not. Counting two unresolved fields as two missing players would introduce another error. The roster can contain eleven visible cards without eleven confirmed identities or fully confirmed captain assignments.

After checking the original selection, the reader confirms Ajay Patel and supplies the marker. Now compare the corrected transcription with all eleven cards again. Confirm that no card was omitted or counted twice, and that the reported captain and vice-captain refer to the intended players.

This example is invented. It is a method for handling uncertainty, not a measured error rate for any provider.

## Separate three different checks

| Check | The question it answers | What passing it does not prove |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Transcription | Does the extracted list match the image and corrections? | That the lineup is valid for this game |
| Eligibility | Does the selection meet the applicable player-pool and roster rules? | That each player will participate |
| Performance assessment | What evidence and assumptions support the review? | That the team will achieve a particular score or rank |

A credit total can be correct even when one player has been misidentified. Likewise, correctly reading eleven names cannot validate the game mode. Keep the results of these checks separate rather than compressing them into one unexplained green score.

Once the list is correct, use the game’s current rules for roles, limits and scoring. Keep detailed budget comparisons and point calculations in their own references; the purpose here is to make sure those calculations start from the intended selection.

## Preserve a useful correction record

When a reader corrects a name, retain both the original reading and the confirmed replacement in the review history. Recheck any affected role count or other constraint instead of changing the display name while leaving earlier calculations untouched.

Later team news is a different kind of change. It can alter participation information without changing what the screenshot says. Show the lineup-news source and time separately from the image-capture time. A fresh review of an old image does not make the image itself current.

Before sharing an image, crop account names, balances and unrelated personal information. Keep the necessary fixture and player details, or provide them as text. Reviewing a selection should not require sharing a password or a full account screen.

A useful final review says which fixture and mode it covers, displays the corrected roster, identifies any unresolved fields and explains the basis of any further assessment. If identity remains uncertain, stop the affected assessment and ask for the missing detail. That is more useful than confidently reviewing a different team.
