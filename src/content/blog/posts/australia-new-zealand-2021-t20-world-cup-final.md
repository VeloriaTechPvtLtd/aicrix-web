Australia won their first men's T20 World Cup title in Dubai on 14 November 2021, beating New Zealand by eight wickets. New Zealand made 172 for four; Australia reached 173 for two in 18.5 overs. Mitchell Marsh's unbeaten 77 earned Player of the Match, but Josh Hazlewood's three for 16 was an equally important part of the route to the trophy. [ICC's final report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/magical-marsh-innings-fires-australia-to-maiden-icc-mens-t20-world-cup-crown)

## New Zealand needed a second-half change

New Zealand were 32 for one after six overs and 57 for one after ten. Williamson helped them reach the eventual 172, with his own innings producing 85 from 48 balls. Hazlewood's figures stood out within Australia's attack. [ICC's final report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/magical-marsh-innings-fires-australia-to-maiden-icc-mens-t20-world-cup-crown), [ICC's post-match coverage](https://www.icc-cricket.com/media-releases/marsh-hails-selection-masterstroke-after-propelling-australia-to-glory)

The team added 115 in the final ten overs, calculated by subtracting 57 from 172. That is not Williamson's personal second-half score: it includes every batter's runs and extras during that part of the innings. Cricket Australia independently records the 115-run second half. [Cricket Australia’s match report](https://www.cricket.com.au/news/3310714/champions-marshs-finest-hour-leads-aussies-to-glory) Keeping the team calculation separate from an individual score avoids attributing all of an acceleration to one player.

The two halves also illustrate why an innings run rate can conceal its shape. New Zealand's first ten overs averaged 5.7 runs per over, while the final ten averaged 11.5. The overall total blends both passages; neither phase alone describes the whole innings.

## Marsh entered with a defined role

Marsh came in after Aaron Finch's early dismissal and struck his first three deliveries for six, four and four. He later explained that Australia's staff had backed him at number three before the tournament. David Warner contributed 53 from 38 balls and was named Player of the Tournament. [ICC's post-match coverage](https://www.icc-cricket.com/media-releases/marsh-hails-selection-masterstroke-after-propelling-australia-to-glory)

## The chase continued after Warner departed

Trent Boult bowled Warner to break the partnership, but Marsh remained unbeaten on 77 from 50 deliveries. Glenn Maxwell struck the winning boundary with a switch-hit against Tim Southee. The ICC's match account describes Marsh maintaining the pace after Warner's dismissal rather than allowing the innings to stall. [ICC's chase report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/marsh-and-warner-take-australia-to-t20-world-cup-glory)

The recorded 18.5 overs represent 113 legal deliveries. In a 20-over innings, that leaves seven, not nine. This is cricket's overs-and-balls notation, not decimal arithmetic. The finishing score and wickets remaining together describe the outcome more accurately than an adjective such as “easy.” [ICC's final score summary](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/magical-marsh-innings-fires-australia-to-maiden-icc-mens-t20-world-cup-crown)

## Two performances, different outcomes

The final's central story is therefore not that New Zealand failed to accelerate. They did. Australia combined a particularly effective Hazlewood spell with a chase that kept finding runs across partnerships. The title followed that combination, while the separate match and tournament awards recognised different scales of contribution.
