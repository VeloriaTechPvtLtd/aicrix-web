**Sachin Tendulkar leads men's Test cricket with 15,921 runs.** Joe Root is second on 14,278 in the records checked on 14 November 2026. Seven players have reached 12,000 Test runs; the next player below that threshold is Brian Lara on 11,953. This is an overall career list, not a calendar-year or World Test Championship table. [ACS career records](https://stats.acscricket.com/Records/Test/Overall/Batting/Most_Career_Runs.html).

## How far is Root behind Tendulkar

Root needs **1,643 runs to equal** Tendulkar's total and **1,644 to pass** it, based on the checked figures. Cricket Australia's current player table independently lists Root's 14,278 runs from 169 Tests and 309 batting innings. [Joe Root statistics](https://www.cricket.com.au/players/CA:193).

These are arithmetic gaps, not a forecast of when or whether he will close them. A career average cannot supply an exact number of future Tests: matches may contain one or two batting innings, and an innings can end not out or without a batting opportunity.

Root moved above Ponting during his Old Trafford hundred against India on **25 July 2025**. During that innings he also passed Dravid and Kallis. An older biography describing Ponting as second can therefore preserve an accurate career total while carrying an outdated rank. [ICC report of Root's move into second](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/root-inches-closer-to-tendulkar-s-record-test-tally).

## Why Tendulkar remains the benchmark

Tendulkar's 15,921 came across 200 Tests, and his career included 51 Test hundreds. His final Test was in November 2013. The aggregate is a finished career total rather than a number that needs an update after each new international fixture. [ICC Hall of Fame profile](https://www.icc-cricket.com/hall-of-fame/hall-of-famers/hall-of-famer-sachin-tendulkar).

That differs from Root's moving tally. A useful comparison needs a date beside the changing number, not a fresh year inserted into an unchanged headline. Here, the date identifies when the displayed records were checked; it does not claim to describe the eventual end of the 2026 season.

## Overall career runs are not always country runs

Dravid provides a useful example. The BCCI's retirement statistics separate **13,265 runs for India** from **23 for the ICC World XI**. Together they make his overall Test total of 13,288. His 164 Tests similarly comprise 163 for India and one for the World XI. [BCCI career breakdown](https://www.bcci.tv/news/article/dravid-in-tests-a-numbers-guide).

Both 13,265 and 13,288 can therefore be correct, but they answer different questions. A page headed “runs for India” should not silently borrow the overall total. The one-run difference between Kallis and Dravid in the career table is meaningful only when both figures use the same overall scope.

## Most runs and highest average answer different questions

Sangakkara's 57.40 career average is higher than Tendulkar's 53.78, even though Tendulkar made more runs. The ICC's [Sangakkara profile](https://www.icc-cricket.com/hall-of-fame/hall-of-famers/hall-of-famer-kumar-sangakkara) records his average, while the [overall ledger](https://stats.acscricket.com/Records/Test/Overall/Batting/Most_Career_Runs.html) shows the different career workloads.

An aggregate ranks accumulated scoring. An average relates runs to dismissals. Neither number, by itself, adjusts for opposition, conditions or era, and a larger aggregate is not proof of a superior average.

For a quick answer, use the runs column. For a comparison between players, keep the format, overall-versus-country scope and statistical cutoff consistent before drawing a conclusion.
