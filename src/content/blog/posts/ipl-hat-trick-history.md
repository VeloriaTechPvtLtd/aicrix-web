*Selected historical milestones through 30 April 2025; not an exhaustive all-time list.*

**Lakshmipathy Balaji took the first IPL hat-trick**, for Chennai Super Kings against Kings XI Punjab on **10 May 2008**. Rohit Sharma also achieved the feat, but his came for **Deccan Chargers against Mumbai Indians on 6 May 2009**, not while representing Mumbai. Keeping the team and date attached prevents two common historical mix-ups.

A hat-trick concerns a sequence of wickets, whereas figures such as 5/24 describe the entire bowling innings. The selected milestones below show why those records need separate columns rather than being treated as interchangeable achievements.

## Balaji took the first in the final over

At Chennai, Balaji dismissed Irfan Pathan, Piyush Chawla and VRV Singh on **19.3, 19.4 and 19.5**. Kings XI finished 163/9 in response to CSK’s 181/4, an 18-run defeat. Balaji’s full return was **5/24 from four overs**. [Complete scorecard](https://www.mykhel.com/cricket/punjab-vs-chennai-scorecard-ipl-s9971-161042/), [contemporary report](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/balaji-shines-with-a-hat-trick-in-chennai-super-kings-win/articleshow/3028971.cms).

The three consecutive wickets supplied three of his five dismissals. His other two, Shaun Marsh and Ramnaresh Sarwan, had fallen earlier. That is why “hat-trick” does not imply an innings return of exactly three wickets. The scorecard also leaves one legal ball after 19.5; the hat-trick did not itself make the opposition all out.

## Rohit’s sequence crossed two overs

Rohit removed Abhishek Nayar at **15.5**, Harbhajan Singh at **15.6** and JP Duminy at **17.1** in Centurion. Another bowler’s over came between his two overs, but these were consecutive deliveries by Rohit. The hat-trick did not need to occupy three consecutive balls of the innings. [Fall of wickets and dismissal record](https://www.mykhel.com/cricket/mumbai-vs-deccan-scorecard-ipl-s9972-213884/).

He added Saurabh Tiwary’s wicket to finish with **4/6 in two overs**, having also scored 38. Deccan’s 145/6 beat Mumbai’s 126/8 by 19 runs. [Mumbai Indians’ historical match centre](https://www.mumbaiindians.com/scores/dch-vs-mi-match-dcmi050620095116).

That is an economy rate of **3.00 runs per over**. Balaji’s 5/24 over four overs gives **6.00**. Rohit conceded fewer runs per over in these two spells, while Balaji took more wickets. Neither comparison changes who was first, or says which performance should be predicted again.

## Repeating the feat is another kind of record

| Historical milestone | Player | Verified context |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First IPL hat-trick | Lakshmipathy Balaji | CSK against Kings XI Punjab, 10 May 2008 |
| Two in one season | Yuvraj Singh | Kings XI Punjab against RCB and Deccan, 2009 |
| Third IPL hat-trick reached | Amit Mishra | Sunrisers Hyderabad against Pune Warriors, 17 April 2013 |
| Second IPL hat-trick reached | Yuzvendra Chahal | Punjab Kings against CSK, 30 April 2025 |

This is a milestone selection, not a list of every IPL hat-trick or a current ranking of every bowler.

Yuvraj’s second of 2009 also crossed overs: Herschelle Gibbs fell on the last ball of the 12th, followed by Andrew Symonds and Venugopal Rao at the start of his next over. Kings XI won by just one run. The sequence made him the first bowler with two hat-tricks in one IPL season. [Punjab Kings’ retrospective](https://www.punjabkingsipl.in/features/kings-legacy-when-yuvraj-stopped-the-deccan-charge-1481).

Mishra’s third came in 2013. The IPL’s contemporary statistics report identifies three as his total at that point; it also records Pune falling from 101/4 to 108 all out. The entire collapse was six wickets for seven runs, not six wickets credited to the hat-trick. [IPL’s 17 April 2013 record](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/quick-stats-match-22-pwi-v-srh).

## Chahal took four wickets in the over but three in the hat trick

Against CSK in April 2025, Chahal dismissed MS Dhoni and then completed a hat-trick with **Deepak Hooda, Anshul Kamboj and Noor Ahmad**. His final figures were 4/32. The official report identifies this as his second IPL hat-trick. [IPL match report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/tata-ipl-2025-match-49-csk-vs-pbks-match-report).

Dhoni belongs in the four-wicket-over account, not the named three-wicket sequence. When reading a historical claim, check the bowler, franchise, match date, three victims and delivery order first. Then add the full spell and match result. That preserves what the achievement actually was without turning a memorable burst into a season-long performance measure.

## Keep historical franchise names attached

The team in a milestone row is the team the player represented in that match, not necessarily the team most closely associated with his later career. Rohit’s feat belongs to Deccan Chargers’ 2009 match against Mumbai; reversing those sides changes the meaning of the record. Likewise, the contemporary Kings XI Punjab name remains appropriate for the 2008 and 2009 matches, while Punjab Kings identifies Chahal’s 2025 side.

A selected history can answer who achieved a particular first without claiming to settle every later record. Expanding this page into a complete register would require checking each match, sequence and repeat occurrence, not simply appending a total from a headline. For the milestones here, the date, victims and full spell provide the useful context. A hat-trick is remarkable on its own; it does not need an unsupported claim about the next match or an inflated wicket total to make it interesting.
