Sri Lanka beat Australia by seven wickets at Colombo on 27 September 2002. Aravinda de Silva was player of the match after bowling ten overs for 16 with one wicket; he later finished on two not out. [Contemporary scorecard](https://img.cricinfo.com/link_to_database/ARCHIVE/2002-03/OD_TOURNEYS/ICCCT/SCORECARDS/KNOCKOUTS/AUS_SL_ICCCT_ODI-SEMI2_27SEP2002.html)

## The opening stand gave way to a collapse

Australia went from 49 without loss to 57/4. De Silva bowled Matthew Hayden, and Kumar Dharmasena removed Adam Gilchrist. Australia eventually made 162 in 48.4 overs. [Innings progression](https://img.cricinfo.com/link_to_database/ARCHIVE/2002-03/OD_TOURNEYS/ICCCT/SCORECARDS/KNOCKOUTS/AUS_SL_ICCCT_ODI-SEMI2_27SEP2002.html)

Four wickets for eight runs changed the innings from an opening platform into a rebuilding task. The eventual total required a long recovery, not simply a quick response to one dismissal.

## Restriction beyond the wicket column

Muttiah Muralitharan took 3/26 and Dharmasena 2/30. Damien Martyn and Darren Lehmann were run out. [Dismissal record](https://img.cricinfo.com/link_to_database/ARCHIVE/2002-03/OD_TOURNEYS/ICCCT/SCORECARDS/KNOCKOUTS/AUS_SL_ICCCT_ODI-SEMI2_27SEP2002.html)

De Silva’s economy rate was 1.60. His ten overs accounted for 60 of the innings’ 292 legal balls, approximately 20.5%. That measures the duration of the restriction, while his wicket measures a separate contribution. The run-outs also remind us that not every dismissal belongs in a bowler’s tally.

## A different shape to the chase

Sri Lanka reached 163/3 in 40 overs. Sanath Jayasuriya made 42, Marvan Atapattu 51 and Kumar Sangakkara 48. The first two stands contributed 67 and 75. [Chase record](https://img.cricinfo.com/link_to_database/ARCHIVE/2002-03/OD_TOURNEYS/ICCCT/SCORECARDS/KNOCKOUTS/AUS_SL_ICCCT_ODI-SEMI2_27SEP2002.html)

Together those stands brought Sri Lanka to 142, approximately 87.1% of the target. De Silva did not need a major innings because much of the pursuit had already been completed. His two unbeaten runs should be read alongside the earlier spell, not mistaken for the whole measure of his match.
