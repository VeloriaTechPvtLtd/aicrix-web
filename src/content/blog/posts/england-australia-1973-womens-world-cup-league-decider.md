England beat Australia by 92 runs at Edgbaston on 28 July 1973 to win the first Cricket World Cup. The women's event began before the men's competition. Although the decisive meeting is often called a final, it was the last match of a round-robin tournament. [ICC anniversary account](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/icc-celebrates-50-years-of-the-first-ever-cricket-world-cup)

## Why one league result determined the champion

Seven teams competed, including an International XI and Young England. England entered the last fixture one point behind Australia. Victory moved England above their opponents and settled the title. There was no separate knockout final to win afterwards. [ICC format history](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/womens-world-cup-a-trip-down-memory-lane)

The distinction matters when comparing tournament records: this was a title decider because of the standings. Its championship importance does not turn it into a different competition stage.

## Bakewell's hundred and the batting platform

England scored 279/3 in sixty overs. Enid Bakewell made 118, Rachael Heyhoe-Flint 64 and Lynne Thomas 40. The first wicket fell at 101; the second at 218. Those checkpoints give stands of 101 and 117, without requiring an invented ball-by-ball reconstruction. [Archived scorecard](https://i.imgci.com/link_to_database/ARCHIVE/WORLD_CUPS/WWC73/AUS-WOMEN_ENG-WOMEN_WWC73_WODI21_28JUL1973.html)

The opening platform and second partnership meant England extended an advantage rather than repeatedly rebuilding after early losses. Bakewell's century was central, but the surrounding contributions explain how the innings reached 279 with only three wickets lost.

## Winning without taking all ten wickets

Australia finished on 187/9 after their sixty overs. Jackie Potter scored 57, while Bakewell also contributed 2/28 from twelve overs. [Bowling and chase record](https://i.imgci.com/link_to_database/ARCHIVE/WORLD_CUPS/WWC73/AUS-WOMEN_ENG-WOMEN_WWC73_WODI21_28JUL1973.html)

England did not need the final wicket. Once the scheduled allocation ended, Australia's total remained below England's. The margin was 279 − 187 = 92 runs; the winning target had been 280. Both innings must be read in their sixty-over setting, rather than compared as though they had today's usual fifty-over allocation.

Bakewell's batting and bowling helped win the match. The league table made that victory a championship, giving the first World Cup its conclusion without a knockout bracket.
