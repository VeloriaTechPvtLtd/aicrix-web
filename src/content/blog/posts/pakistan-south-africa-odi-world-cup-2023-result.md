South Africa beat Pakistan by one wicket at MA Chidambaram Stadium in Chennai on 27 October 2023. Pakistan were all out for 270 in 46.4 overs; South Africa reached 271/9 in 47.2. This was Match 26 of the men's ODI World Cup, not a T20 international. [BCCI match details](https://www.bcci.tv/matches/216777/More/match-details).

The finish illustrates why a low required run rate does not necessarily make a chase secure. South Africa had deliveries available but eventually only one wicket left. Keshav Maharaj and Tabraiz Shamsi had to protect that final resource while finding the remaining runs.

## Pakistan did not bat all fifty overs

Babar Azam made 50 and Saud Shakeel 52, while Shamsi's four wickets helped South Africa keep Pakistan to 270. Aiden Markram later provided the largest individual contribution of the match with 91, but he was dismissed before the chase was completed. [ICC match report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/last-wicket-thriller-as-south-africa-beat-pakistan).

Pakistan's scorecard shows a sixth-wicket partnership of 84 between Shakeel and Shadab Khan, lifting the total from 141/5 to 225/6. Pakistan then added 45 while losing its remaining four wickets. That sequence explains both the recovery and its limit: the partnership rebuilt the innings without guaranteeing a full allocation at the end. [Full scorecard](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/75535/pak-vs-rsa-26th-match-icc-cricket-world-cup-2023).

Forty-six overs and four balls is 280 legal deliveries. Pakistan therefore left 20 of the scheduled 300 unused. Those were unused opportunities, not runs that can simply be credited to a hypothetical final score. Any claim that Pakistan would certainly have made 290 or 300 would require an assumption, not a scorecard calculation.

## The chase moved from runs to survival

After Marco Jansen's dismissal, South Africa were 235/6 at 36.5 overs. They needed 36 from 79 balls, only 2.73 runs per over. Markram's subsequent dismissal and further wickets changed the practical difficulty without turning the arithmetic into a large asking rate.

At 260/9 after 45.3 overs, the requirement was 11 from 27 balls: 2.44 per over. Numerically, the required rate was lower than at Jansen's dismissal. Yet the batting side now had no spare wicket. Comparing those two moments shows why the rate alone is an incomplete account of pressure.

## Eleven runs from the final pair

Maharaj and Shamsi added an unbeaten 11 in 11 legal balls. Maharaj finished on seven and Shamsi on four; those are their complete individual innings totals, not their separate contributions to that last partnership. The winning shot was Maharaj's boundary. [ICC's account of the finish](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/last-wicket-thriller-as-south-africa-beat-pakistan).

Earlier in the closing passage, Shamsi survived an lbw review on umpire's call. That event belongs in the historical account as the decision that stood, not as proof of an alternative result. This archive does not rejudge it under later playing conditions or claim that a different decision was required.

South Africa completed the chase after 284 legal balls, leaving 16 unused. Having deliveries left and winning by only one wicket are entirely compatible: one describes time, the other the surviving batting resources.

The match's lasting numerical contrast is therefore not merely 270 against 271. Pakistan's recovery ended before its full allocation, South Africa's chase lost its established batters near the target, and the last pair supplied the eleven runs that prevented a late reversal. None of those observations turns one completed ODI into a forecast for this rivalry.
