Punjab Kings and Gujarat Titans finished their **IPL 2026 meetings level at one win each**. Punjab won by three wickets in New Chandigarh on 31 March; Gujarat answered with a four-wicket victory in Ahmedabad on 3 May.

Both targets were in the low 160s, and both chases reached the final over. The results therefore need more context than “the chasing team won”: each match contained a substantial batting setback and a recovery that kept the contest alive.

Punjab did not enter the playoffs: the [Qualifier 1](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/tata-ipl-2026-qualifier-1-rcb-vs-gt-match-report) and [Eliminator](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/tata-ipl-2026-eliminator-srh-v-rr-match-report) reports identify RCB, GT, Rajasthan Royals and Sunrisers Hyderabad as the four participants.

## Connolly completes the recovery in New Chandigarh

Punjab's bowlers limited Gujarat to 162/6. Shubman Gill made 39 from 27 balls and Jos Buttler 38 from 33, but Yuzvendra Chahal's 2/28 and Vijaykumar Vyshak's 3/34 prevented a larger total.

The chase initially looked manageable. Prabhsimran Singh contributed 37 from 24 deliveries, but Punjab then slipped from 110/2 to 118/6. Prasidh Krishna finished with 3/29, turning a promising position into a test of the remaining batting.

Cooper Connolly supplied the decisive innings on his IPL debut: **72 not out from 46 balls**. Xavier Bartlett added an unbeaten 11, and Punjab completed the chase in the 20th over. Connolly was Player of the Match. The [official Match 4 report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/tata-ipl-2026-match-4-pbks-vs-gt-match-report) shows why his contribution mattered: he stayed through the collapse rather than simply accelerating from an already secure position.

## Gujarat chase down the target in Ahmedabad

Mohammed Siraj removed Priyansh Arya and Connolly on consecutive deliveries in the opening over. Punjab later reached 47/5 before Suryansh Shedge and Marcus Stoinis rebuilt with a 79-run partnership.

Shedge made 57 from 29 balls, Stoinis 40 from 31 and Marco Jansen added 20. Punjab recovered to 163/9, while Jason Holder's four-wicket performance earned him the Player of the Match award.

Gujarat's reply depended on a different combination. Sai Sudharsan scored 57 from 41 deliveries, then Washington Sundar's 40 from 23 helped complete the chase. Punjab kept taking wickets: Vyshak returned 2/31 and Arshdeep Singh 2/24. Gujarat nevertheless reached 167/6 with one ball left. ## Similar targets with different pressure points

The targets differed by only one run: 163 in March and 164 in May. Punjab used **115 legal balls** to win the first match; Gujarat used **119** in the second. Gujarat retained one more wicket but needed four more deliveries. Neither margin alone captures both dimensions of a chase.

The batting reversals also happened at different stages. Punjab lost four wickets for eight runs during the March chase. In May, their recovery came while setting a target, after losing five wickets for 47. The first demanded preserving a chase; the second demanded creating a defendable total. That distinction is more informative than treating every innings near 160 as the same performance.

Vyshak offers one useful cross-match comparison: his returns add to **five wickets for 65 runs conceded**. That is 13 runs per wicket in these two games, not his season bowling average. Keeping the sample attached to the number prevents a strong opponent-specific return from becoming an unsupported claim about every match he played.
