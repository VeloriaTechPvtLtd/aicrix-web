## Boundaries explain much of the tempo

Harris hit 38 fours and eight sixes. Those boundaries supplied 200 runs: 152 from fours and 48 from sixes. That is 84.39% of her 237 runs, leaving 37 runs not accounted for by those boundary counts.

This calculation describes where her runs came from, not how many scoring shots she attempted or how likely she was to clear the boundary next time. It also does not identify a powerplay strike rate: the table covers entire innings, which can extend beyond the first six overs.

On 12 January, her 85 from 40 balls helped RCB chase 144 with nine wickets and 47 balls remaining. The official report identifies her as Player of the Match and records a 137-run opening partnership with Smriti Mandhana. ## A fast season rate did not mean a big score every game

The same ledger includes four single-figure scores: 1, 1, 9 and 9. A high aggregate strike rate can coexist with early dismissals because it measures runs per ball, not consistency of innings size. Harris’s median score across the nine innings was 15, while her average was 26.33.

Neither number replaces the other. The median locates the middle observed innings; the average includes the full weight of the two substantial scores. Together with the complete log, they give a more honest account of the season than suggesting she usually scored at or above her best innings.

## Her bowling record was one three-over innings

Harris’s only bowling appearance came on 29 January against UP Warriorz. She returned 2/22 from three overs and then scored 75 from 37 balls as RCB won by eight wickets and secured a direct place in the final. This was a verified all-round performance within a season otherwise dominated by batting opportunity. The other eight appearances must remain in any participation summary, even though they contribute no bowling deliveries.
