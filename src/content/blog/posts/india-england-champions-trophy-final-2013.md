India won the 2013 Champions Trophy final by five runs at Edgbaston, Birmingham, on 23 June. After rain reduced the match to 20 overs per side, India made 129/7 and held England to 124/8. Ravindra Jadeja was Player of the Match for 33 not out and 2/24. The match remained an ODI despite its shortened length. [Completed match record](https://cricketdatabase.com/matches/2013-06-23-england-v-india)

## Why a 20 over final was still an ODI

England won the toss and chose to field, but the originally scheduled 50-over contest could not start on time. The ICC’s contemporary report records a 4:20pm start after the finish deadline was extended to 8:30pm. Both sides ultimately received 20 overs. Rain changed the available playing time; it did not turn the Champions Trophy final into a World Twenty20 fixture. [ICC report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/ashwin-jadeja-spin-india-to-elusive-title)

England’s target was 130. That is one more than India’s completed total, not a separately raised rain-adjusted target. The five-run result compares 129 with 124; the six runs England still needed to win are a different number. Keeping those two calculations separate prevents an apparent contradiction in the final-ball requirement. [Scorecard and target](https://cricketdatabase.com/matches/2013-06-23-england-v-india)

## India recovered from 66 for five

Ravi Bopara’s double-wicket maiden removed Suresh Raina and MS Dhoni, leaving India 66/5. Kohli and Jadeja then added 47 for the sixth wicket before Kohli was caught for 43. Jadeja stayed through the finish, and India added 12 in the last over. [BCCI match report](https://www.bcci.tv/news/article/report-eng-vs-ind-final-ct-2013)

The scorecard places the fifth wicket at the end of the 13th over. India therefore made 63 from its final seven overs, a calculated nine runs per over, after scoring 66 from the first 13 at 5.08. This comparison shows the scale of the recovery without calling 129 a universally safe score. It describes this innings, not a venue benchmark. [Wicket checkpoints](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/11562/eng-vs-ind-final-icc-champions-trophy-2013)

## Morgan and Bopara repaired the chase

England fell to 46/4 before Morgan and Bopara added 64. Their partnership took the total to 110, but Ishant Sharma dismissed both in consecutive deliveries in the 18th over. Jadeja then bowled Jos Buttler, and Tim Bresnan was run out. The rebuilding pair’s departure mattered because the closing task passed to new batters. [ICC account](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/ashwin-jadeja-spin-india-to-elusive-title)

A partnership total is not always the sum of two complete individual scores. Morgan’s 33 and Bopara’s 30 do not by themselves explain every run added between the fourth and fifth wickets. The team score checkpoints are the appropriate boundaries for measuring the stand.

## The final two overs

| Chase state | Runs needed to win | Balls remaining | Required runs per over |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| After 18 overs | 19 | 12 | 9.50 |
| After 19 overs | 15 | 6 | 15.00 |
| Before the last ball | 6 | 1 | 36.00 |

The first two requirements come from BCCI’s contemporary report; the final-ball requirement is confirmed by the ICC. The calculated rates express the task, not England’s chance of success. The 19th over yielded only four runs, so a chase needing fewer total runs nevertheless became harder on a per-ball basis. [BCCI](https://www.bcci.tv/news/article/report-eng-vs-ind-final-ct-2013), [ICC](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/ashwin-jadeja-spin-india-to-elusive-title)

Ashwin conceded nine in the final over. Tredwell missed its last delivery, leaving England on 124/8. India did not need to take the final two wickets: completing the allocated overs with England below 130 was sufficient. [ICC finish](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/ashwin-jadeja-spin-india-to-elusive-title)

## Read the spells alongside the late wickets

Ashwin finished with 2/15 and Jadeja with 2/24 from four overs each. Together they conceded 39 in eight overs, a calculated 4.88 per over. Ishant’s 2/36 was more expensive overall, yet included the two consecutive wickets that broke the established partnership. Aggregate economy and the timing of wickets describe different parts of the defence; neither should erase the other. [Bowling ledger](https://cricketdatabase.com/matches/2013-06-23-england-v-india)
