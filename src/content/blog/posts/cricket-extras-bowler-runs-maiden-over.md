Not every run added to the team score is charged to the bowler. Runs off the bat, wides and no-ball extras enter the bowler’s conceded-runs total. Byes and leg byes do not. That difference explains how a team can score during a maiden over.

MCC’s [Law 18.10](https://www.lords.org/getattachment/e8b12929-1e84-4927-af4e-2a5875b53589/Laws-of-Cricket-2017-Code-4th-Edition-%282026%29.pdf) separates the batter’s credit, extras and the bowler’s debit. A no-ball does not collapse those categories into one: if additional runs are byes or leg byes, only the no-ball penalty is recorded as the no-ball extra.

## Four runs from the delivery can mean different things

These are constructed scoring examples under the standard MCC Laws, not real-match records.

| Delivery outcome | Team runs | Batter runs | Runs charged to bowler |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Legal ball hit for four | 4 | 4 | 4 |
| Legal ball passes untouched for four byes | 4 | 0 | 0 |
| No-ball hit for four | 5 | 4 | 5 |
| No-ball passes untouched for four byes | 5 | 0 | 1 |

The final two rows both move the team score by five, but the bowler’s figures differ by four. A data process that copies every team run into the bowler’s column would incorrectly charge five in the last case.

The [NSW scorers’ guide](https://www.nswcusa.com.au/_files/ugd/aac121_4b2be606372342dfa5aadbf7a3d33492.pdf) explicitly describes recording one no-ball plus the accompanying byes or leg byes when the umpire signals both. Those non-bat runs are not credited to the batter either.

## Can a maiden over contain byes

Yes. A maiden is an over with no runs charged to its bowler, so byes or leg byes do not spoil it. Wides and no-balls do because their penalties are charged to the bowler. An incomplete part-over is not a maiden; the NSW guide also notes that an umpire-miscounted completed over can be one. [Maiden definition](https://www.nswcusa.com.au/_files/ugd/aac121_4b2be606372342dfa5aadbf7a3d33492.pdf).

Suppose one legal ball produces four leg byes and the other five legal balls produce no runs. The team has added four, but the bowler has conceded zero. It is still a maiden. If one of those deliveries were instead a wide, the bowler would have conceded a run and the over would not be a maiden.

## No balls and the over count

A no-ball adds its penalty without using a ball of the over. It does not become dead merely because no-ball was called, so further action can still produce runs. [MCC Laws 21.13–21.16](https://www.lords.org/getattachment/e8b12929-1e84-4927-af4e-2a5875b53589/Laws-of-Cricket-2017-Code-4th-Edition-%282026%29.pdf).

For example, six legal scoreless deliveries plus a separate no-ball penalty require seven actual deliveries. The completed over costs the bowler one run, not zero. Distinguish deliveries recorded from legal balls credited when reading a detailed score log.

## Checking that a scorecard balances

One useful audit is to compare the sum of all batter runs plus all extras with the team total. Separately, compare the bowlers’ combined conceded runs plus byes, leg byes and applicable penalty runs with that same total. Do not add wides and no-ball extras a second time to the bowling sum.

The accounting category matters more than an assumption about who was at fault. Read the umpire’s signal and the official score record. For competition-specific variations, use that competition’s conditions alongside the Laws.
