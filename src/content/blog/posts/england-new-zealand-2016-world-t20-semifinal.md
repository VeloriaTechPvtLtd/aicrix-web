**England beat New Zealand by seven wickets in Delhi on 30 March 2016 to reach the men’s World T20 final.** New Zealand made 153/8 from 20 overs; England finished on 159/3 after 17.1 overs. Jason Roy’s 78 from 44 balls earned the match award. [Match record](https://www.crictracker.com/live-scores/eng-vs-nz-1st-semi-final-t20i-icc-mens-t20-world-cup-2016-1459344600/full-scorecard/).

The winning target was 154, not 159. England’s final score went beyond the target, and 17.1 overs means 103 legal balls used, leaving 17 unused. The seven-wicket margin describes the wickets remaining, not a subtraction of the teams’ runs.

## New Zealand built a platform but could not sustain it

England chose to field. After Martin Guptill’s early dismissal, Kane Williamson and Colin Munro added 74 for the second wicket. Williamson made 32 and Munro 46; Corey Anderson subsequently contributed 28. Those three innings accounted for 106 of New Zealand’s 153 runs. [Batting and wicket ledger](https://www.crictracker.com/live-scores/eng-vs-nz-1st-semi-final-t20i-icc-mens-t20-world-cup-2016-1459344600/full-scorecard/).

The second-wicket stand moved the score from 17/1 after 2.1 overs to 91/2 after 10.3. That is 74 runs across 50 legal balls, a rate of 8.88 per six balls. It was a productive middle passage, but it did not guarantee a matching finish.

Ross Taylor’s dismissal left New Zealand 134/4 after 16.3 overs. Only 19 more runs arrived from the remaining 21 legal balls, with four further wickets lost. That closing segment ran at 5.43 per six balls. The change matters more than simply attaching the word “collapse” to the final score: it shows exactly how little the total advanced after the fourth wicket.

Ben Stokes dismissed Luke Ronchi and Anderson on consecutive deliveries at 17.3 and 17.4, then removed Mitchell Santner in the last over. He finished with 3/26. Chris Jordan’s four overs cost 24 and brought Taylor’s wicket. [Full bowling figures and dismissal timings](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/15802/eng-vs-nz-1st-semi-final-icc-world-t20-2016).

Stokes and Jordan together conceded 50 in eight overs, or 6.25 per over, while taking four wickets. That combined rate covers their complete spells; it should not be mistaken for a calculation of the last eight overs of the innings.

## Roy changed the chase before the middle order arrived

Roy and Alex Hales put on 82 for the first wicket. Hales contributed 20 from 19 balls, while Roy continued to 78 before Ish Sodhi bowled him. The ICC’s contemporary account describes the opening stand and Roy’s dismissal, followed immediately by Eoin Morgan’s first-ball dismissal. [ICC match report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/jason-roy-leads-england-march-to-the-final).

Roy struck eleven fours and two sixes: 56 boundary runs within his 78. The other 22 were also part of the innings. Boundaries supplied 71.8% of his runs, but the opening partnership was a shared team total rather than Roy’s eventual individual score.

| Start, target 154 | 154 | 120 | 7.70 |
| 82/1 after 8.2 overs | 72 | 70 | 6.17 |
| 110/3 after 12.2 overs | 44 | 46 | 5.74 |

The requirements are calculated from the [recorded wicket checkpoints](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/15802/eng-vs-nz-1st-semi-final-icc-world-t20-2016). Even after Sodhi’s two wickets in two balls, England needed fewer than a run a ball. The wickets interrupted the chase, but did not restore its original scoring requirement.

## Root and Buttler finished without another wicket

Joe Root finished unbeaten on 27 and Jos Buttler on 32. Their unbroken stand took England from 110/3 to 159/3, adding 49 in 29 legal balls. The ICC report confirms that they completed the win with seven wickets and 17 balls remaining. [ICC](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/jason-roy-leads-england-march-to-the-final).

The stand’s rate was 49 × 6 ÷ 29 = 10.14 per six balls, well above the 5.74 requirement when it began. Root’s and Buttler’s full scores sum to 59, not 49, because Root had already scored before Buttler joined him. Partnership runs and combined innings runs answer different questions.

England’s victory therefore had two complementary parts: wickets restricted the end of New Zealand’s innings, then Roy’s start reduced the burden on the remaining batters. The semifinal secured a place in the [final against West Indies](/blog/west-indies-england-2016-world-t20-final); it did not itself win the tournament.
