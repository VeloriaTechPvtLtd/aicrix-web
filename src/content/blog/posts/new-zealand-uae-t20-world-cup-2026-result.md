New Zealand beat the UAE by ten wickets at MA Chidambaram Stadium in Chennai on 10 February 2026. After the UAE made 173/6, Tim Seifert and Finn Allen completed an unbroken chase of 175/0 in 15.2 overs. Seifert was Player of the Match. [BCCI match details](https://www.bcci.tv/matches/b98d7b19-c7a4-4530-ba64-089c6c8f0084/new-zealand-vs-united-arab-emirates/match-details).

The two halves of the game are worth separating. The UAE produced two substantial individual scores and a century partnership. New Zealand then reached the target without exposing its middle order, with 28 deliveries still available.

The target was 174. A winning stroke can carry a chasing team beyond the exact target, so 175/0 is consistent with a chase that required 174 rather than evidence of an extra target adjustment.

## The UAE built around Waseem and Sharafu

Muhammad Waseem finished unbeaten on 66 from 45 balls, while Alishan Sharafu made 55 from 47. Their second-wicket partnership added 107 after the first wicket fell at 12. The innings ultimately reached 173, including 14 extras. [Full match scorecard](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/139062/nz-vs-uae).

A long partnership can establish a platform without fixing what happens at the finish. The UAE had wickets available, but the value of that position depended on the runs added and the deliveries used, not simply how long the stand survived.

Matt Henry took 2/37. Mitchell Santner conceded 23 in four overs and took one wicket, while New Zealand used seven bowlers in total. Those are different measures of contribution: Henry led the wicket count, while Santner's full spell cost 5.75 runs per over. ## The opening pair finished the job

Seifert made 89 not out from 42 balls and Allen 84 not out from 50. Their combined 173 runs, plus two wides, reconcile exactly to the team's 175. Neither opener was dismissed, so no New Zealand batter below them was required to take strike.

The powerplay comparison helps explain the different positions. The UAE reached 50/1 after six overs; New Zealand reached 78/0. That was a 28-run lead at the same point, with one fewer wicket lost. It describes the start of this chase, not an expected advantage for every future batting side in Chennai. [NZC innings and powerplay notes](https://archive.nzc.nz/Scorecards/1427/1427167.html).

Seifert hit 12 fours and three sixes, producing 66 boundary runs. Allen's five fours and five sixes produced 50. Together those boundaries contributed 116 of the team's 175, or 66.29%. This calculation uses the team total including extras; it is not the proportion of deliveries hit for a boundary.

## How many balls were left

In score notation, 15.2 means 15 completed overs and two legal deliveries, or 92 balls. Subtracting 92 from the allotted 120 gives 28 unused. Dividing 175 by 92 and multiplying by six produces an innings scoring rate of 11.41 runs per over.

That rate is not a prediction, and it is not New Zealand's cumulative tournament net run rate. It simply describes this completed innings. Likewise, a ten-wicket margin establishes that the opening partnership survived; the balls remaining show how early the target was reached.

For a retrospective performance review, preserve both facts. The undefeated pair supplied all New Zealand's batter runs, while the rest of the batting lineup had no batting opportunity in this match. A “did not bat” entry is not an innings of zero.
