Smriti Mandhana leads women’s T20I career scoring with 4,923 runs in the records checked on 14 November 2026. Suzie Bates follows on 4,758, a gap of 165. Eight batters have reached 3,500 runs. These are international career totals, not runs in a single World Cup or in domestic competitions such as the WPL and WBBL. ## Why an older leaderboard can name a different leader

Mandhana passed Bates during the Asian Games semifinal against Bangladesh on 20 September 2026. The [ICC’s contemporary report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/star-batter-mandhana-sets-historic-milestone-in-women-s-t20is) records that change. It is a dated milestone, not a live career database: the current 4,923 should not be substituted into a description of her total on that day.

Recent innings also move positions below the leader. Mooney’s 38 and 39 against New Zealand on 18 and 20 October added 77 runs. Against her earlier 3,783, that gives 3,860 and moves her above Devine’s 3,817. An August list with Devine fifth is therefore not the same snapshot as this one. [Mooney’s recent innings and career table](https://www.cricbuzz.com/profiles/9989/beth-mooney), [Devine’s record](https://www.cricbuzz.com/profiles/9165/sophie-devine).

## Runs per innings are not batting average

Career runs measure accumulation. Batting opportunities help explain how that accumulation differs. Mooney’s 3,860 came in 121 innings, or 31.90 runs per innings. Her 28 not outs leave 93 dismissals, producing a calculated batting average of 41.51 when rounded to two decimal places. ACS displays 41.50, consistent with truncation; the underlying runs and dismissals agree. Those two calculations use the same runs but answer different questions.

Mandhana’s 4,923 across 174 innings give 28.29 runs per innings; Bates’s 4,758 across 178 give 26.73. Mandhana therefore leads this pair in both aggregate runs and runs per batting innings at this cutoff. That is a retrospective comparison, not a prediction of their next score or proof of equal opposition, conditions or batting roles.

An appearance is not automatically a batting innings. Harmanpreet’s record contains 213 matches but 190 innings. Dividing her runs by appearances would include matches in which she never batted and would not produce the conventional batting average. [Harmanpreet’s record](https://www.cricbuzz.com/profiles/9082/harmanpreet-kaur).

## Keep the record and the match question separate

For a career-record question, use the total and its date. For a forthcoming match, begin again with availability, recent international innings and the likely batting position. A long career total cannot establish how many balls a player will face in a particular chase, and a World Cup leaderboard cannot replace the all-opposition career list.

The boundary deserves the same attention as first place. Oza and Verma can enter this group without anyone above them losing a run. Refresh both names, the qualifying players and the full ranking together rather than updating only the leader. Domestic innings should never be added to make a player cross an international threshold.

## What the Taylor discrepancy does and does not establish

The West Indies governing-body table lists 3,740 runs from 138 innings, with 33 not outs. Cricbuzz lists 3,684 from 137 innings, with 32 not outs. The numerical difference is 56 runs, one innings and one not-out innings. That does not, by itself, identify an omitted match or explain why the providers differ. The table above attributes the selected total rather than treating a majority of sources as proof.

Taylor’s recent international ledger records unbeaten scores of 1, 9 and 64 against Zimbabwe on 3, 5 and 7 October 2026. Those innings add 74, but checking three matches is not the same as auditing her complete career. Her rank and qualification are unchanged under either published total. An exact career-record claim should retain the discrepancy until it has been reconciled, and no strike-rate or average comparison for Taylor is calculated here.
