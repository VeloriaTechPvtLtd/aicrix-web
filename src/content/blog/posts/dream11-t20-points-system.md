*Rules checked on 14 November 2026. All three scorecards below are fictional teaching examples.*

A cricket scorecard gives you runs, balls, wickets and catches. To calculate fantasy points, convert the relevant contributions, check which adjustments apply, then apply any player multiplier. Keeping those steps separate makes the total easier to check.

## Start with the correct scoring format

The [official Dream11 T20 reference](https://www.dream11.com/features/how-to-play) supplies these values used in the examples:

- A run earns 1 point, a four adds a 4-point bonus, and a six adds a 6-point bonus. A century earns 16 bonus points instead of the lower batting milestone bonuses.

- A bowler-credited wicket earns 30, with another 8 for bowled or LBW; each qualifying dot earns 1. Three wickets earn a 4-point bonus.

- A catch earns 8. An announced starter who actually starts earns 4.

- Strike-rate adjustments exclude players classified as bowlers and require at least 10 balls faced. A rate of 150.01–170 earns 4.

- Economy adjustments require at least two overs. An economy of 6–7 earns 2.

- Captain points are doubled; vice-captain points are multiplied by 1.5.

This is a selected reference, not the complete matrix. Check the format and mode before applying it. Dream11’s second-innings mode excludes announcement points and counts contributions from that innings only.

## Example 1 A century

Imagine a batter who starts, makes 100 from 60 balls, hits eight fours and four sixes, and has no bowling or fielding contributions.

First check the underlying cricket score. Eight fours contribute 32 runs and four sixes contribute 24, leaving 44 runs obtained another way. All 100 runs already belong in the run total; the boundary entries below are fantasy bonuses.

| Component | Calculation | Points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Runs | 100 × 1 | 100 |
| Four bonuses | 8 × 4 | 32 |
| Six bonuses | 4 × 6 | 24 |
| Century bonus | One qualifying century | 16 |
| Strike rate | 100 ÷ 60 × 100 = 166.67 | 4 |
| Starting appearance | Eligible starter | 4 |
| Total | Sum before multiplier | 180 |

The milestone entry is 16, not 4 + 8 + 12 + 16. Adding all four batting milestones would inflate this example by 24 points.

If selected as captain, the result is 180 × 2 = **360 points**. As vice-captain, it is 180 × 1.5 = **270 points**. These are alternative roles, not two multipliers to apply together.

## Example 2 A three wicket spell

Imagine a starting player with figures of 4–0–24–3, 12 qualifying dot deliveries and one catch. Two wickets are bowled and the third is caught. The player does not bat.

Break the performance into entries:

- Wickets: 3 × 30 = 90.

- Bowled bonuses: 2 × 8 = 16.

- Dots: 12 × 1 = 12.

- Three-wicket bonus: 4.

- Economy: 24 ÷ 4 = 6, giving 2.

- Catch: 8.

- Starting appearance: 4.

The total is **90 + 16 + 12 + 4 + 2 + 8 + 4 = 136 points**.

The assumed figures contain no maiden. To reproduce the total, a reader needs the dismissal types and dot count alongside overs, wickets and runs conceded. A basic bowling summary alone does not contain all those inputs.

## Example 3 A nine ball cameo

A starting batter scores 18 from nine balls, including two fours and one six, with no other contributions.

The calculation is **18 + (2 × 4) + (1 × 6) + 4 = 36 points**.

The arithmetic strike rate is 200, but the innings contains only nine balls. The strike-rate adjustment stays at zero because the eligibility threshold has not been reached. Calculating the rate first and checking the minimum second avoids inserting an extra six points by mistake.

## Check a total one category at a time

Record the format, mode, player’s listed role and exact scorecard inputs. Keep runs separate from boundary counts, and wickets separate from dismissal types. Label each adjustment so another reader can trace it to an event or condition.

Add the components before applying the player’s multiplier. If your result differs from the displayed total, compare the categories individually rather than changing several assumptions together.

The official reference excludes Super Over and Super Five actions and says live totals can change before the match is finalized. Save the rules-check date with the calculation, and revisit an old example against the applicable scoring version rather than silently treating today’s table as historical rules.
