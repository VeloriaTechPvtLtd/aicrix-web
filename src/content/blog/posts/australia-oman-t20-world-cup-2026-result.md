Australia beat Oman by **nine wickets with 62 balls remaining** at Pallekele on 20 February 2026. Oman were all out for 104, and Australia reached 108/1 in 9.4 overs. Adam Zampa took 4/21 and Mitchell Marsh finished unbeaten on 64. The [full match scorecard](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/139351/aus-vs-oman-40th-match-group-b-icc-mens-t20-world-cup-2026) confirms the result and innings totals.

The game was Australia’s last group fixture at the men’s T20 World Cup. It later became significant for another reason: after Glenn Maxwell announced his international retirement in November, the ICC identified this match as his final appearance for Australia.

## Zampa finished an innings that never settled

Xavier Bartlett bowled Aamir Kaleem with the first delivery. Oman reached 47/3 after six overs, but Wasim Ali’s 32 from 33 balls was the only innings above 20.

Zampa dismissed Hammad Mirza and Mohammad Nadeem before returning to remove Shakeel Ahmed and Shafiq Jan with the first two deliveries of the 17th over. Those last two wickets ended the innings at 104.

Oman added 57 runs while losing their remaining seven wickets after the powerplay. That passage occupied 62 legal balls: 98 for the completed innings minus the first 36. Its scoring rate was therefore about 5.52 runs per over, compared with 7.83 in the powerplay. The falling rate and repeated wickets left Australia a modest target.

Six Oman batters were bowled: Kaleem, Karan Sonavale, Jatinder Singh, Mirza, Wasim and Jan. That count describes dismissal types, not six wickets for one bowler.

Fifteen fours and four sixes produced 84 runs, or 77.8% of Australia’s total. Marsh alone hit 52 runs in boundaries. The boundary count helps explain how Australia completed a target of 105 before the halfway point of a full 20-over innings.

## Why the final score was 108 rather than 105

Australia needed 105 to win. A winning boundary can take the score beyond that target, so 108/1 is consistent with a successful chase of 105.

The overs notation also matters. **9.4 means nine completed overs and four balls**, or 58 legal deliveries. Subtracting 58 from the available 120 gives the reported 62 balls remaining. The result is expressed as nine wickets because Australia had lost one wicket when the chase ended.

## Maxwell’s last international match

Maxwell took 2/13 in three overs and did not need to bat. His wickets were Jiten Ramanandi, lbw, and Wasim Ali, bowled. Nadeem’s lbw dismissal belonged to Zampa.

On **2 November 2026**, the [ICC’s retirement announcement](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/australia-great-announces-retirement-from-international-cricket?appview=true) confirmed that Maxwell’s final international appearance had been this Oman match. His last match date and retirement announcement date are therefore different.

Retirement from internationals did not mean retirement from all T20 cricket. [Cricket Australia’s November announcement](https://www.cricket.com.au/news/4603301/glenn-maxwell-retirement-stumps-calls-time-t20is-first-class-cricket-australia-continue-melbourne-stars-big-bash-bbl) said he would continue with Melbourne Stars.

## Why the win did not take Australia into the Super Eight

Australia were already eliminated before playing Oman. They had beaten Ireland but lost to Zimbabwe and Sri Lanka, and the closing win left them third in Group B. Zimbabwe and Sri Lanka advanced. [Cricket Australia’s match review](https://www.cricket.com.au/news/4454231/australia-oman-t20-world-cup-match-40-report-scores-pallekele-stadium-kandy-final-match-eliminated-zampa-maxwell-wickets-marsh-half-century?tags=9037).

The result was a dominant finish to Australia’s group campaign, but it did not change which two teams qualified. For the complete tournament picture, this match belongs alongside the final Group B standings rather than a prediction about later fixtures.
