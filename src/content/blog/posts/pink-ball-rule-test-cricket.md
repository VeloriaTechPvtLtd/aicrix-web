A daytime Test can use a pink ball under the ICC trial, but the teams must agree before the match begins. That colour choice lasts throughout the Test. It is not permission to start with red, change to pink when clouds arrive, and switch back when the sun returns. [ICC announcement, 1 October 2026](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/key-changes-across-formats-with-new-icc-playing-conditions).

Two decisions must stay separate: choosing the match’s ball and deciding whether conditions permit play. A pink ball does not guarantee an uninterrupted day.

## Before the match or during it

| Hypothetical situation | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Both teams agree to pink before an eligible daytime Test | Pink is the match-long choice |
| A red-ball Test is underway and visibility deteriorates | No mid-match red-to-pink switch |
| A pink-ball Test becomes brighter on day two | The agreement cannot be reversed to red |
| Either colour is in use and conditions become unfit | Apply the fitness-for-play process, not a colour swap |

These examples illustrate timing, not actual fixtures. The October announcement applies its changes to international series starting on or after 1 October 2026; an ongoing series retains its previous regulations. A match date alone therefore does not establish which edition governs it.

## Who decides that bad light stops play

Under the [October 2026 men’s Test conditions, clauses 2.7–2.8](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/a8114zro5tkioifz0jqg.pdf), the umpires jointly judge whether conditions are dangerous or unreasonable. They consult the ICC Match Referee for the suspension decision. Conditions need not be perfect, but foreseeable danger and conditions in which proceeding is not sensible are different from mere inconvenience.

Light-meter readings guide that judgment and can provide benchmarks within the match. They are not a published universal number that spectators can apply to every ground. After a suspension, the umpires inspect conditions and call players back when they jointly consider them fit.

**Hypothetical example:** teams agreed to pink before the Test, but the umpires later find visibility unacceptable. The agreement does not overrule that judgment. Conversely, a grey sky by itself does not establish that the criteria for stopping have been met.

## What to check before interpreting a live match

Find the applicable series conditions and the confirmed pre-match ball agreement first. Then follow the officials’ decision on the actual conditions. Do not treat an old headline about a proposed trial as confirmation that today’s match uses it.

The trial aims to reduce play lost to poor light. That aim is not a promise of a particular number of extra overs, predictable swing or a result. An analysis claiming a competitive advantage would need match-specific evidence beyond the existence of the rule.
