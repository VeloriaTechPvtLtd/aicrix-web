**India beat Sri Lanka by six wickets in the men’s ODI World Cup final at Wankhede Stadium, Mumbai, on 2 April 2011.** Sri Lanka made 274/6; India reached 277/4 in 48.2 overs, with ten legal deliveries unused. MS Dhoni was Player of the Match. [Complete match scorecard](https://cricketdatabase.com/matches/2011-04-02-india-v-sri-lanka).

The final is remembered for Dhoni’s winning six, but the route to it included an early Indian setback, two substantial recovery stands and a demanding Sri Lankan finish. Reading those phases together explains more than the final margin alone.

## Final score and winning target

| Team | Total | Overs | Leading innings |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sri Lanka | 274/6 | 50 | Mahela Jayawardene 103 not out from 88 balls |
| India | 277/4 | 48.2 | Gautam Gambhir 97 from 122; MS Dhoni 91 not out from 79 |

The winning target was **275**, one more than Sri Lanka’s total. India did not need 277 at the start; its final score passed the target because the winning shot was a six. Both innings and the dismissal records are available in the [full ledger](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/2215/ind-vs-sl-final-icc-world-cup-2011).

## Sri Lanka’s finish changed the size of the chase

Jayawardene remained unbeaten while Kumar Sangakkara contributed 48. Sri Lanka were 182/5 when Chamara Kapugedera fell in the 40th over, but Nuwan Kulasekara’s 32 and Thisara Perera’s 22 not out supported a substantial late acceleration. [BCCI match report](https://www.bcci.tv/news/article/match-report-ind-v-sl-2011-cwc-final).

The final five overs produced 63, as the [ICC’s contemporary account](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/dhoni-gambhir-lead-india-to-the-cup-that-counts) records. Subtracting that burst from 274 leaves 211 after 45 overs. The closing five therefore supplied almost 23% of the total in only 10% of the scheduled overs.

That arithmetic identifies the acceleration without asserting what a different bowling choice would have produced. Zaheer Khan and Yuvraj Singh each took two wickets, but those figures do not make every stage of Sri Lanka’s innings equally quiet.

## Three Indian partnerships with different jobs

Lasith Malinga dismissed Virender Sehwag for nought and Sachin Tendulkar for 18, leaving India 31/2 after 6.1 overs. Gambhir and Virat Kohli then added 83 before Kohli fell for 35. Dhoni entered at No. 5 and added 109 with Gambhir. [BCCI’s chase account](https://www.bcci.tv/news/article/match-report-ind-v-sl-2011-cwc-final), [ICC report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/dhoni-gambhir-lead-india-to-the-cup-that-counts).

| Partnership | Team score at start | Team score at end | Stand |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gambhir and Kohli, third wicket | 31/2 | 114/3 | 83 |
| Gambhir and Dhoni, fourth wicket | 114/3 | 223/4 | 109 |
| Dhoni and Yuvraj, fifth wicket | 223/4 | 277/4 | 54 unbroken |

These are team partnership runs, including extras, not sums of the players’ complete individual scores. The [fall-of-wickets record](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/2215/ind-vs-sl-final-icc-world-cup-2011) supplies the endpoints; the ICC report corroborates the three stands.

When Dhoni arrived at 114/3 after 21.4 overs, India needed 161 from 170 legal balls, approximately 5.68 per over. Gambhir’s dismissal at 223/4 after 41.2 left 52 needed from 52 balls. The fourth-wicket stand had kept the requirement manageable while taking the chase deep.

Those are descriptions of the score state, not reconstructed win probabilities. They also show why Gambhir’s 97 and Dhoni’s 91 answer a different question from their 109-run partnership: each batter scored runs with other partners.

## The final two overs began with five needed

India were 270/4 after 48 overs. Yuvraj took a single from Kulasekara’s next delivery, leaving four required; Dhoni then hit six. The [closing delivery sequence](https://cricsearch.com/match/ind-vs-sl-final-icc-cricket-world-cup-201011-1585/commentary) records the single and winning boundary, with the winning six also recorded in the [BCCI report](https://www.bcci.tv/news/article/match-report-ind-v-sl-2011-cwc-final).

Yuvraj finished on 21 not out. The final pair added 54 although only 52 were needed when they joined: the winning boundary explains the difference. The score is not an error and the chase did not require every scheduled over.

## Match award, tournament award and match date

Dhoni received the match award for his unbeaten 91. Yuvraj was Player of the Tournament, a separate award recognizing the whole campaign. [BCCI report](https://www.bcci.tv/news/article/match-report-ind-v-sl-2011-cwc-final). Neither label erases the contribution of Gambhir or Jayawardene to this final.

The match date is 2 April, even though the contemporary BCCI and ICC reports carry 3 April publication dates. An archive should retain the date of play rather than turn the later news timestamp into a second fixture.
