Breaking your own stumps is not automatically hit wicket. The decision depends on what the striker was doing, when the wicket broke and whether an exception applies. The same contact with a stump can therefore produce different decisions.

This guide explains the [MCC 2026 Laws](https://www.lords.org/getattachment/e8b12929-1e84-4927-af4e-2a5875b53589/Laws-of-Cricket-2017-Code-4th-Edition-%282026%29.pdf). Check the playing conditions of the particular competition before applying these examples.

## The action matters more than the falling bail

Law 35 covers receiving the delivery, recovering balance, and immediately starting the first run. The ball must be live and the bowler must have entered the delivery stride. Before that stride, the specified self-breaking calls for dead ball instead. [MCC Law 35](https://www.lords.org/getattachment/e8b12929-1e84-4927-af4e-2a5875b53589/Laws-of-Cricket-2017-Code-4th-Edition-%282026%29.pdf)

Consider two fictional deliveries. In the first, A pushes the ball into a gap and immediately starts a single, knocking a bail off with the bat. In the second, A completes one run and knocks the wicket while turning for a second. Assuming the wicket is properly broken and no exception applies, the first is hit wicket; the second is not hit wicket merely because A caused the damage.

The first-run provision also covers an immediate departure after an opportunity to play, even without an attempted stroke. Later running, avoiding a run-out or stumping, and avoiding a throw are expressly excluded. [MCC Law 35.1–35.2](https://www.lords.org/getattachment/e8b12929-1e84-4927-af4e-2a5875b53589/Laws-of-Cricket-2017-Code-4th-Edition-%282026%29.pdf)

## A completed shot does not necessarily end the danger

MCC’s [February 2026 explanation](https://www.lords.org/getmedia/86ac7ad2-1031-4e2b-bdea-ff407e7b0db0/Ten-major-changes-to-Laws-for-2026-edition_6.pdf) says balance recovery remains part of receiving the ball. It also distinguishes contact that causes a batter to fall into the wicket from an incidental brush with a fielder. The latter is not an automatic escape from dismissal.

An instructive replay starts before the stumble, not with the bail landing. Did the striker regain balance and begin an unrelated movement, or was the stumble still the consequence of receiving the ball? Counting seconds alone cannot answer that causal question.

For detached equipment or a bat no longer held, intervening contact with another person is separately protected. Do not apply the same causal test as for the striker’s body or a held bat. [MCC’s explanation of the 2026 changes](https://www.lords.org/getmedia/86ac7ad2-1031-4e2b-bdea-ff407e7b0db0/Ten-major-changes-to-Laws-for-2026-edition_6.pdf)

## Avoiding injury is not one universal exception

Law 35 does not provide a blanket exemption for every evasive movement while receiving a delivery. Its protection for avoiding a throw is specific. The injury-avoidance exception in obstruction is a different provision, not a rule to import into hit wicket. [MCC Laws 35 and 37.2](https://www.lords.org/getattachment/e8b12929-1e84-4927-af4e-2a5875b53589/Laws-of-Cricket-2017-Code-4th-Edition-%282026%29.pdf)

Thus “the batter was protecting themselves” is incomplete as a description. Identify whether the action concerned the delivery, a subsequent throw, or contact with a fielder. This distinction explains the rule; it is not advice to prioritize a wicket over personal safety.

## No balls wides and the score

Hit wicket cannot dismiss the striker from a no-ball. A wide can produce the dismissal, with its one-run penalty retained. Otherwise no runs score on a hit-wicket dismissal except applicable five-run penalties. If the bowler does not deliver after entering the stride, dead ball is required. [MCC Law 35.2–35.3](https://www.lords.org/getattachment/e8b12929-1e84-4927-af4e-2a5875b53589/Laws-of-Cricket-2017-Code-4th-Edition-%282026%29.pdf)

Here is a fictional scorebook comparison, with no other penalties or dismissal mode. Starting at 80/2, a valid hit-wicket dismissal from an ordinary delivery leaves 80/3. Change the delivery to a wide: the result is 81/3, not 80/3. Change it to a no-ball: hit wicket is unavailable, so the same self-breaking action cannot be recorded as that wicket.

## Read the whole sequence

Keep the replay questions in order: what broke the wicket, what action caused it, was the delivery a no-ball, and does an exception apply? A freeze-frame of displaced bails answers none of those questions by itself.
