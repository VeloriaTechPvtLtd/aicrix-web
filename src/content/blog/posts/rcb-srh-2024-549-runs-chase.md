Sunrisers Hyderabad made 287/3 against Royal Challengers Bengaluru on 15 April 2024. RCB finished on 262/7, losing by 25 runs despite a huge reply. The two totals added to **549 runs**. [Official IPL match report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/tata-ipl-2024-match-30-rcb-vs-srh-match-report)

## SRH added a final surge

Travis Head scored 102 and Heinrich Klaasen 67. With SRH already 241/3 after 18 overs, Abdul Samad finished unbeaten on 37 from ten balls as the innings reached 287. [IPL batting account](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/tata-ipl-2024-match-30-rcb-vs-srh-match-report)

The last two overs therefore added **46 runs**, or 23 per over. That moved the winning target from a hypothetical 242 at the earlier checkpoint to the actual 288. Those final runs remained part of RCB’s problem even after their own aggressive opening.

## A fast start can still lose ground

RCB scored 79 in the powerplay but were 122/5 halfway through the chase. Dinesh Karthik’s 83 from 35 balls helped lift the eventual total to 262. [IPL chase account](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/tata-ipl-2024-match-30-rcb-vs-srh-match-report)

The equations show the pressure:

- After six overs: 288 − 79 = **209 needed from 84 balls**, approximately 14.93 runs per over.
- After ten overs: 288 − 122 = **166 needed from 60 balls**, or 16.60 per over.
- Across the final ten overs, RCB added **140 runs**, still 26 short of what they needed at halfway.

The eventual defeat was by 25 runs because a tie required 287, while victory required 288. A high scoring rate alone did not mean the chase was on schedule; the target dictated the required pace. RCB’s second-half acceleration was considerable, but it followed a first half that had consumed half the innings and five wickets.
