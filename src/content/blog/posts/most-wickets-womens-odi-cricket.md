## Women’s ODI career wicket leaders

Jhulan Goswami leads women’s ODI cricket with 255 wickets. Her total is 64 ahead of Shabnim Ismail’s 191. The [ICC’s career tribute](https://www.icc-cricket.com/media-releases/icc-congratulates-jhulan-goswami-for-an-excellent-career) confirms Goswami finished with 255 wickets in 204 ODIs, while [Cricket Australia’s retirement report](https://www.cricket.com.au/news/3555872/true-icon-ismail-retires-from-international-cricket) confirms Ismail’s 191.

Twelve bowlers have reached 150 ODI wickets in the records checked on 14 November 2026. The table ranks wickets alone: a better average does not break a tie, and a player’s T20I or domestic-league wickets do not contribute to the total.

Lisa Sthalekar is next with 146, four short of the qualification. This is the complete 150-plus group, not a top ten that drops the tied players at its edge.

## Why the tied positions matter

Fitzpatrick and Mohammed both took 180 ODI wickets, so they share fourth. Kapp’s 181 puts her one ahead of both. The next position is sixth, not fifth. The same principle leaves Sana Mir and Khaka jointly eleventh on 151.

These are equal wicket totals, not identical careers. [ICC’s Fitzpatrick profile](https://www.icc-cricket.com/hall-of-fame/hall-of-famers/hall-of-famer-cathryn-fitzpatrick) confirms her 180 ODI wickets, and [Cricket West Indies’ retirement announcement](https://www.windiescricket.com/news/Westindieswomenconfirmretirement/) confirms Mohammed’s 180. Their Test and T20I records remain outside this comparison.

## Recent matches can change the qualification

Megan Schutt’s October 2026 Bangladesh series illustrates why a career table needs a cutoff. The [ICC’s 9 October report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/schutt-strikes-milestone-as-australia-cruise-to-victory-in-brisbane) records her 150th ODI wicket. Her [current individual ledger](https://www.cricbuzz.com/profiles/9108/megan-schutt) shows 2/19, 4/36 and 4/16 across the three October ODIs, ten wickets in the series and 158 overall. A table ending before that series would incorrectly exclude her from this threshold.

[Deepti Sharma’s ODI ledger](https://www.cricbuzz.com/profiles/11098/deepti-sharma) similarly includes the Zimbabwe series ending 28 October: two wickets, three wickets and then none. Those five wickets take her from 166 to 171. A wicketless final appearance still belongs to the career record; it should not disappear just because it leaves the wickets column unchanged.

## Total wickets and bowling opportunity

Kapp’s 181 wickets came from 6,851 recorded ODI balls, compared with Khaka’s 151 from 5,527. Dividing balls by wickets gives 37.85 and 36.60 respectively. Kapp leads the aggregate, while Khaka has the lower historical balls-per-wicket figure in this comparison. The extra 30 wickets came alongside 1,324 more deliveries, not an equal workload. [Kapp ledger](https://www.cricbuzz.com/profiles/9182/marizanne-kapp), [Khaka ledger](https://www.cricbuzz.com/profiles/11346/ayabonga-khaka).

That calculation is bowling strike rate, not economy. Economy measures runs conceded per over; bowling average measures runs conceded per wicket. None of these describes the probability of a wicket from the next ball, and none adjusts automatically for opposition, conditions, role or era.

## Keep World Cup records separate

World Cup ODI wickets are part of a player’s ODI career, not an extra total to add again. A World Cup-only leaderboard excludes bilateral and other qualifying ODI matches, so its leader or ordering can differ. Likewise, selection in a match is not the same thing as bowling an innings. For workload comparisons, use actual bowling innings or deliveries rather than appearances or a batting-table denominator.
