## Meg Lanning leads with 15 ODI hundreds

Meg Lanning leads the women’s ODI century list with 15. Smriti Mandhana and Hayley Matthews are joint second on 14, while Laura Wolvaardt and Suzie Bates share fourth place with 13.

Eight batters have at least ten centuries in the career ledger retrieved on 14 November 2026. This is an ODI-only table: a Test hundred or a T20I hundred does not increase the count shown here. [ACS women’s ODI hundreds record](https://stats.acscricket.com/Records/Womens_ODI/Overall/Players/Most_Centuries_in_Career.html)

Joint positions are deliberate. Mandhana is not placed ahead of Matthews on an unstated strike-rate, innings or recency tiebreak. Both have 14, so both occupy second place in a ranking determined solely by hundreds. The next position is fourth because three batters are already ahead.

## Lanning’s ODI record is different from her international total

The ICC’s retirement report records Lanning’s 17 international centuries, of which 15 came in ODIs. Her international retirement was announced in November 2023. [ICC retirement report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/australia-captain-announces-shock-retirement-from-international-cricket)

That distinction explains why a headline about “most international centuries” can name a different leader without contradicting this ODI table. The formats have different record sets.

An ODI innings of 150 is one century, not one century plus another entry for passing fifty. A double-century also contributes one hundred-scoring innings to a centuries count. The record tracks the number of qualifying innings, rather than adding one century for every block of 100 runs.

## Mandhana and Matthews are one hundred behind

At 14 each, Mandhana and Matthews need one further ODI century to equal Lanning’s recorded 15, assuming her total remains unchanged. A second additional hundred would put either on 16 and beyond that mark. These are arithmetic scenarios, not forecasts about selection, availability or the next match.

The ICC separately recorded Mandhana’s 14 ODI hundreds in its September 2026 report on a T20I milestone. Her T20I achievements do not add to those 14 ODI centuries. [ICC Mandhana report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/star-batter-mandhana-sets-historic-milestone-in-women-s-t20is)

Wolvaardt provides another useful format check. On 15 September 2026, the ICC reported her fourth T20I century and 18th international century overall. Those 18 are not 18 ODI hundreds; the current ODI table lists 13. [ICC Wolvaardt report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/wolvaardt-record-ton-gives-proteas-unassailable-series-lead)

## What a century ranking measures

A hundreds list measures how often a batter reached three figures. It does not measure all of her useful innings, the speed of scoring or her contribution in every match.

A score of 99 adds nothing to the century column, even though it may be decisive in the match. A short successful chase can also leave an opener without enough runs available to reach 100. These are reasons to read the record as one specific achievement rather than a complete player rating.

For a fuller comparison, keep ODI career runs, batting average and innings played alongside the century count. Do not merge WPL or other domestic T20 statistics into the international column. The formats answer different questions and have different opportunities to build a large innings.

## How to keep the record current

The table should change when a qualifying player scores another ODI hundred or when the underlying record is corrected. Its threshold should not change simply to preserve a convenient number of rows.

If a player on nine reaches ten, she joins the table. If that creates another tie at tenth-century totals, include her alongside the existing players rather than removing someone to keep an “eight-player” list.

## Quick answers

**Who has the most women’s ODI centuries?** Meg Lanning, with 15 in the retrieved record.

**Are Mandhana and Matthews tied?** Yes. Both have 14 and share second place.

**Does a T20I hundred count here?** No. This list counts only ODI centuries.

**How many women have ten or more ODI hundreds?** Eight in the ledger retrieved on 14 November 2026, including both players on exactly ten.
