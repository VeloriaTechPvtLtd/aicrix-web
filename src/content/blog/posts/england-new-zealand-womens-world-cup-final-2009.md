England beat New Zealand by four wickets at North Sydney Oval on 22 March 2009 to win the Women’s ODI World Cup. New Zealand made 166 in 47.2 overs; England reached 167/6 in 46.1. This was the 50-over final, not the [T20 final between the same teams later that year](/blog/england-new-zealand-2009-womens-world-t20-final).

## Shaw contributed twice

Nicky Shaw entered England’s side as a late replacement for Jenny Gunn. She took 4/34, then made an unbeaten 17 from 23 balls to help finish the chase. Her player-of-the-match performance therefore spanned both innings, rather than ending when New Zealand were dismissed.

## New Zealand recovered from 101/7

Lucy Doolan and Nicola Browne added 63 for the eighth wicket after New Zealand slipped to 101/7. Their partnership supplied most of the 65 runs added after that checkpoint and gave the bowlers a larger total to defend.

England began with a 74-run opening stand between Sarah Taylor and Caroline Atkins. But the chase subsequently reached 149/6: 75 more runs had cost six wickets. Doolan’s 3/23 and Aimee Mason’s 2/28 brought New Zealand back into the contest.

## Eighteen needed from 149/6

England still needed 18 when the sixth wicket fell. Shaw’s unbeaten contribution helped complete the chase with 23 legal balls remaining. The calculation is 300 minus 277: 46.1 overs represents 46 completed overs and one further ball, not a decimal fraction of an over.

The result gave England their third Women’s ODI World Cup title and their first outside England. The four-wicket margin alone hides the changing demands of the match: England first broke the top order, New Zealand rebuilt through an eighth-wicket stand, and England then needed a lower-order finish after a strong opening partnership.
