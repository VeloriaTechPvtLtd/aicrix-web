Pakistan won the 2009 men's World Twenty20 final at Lord's by eight wickets, securing their first title in the competition. Shahid Afridi's unbeaten 54 is the defining image, but the result becomes more interesting when the two innings are read together: Sri Lanka had to repair a collapse, while Pakistan could manage a chase with wickets available. [ICC's Lord's finals history](https://www.icc-cricket.com/media-releases/a-history-of-icc-world-cup-finals-at-lord-s?appview=true)

## Sri Lanka's recovery did not erase the damage

Sri Lanka slipped to 32 for four. Mohammad Amir opened with a wicket maiden, dismissing Tillakaratne Dilshan, and Abdul Razzaq removed Jehan Mubarak, Sanath Jayasuriya and Mahela Jayawardene. Pakistan had disrupted the innings before the middle overs. [ICC's contemporary final report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/pakistan-the-lords-of-icc-world-twenty20)

Sangakkara and Angelo Mathews then rescued the innings from 70 for six. Sangakkara finished unbeaten on 64, and Sri Lanka gave Pakistan a target of 139. This is an important distinction: recovery made a defence possible, but did not put the earlier wickets back in hand. The seventh-wicket pair had to build an innings that was already severely constrained. [ICC's Lord's finals history](https://www.icc-cricket.com/media-releases/a-history-of-icc-world-cup-finals-at-lord-s?appview=true)

## Pakistan did not need to sprint throughout

At halfway, Pakistan were 65 for two and needed 74 from 60 balls. They reached 103 for two after 15 overs, leaving 36 from 30. Afridi and Shoaib Malik ultimately added an unbeaten 76, with Malik contributing 24. Afridi's 54 came from 40 balls, following bowling figures of one for 20. The chase ended with eight balls unused; the final scoring event was a leg bye, not a winning boundary. [ICC's contemporary final report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/pakistan-the-lords-of-icc-world-twenty20)

The chase requirement moved from 7.4 runs per over at halfway to 7.2 after 15 overs: 74 × 6 ÷ 60 and 36 × 6 ÷ 30. Pakistan kept all eight remaining wickets through that passage, rather than trading wickets for a sharp acceleration.

## The semi-final adds context, not a prediction rule

Afridi had also contributed 51 from 34 balls and two wickets for 16 in Pakistan's seven-run semi-final victory over South Africa. Pakistan made 149 for four in that match, then defended rather than chased. Umar Gul's 17th and 19th overs conceded six runs apiece as South Africa's pursuit ran out of room.
