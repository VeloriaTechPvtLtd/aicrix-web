**Pushing the boundary rope backwards does not automatically enlarge the field.** Under the [MCC 2026 Laws, 19.3](https://www.lords.org/getattachment/e8b12929-1e84-4927-af4e-2a5875b53589/Laws-of-Cricket-2017-Code-4th-Edition-%282026%29.pdf), the boundary retains its original position when its solid marker is disturbed. The marker must be restored promptly when practical, with restoration during play taking place once the ball is dead.

The important distinction is between the boundary and the object showing it. Normally they coincide. After a dive moves the padding, a photograph of the padding alone may no longer identify the correct limit of the playing area.

## Establish the original edge

MCC Law 19.1 fixes the boundary before the toss for the duration of the match. For a grounded object marking it, Law 19.2 uses the edge nearest the pitch. A white-line boundary has its own marking provisions. [MCC Laws 19.1–19.2](https://www.lords.org/getattachment/e8b12929-1e84-4927-af4e-2a5875b53589/Laws-of-Cricket-2017-Code-4th-Edition-%282026%29.pdf).

That is why a worn strip of grass is evidence to investigate, not an automatic ruling. It might identify the previous position, but the viewer still needs to establish which edge was the match's actual boundary. Padding has width; its outer edge, centre and inner edge are not interchangeable.

This article addresses an established marker moving, not the separate question of setting boundary distances.

## A fictional outward movement

Imagine one straight section whose original pitch-facing edge is 70 metres from a chosen reference point. A previous fielding attempt pushes that section out to 70.5 metres. These are invented distances, not a measurement from a real ground or a permitted tolerance.

| Position along this line | Relationship to the original boundary |
| --- | --- |
| 69.8 metres | 0.2 metres inside |
| 70.0 metres | At the original boundary |
| 70.2 metres | 0.2 metres beyond the original boundary |
| 70.5 metres | New visible marker position, not an enlarged legal field |

Suppose the next ball, struck along the ground, reaches 70.2 metres before a fielder retrieves it. The ball has reached ground beyond the original boundary, despite remaining short of the displaced padding. Treating the visible extra half-metre as playable space would answer the wrong question.

The [October 2026 ICC men's T20I conditions, 19.3–19.4](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/bsxcho7ufe2pjban0wjp.pdf), preserve the original boundary and classify contact with ground beyond it as a boundary event. The later retrieval does not undo that event; clause 20.1 makes the ball dead when the boundary is scored.

Change the example so the ball is stopped at 69.8 metres. It has not crossed the original edge merely because the padding is misplaced elsewhere. That statement isolates the ball's location: the fielder's contact and any other relevant infringement still need their own checks.

## Contact matters as well as the ball position

Under [ICC clauses 19.4–19.5](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/bsxcho7ufe2pjban0wjp.pdf), a grounded fielder beyond the boundary cannot keep a live ball in play by touching it from there. A catch also fails if the fielder becomes grounded beyond the boundary while holding the ball before completing the catch.

In the fictional layout, feet at 70.2 metres are beyond the original edge even though they appear inside the shifted padding. A camera angle showing a gap between shoe and cushion would therefore be insufficient by itself.

The airborne first-touch and landing rules introduce further steps. Do not infer those additional rules from the displaced-rope example. Establish the correct boundary first, then test the catch against it.

## Restoration is a separate step

MCC Law 19.3 also addresses a fence or marker fragment that intrudes into the field: it must be removed promptly when practical, after the ball becomes dead if play is underway. The umpires' boundary checks apply before and during the match under Law 2.4. [MCC 2026 code](https://www.lords.org/getattachment/e8b12929-1e84-4927-af4e-2a5875b53589/Laws-of-Cricket-2017-Code-4th-Edition-%282026%29.pdf).

Do not turn that restoration instruction into permission to move equipment during a live chase. Equally, absence of a television shot showing repairs does not prove that repairs never happened. A broadcast can establish only the sequence it actually shows.

The inward-fragment case is not the simple outward-space example above. A ruling involving contact with displaced material needs the relevant object-contact provisions as well as the original-position rule. Merely measuring the ball's distance does not resolve every obstruction scenario.

## A useful replay record

Record four things separately: the established boundary before the disturbance; the movement of the marker; the ball and fielder contacts during the disputed play; and any later restoration. Keep frames in time order. A still taken after the event cannot reveal when the padding first moved.

Also identify the competition and rule edition. This current guide does not re-adjudicate a famous historical catch, infer deliberate misconduct from a scuff mark or reverse an official result. It explains the distinction a replay must resolve: a moved marker is not, by itself, a moved boundary.
