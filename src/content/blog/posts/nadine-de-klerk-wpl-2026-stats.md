Nadine de Klerk finished WPL 2026 with 133 runs and 16 wickets for Royal Challengers Bengaluru, but those totals came from different amounts of opportunity. She bowled in all nine matches and batted in six. Three of those batting innings ended unbeaten, producing an average of 44.33, while her bowling average was 15.69. [Season-specific player record](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/sports/cricket/wpl/player-stats/nadine-de-klerk/67302)

That distinction is central to understanding her season. The all-rounder label did not mean a long innings and four overs in every game. Sometimes she contributed with both skills; sometimes RCB’s chase finished without her needing to bat.

On 9 January against Mumbai Indians, de Klerk took 4/26 and then made 63 not out from 44 balls in RCB’s successful chase. The franchise’s own account records both contributions and the last-ball finish. This is a genuine combined performance, rather than two season totals placed together without showing whether they happened in the same match. ## Why a 44.33 average is not runs per match

| Measure calculated from the season ledger | Calculation | Result |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Batting average | 133 runs / 3 dismissals | 44.33 |
| Runs per batting innings | 133 / 6 innings | 22.17 |
| Runs per appearance | 133 / 9 matches | 14.78 |
| Batting strike rate | 133 / 96 balls × 100 | 138.54 |

These answer different questions. The average uses dismissals, so the three not-outs matter. Runs per innings describes how the total was distributed across batting opportunities. Runs per appearance also includes the three games in which she did not bat. None should be advertised as the number she was likely to score next time.

The distinction is especially clear in the final: she finished on 7 not out from five balls. That small unbeaten contribution belongs in both the runs total and the not-out count; it is not equivalent to her unbeaten 63 in terms of innings size. ## Bowling was more regular than batting

De Klerk completed four overs in seven games, three overs in one and one over in another. She took at least one wicket in eight of her nine appearances. Across the season, 192 legal balls divided by 16 wickets gives a bowling strike rate of 12.00; 251 runs divided by 32 overs gives an economy rate of 7.84.

Those aggregate rates still contain different matches: the final cost 48 runs for one wicket, whereas the second Gujarat meeting brought 2/17. The latter was a four-over return, not a brief bowling contribution whose economy looked low because it covered only a handful of deliveries. For a useful comparison with another all-rounder, keep the batting and bowling denominators separate. Check innings, balls faced and dismissals on one side; bowling innings, legal balls and wickets on the other. De Klerk’s season shows why a player can have regular bowling involvement while batting opportunity remains dependent on how the rest of the team performs. It does not establish a guaranteed future batting position, allocation or fantasy return.
