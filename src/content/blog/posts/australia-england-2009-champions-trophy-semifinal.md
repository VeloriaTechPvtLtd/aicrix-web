Australia beat England by nine wickets in the 2009 Champions Trophy semifinal at Centurion. The match contained two different rebuilding efforts: England rescued its innings, while Australia made sure an early wicket did not become a collapse.

## Bresnan and Wright gave England a total

Tim Bresnan’s 80 and Luke Wright’s 48 helped England recover to 257 all out in 47.4 overs on 2 October. Those runs gave England’s bowlers a meaningful target to defend, rather than allowing the early damage to decide the match immediately. [ICC retrospective](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/australia-v-england-icc-champions-trophy-2017-a-look-ahead)

## Australia’s partnership finished the job

Shane Watson and Ricky Ponting then added an unbeaten 252 for the second wicket. Watson finished on 136 not out and Ponting on 111 not out, taking Australia into the final.

An unbroken stand is important here for a concrete reason: after the opening wicket, England never dismissed either of the two batters completing the chase. Australia did not require a second lower-order repair comparable to England’s. The two centuries belonged to one continuous partnership, not separate phases divided by wickets.

## Watson’s own retrospective

In a 2025 interview published by the ICC, Watson identified the semifinal hundred as his favourite innings. He separately recalled the winning six and century against New Zealand in the final. Keeping those memories separate prevents the two 2009 knockout performances from becoming one blurred account. [Watson’s interview](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/the-moments-you-dream-of-shane-watson-on-champions-trophy-knockouts)

The nine-wicket margin should not erase England’s recovery, but neither does that recovery show that the total ought to have been defended. Bresnan and Wright repaired the first innings; Watson and Ponting completed a different task, sustaining their partnership until the target was reached.
