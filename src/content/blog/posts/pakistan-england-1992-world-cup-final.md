Pakistan defeated England by 22 runs at Melbourne on 25 March 1992 to win their first men's ODI World Cup. Imran Khan made 72, Javed Miandad 58, Inzamam-ul-Haq 42 and Wasim Akram 33. The result is often remembered through Akram's bowling, but the batting order had already solved an important problem before England began their chase. [ICC's England–Pakistan World Cup history](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/cricketworldcup/news/cwc-memories-england-v-pakistan)

## From 24 for two to a defendable innings

Pakistan lost two wickets with only 24 on the board. Imran and Miandad rebuilt, and Pakistan eventually reached 249 for six. The ICC's tournament overview records Imran as his team's leading scorer in the final and England's eventual total as 227. It also identifies this as Pakistan's first World Cup title. [ICC's 1992 overview](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/cricketworldcup/news/mens-cricket-world-cup-1992-overview)

In a 2010 interview, Akram described Pakistan's intention to retain wickets before increasing the tempo. He recalled the dressing room understanding the rebuilding approach. The third-wicket partnership was worth 139, and his later partnership with Inzamam added 52. This is a participant's retrospective account of the plan, rather than a reconstructed dressing-room conversation. [ICC interview with Akram](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/cwc-1992-gave-pakistan-the-belief-says-akram-x3061)

## Why the shorter innings mattered

Inzamam scored his 42 from 35 balls; Akram's 33 took only 18. Those contributions were smaller than the two senior batters' totals, but arrived with a different job to do. Comparing them solely by runs would miss the change of tempo. [ICC's England–Pakistan history](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/cricketworldcup/news/cwc-memories-england-v-pakistan)

## England's resistance and the decisive interruption

Neil Fairbrother made 62 and Allan Lamb 31. Their fifth-wicket stand took England to 141 for four before Akram dismissed Lamb and Chris Lewis without another run being added. Mushtaq Ahmed also took three wickets, finishing with three for 41; Akram returned three for 49. England were dismissed in 49.2 overs. [ICC's historical finals review](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/six-months-to-cwc-2011-final)

The two Akram dismissals came on successive deliveries. He had also removed Ian Botham earlier, and the ICC identifies him as Player of the Final as well as the tournament's leading wicket-taker. [ICC's World Cup wicket-takers review](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/icc-cricket-world-cup-leading-wicket-takers)

## What the result does and does not tell us

Pakistan's 249 meant England needed 250 to win. The 22-run victory margin is the difference between the teams' completed scores, not the distance England had been from victory at every earlier point. Likewise, 49.2 denotes 49 overs and two legal balls, rather than a decimal fraction of an over.
