Deepti Sharma finished the 2025 Women’s ODI World Cup with 215 runs and a tournament-leading 22 wickets, earning the Player of the Tournament award. Her record covered nine appearances, but only seven batting innings. She bowled in every match, including the rain-affected no-result against Bangladesh. [ICC tournament recognition](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/womens-cricket-worldcup-2025/news/icc-women-s-cricket-world-cup-team-of-the-tournament-revealed).

The distinction matters: her batting and bowling totals came from different numbers of opportunities. This is the completed 50-over tournament ending on 2 November 2025, not her current career record or a WPL season.

## The bowling contribution extended beyond the final

Deepti took at least one wicket in all nine appearances. Her five wickets in the final supplied 22.73% of the tournament total, leaving 17 from the preceding eight games. The championship performance was the peak, but not the whole bowling campaign.

Adding the spells gives 488 legal balls, 449 runs conceded and 22 wickets. That is 81 overs and two balls, not 81.2 decimal overs. These totals are the sum of the nine linked scorecards.

Recalculated from the integer totals, her bowling average was 20.41 runs per wicket, economy 5.52 runs per six balls and bowling strike rate 22.18 balls per wicket, rounded to two decimals. Each rate answers a different question; none is the probability of a wicket on her next delivery.

## A no-result still supplied a real spell

Against Bangladesh on 26 October, Deepti delivered five overs, including two maidens, and took 1/24. India’s chase stopped at 57/0 after 8.4 overs, leaving her without a batting innings. The match ended without a result because of rain. [ICC match account](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/womens-cricket-worldcup-2025/news/live-two-semi-finalists-in-action-as-double-header-hits-cwc25).

Dropping that row would incorrectly reduce her record to 21 wickets from 458 balls. The abandoned outcome did not erase the play that had occurred. Conversely, inserting a batting zero would create an innings she never played.

## Three fifties supplied most of the runs

Her 53 against Sri Lanka, 50 against England and 58 in the final totalled 161 runs, or 74.88% of her 215. The other four innings contributed 54. This concentration describes where the runs came from without pretending that every appearance delivered a similar return.

The seven innings used 238 balls. With seven dismissals, the batting average was 30.71, while the combined strike rate was 90.34. Neither should be calculated by dividing runs by nine appearances. Her highest score in this tournament was 58: the complete innings list contains three fifties and no century.

Opportunity also varied sharply. She faced six balls against Australia in the league stage, compared with 57 against England and 58 in the final. A small score and an unused batting opportunity should remain different entries when assessing her tournament.

## The two knockout spells were very different

Deepti conceded 73 runs for two wickets from 9.5 overs in the semifinal, then took 5/39 from 9.3 overs in the final. Together those matches produced seven wickets for 112 runs from 116 balls. A combined average of 16.00 is accurate, but it hides the contrast between the two spells.

She also contributed 24 from 17 balls in the semifinal chase before being run out. The ICC’s match report records her supporting contribution alongside the larger innings that carried India to the final. [ICC semifinal report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/media-releases/awesome-rodrigues-leads-india-to-icc-women-s-cricket-world-cup-2025-final).
