Australia defended the Champions Trophy by beating New Zealand by six wickets at Centurion on 5 October 2009. Shane Watson’s unbeaten hundred was the headline, but Cameron White helped repair a chase that began at six for two.

## New Zealand’s total left room to recover

New Zealand made 200 for nine. Martin Guptill’s 40 was the highest individual score; Neil Broom and James Franklin added 65 after the fifth wicket fell at 94. Nathan Hauritz took three for 37. Australia then lost Tim Paine and Ricky Ponting for one each. [NZC archive scorecard](https://archive.nzc.nz/Scorecards/251/251358.html)

## What 18 for two after ten overs meant

Australia’s first ten overs produced only 18 runs. The remaining equation was 183 from 240 balls, approximately 4.58 runs per over. The quiet start had increased the rate required, but the modest target still allowed the partnership to rebuild rather than demand immediate boundary hitting.

White and Watson added 128 before White was dismissed for 62. Michael Hussey followed later, and James Hopes finished unbeaten on 22 alongside Watson. Their last stand was 50.

## The hundred and the winning stroke arrived together

Watson ended on 105 not out from 129 balls, with Australia reaching 206 for four in 45.2 overs. In his later ICC recollection, Watson described hitting the six that both completed his century and won the tournament. [Watson’s retrospective](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/the-moments-you-dream-of-shane-watson-on-champions-trophy-knockouts)

That explains why the final total was 206 although the target was 201: the winning boundary counted in full. It also connects the ending to the earlier repair. Watson did not begin with a free-scoring procession; he stayed through the slow opening, the long White partnership and the final stand with Hopes.

The match offers a specific lesson in reading a chase: compare the runs needed with the balls still available at each phase, not simply the eventual winning margin.
