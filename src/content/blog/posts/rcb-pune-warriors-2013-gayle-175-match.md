Royal Challengers Bangalore beat Pune Warriors India by **130 runs** at M Chinnaswamy Stadium on **23 April 2013**. Chris Gayle made 175 not out from 66 balls, and RCB finished on 263/5. Pune replied with 133/9 from its full twenty overs. Gayle was Player of the Match. [Official IPL report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/report-match-31-rcb-vs-pwi)

The famous individual score is only one part of the match. RCB’s supporting contributions, Pune’s sharply uneven bowling returns and a chase damaged by early wickets explain how the two team totals developed.

## How RCB reached 263

The [full batting card](https://www.cricketsky.com/rcb-vs-pwi-31st-match-ipl-2013-scorecard-359/) gives the following contributions. Gayle remained unbeaten; the other five listed batters were dismissed.

| Batter or category | Runs |
| --- | ---: |
| Chris Gayle | 175 not out |
| Tillakaratne Dilshan | 33 |
| Virat Kohli | 11 |
| AB de Villiers | 31 |
| Saurabh Tiwary | 2 |
| Ravi Rampaul | 0 |
| Extras | 11 |
| Team total | 263/5 |

The six individual scores total 252, and 11 extras complete the 263. Gayle supplied 66.5% of the team’s runs. The remaining 88 were 77 from the other batters plus those extras, not an unexplained addition to his innings.

Gayle and Dilshan added 167 for the first wicket, confirmed by the [IPL’s same-day statistics report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/quick-stats-match-31-rcb-vs-pwi). After that partnership ended, RCB added a further 96. De Villiers’ 31 from eight balls was an especially short contribution in a total dominated by the opener.

The figures do not justify treating the opening stand as 175 plus 33. Gayle continued after Dilshan’s dismissal. His final score belongs to the whole innings rather than only their time together.

## The bowling figures were not uniformly expensive

Pune used seven bowlers. Bhuvneshwar Kumar conceded 23 from four overs and Luke Wright 26 from four. Their combined eight overs cost 49. The other five bowlers delivered twelve overs and conceded 211. These inputs agree in the [Cricbuzz card](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/11895/rcb-vs-pwi-31st-match-indian-premier-league-2013) and [CricketAddictor card](https://cricketaddictor.com/livescore/royal-challengers-bangalore-vs-pune-warriors-31st-match-23-apr-2013/scorecard/).

| Bowling group | Overs | Runs charged | Calculated economy |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Bhuvneshwar Kumar and Luke Wright | 8 | 49 | 6.13 |
| Other five Pune bowlers | 12 | 211 | 17.58 |
| All Pune bowlers | 20 | 260 | 13.00 |

The bowling sum is 260 rather than 263 because RCB’s three leg-byes were team runs not charged to a bowler. This makes the comparison internally consistent rather than forcing the bowling column to equal the team score.

The contrast also puts a limit on the phrase “every bowler was hit everywhere.” Two full spells were far less expensive than the others. It does not prove that moving those overs to another phase would have produced the same figures: these are observed returns, not a replay of alternative captaincy.

## Pune’s recovery began too far behind

Pune lost four wickets by 42. Steven Smith and Mitchell Marsh then took the score to 100 before Smith fell; their partnership added 58. Smith finished with 41 and Marsh with 24. The [official report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/report-match-31-rcb-vs-pwi) describes their resistance and Gayle’s two wickets in the final over.

The target was 264, so at 42/4 Pune still needed 222. At 100/5 it still needed 164. The partnership added runs, but it did not reset the target or replace the wickets already lost.

The [fall-of-wicket ledger](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/11895/rcb-vs-pwi-31st-match-indian-premier-league-2013) places Smith’s dismissal at 13.3 overs. That means 81 valid deliveries had been completed and 39 remained in the twenty-over innings. The remaining requirement was therefore 164 from 39, not 164 from a fresh set of twenty overs.

Pune eventually finished 133/9 rather than all out. Its eleven batting scores sum to 129; four extras bring the total to 133 in the corroborating cards. Gayle finished with 2/5, while Rampaul and Unadkat also took two wickets each.

## The margin and the target answer different questions

RCB’s winning margin is 263 − 133 = 130. Pune was 131 short of the winning target of 264. Both numbers are correct, but only the first is the official runs margin.

The match therefore needs three separate headline fields: Gayle’s 175 not out, RCB’s 263/5 and the 130-run victory. Substituting one for another loses either the individual achievement or the actual result.

This archive concerns what happened in Bengaluru that afternoon, not a claim that every record described in 2013 remains unchanged today.
