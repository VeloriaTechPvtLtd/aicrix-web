Australia won their sixth Women’s ODI World Cup title at Brabourne Stadium in Mumbai on 17 February 2013. They made 259/7 before dismissing West Indies for 145 in 43.1 overs, a 114-run victory. Jess Cameron scored 75 and was named player of the match.

## A strong platform met a four-wicket setback

Rachael Haynes made 52. Cameron’s 75 came from 76 balls, including eight fours and two sixes. Those boundaries supplied 44 runs, leaving 31 from other scoring outcomes.

Australia were 181/3 before Cameron’s dismissal and then slipped to 209/7. West Indies had taken four wickets for 28 runs, creating a chance to restrict the finish. Shaquana Quintyne completed ten overs with 3/27.

## Fields and Perry added 50 without separation

Jodie Fields finished unbeaten on 36 and Ellyse Perry on 25. Their eighth-wicket partnership lifted the score from 209/7 to 259/7.

The pair’s final individual scores add to 61, not 50. That is not a scorecard contradiction: Fields had already scored before Perry joined her. A partnership measures runs added while two batters are together, including extras, rather than the sum of their entire innings.

## Perry removed the top three

Perry then dismissed both openers and Stafanie Taylor, leaving West Indies at 41/3. She finished with 3/19; Lisa Sthalekar took 2/20. Kyshona Knight retired hurt and later returned during the chase.

The target was 260. West Indies’ 145 was 115 short of that winning target, while the official 114-run margin comes from comparing their score with Australia’s 259. The arithmetic helps keep the result separate from the target equation.

Australia’s win was decisive, but the first innings was not uninterrupted dominance. West Indies’ middle-innings wickets were followed by an unbroken Australian recovery, and Perry’s early wickets then disrupted the reply.
