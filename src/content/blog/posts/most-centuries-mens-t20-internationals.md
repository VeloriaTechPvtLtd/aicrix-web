**Rohit Sharma and Glenn Maxwell share the record for the most centuries in men’s T20 internationals, with five each**, in the career table checked on **14 November 2026**. The next group has four hundreds apiece: Suryakumar Yadav, Karanbir Singh, Phil Salt and Abhishek Sharma. [ACS men’s T20I century table](https://stats.acscricket.com/Records/International_Twenty20/Overall/Players/Most_Centuries_in_Career.html).

The word **international** matters. A T20I record includes recognized international matches, while an all-T20 record also includes recognized domestic leagues. Chris Gayle’s 22 T20 hundreds belong to that wider category, not to a list of 22 international hundreds.

The threshold keeps all players in the four-century tie together. It is not a list of six uniquely ranked positions. An alphabetical order or a website’s default sorting cannot make one player’s four hundreds more than another’s.

Karanbir Singh’s inclusion is important: a worldwide international leaderboard includes eligible performances for Associate nations as well as the more frequently televised teams. His [ICC player profile](https://www.icc-cricket.com/rankings/106463/karanbir-singh) identifies Austria as his national side.

## How Rohit reached five

Rohit became the first batter to reach five men’s T20I centuries against Afghanistan in Bengaluru on **17 January 2024**. India had fallen to 22/4 before his recovery with Rinku Singh. Rohit finished unbeaten on **121**, and India reached 212/4.

Afghanistan matched that total, and India eventually won after two Super Overs. The century count comes from Rohit’s main innings; the tie-break batting is a separate part of the match. [ICC report of Rohit’s fifth century](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/record-ton-for-rohit-sharma-in-strong-comeback-knock).

The date also explains older leaderboards. Immediately after this match, Rohit stood alone on five while Maxwell remained on four. That was a valid historical snapshot, but it changed the following month.

## How Maxwell drew level

Maxwell made **120 not out from 55 balls** against West Indies at Adelaide Oval on **11 February 2024**. It was his fifth T20I hundred and brought him level with Rohit.

He reached his fifty after 25 deliveries and his hundred after another 25. Those milestones describe the pace of this particular innings; the final score of 120 came from 55 balls. Australia made 241/4 and won by 34 runs. [ICC report of Maxwell’s record-equalling hundred](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/glenn-maxwell-equals-rohit-sharma-s-record-with-stunning-hundred?sf186249147=1).

Maxwell subsequently ended his international career. The ICC’s **3 November 2026** retrospective confirms his retirement, so a current comparison should not describe him as an active international challenger. [ICC retirement retrospective](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/maxwell-s-best-five-standout-international-performances).

## The four-century group has changed

The leading pair’s record can stay unchanged while the chasing group develops. Abhishek Sharma made his **fourth T20I hundred against New Zealand in Wellington on 27 October 2026**.

His 113 helped India chase a target of 199. The ICC explicitly calls it his fourth hundred in the format, providing a dated check against older lists that show a smaller career total. [ICC Wellington report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/india-race-to-a-historic-chase-to-seal-t20i-series-in-wellington).

For active players, the “last checked” date is part of the answer. A record table should be refreshed after relevant international matches, with the headline and explanatory text updated from the same figures.

## T20 centuries and T20I centuries are different counts

The ACS all-T20 table lists **Gayle with 22 hundreds**. Its international table lists him with **two**. Both figures can be correct because they answer different questions. [ACS all-T20 records](https://stats.acscricket.com/Records/Twenty20/Overall/Players/Most_Centuries_in_Career.html); [ACS T20I records](https://stats.acscricket.com/Records/International_Twenty20/Overall/Players/Most_Centuries_in_Career.html).

Use these scopes when reading a century list:

- **T20I career:** eligible international innings across a player’s career.

- **All-T20 career:** recognized international and domestic T20 innings combined.

- **IPL career:** performances in that particular domestic league.

- **T20 World Cup:** performances within the specified competition and tournament scope.

The categories overlap. An IPL hundred belongs within the player’s all-T20 total, so adding an IPL total to an all-T20 total counts those innings twice. The same applies when adding T20I hundreds to an already inclusive all-T20 figure.

## What a century leaderboard does and does not measure

A career-century table counts how many innings reached 100. It does not measure how frequently that happened, how quickly the runs arrived or how much batting opportunity the player usually received.

Consider a hypothetical comparison. Five hundreds in 100 innings is **5%** of innings; four in 40 is **10%**. The first batter leads by total hundreds, while the second has the higher historical century rate in those samples. Neither percentage is a reliable forecast for the next innings without considering the opposition, batting position and conditions.

For fantasy research, recent role, expected opportunity and confirmed availability are more immediately relevant than a career-best milestone alone. Use the century record to understand a player’s history, then assess the coming match separately.

The record answer should therefore always carry its scope: **men’s T20 internationals**, with tied leaders preserved and domestic T20 achievements kept in their correct category.
