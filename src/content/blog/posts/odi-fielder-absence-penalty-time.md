A player returning to the field is not necessarily ready to bowl immediately. In men’s ODIs, the useful distinction is between permission to return and having served any outstanding penalty time. Neither a substitute’s presence nor the start of a new innings automatically settles that second question.

## The threshold and exceptions

The October 2026 ICC conditions trigger restrictions for an absence longer than eight minutes, with outstanding penalty time capped at 120 minutes. Eight minutes is a threshold, not a deduction. Exemptions cover absences of eight minutes or less, a justified departure following an external blow during the match, and other reasons the umpires accept; illness and internal injury are excluded from that last exemption. [Current conditions, clauses 24.2–24.3](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/bxkzkcyvfn6tvi70zh0w.pdf).

## Worked timeline

Here is a hypothetical, non-exempt absence with no earlier balance. All times are illustrative, and play is continuous except for the intervals explicitly shown.

| Time | Event | Calculation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 14:00 | Player leaves | Start of absence |
| 14:20 | Returns with permission | 20 minutes outstanding, not 12 |
| 14:30 | Fielding innings ends | 10 minutes served; 10 remain |
| 14:30–15:00 | Innings interval | Balance remains 10 |
| 15:00 | His team starts batting | Countdown resumes |
| 15:10 | Ten playing minutes completed | Balance reaches zero |

Assume the side has not lost five wickets before 15:10. In this example, the player cannot open the chase at 15:00. The calculation is 20 minus 10 minus 10, not 20 minus the eight-minute threshold. Had the fielding innings continued uninterrupted instead, the same initial absence would have been worked off by 14:40.

## Which clock counts

Fielding after return or subsequent team batting serves the balance. Scheduled intervals and official drinks do not. Unserved time carries across innings; additional non-exempt absence adds to the balance, subject to the cap. An unscheduled stoppage can count under clause 24.2.7: return/readiness conditions matter, including personal notification to an umpire for someone already absent.

Batting is permitted once the required playing time has elapsed or the side has lost five wickets. That batting exception does not erase unserved time for bowling; time can continue to expire after the player is dismissed. [Clauses 24.2 and 25.3](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/bxkzkcyvfn6tvi70zh0w.pdf).

## Why an absence alone does not answer the question

For a real match, record the absence reason accepted by the umpires, the playing minutes missed, the existing balance and the timing of interruptions. Without those inputs, a broadcast clock alone cannot establish the earliest eligible delivery. Do not diagnose the cause from a player walking off or assume a planned bowling change proves misconduct.

Ordinary substitute eligibility and concussion replacement approval are separate issues. For this calculation, start with the applicable competition rulebook and the official absence record, not a rule remembered from another format.
