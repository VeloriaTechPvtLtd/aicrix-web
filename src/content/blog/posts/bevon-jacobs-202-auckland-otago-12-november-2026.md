Bevon Jacobs scored 202 not out for Auckland against Otago on 12 November 2026, but the double century did not secure victory. Auckland finished on 334/8; Otago reached 338/5 in 49.1 overs to win by five wickets. The completed scorecard places the Ford Trophy match at Eden Park Outer Oval, Auckland. This was domestic List A cricket, not an international ODI. The distinction is important when describing the innings: Jacobs's achievement belongs to a domestic fifty-over match, while the result belongs to Otago's successful pursuit. A headline focused only on the double century would leave out half the contest.

## How the 202 was constructed

Jacobs faced 137 balls and hit seven fours and 21 sixes. Contemporary reporting independently confirms that combination. [Honeyball match-day report](https://honeyball.com.au/2026/11/12/heat-import-blasts-incredible-double-century-in-nz-comp/)

Those boundaries supplied 154 runs: 28 from fours and 126 from sixes. That is approximately 76.24% of his individual total. The other 48 runs came without a boundary being credited to the batter. His strike rate was 147.45, calculated from 202 divided by 137 and multiplied by 100.

The volume of sixes explains the attention, but the team contribution adds another dimension. Jacobs supplied approximately 60.48% of Auckland's 334. That percentage uses the whole team score, including extras, so it should not be described as his share of runs scored exclusively by the batters.

## Auckland's late acceleration

Auckland were 39/3 when Cam Fletcher departed, later 249/7, and then 275/8 at the end of the 47th over. They nevertheless completed the innings on 334 without losing another wicket. [Scorecard checkpoints](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/170503/auc-vs-otg-13th-match-the-ford-trophy-2026-27)

The final three overs therefore produced 59 runs, equivalent to 19.67 per over. This is a team-phase calculation, not a claim that Jacobs personally scored every run in that period. It shows why the final total cannot be inferred from the eighth-wicket checkpoint alone: a batting side can still accelerate sharply with few wickets remaining.

That finish left Otago needing 335. The target was one more than Auckland's completed score, regardless of how spectacular the largest individual innings had been. Otago did not need a double century of their own; they needed enough contributions to reach that team requirement.

## Otago's finish settled the match

Max Chu finished unbeaten on 75, with Jamal Todd 21 not out. Otago had been 280/5 at 45.1 overs before closing on 338/5 at 49.1. A second scorecard also lists the checkpoints and completed result. At that fifth-wicket checkpoint, Otago needed 55 from 29 legal balls: a required rate of approximately 11.38. The unbeaten closing stand added 58 in 24 legal balls, or 14.50 per over, leaving five deliveries unused. The final score exceeded the target because the winning boundary carried it beyond 335.

The result should therefore be recorded as a five-wicket win, not a four-run win based on subtracting the two innings totals. Jacobs provided the standout individual score; Otago provided the successful chase. Both facts belong in a faithful account of this match, without turning either into a prediction about the next fixture.
