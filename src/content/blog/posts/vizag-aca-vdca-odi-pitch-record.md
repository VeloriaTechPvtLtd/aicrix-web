**The five latest men's ODIs in the checked ACA-VDCA venue record produced a first-innings average of 262 runs.** They include three successful chases, one successful defence and one tie. The matches span **17 December 2017 to 6 December 2025**, so “last five” does not mean five recent months.

The ground is the Dr. Y.S. Rajasekhara Reddy ACA-VDCA Cricket Stadium in **Visakhapatnam**, commonly called Vizag. Its men's ODI record should not be merged with IPL matches, T20 internationals or women's ODIs simply because they use the same ground.

The totals include innings that ended all out before 50 overs. They are actual first-innings scores, not projections of what each side might have made if it had survived its full quota.

## How 262 is calculated

The first-innings totals add to **1,310**:

270 + 117 + 387 + 321 + 215 = 1,310.

Dividing by five gives **262**. Sorted into order, the totals are 117, 215, 270, 321 and 387, so the **median is 270**. The difference between the highest and lowest is also 270 runs.

That wide spread is more informative than labeling the ground simply “batting friendly” or “bowling friendly.” India were dismissed for 117 in one fixture but made 387/5 in another. A single venue mean compresses those very different outcomes.

The rows above make the calculation reproducible rather than leaving the sample hidden behind a venue-average label.

As a sensitivity check, removing the 117 would raise the remaining mean to **298.25**; removing the 387 would lower it to **230.75**. Neither score should be discarded merely because it changes the headline. Both checks show how much one match influences a five-match summary.

## The tied match changes the denominator

A successful chase occurred in three of the five matches. That is **60% of all five listed games**.

If the question is instead “what share of decisive results went to the chasing side?”, remove the tie from the denominator. Three chase wins divided by four decisive results gives **75%**. One successful defence divided by four gives 25%.

Both 60% and 75% can therefore be correct, provided the question is stated. The 2018 tie remains part of the scoring average: both innings were completed, and the first-innings total is a valid observation. It is not a chasing win or a defending win.

The [ICC's contemporary account](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/we-did-a-good-job-as-a-bowling-unit-kuldeep-yadav) records Shai Hope's final-ball boundary bringing West Indies level after India made 321/6. Do not turn that result into a defeat because the chase fell short of the winning target.

## Chase totals are not full scoring capacity

The second innings in this sample average **242.4**, from 271, 121, 280, 321 and 219. That is a description of the observed finishing totals, not evidence that batting second intrinsically costs 19.6 runs.

Australia's 121/0 in 11 overs in 2023 is the clearest illustration. The team had already won; it did not need to keep batting until over 50. Comparing 121 with a completed 50-over first innings without its target and result would misread the match.

The same issue applies to India's successful replies of 219/2 and 271/1. For a chase analysis, keep the target, wickets and overs alongside the total rather than treating every finishing score as an estimate of a side's maximum output.

## Why a new match needs its own conditions

The 2018 report contains a specific account from Kuldeep Yadav of difficulty gripping a wet ball in dew. That supports a statement about that evening, not a guarantee that dew will shape every future Vizag ODI. [Dated ICC account](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/we-did-a-good-job-as-a-bowling-unit-kuldeep-yadav).

Likewise, the sample's three chase wins do not prove that winning the toss causes victory, or that the chasing side in the next fixture has a 75% chance. Opponents, lineups, weather and the prepared strip differ across these years.
