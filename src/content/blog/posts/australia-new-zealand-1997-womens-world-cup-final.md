Australia beat New Zealand by five wickets at Eden Gardens on 29 December 1997. New Zealand made 164; Australia reached 165/5 in 47.4 overs. Debbie Hockley scored 79 and was player of the match. [New Zealand Cricket archive scorecard](https://archive.blackcaps.co.nz/Scorecards/65/65086.html)

## Hockley carried the larger individual share

Hockley’s contribution was about 48.2% of New Zealand’s total: 79 ÷ 164 × 100. Australia’s leading scorer, Belinda Clark, made 52, about 31.5% of 165. The losing side therefore contained the larger individual contribution, both in runs and as a share of its team total.

That is why the player-of-the-match award and the match result are not contradictory. An outstanding individual innings and a successful collective chase measure different things.

Hockley also finished as the tournament’s leading run-scorer with 456. That tournament aggregate should be kept separate from her 79 in the final. [ICC tournament history](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/womens-world-cup-a-trip-down-memory-lane)

## Australia had several contributions

Michelle Goszko made 37, Karen Rolton 24 and Mel Jones 17. Alongside Clark’s 52, those innings provided 130 runs: approximately 78.8% of the final total. Australia progressed from 107/2 to 117/3, then 153/4 and 160/5 before finishing. Katrina Withers took 2/23 for New Zealand. [Full innings and fall of wickets](https://archive.blackcaps.co.nz/Scorecards/65/65086.html)

The last two wickets fell near the target, rather than triggering a collapse far from it. At 160/5, Australia needed five more runs to reach 165, with five wickets available.

## What the margin leaves out

Five wickets describes the wickets remaining, not the time remaining. A finish at 47.4 overs in a 50-over match leaves 14 legal deliveries: 300 minus 286. Thus Australia retained substantial batting resources, but the chase still extended into the 48th over.

For this final, a useful summary pairs the winning margin with the time used and the distribution of runs. Hockley’s innings was the standout individual performance; Australia assembled the more successful team total.
