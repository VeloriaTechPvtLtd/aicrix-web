This is the completed season, not an interim league-stage snapshot. Rajasthan’s campaign ended against Gujarat Titans in Qualifier 2 on 29 May. ## Opening position did not guarantee a long innings

The batting order was consistent, but the amount of batting was not. Seven of the 16 innings lasted nine balls or fewer. At the other end, he faced 36 balls twice. The middle pair of innings lengths was 12 and 17 balls, giving a median of 14.5; the arithmetic mean was 17.5.

Those are observed opportunities, not a forecast of how long an opener will bat next time. A batting position creates access to the start of an innings. It does not remove dismissal risk, the effect of a completed chase or a reduction in the team’s allocation. A reader comparing seasons should therefore keep position, innings length and scoring rate separate.

The table also prevents the three fifties from standing in for the whole season. His 55 against Gujarat, 77* against Mumbai and 51 against Punjab were substantial contributions, but the shorter innings belong in the same completed record. Selecting only the highlights would hide much of the actual sample.

## Why the 77* needs its 11-over context

The [official IPL match report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/tata-ipl-2026-match-13-rr-vs-mi-match-report) confirms the rain-shortened contest on 7 April: Rajasthan made 150/3 and Mumbai replied with 123/9. Jaiswal’s 77 came from 32 balls, and he remained unbeaten when Rajasthan’s allocated innings ended.

That performance counts fully in the official season figures. For context, removing just that match leaves 350 runs from 248 balls across the other 15 innings, a strike rate of 141.13. This is a descriptive split, not a corrected ability rating. It neither discounts the 77 nor supports extrapolating it to a hypothetical 20-over score.

The other not-out tells a different story. Against Chennai on 30 March, Jaiswal finished on 38 from 36 balls as RR completed a target of 128 in 12.1 overs. His innings ended because the chase was won. Both scores reduce the season’s dismissal denominator, but one came in a shortened first innings and the other in a completed chase.

## Reading the final record without overclaiming

Rajasthan’s [dated season review](https://www.rajasthanroyals.com/latest-news/rr-indian-premier-league-ipl-2026-top-run-scorers) places Jaiswal third among the team’s run-scorers, behind Vaibhav Sooryavanshi and Dhruv Jurel. That is a team-season ranking, not an all-time or current-form label.

For a useful comparison with another opening season, start with the same endpoint: include playoffs, retain not-outs, and flag reduced-allocation games. Then compare scoring rate alongside actual balls faced. This ledger establishes what happened in 2026; it does not establish powerplay-only output, a preferred future role or a fantasy-points expectation.
