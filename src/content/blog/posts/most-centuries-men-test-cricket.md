## What a century count includes

A century is an individual innings of at least 100 runs. A double-century or triple-century still contributes one innings to the hundreds column. Two centuries in the same Test contribute two because they come in separate innings.

Root’s Lord’s Test against Sri Lanka in 2024 provides a concrete example. His first-innings 143 was followed by 103 in the second innings, taking his career total from 32 to 34 in one match. [ICC’s account of the two hundreds](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/world-test-championship/news/joe-root-goes-to-the-top-of-english-records?sf199704321=1) and [Lord’s honours-board entry](https://www.lords.org/lords/our-history/honours-boards/j-e-root/j-e-root-103-v-sri-lanka)

A batter’s not-out status affects batting average, but an unbeaten hundred and a hundred followed by dismissal each add one to this column. ODI, T20I and domestic first-class hundreds belong to different record categories.

## Why some online tables put Root first

Check the coverage before comparing the headline. A table built from available ball-by-ball files can be accurate about its own sample while omitting much of an older player’s career.

For example, the [CricInto table inspected on 14 November](https://cricinto.com/records/test/most-hundreds) describes its coverage as 2001–2026 and 899 matches with ball-by-ball data. It places Root first in that sample and lists Tendulkar with 18 hundreds. Those 18 hundreds are counted within that sample, not a replacement for Tendulkar’s complete-career 51.

The stated time window alone also does not prove that every match within those years is included. For an all-time answer, use a complete career record; for a ball-by-ball analysis, keep the dataset’s coverage visible. A search result can look contradictory simply because it answers a narrower question.

## Root’s position and the next milestones

Root reached 41 Test hundreds at Sydney on **5 January 2026**, drawing level with Ponting. He went on to make 160 in that innings. [ICC’s dated milestone report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/world-test-championship/news/joe-root-equals-ricky-ponting-with-41st-test-hundred)

At this dated snapshot, the arithmetic is:

- Four more hundreds would take Root to Kallis’s 45.

- Ten more would take him to Tendulkar’s 51.

- Eleven more would put him above Tendulkar.

These are numerical gaps, not forecasts of how many matches Root needs or whether he will reach them. A Test can offer two batting innings, one or none, and a future appearance does not guarantee a hundred.

Smith’s record also needs dated context. The ICC’s [21 October 2026 update](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/smith-joins-ponting-border-in-exclusive-aussie-test-club) still listed 37, sixth overall. A historical milestone story should be paired with a current career table rather than assumed to be the latest total indefinitely.

## Reading the list fairly

The centuries column measures how often a batter reached three figures across a career. It does not adjust for innings played, batting position, opposition or conditions. A high total can reflect both sustained performance and a long career.

Use the same scope for every comparison: men’s Tests, full careers and a visible check date. Then choose a separate measure if the question is frequency, conversion rate or current form. Those questions need their own denominators and evidence.
