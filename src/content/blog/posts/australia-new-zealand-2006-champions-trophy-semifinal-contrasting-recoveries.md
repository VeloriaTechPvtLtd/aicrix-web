Australia won the Champions Trophy semifinal at Mohali on 1 November 2006 by 34 runs. Australia recovered from 4/2 to 240/9; New Zealand rebuilt from 35/6 but were dismissed for 206. Glenn McGrath was player of the match. [New Zealand Cricket archive](https://archive.blackcaps.co.nz/Scorecards/97/97871.html)

## Australia spread the rebuilding

Ricky Ponting and Andrew Symonds each made 58. Ponting and Damien Martyn added 66 for the third wicket; Symonds and Michael Hussey contributed 65 for the fifth. Kyle Mills took 4/38. [Full scorecard](https://archive.blackcaps.co.nz/Scorecards/97/97871.html)

The two stands together supplied 131 runs, approximately 54.6% of the total. They were separate partnerships at different stages, not a single rescue carried all the way to the finish. Australia still had eight wickets available when the initial rebuilding began.

## New Zealand had fewer resources left

McGrath took 3/22 from ten overs. New Zealand’s sixth wicket fell at 35, leaving Jacob Oram and Daniel Vettori to add 103 for the seventh wicket. Oram made 43 and Vettori 79. [Bowling and partnerships](https://archive.blackcaps.co.nz/Scorecards/97/97871.html)

That stand accounted for exactly half of New Zealand’s eventual 206. Its scale is worth recognising, but it began with only four wickets available rather than Australia’s eight. The similar language of “recovery” conceals very different starting positions.

At 35/6, New Zealand needed 206 more to reach 241. At 138/7, after the 103-run partnership, they still needed 103. The stand had halved the remaining requirement while using one of the four surviving wickets.

## Why the recovery was not enough

New Zealand were all out in 46 overs. [Innings record](https://archive.blackcaps.co.nz/Scorecards/97/97871.html)

The chase ended with 24 legal balls unused. Time was still available; batting resources were not. The 34-run margin is 240−206, whereas the shortfall from the winning target was 35.

Australia’s recovery was distributed across partnerships and preserved enough runs for a defence. New Zealand’s substantial seventh-wicket stand prolonged the pursuit, but could not undo the severity of the early collapse. Both repairs mattered; only one produced a completed winning performance.
