---
title: "Why India and Sri Lanka shared the 2002 Champions Trophy"
excerpt: "Two separate rain-abandoned finals, rather than one resumed match or tied scores, produced the shared 2002 trophy."
status: draft
---

India and Sri Lanka shared the 2002 Champions Trophy because neither attempt to play the final produced a result. The second day was a fresh match, not a continuation of the first chase.

## September 29: the first attempt

At Colombo’s R. Premadasa Stadium, Sri Lanka scored 244 for five, with Sanath Jayasuriya making 74 and Kumar Sangakkara 54. Harbhajan Singh took three for 27. India reached 14 without loss after two overs before rain ended play. The scorecard records no result and a replay the next day. [September 29 scorecard](https://i.imgci.com/db/ARCHIVE/2002-03/OD_TOURNEYS/ICCCT/SCORECARDS/KNOCKOUTS/IND_SL_ICCCT_ODI-FINAL_29SEP2002.html)

## September 30: start again

The ICC’s announcement explicitly said the replay would be a new match. It also specified that at least 25 overs per side were needed to constitute a match, and that an uncompleted replay would mean sharing the trophy and prize money. Those were the conditions announced for this historical final, not a statement of today’s minimum-overs rules. [ICC replay announcement](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/final-of-icc-champions-trophy-sri-lanka-2002)

Sri Lanka therefore batted again. Jayasuriya was bowled by Zaheer Khan from the first delivery. From 71 for four, Mahela Jayawardene and Russel Arnold added 118; Jayawardene made 77 and Arnold finished unbeaten on 56 in a total of 222 for seven.

India were 38 for one after 8.4 overs when the second attempt also ended without a result. The scorecard records the trophy as shared. [September 30 scorecard](https://img.cricinfo.com/db/ARCHIVE/2002-03/OD_TOURNEYS/ICCCT/SCORECARDS/KNOCKOUTS/IND_SL_ICCCT_ODI-FINAL_30SEP2002.html)

## Three distinctions that prevent a misleading summary

- India’s first-day 14 did not carry into the second-day chase.
- Sri Lanka’s two totals belonged to separate matches; adding them together has no competitive meaning.
- A shared trophy did not mean the teams finished with tied scores.

The unusual outcome came from the replay arrangements and the weather preventing a completed final on both dates, not from either side successfully defending or chasing its opponent’s total.
