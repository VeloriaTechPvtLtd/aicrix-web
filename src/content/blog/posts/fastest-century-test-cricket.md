Brendon McCullum holds the men’s Test record for the fastest century by balls faced: 54 deliveries against Australia at Hagley Oval, Christchurch, on 20 February 2016. His final innings was 145 from 79 balls. Those are two different measurements, and the 79-ball scorecard entry does not change the 54-ball record. [ICC match report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/new-zealand-posts-370-after-mccullums-record-ton).

Four innings appear within the 60-ball threshold in the recorded all-time list checked on 14 November 2026. The next entry is Jack Gregory’s 67-ball hundred, so this is the complete leading group within that cutoff, not a selection of famous examples. Historical ball counts are not available for every Test innings; the scope is recorded hundreds by balls faced. [Association of Cricket Statisticians and Historians](https://stats.acscricket.com/Records/Test/Overall/Batting/Fastest_Hundreds_by_Balls.html).

Ranks use the number of innings ahead: two players share second place, leaving Gilchrist fourth, not third. Dates identify the day of the hundred rather than necessarily the opening day of the Test. The [ACS leaderboard](https://stats.acscricket.com/Records/Test/Overall/Batting/Fastest_Hundreds_by_Balls.html), [Guinness record details](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/385416-oldest-player-to-score-two-hundreds-in-a-test-match) and [contemporary Perth report](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2006-12-16/gilchrist-races-to-stunning-century/2155522) establish the milestones and dates.

## What changed between Richards and McCullum

Richards set the 56-ball mark in 1986. Misbah matched it in 2014 rather than beating it. McCullum then reduced the record by two deliveries in 2016, in his 101st and final Test. That sequence matters when reading archive headlines: an innings described as the fastest at the time may now be a joint second-place entry. [ICC retrospective](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/this-month-in-1986) and [Guinness current record](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/fastest-test-match-hundred-male).

The spread between first and fourth is only three balls. The gap from Gilchrist’s 57 to Gregory’s 67 is ten. In other words, the four leading recorded performances occupy a much tighter band than the jump to the next entry. This describes the leaderboard, not how easy those final deliveries were or which innings was the greatest.

## Milestone balls are not final innings balls

McCullum faced another 25 deliveries after reaching his hundred: 79 minus 54. Misbah’s distinction is smaller but just as important. He reached 100 from 56 balls and finished 101 not out from 57. A table that takes his final 57-ball innings length as the century milestone would wrongly move him alongside Gilchrist. [ICC](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/new-zealand-posts-370-after-mccullums-record-ton) and [Guinness](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/385416-oldest-player-to-score-two-hundreds-in-a-test-match).

Misbah was on 92 after 54 balls, then struck consecutive fours to reach 100 after 56. That is why a milestone needs its own record, rather than an estimate from the final strike rate. His eventual unbeaten score and the delivery that completed his century answer separate questions. [Cricket Australia’s report of 2 November 2014](https://www.cricket.com.au/news/3259005/misbah-blazes-fastest-ever-ton).

## How to compare the pace fairly

Using exactly 100 runs as a common benchmark gives 185.19 runs per 100 balls at 54 deliveries, 178.57 at 56 and 175.44 at 57. These are calculated milestone benchmarks, not necessarily the actual strike rate displayed at the moment of celebration: a boundary can carry a batter beyond 100.

Nor should balls be confused with elapsed minutes or team overs. The batter may spend deliveries at the non-striker’s end, and interruptions affect time without adding balls faced. The record here answers one narrow question: how many recorded deliveries did that batter need to reach a Test hundred?

Gilchrist’s 57-ball effort remains the Australian entry in this leading group, and Cricket Australia identifies it with Perth in 2006. It belongs in a Test comparison, not a merged list with ODI, T20I or franchise hundreds. Keeping those formats separate preserves the meaning of the record.
