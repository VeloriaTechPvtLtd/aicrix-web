Australia secured their first Women's World Cup title by beating England by eight wickets in Hyderabad in January 1978. England made 96/8 in fifty overs; Australia reached 100/2 in 31.3. Sharon Tredrea took 4/25, and Margaret Jennings finished unbeaten on 57. [Cricket Australia's historical exhibition](https://artsandculture.google.com/story/australia-s-unparalleled-world-cup-history-cricket-australia/5AXBSSMaUTXgIw?hl=en)

## A title decider, not a knockout final

Only four teams contested the round-robin competition: Australia, England, New Zealand and host India. Australia finished unbeaten, England won twice and lost once, New Zealand won once and India remained winless. [ICC history](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/womens-world-cup-a-trip-down-memory-lane)

Cricket Australia's account records Australia and England arriving at their meeting level on four points. The winner would therefore finish above the other. That made the match decisive without creating a separately scheduled final. [Cricket Australia](https://artsandculture.google.com/story/australia-s-unparalleled-world-cup-history-cricket-australia/5AXBSSMaUTXgIw?hl=en)

## Restriction first, then a completed chase

The bowling performance left Australia a target of 97. Jennings' unbeaten innings supplied more than half Australia's eventual runs, while the team lost only two wickets. The results archive independently corroborates the totals, overs and eight-wicket outcome. [Tournament archive](https://i.imgci.com/link_to_database/ARCHIVE/WORLD_CUPS/WWC78/WWC78_SUMMARY.html)

Australia's recorded 100 is the finishing score, not the original target. A winning scoring action can take a team beyond the minimum required, but the total alone does not identify the exact shot. No delivery reconstruction is needed to explain this result.

The time calculation is equally specific: 31.3 overs means 189 legal balls, not 31.3 multiplied by six. From a fifty-over allocation of 300 balls, that leaves 111 unused. Together, the target, wickets remaining and unused balls describe the chase more clearly than the wicket margin alone.

## Keep the two early championships distinct

Australia's first title in 1978 came through the league standings. Their successful defence in 1982 included a designated final after a qualifying league. Keeping those formats separate makes the historical record clearer: both campaigns produced champions, but they did not reach that outcome through the same tournament structure.
