VJD is the rain-adjustment method named after V Jayadevan. It sets revised targets in interrupted limited-overs cricket and appears on Indian domestic scorecards. When a result says VJD, keep that label: it is not another spelling of Duckworth–Lewis–Stern.

The [VJD software provider’s description](https://vjd.sportecindia.com/index.html) explains the method through a normal-score curve and a target-score curve. The normal curve includes wickets lost as well as innings progress; the target curve is not directly dependent on wickets in hand. So the shortcut “VJD ignores wickets” is misleading.

## A current official example

On 29 October 2026, Pondicherry Women beat Jharkhand Women by **11 runs under VJD** in the Senior Women’s T20 Trophy Elite at Pune. BCCI lists Pondicherry at 158/6 from 20 overs and Jharkhand at 126/6 from 18.1. [Official match record](https://www.bcci.tv/matches/5f0add07-ea37-40d2-ad50-45754cd07535/pondicherry-women-vs-jharkhand-women/match-details).

| Item | Officially recorded value |
| --- | --- |
| Competition | Senior Women’s T20 Trophy Elite |
| Date | 29 October 2026 |
| First innings | Pondicherry Women 158/6, 20 overs |
| Second innings | Jharkhand Women 126/6, 18.1 overs |
| Result | Pondicherry Women won by 11 runs, VJD |

The raw totals differ by 32 runs: 158 − 126 = 32. Yet the official winning margin is 11. This is exactly why subtracting the final scores is not a safe way to reconstruct a rain-adjusted result.

Likewise, 18.1 means 109 legal balls, not 18.1 decimal overs. Accurate ball notation matters, but converting it correctly still does not supply the official revised target or interruption history. Keep the scorecard’s outcome instead of trying to reverse-engineer missing inputs.

## What the two curves are intended to represent

The provider describes the normal curve as the scoring progression up to an interruption and the target curve as the scoring requirement over the overs remaining. It also describes software selecting between table sets according to the first side’s score or projected score. That is a model of the innings, not a rule that simply removes the same percentage of runs as overs. [Provider explanation](https://vjd.sportecindia.com/index.html).

Use that description to understand the idea, not to manufacture a target from an unofficial spreadsheet. A reader needs the number issued for the match; reproducing a model requires its correct version and complete inputs.

## Is VJD the same as the ICC method

No. In a report published on 1 June 2012, the ICC said its Cricket Committee had considered VJD as a replacement for D/L and decided to retain D/L. That is a dated decision about the methods reviewed then, not proof that one is universally superior today. [ICC committee report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/results-of-the-icc-cricket-committee-meeting-at-lords). Current international DLS documentation is a separate rules source.

The Pune result establishes VJD use in this specific senior domestic competition in 2026. It does not establish the rule for every match played in India. An international, a franchise league and a domestic state competition can require different checks.

## How to read a VJD update

Look for the competition and season, the official method label, score and wickets, overs completed, revised allocation and the announced target or result. Compare updates only when they describe the same interruption state. If play resumes, the earlier update is history rather than the current chase requirement.

Keep standings and fantasy settlement separate. The match result does not by itself tell you how that tournament credits net run rate or how a particular fantasy platform settles an interrupted contest.
