Afghanistan beat Bangladesh by eight runs under the DLS method at Arnos Vale Ground in Kingstown, St Vincent, to reach the semifinals of the 2024 men's T20 World Cup. Afghanistan made 115/5; Bangladesh, chasing a revised 114 from 19 overs, were dismissed for 105 in 17.5 overs. [Full scorecard](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/87857/afg-vs-ban-52nd-match-super-8-group-1-icc-mens-t20-world-cup-2024).

The match began on the evening of 24 June locally. Its 8:30 pm start corresponded to 00:30 UTC on 25 June, explaining why both dates appear in coverage. The [ICC fixture list](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/groups-fixtures-confirmed-for-icc-men-s-t20-world-cup-2024?sf171907356=1) assigns the St Vincent evening fixture to 24 June; this archive uses that local starting date.

## Three numbers explain the DLS result

Afghanistan's first-innings 115 was not the number used to calculate the eventual winning margin. After rain reduced Bangladesh's allocation, the revised target was 114. At the full revised allocation, the score to tie was therefore 113.

Bangladesh finished on 105, eight below 113. That is why the official margin was eight runs, not ten from subtracting the two innings totals, and not nine from subtracting 105 from the target. Nine was the number Bangladesh still needed to win when its innings ended.

The innings lasted 107 legal balls: 17 multiplied by six, plus five. The revised allowance was 114 balls, leaving seven deliveries unused when the last wicket fell. Bangladesh were all out; this was not a chase that simply ran out of time.

## A modest total became enough

Afghanistan chose to bat. Rahmanullah Gurbaz made 43 from 55 balls, and Rashid Khan's unbeaten 19 from ten provided a late contribution. The ICC report describes an innings in which preserving wickets did not translate into sustained rapid scoring. [Official match report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/live-semi-final-spot-on-the-line-as-afghanistan-meets-bangladesh-in-pivotal-super-eight-clash).

The scorecard shows Afghanistan at 93/5 after 17.4 overs. They added 22 across the remaining 14 legal deliveries, equivalent to 9.43 runs per over in that closing segment. That comparison identifies a late acceleration; it does not show how many runs would have been scored with a different batting order or approach.

## Wickets kept the chase within reach

Litton Das remained unbeaten on 54 from 49 balls, but Bangladesh lost partners around him. Rashid took 4/23 and Naveen-ul-Haq 4/26. Together they claimed eight of the ten wickets, accounting for 80% of the dismissals.

Naveen removed Taskin Ahmed and Mustafizur Rahman consecutively to finish the match. The fall-of-wickets ledger places both dismissals at a team score of 105, at 17.4 and 17.5 respectively. Litton's unbeaten fifty therefore coexisted with an all-out innings: the last batter dismissed was at the other end, not the set opener.

## Winning the game and qualifying were separate questions

Bangladesh began the chase needing a sufficiently fast victory to improve its net run rate enough to reach the semifinals. Once that qualification opportunity disappeared, it could still win the match and affect which other side advanced. Afghanistan needed the victory to settle the contest in its favor. [ICC qualification context](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/live-semi-final-spot-on-the-line-as-afghanistan-meets-bangladesh-in-pivotal-super-eight-clash).

The final result sent Afghanistan through alongside India from Super Eight Group 1 and eliminated Australia. It also ended Bangladesh's campaign. [Reuters' completed-match report](https://www.reuters.com/sports/cricket/afghanistan-beat-bangladesh-reach-semi-finals-australia-go-home-2024-06-25/).

This was not a semifinal itself. It was the Super Eight match that delivered Afghanistan's place in one, with the revised target, wickets remaining and qualification race all influencing how the closing overs were understood.
