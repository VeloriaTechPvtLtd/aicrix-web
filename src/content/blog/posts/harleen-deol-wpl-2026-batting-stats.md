## A position split is a description not a selection verdict

At No. 4, Harleen accumulated 156 runs from 130 balls, a strike rate of exactly 120.00. Her one opening innings had a strike rate of 78.57. Those numbers describe unequal historical samples: seven innings against one. They cannot establish that a particular slot caused better batting or that she should never open again.

Even the seven innings at No. 4 varied widely in length. She faced between two and 39 deliveries in them. The slot identifies when she came into the batting order, but not how many overs remained, how many balls she would receive or which bowlers she would face. A batting-position label is therefore useful context, not a substitute for the actual innings log.

## The chase against Mumbai

She struck 12 fours, while Chloe Tryon finished alongside her on 27 not out. The result was UPW’s first win of the season. ## Keep the retirement state intact

The preceding match against Delhi, on 14 January, ended Harleen’s innings at 47 from 36 balls with the scorecard designation retired out. It is not a 47 not out entry. With eight innings and one not-out, the season average uses seven dismissals: 167 divided by seven equals 23.857, rounded to 23.86.

That accounting point should not be turned into a verdict about the tactical decision. Nor does the following day’s half-century establish that retirement caused a change in performance. The two matches had different opponents and situations; the verified sequence is enough without adding a motivational story.

Harleen’s most frequent position was No. 4, but her contribution there ranged from an early dismissal to a completed chase.
