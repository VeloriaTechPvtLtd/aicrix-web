Muttiah Muralitharan holds the men’s Test career wicket record with 800. Nine bowlers have taken at least 500 Test wickets as checked on 14 November 2026.

Lyon’s current total is 577, including the completed Cape Town Test against South Africa that began on 27 October and ended on 31 October 2026. That is the playing cutoff for his entry, not 14 November: the later date records when this list was checked.

This is an overall Test-career list, not a World Test Championship-cycle table or an all-format international total. It includes all matches recognised as Tests, including representative-team appearances where applicable. National-team-only filters can therefore produce different totals.

The ICC’s published [career leaderboard](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/world-test-championship/news/starc-eyes-kapil-dev-s-mark-as-aussies-focus-on-bangladesh) identifies these nine members. The [full career table](https://crickrida.com/records/men/test/most-wickets/) and current player ledgers update Lyon’s older figure; the next-highest bowler, [Mitchell Starc](https://www.cricbuzz.com/profiles/7710/mitchell-starc), remains below the threshold at 460.

## Lyon is sixth and 27 wickets behind Broad

Lyon is 14 wickets clear of McGrath and 27 behind Broad. He needs 27 more to equal 604 and 28 to move ahead. Reaching 600 would require 23, but that milestone alone would not change his sixth-place ranking.

The difference between a milestone and a ranking target matters. Warne finished only four wickets ahead of Anderson, whereas Muralitharan’s advantage over Warne is 92. Adjacent rows need not represent equally small gaps.

Lyon took two wickets in each South African innings at Newlands, moving from 573 to 577. His [career ledger](https://www.cricbuzz.com/profiles/7850/nathan-lyon) records 145 Tests and 269 bowling innings. [Cricket Australia’s final-day report](https://www.cricket.com.au/news/4602015/) confirms the match concluded on 31 October.

## Tests and bowling innings measure different opportunities

A Test appearance is not automatically two bowling innings. An innings victory, weather or simply not being called on to bowl can reduce a player’s bowling opportunities. Using twice the match count would give Lyon 290 possible innings, not his actual 269.

Muralitharan took 3.48 wickets per bowling innings, Anderson 2.01 and Lyon 2.14. These are career wickets divided by recorded bowling innings. They describe wicket yield per opportunity, not bowling average, which divides runs conceded by wickets.

Ball counts add another perspective. Muralitharan delivered 44,039 balls for 800 wickets, or 55.05 balls per wicket. Anderson’s 40,037 for 704 gives 56.87; Lyon’s 35,771 for 577 gives 61.99. The lower number means wickets arrived more frequently per delivery over that recorded career. The raw counts are in the [Muralitharan](https://www.cricbuzz.com/profiles/110/muthiah-muralidaran), [Anderson](https://www.cricbuzz.com/profiles/65/james-anderson) and Lyon career bowling tables.

These are descriptive comparisons, not era-adjusted rankings. Workload, role, opposition and conditions are not equalised by dividing two totals. A bowler can have more wickets overall without leading every rate statistic.

## Which record belongs in which table

Only wickets credited to the bowler belong in a bowling career total. Team dismissals such as run-outs must not be added. Likewise, first-class wickets outside Tests and ODI or T20I wickets do not count toward this threshold.

The global list and an India-only list answer different questions. Kumble and Ashwin qualify here, but a country ranking includes other Indian bowlers below 500. This global threshold should not be treated as a complete Indian leaderboard.

For future updates, retain the exact completed-match cutoff and change innings, balls and wickets together. A scheduled Test adds no wickets, and an older article’s publication date does not make its career totals current.
