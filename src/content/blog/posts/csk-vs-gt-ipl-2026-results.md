Gujarat Titans won the two IPL 2026 meetings with Chennai Super Kings compared here: **by eight wickets in Chennai on 26 April and by 89 runs in Ahmedabad on 21 May**. One win came while chasing; the other came after setting a target. The season's two results therefore should not be compressed into a claim that batting first or second was the decisive advantage. [April match details](https://www.iplt20.com/matches/237330/chennai-super-kings-vs-gujarat-titans/match-details), [May match details](https://www.iplt20.com/matches/b862c40e-5873-406c-9158-8dc824e2e0e6/gujarat-titans-vs-chennai-super-kings/match-details).

## Chennai recovered but Gujarat still chased comfortably

On 26 April, CSK slipped to 37/4 before Ruturaj Gaikwad and Shivam Dube added 59. Gaikwad finished unbeaten on 74 from 60 balls, while Rabada's four overs cost 25 and produced three wickets. Sai Sudharsan then made 87 from 46, with Jos Buttler unbeaten on 39 from 30. Their second-wicket partnership was 97. [April scorecard](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/151913/csk-vs-gt-37th-match-ipl-2026).

The chase ended after 100 legal balls: 16 overs plus four deliveries. That left **20 balls unused**, despite the final total being 162 rather than the target of 159. A winning boundary can carry the final score beyond the target; the two figures are not competing versions of the same number.

## Ahmedabad brought a larger target and an early collapse

In the return meeting, Sudharsan and Shubman Gill added 125 for the first wicket. Sudharsan made 84 from 53, Gill 64 from 37, and Buttler's unbeaten 57 from 27 helped Gujarat reach 229/4. Siraj removed Sanju Samson with the first ball of the chase and also dismissed Gaikwad and Urvil Patel. CSK were eventually dismissed for 140, and the defeat ended their playoff hopes. [Official May match report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/tata-ipl-2026-match-66-gt-v-csk-match-report).

A target of 230 required **11.50 runs per over** across the full twenty overs. Chennai's innings ended after 82 legal balls, with 38 still unused. Those remaining balls were not an opportunity the team could carry forward: losing all ten wickets ended the chase. The margin is the difference between completed totals, **229−140=89**, not the difference between the target and Chennai's score. [Completed match record](https://www.iplt20.com/matches/b862c40e-5873-406c-9158-8dc824e2e0e6/gujarat-titans-vs-chennai-super-kings/match-details).

## Sudharsan scored nearly the same runs at different speeds

His 87 in Chennai and 84 in Ahmedabad differed by only three runs, but used 46 and 53 balls. Those innings correspond to strike rates of **189.13 and 158.49**. Across both, he made **171 from 99 balls**, an aggregate strike rate of **172.73**. That aggregate comes from total runs divided by total balls, not an unweighted average of the two displayed strike rates. [April scorecard](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/151913/csk-vs-gt-37th-match-ipl-2026), [May report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/tata-ipl-2026-match-66-gt-v-csk-match-report).

The difference illustrates why an innings summary needs both runs and balls. It does not prove that one ground was inherently harder or that a batter chose an optimal pace. These were different match situations, with different targets and teammates' contributions. Together, these two results give Gujarat a 2–0 advantage in this comparison; they are not a forecast for the next meeting.
