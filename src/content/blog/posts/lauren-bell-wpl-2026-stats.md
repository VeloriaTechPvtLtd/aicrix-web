Lauren Bell finished WPL 2026 with 12 wickets from nine matches for Royal Challengers Bengaluru. Her workload was unusually consistent: four overs in every appearance, including the final. Across 216 legal deliveries she conceded 199 runs, giving a bowling average of 16.58 and a recalculated economy rate of 5.53 runs per over. Her best return was 3/26 against Delhi Capitals. [Season and player record](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/sports/cricket/wpl/player-stats/lauren-bell/69906)

The wickets alone do not explain her contribution. Bell’s two three-wicket games supplied half her season tally, but her final was a wicketless four-over return that cost only 19 runs. The useful comparison is therefore between wicket-taking, runs conceded and the opportunity to bowl, rather than treating any one figure as a complete performance rating.

## The wicket rate changed more than the scoring rate

Splitting the ledger after her first four appearances exposes a difference that the season average hides. Those four matches brought eight wickets for 85 runs in 16 overs. The remaining five brought four wickets for 114 runs in 20 overs.

| Runs per over | 5.31 | 5.70 |
| Legal balls per wicket | 12.00 | 30.00 |

Her later wicket return was lower, but the scoring rate against her increased by only about 0.39 runs per over. Calling that stretch simply a loss of form would miss the distinction between taking wickets and limiting runs. The split describes these particular games; it does not establish a change in skill, explain the causes or predict the next season.

## Why the final matters despite zero wickets

Delhi made 203/4 in 20 overs in the final, a team scoring rate of 10.15. Bell’s own four overs cost 19, or 4.75 per over. Those are different denominators, but together they show why a zero in the wickets column should not automatically be read as an ineffective bowling performance. RCB subsequently won by six wickets. ## Read workload separately from phase and future selection

Bell opened the tournament with a maiden and removed Amelia Kerr during the powerplay. That is a concrete example of new-ball use, rather than a role inferred from her bowling style. [Official opening-match report](https://www.wplt20.com/news/article/match-report-tata-wpl-2026-match-1)

A full four-over allocation does not reveal how many overs came at the start or end of an innings. Match totals therefore cannot be relabelled as powerplay or death-over statistics. Nor does nine consecutive full allocations guarantee four overs in a future fixture: selection, innings length and captaincy decisions remain separate questions. For this completed season, the firm conclusion is sustained workload with economical returns, including occasions when the wickets went to someone else.
