South Africa defeated India by 76 runs in their T20 World Cup Super Eight meeting at Narendra Modi Stadium, Ahmedabad, on 22 February 2026. South Africa made 187/7 in 20 overs, then dismissed India for 111 in 18.5. The BCCI match centre confirms the date, venue and result. This was a Super Eight match, not the final between these countries in 2024. [Official match centre](https://www.bcci.tv/matches/c2362d8c-07f8-4fd5-92b3-ff8ccf3c5d67/india-vs-south-africa)

The central contrast was how the two batting sides responded to early wickets. South Africa found a substantial rebuilding partnership and continued scoring; India's pursuit lost both wickets and momentum. An early advantage with the ball did not survive into India's chase.

## South Africa rebuilt without abandoning attack

South Africa were 20/3 before David Miller and Dewald Brevis added 97. Miller made 63 from 35 balls, Brevis 45, and Tristan Stubbs finished unbeaten on 44 from 24. The ICC report identifies their recovery as the foundation of the eventual total. [ICC match report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/mens-t20-world-cup-2026/news/live-india-face-south-africa-in-marquee-clash)

The fourth-wicket stand was worth more than half the completed innings: 97 divided by 187 is approximately 51.87%. That figure describes the partnership's share of the team total, including any extras within the stand. It should not be confused with adding Miller's and Brevis's individual scores, which include runs outside their time together.

From 20/3 to 187/7, South Africa added 167 while losing four further wickets. The recovery therefore did more than repair the opening damage. It created enough scoring room for the innings to withstand later dismissals rather than depending on an uninterrupted finish.

## India could not turn early wickets into complete control

Jasprit Bumrah finished with 3/15 from four overs. India's other five bowlers together conceded 169 in 16 overs; the remaining three runs in South Africa's total were byes and leg-byes. Those figures come from the full bowling card, not from subtracting Bumrah's conceded runs from the team total without checking extras. [Scorecard](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/139381/ind-vs-rsa)

Bumrah's economy was 3.75 runs per over; the combined rate for the other bowlers was approximately 10.56. That comparison shows the uneven distribution of scoring opportunities. It does not mean every other bowler performed identically, or establish why each delivery succeeded or failed. An aggregate is useful for locating the gap, not for replacing individual analysis.

This also illustrates why a strong individual spell and an expensive team innings can coexist. The allocation of overs matters alongside the best bowling figures on display.

## The chase equation moved away from India

India were 31/3 after six overs. The ICC report records Marco Jansen's four wickets and Shivam Dube's 42, but no comparable Indian recovery to South Africa's fourth-wicket partnership. [ICC report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/mens-t20-world-cup-2026/news/live-india-face-south-africa-in-marquee-clash)

With a target of 188, the powerplay checkpoint left 157 needed from 84 balls: a required rate of approximately 11.21. At 51/5 after 9.1 overs, the equation had become 137 from 65 balls, or 12.65 an over. The later checkpoint is confirmed by the fall-of-wickets ledger. [Scorecard](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/139381/ind-vs-rsa)

Those equations explain why merely keeping wickets intact would not have been enough at that stage. India needed a sharp increase in scoring alongside a partnership. Instead, the innings ended with seven scheduled deliveries unused. The winning margin is 187 minus 111, or 76; the difference between the target and India's score is 77, which is not the official margin. South Africa's recovery and India's escalating requirement together explain the result more clearly than either total alone.
