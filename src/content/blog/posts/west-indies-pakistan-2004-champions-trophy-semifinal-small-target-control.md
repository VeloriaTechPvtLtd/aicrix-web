West Indies beat Pakistan by seven wickets at Southampton on 22 September 2004. Pakistan were dismissed for 131 in 38.2 overs; West Indies reached 132/3 in 28.1. Ramnaresh Sarwan finished unbeaten on 56 and was player of the match. [Contemporary scorecard](https://img.cricinfo.com/db/ARCHIVE/2004/OD_TOURNEYS/ICCCT/SCORECARDS/KNOCKOUTS/PAK_WI_ICCCT_ODI-SEMI2_22SEP2004.html)

## Eight bowling wickets, two run-outs

Corey Collymore, Dwayne Bravo and Wavell Hinds took two wickets each. Ian Bradshaw and Chris Gayle took one apiece; two run-outs completed the ten dismissals. [Dismissal record](https://img.cricinfo.com/db/ARCHIVE/2004/OD_TOURNEYS/ICCCT/SCORECARDS/KNOCKOUTS/PAK_WI_ICCCT_ODI-SEMI2_22SEP2004.html)

The bowling figures therefore account for eight wickets, not ten. Pakistan also left 70 legal deliveries unused: 300 minus 230. West Indies restricted the scoring and ended the innings well before the allocation expired.

## Early wickets still needed an answer

Shoaib Akhtar removed both openers, leaving West Indies 20/2. He finished with 2/18 from seven overs. Brian Lara later retired hurt on 31 at 76/2; Chanderpaul’s dismissal at 102 was the third wicket. [Chase record and retirement note](https://img.cricinfo.com/db/ARCHIVE/2004/OD_TOURNEYS/ICCCT/SCORECARDS/KNOCKOUTS/PAK_WI_ICCCT_ODI-SEMI2_22SEP2004.html)

Lara leaving the field did not increase the wickets-lost column. The partnership had added 56 from the second wicket until his retirement, but it was not a completed 56-run wicket partnership: the next dismissal happened later.

At 76, West Indies still needed 56 to win. That happened to equal Sarwan’s eventual individual score, but the two figures describe different things. Some of Sarwan’s runs had already been scored by then; the remainder of the chase was not his contribution alone.

## Room to rebuild

West Indies finished with 131 legal balls unused, calculated as 300 minus 169. The target gave them time to respond to Akhtar’s early wickets without demanding continuous high-risk scoring. Sarwan’s unbeaten innings linked those adjustments to the finish. A small target made the task favourable, but the batting still had to manage dismissals and a retirement accurately.
