India beat England by seven runs in the men's T20 World Cup semifinal at Wankhede Stadium, Mumbai, on 5 March 2026. India made 253/7 and England finished on 246/7, both from 20 overs. England had chosen to field; Sanju Samson was named Player of the Match after making 89 from 42 balls. [BCCI match details](https://www.bcci.tv/matches/c5677298-f888-4c23-8ed8-4a3566086e5c/england-vs-india/match-details)

For readers returning to this match, the final totals conceal an important distinction. England matched India's scoring closely enough to threaten the target, but had to do so while carrying a larger early wicket deficit. The eventual seven-run gap came in a contest where neither innings could be understood through runs alone.

## Samson's platform and the supporting acceleration

Samson and Ishan Kishan added 97, followed by a 43-run partnership between Samson and Shivam Dube. India ended the powerplay at 67/1 and continued attacking beyond the fielding restrictions. The ICC report also records contributions from Kishan, Dube, Hardik Pandya and Tilak Varma rather than presenting the total as a single-batter effort. [ICC match report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/mens-t20-world-cup-2026/news/live-india-face-familiar-foes-england-in-semi-finals)

Samson's 89 represented approximately 35.18% of India's 253. The remaining 164 team runs demonstrate why the innings cannot be reduced to his contribution alone. That remainder includes other batters' runs and extras; it is not an individual supporting player's score or a single partnership.

Pandya scored 27 from 12 balls and Varma 21 from seven. Combined, their separate innings supplied 48 from 19 balls, an aggregate strike rate of 252.63. This is a comparison of their batting output, not a claim that they shared a 48-run stand. [Full scorecard](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/139478/eng-vs-ind)

## England kept the rate alive despite losing wickets

England finished the powerplay at 68/3, one run ahead of India's corresponding score but two wickets worse off. Jacob Bethell's 105 from 48 balls kept the chase competitive. His stand with Will Jacks produced 77 before a relay catch involving Axar Patel and Dube ended Jacks's innings. [ICC report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/mens-t20-world-cup-2026/news/live-india-face-familiar-foes-england-in-semi-finals)

The powerplay equation was 186 needed from 84 balls, or approximately 13.29 runs per over. England had scored quickly, yet the requirement remained demanding because the target was 254. Being ahead of the opposition's score at the same over does not automatically mean a chasing side is ahead of the game: remaining wickets and the eventual target also matter.

At Jacks's dismissal, England were 172/5 after 14 overs. That left 82 from 36 balls, or 13.67 an over. Bethell was later run out at 225/7 after 19.1 overs. [Scorecard](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/139478/eng-vs-ind)

## Why economical overs mattered in a high-scoring match

Jasprit Bumrah conceded 33 in four overs; Pandya conceded 38 in four. Together, those eight overs cost 71, an economy of 8.875. The scorecard credits them with three wickets between them. [Scorecard](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/139478/eng-vs-ind)

An economy comparison is especially useful here because the required starting rate was 12.70. Overs below that rate pushed more scoring work into other parts of England's chase. It does not prove that every economical delivery caused a later wicket, but it shows why containment retained value even when the match total was large.

England's 246 was seven below India's score and eight short of the winning target. The official result is therefore a seven-run defeat, not eight. India's reward was qualification for the final; this article preserves the semifinal's own sequence rather than treating the later title match as part of the same result.
