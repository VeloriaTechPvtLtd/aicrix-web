*Career IPL statistics through the completed 2026 season; checked on 14 November 2026.*

Virat Kohli leads the IPL career run-scoring list with 9,336 runs through the completed 2026 season. Rohit Sharma is second on 7,329. This is a cumulative IPL leaderboard, including playoff appearances, not the Orange Cap table for one edition or an all-T20 ranking. Reuters confirmed Kohli’s career total after the 2026 final. [Final-season report](https://www.reuters.com/sports/cricket/kohli-fulfils-dream-with-title-winning-stroke-bengaluru-retain-ipl-title-2026-05-31/).

## Where the ranking is close

The difference between ninth and tenth is only 19 runs: Samson’s 5,181 against de Villiers’ 5,162. Seventh and eighth are separated by 72, while Raina leads Dhoni by 89. Those are much tighter historical margins than Kohli’s 2,007-run advantage over Rohit.

Rank movement therefore requires more than reading a position number. A small gap can change with a modest additional contribution, but only if a player actually appears and scores. These margins are arithmetic comparisons at the cutoff, not forecasts about selection, retirement or the next season.

Kohli is 664 runs short of 10,000. Reaching that landmark would require another 664 credited IPL runs, regardless of how many matches or seasons it takes. It is not a prediction that he will reach it in 2027.

## Similar innings counts, different scoring opportunities

Kohli’s record contains 275 batting innings; Rohit’s contains 276. Yet Kohli has faced 6,926 balls against Rohit’s 5,514, a difference of 1,412 deliveries. Similar innings counts do not mean similar time at the crease. Their career strike rates are 134.80 and 132.92 respectively. [Kohli ledger](https://www.cricbuzz.com/profiles/1413/virat-kohli) and [Rohit ledger](https://www.cricbuzz.com/profiles/576/rohit-sharma).

Runs per batting innings are approximately 33.95 for Kohli and 26.55 for Rohit. Those are not batting averages: a batting average divides runs by dismissals and excludes not outs from its denominator. The distinction matters when comparing players whose innings end unbeaten at different frequencies.

Rahul offers another perspective. His 5,815 runs came in 150 innings, or approximately 38.77 runs per innings, despite ranking fifth by aggregate. His total is lower than Kohli’s, but the sample of batting opportunities is also considerably smaller. Neither number alone settles who was the more effective batter in particular conditions. [Rahul ledger](https://www.cricbuzz.com/profiles/8733/kl-rahul).

## What this list does not measure

Career runs reward accumulated production. They do not directly measure scoring against a particular bowling attack, powerplay versus death-over performance, or the value of a short innings in a difficult chase. Those questions need more specific evidence.

Likewise, a player can lead an individual season without leading the career table. Keep the season’s Orange Cap race separate from this historical list. When the next IPL begins, update the cutoff and all relevant totals together rather than mixing a live player figure with an older ranking.
