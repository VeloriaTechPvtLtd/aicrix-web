**Australia won the men’s Ashes 2025–26 series 4–1**, beating England in Perth, Brisbane, Adelaide and Sydney. England’s only victory came in the fourth Test at Melbourne. The series ended on 8 January 2026, when Australia completed a five-wicket win at the Sydney Cricket Ground. [ICC’s completed-series report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/australia-seal-4-1-ashes-win-in-sydney).

Australia secured the series in Adelaide. Winning the first three Tests produced a 3–0 lead with two matches remaining, so England could no longer catch them. Melbourne reduced the margin to 3–1 before Sydney settled the final score.

## All five Ashes Test results

These are **actual playing dates in the host city**, rather than the full period originally reserved for each Test.

- **Perth Stadium, 21–22 November 2025:** Australia won by eight wickets. Series score: Australia 1–0. [Perth report](https://www.cricket.com.au/news/4407192).

- **The Gabba, Brisbane, 4–7 December 2025:** Australia won by eight wickets. Series score: Australia 2–0. [Brisbane report](https://www.cricket.com.au/news/4415742).

- **Adelaide Oval, 17–21 December 2025:** Australia won by 82 runs. Series score: Australia 3–0. [Adelaide report](https://www.cricket.com.au/news/4423039/).

- **Melbourne Cricket Ground, 26–27 December 2025:** England won by four wickets. Series score: Australia 3–1. [Melbourne report](https://www.cricket.com.au/news/4425005/match-report-ashes-2025-australia-england-boxing-day-fourth-test-mcg-day-two-jacob-bethell-innings-duckett-crawley-root-stokes-carse-wickets).

- **Sydney Cricket Ground, 4–8 January 2026:** Australia won by five wickets. Final series score: Australia 4–1. [Sydney report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/australia-seal-4-1-ashes-win-in-sydney).

## Perth and England’s first innings advantage

England made **172 and 164**; Australia replied with **132 and 205/2**. England held a 40-run first-innings lead but could not turn it into a winning position. [Official Perth scorecard](https://www.cricket.com.au/matches/CA%3A21888/australia-men-england-men-men-s-ashes-2025-26).

Promoted to open in the chase, Travis Head scored 123 from 83 balls. His century arrived from 69 deliveries, and Marnus Labuschagne contributed an unbeaten 51 as Australia reached the 205 target inside two days. Mitchell Starc finished with ten wickets in the match. [Cricket Australia’s Perth report](https://www.cricket.com.au/news/4407192).

## Brisbane and the 177 run lead

England scored **334 and 241**, while Australia made **511 and 69/2**. Australia’s first-innings advantage was 177, leaving a fourth-innings target of 65. [Official Brisbane scorecard](https://www.cricket.com.au/matches/CA%3A21889/australia-men-england-men-men-s-ashes-2025-26).

The day-night Test ended on its fourth day. Michael Neser took 5/42 in England’s second innings, helping Australia move to 2–0. England’s resistance included a 96-run partnership between Ben Stokes and Will Jacks. Australia’s finishing total of 69 exceeded the target because Steve Smith completed the chase with a six. [Cricket Australia’s Brisbane report](https://www.cricket.com.au/news/4415742).

## Adelaide and the decisive third win

Australia made **371 and 349**; England scored **286 and 352**. An 85-run first-innings lead helped Australia set England 435 to win. England’s 352 left Australia with an 82-run victory. [Official Adelaide scorecard](https://www.cricket.com.au/matches/CA%3A21890/australia-men-england-men-men-s-ashes-2025-26).

Alex Carey was named player of the match after scoring 106 and 72, taking six catches and completing a stumping. England extended the contest into its fifth day, but Australia’s victory made it 3–0. Even two subsequent England wins could then have produced only a 3–2 Australian series victory. [Cricket Australia’s Adelaide report](https://www.cricket.com.au/news/4423039/).

The difference between the target and the winning margin is one run. England needed 435 to win, but 434 would have levelled the aggregate scores. Finishing on 352 meant an 82-run loss, not an 83-run margin.

## Melbourne and England’s only victory

Australia scored **152 and 132**, and England answered with **110 and 178/6**. Although Australia led by 42 after the first innings, England chased their target of 175 to win by four wickets. [Official Melbourne scorecard](https://www.cricket.com.au/matches/CA%3A21891).

The match lasted two days. Jacob Bethell’s 40, Zak Crawley’s 37 and Ben Duckett’s 34 helped England complete the chase. It was England’s first Test win in Australia since the 2010–11 tour, ending an 18-match winless sequence there. The result prevented a 5–0 finish, but the series had already been decided. [Cricket Australia’s Melbourne report](https://www.cricket.com.au/news/4425005/match-report-ashes-2025-australia-england-boxing-day-fourth-test-mcg-day-two-jacob-bethell-innings-duckett-crawley-root-stokes-carse-wickets).

## Sydney and the final series score

England made **384 and 342**, while Australia scored **567 and 161/5**. Australia’s 183-run first-innings lead reduced the final target to 160. [Official Sydney scorecard](https://www.cricket.com.au/matches/CA%3A21892/australia-men-england-men-5th-test).

Bethell’s 154 kept England competitive, but Australia completed the chase on day five. Cameron Green finished unbeaten on 22 and Carey on 16, with Carey hitting the winning boundary. The victory gave Usman Khawaja a winning farewell in his final Test. [ICC’s Sydney report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/australia-seal-4-1-ashes-win-in-sydney).

## How the targets add up

A fourth-innings target is the opposition’s combined runs, minus the chasing team’s first innings, plus one:

- Perth: 172 + 164 − 132 + 1 = **205**.

- Brisbane: 334 + 241 − 511 + 1 = **65**.

- Adelaide: 371 + 349 − 286 + 1 = **435**.

- Melbourne: 152 + 132 − 110 + 1 = **175**.

- Sydney: 384 + 342 − 567 + 1 = **160**.

Four Tests ended in successful chases; Adelaide was the only victory by a runs margin. The first-innings leader lost in Perth and Melbourne. Those observations follow from the five scorecards above.
