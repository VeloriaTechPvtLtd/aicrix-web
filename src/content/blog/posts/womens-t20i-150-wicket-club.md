## Women’s T20I 150-wicket club: two snapshots, one important change

The women’s T20I 150-wicket club cannot safely be described as a seven-player list without attaching a record cutoff. On **14 November 2026**, the [ACS career table](https://stats.acscricket.com/Records/Womens_International_Twenty20/Overall/Players/Most_Wickets_in_Career.html) lists eight bowlers at or above 150, including Uganda’s Concy Aweko on 151. The [Crickrida full table](https://crickrida.com/records/women/t20i/most-wickets/), labelled as a **9 November 2026 snapshot**, still lists seven, with Aweko on 148.

Those are different published snapshots, not an independently reconciled live ranking. The table below preserves both columns so a reader can see which membership question remains open. The original seven names qualify in both records; Aweko’s newer ACS entry crosses the boundary. Her latest included matches still need primary-scorecard reconciliation before a definitive current list is published.

## Membership and position are different questions

A one-wicket difference can leave membership unchanged while altering a tie. Kary Chan qualifies at both 154 and 155. Only the former would put her level with Ecclestone. Selecting a joint rank before resolving the underlying entries would make the comparison more certain than its evidence.

Aweko is a different case because her two displayed totals straddle the threshold. At 148 she is two short; at 151 she is one above it. The three-wicket difference cannot simply be labelled an error, or credited to a specific match, without checking what each provider has included. A retrieval date alone does not establish the date of the last match incorporated.

Ishimwe and Kamchomphu each have two more wickets in ACS than in the older-labelled table. Putthawong has one more. Those changes show why updating only the leading name is not enough. A proper refresh must review every member, the possible new entrant and the players immediately below the cutoff together. It must also distinguish newly included matches from corrections to older records.

## What the milestone measures

A career wicket total measures accumulated dismissals credited to the bowler. It is not the same as team wickets: a run-out does not automatically become a bowler’s wicket. Nor does reaching 150 establish that a bowler has been more economical, more difficult to score against, or more effective in a particular phase than every player below the threshold.

Those are separate questions with separate denominators. Bowling average needs runs conceded and wickets; bowling strike rate needs legal balls and wickets; economy needs runs conceded and legal balls converted to overs. A match appearance alone does not tell you how many deliveries a player bowled. Comparing career rates requires compatible inputs from the same record window, not figures assembled from differently updated pages.

## Keep the format and cutoff attached

Women’s T20I totals exclude ODI and Test wickets and should not absorb franchise or domestic T20 returns. Putthawong’s individual statistics, for example, separate ODI and T20I bowling records. Combining those categories would answer a different question. [Putthawong’s format tables](https://crickrida.com/players/thipatcha-putthawong/).

A World Cup leaderboard is narrower again: it covers one tournament or that competition’s history, not every eligible international. Read this club as a career milestone reference with provisional totals. For an exact position, tie or rate, use a reconciled record with the latest included international stated explicitly; a page’s retrieval date is not proof that every recent match is included.
