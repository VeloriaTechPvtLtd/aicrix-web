A free hit is protection against certain dismissals, not a promise of free runs. In the October 2026 ICC men's ODI and T20I conditions, it follows any type of no-ball, not only an overstep. A wide by itself does not create one. [ODI clause 21.18](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/bxkzkcyvfn6tvi70zh0w.pdf) and [T20I clause 21.18](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/bsxcho7ufe2pjban0wjp.pdf).

## What if the free-hit delivery is also a wide?

The protection carries forward after a wide or another no-ball. Think of it as a pending state rather than one particular ball number. A legitimate delivery uses that pending free hit. [ICC ODI clause 21.18.1](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/bxkzkcyvfn6tvi70zh0w.pdf).

Consider this invented sequence. There are no other penalties:

| Delivery | Event | Team runs added | Free hit still pending? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | No-ball, no runs taken | 1 | Yes |
| B | Wide, no runs taken | 1 | Yes |
| C | Legal delivery, hit for four | 4 | No |

The sequence adds six runs. It is not three legal balls, nor does the word “free” award an extra boundary. The six is ordinary addition: one plus one plus four. [No-ball and wide scoring: ICC ODI clauses 21.14–21.16 and 22.6–22.8](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/bxkzkcyvfn6tvi70zh0w.pdf).

## Can the batter still be out?

Yes. Run out, obstructing the field and hitting the ball twice remain possible. Bowled, caught, lbw, stumped and hit wicket do not. These are the dismissal exceptions specified by [ICC clauses 21.17–21.18](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/bxkzkcyvfn6tvi70zh0w.pdf), not a general exemption from every dismissal.

Do not confuse “run out is possible” with “anything a wicketkeeper does is a run-out.” The no-ball dismissal protection includes an exception when the striker is not attempting a run and the keeper breaks the wicket without another fielder's intervention. [ICC ODI clauses 21.18.2 and 38.2.2.2](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/bxkzkcyvfn6tvi70zh0w.pdf).

For a disputed clip, write down whether a run was attempted and who touched the ball. Those observations are more useful than relabelling an unavailable stumping.

## Can the captain move the field?

Normally not. The current ODI rule permits changes when the striker changes or to correct the fielding-restriction breach that caused the no-ball. [ICC clause 21.18.3](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/bxkzkcyvfn6tvi70zh0w.pdf).

For analysis, keep two separate questions: was the field change permitted, and was it tactically useful? A strong tactical reason does not establish permission.

## The rulebook check before a local match

Tell the scorer and both captains which competition regulations apply before play. This guide is scoped to the cited men's international conditions, not an assumption about every school, club or franchise tournament. The cited playing conditions are the governing source for the international examples here.

The simplest live checklist is: what triggered the free hit, is it still pending, and which dismissal is being considered? Keeping those questions separate prevents most of the confusion.
