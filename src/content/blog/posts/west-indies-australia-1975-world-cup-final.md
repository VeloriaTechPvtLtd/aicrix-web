West Indies won the first men's Cricket World Cup final, beating Australia by 17 runs at Lord's in 1975. Clive Lloyd made 102 from 85 balls, West Indies reached 291 for eight, and five Australian batters were run out in the chase. It was a final in which the fielding deserves to stand beside the century. [MCC's Father Time Wall history](https://www.lords.org/lords/our-history/father-time-wall/1975-west-indies-beat-australia-to-win-the-first-m)

To understand this game, begin by putting aside an assumption associated with modern ODI cricket: the innings allocation was 60 overs, not 50. That changes how the total and the duration of the contest should be read. A historical score should remain attached to the format in which it was made.

## Lloyd arrived with rebuilding to do

Australia chose to field in the final on 21 June. West Indies were 50 for three when Lloyd joined Rohan Kanhai. Their fourth-wicket partnership produced 149 runs, and Kanhai contributed 55. The innings therefore depended on a partnership, rather than a century produced after an easy start. [MCC's archival account](https://www.lords.org/lords/news-stories/world-cup-finals-pt-1-the-prudential-cup-era)

The final also contains an unusual early dismissal: Roy Fredericks hit a ball towards the crowd but fell onto his stumps and was out hit wicket. MCC's account records the incident as part of Australia's initial success. It is a reminder to read the dismissal entry rather than assume that an impressive-looking shot produced the expected outcome. [MCC's archival account](https://www.lords.org/lords/news-stories/world-cup-finals-pt-1-the-prudential-cup-era)

## Recovery still needed a finish

Later, West Indies were 209 for six. Keith Boyce and Bernard Julien added a half-century partnership for the seventh wicket, helping the side reach 291 for eight. Gary Gilmour took five wickets for Australia, following his six-wicket semi-final performance. [ICC's 1975 overview](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/cricketworldcup/news/mens-cricket-world-cup-1975-overview)

MCC records Gilmour's final figures as five for 48. That substantial return did not produce a team victory. It is a useful example of why individual bowling figures and the match result answer different questions: an excellent spell can coexist with an opposition total large enough to defend. [MCC's Father Time Wall history](https://www.lords.org/lords/our-history/father-time-wall/1975-west-indies-beat-australia-to-win-the-first-m)

## Fielding interrupted Australia's chase

Viv Richards was involved in three run-outs, including two direct hits and a combined effort with Lloyd. Boyce also took four wickets. Australia reached 233 for nine, before Dennis Lillee and Jeff Thomson prolonged the contest; the innings eventually ended on 274. [ICC's 1975 overview](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/cricketworldcup/news/mens-cricket-world-cup-1975-overview)

A run-out is not credited to a bowler, which is why a list of bowling figures alone cannot explain all ten dismissals here. ## Why the last partnership still matters

The difference between Australia's 233 for nine and their finishing total was 41 runs. That is the recovery made by the final pair, calculated from the ICC's checkpoints. It did not reverse the result, but it did prevent the match from ending as soon as the ninth wicket fell.
