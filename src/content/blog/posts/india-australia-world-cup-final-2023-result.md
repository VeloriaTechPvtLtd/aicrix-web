Australia beat India by six wickets in the men's ODI World Cup final at Narendra Modi Stadium, Ahmedabad, on 19 November 2023. After choosing to field, Australia dismissed India for 240 in 50 overs and reached 241/4 in 43 overs. [BCCI match record](https://www.bcci.tv/matches/092337b2-25ac-47b5-b4b7-d40447f16c4a/india-vs-australia/match-details).

The result combined two separate achievements: limiting India's total after a fast start, then recovering from three early wickets. Treating it only as a comfortable chase loses the uncertainty of Australia's opening overs. Treating it only as India's batting slowdown misses the partnership that settled the final.

## India's start and the innings that followed

Rohit Sharma scored 47 from 31 balls, and India reached 80/2 after ten overs. Virat Kohli contributed 54 and KL Rahul 66, but the innings ended at 240. Mitchell Starc took three wickets, while Pat Cummins and Josh Hazlewood took two each, as the [ICC match report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/awesome-australia-beat-india-to-win-the-icc-mens-cricket-world-cup) records.

That first-ten-over checkpoint makes the change of pace measurable. India scored at 8.00 runs per over initially, then added 160 over the remaining 40 overs, a rate of 4.00. The later period occupied four-fifths of the available overs but supplied two-thirds of the total. This describes the innings; it does not establish what an alternative batting strategy would have produced.

Kohli and Rahul's 67-run partnership gave India continuity after the early dismissals. Yet partnership size and scoring speed answer different questions. A stand can prevent an immediate collapse without restoring the earlier rate. Neither the final total nor one batter's strike rate, considered alone, explains all the decisions made against the bowling and field settings.

## From 47/3 to a controlled pursuit

Australia slipped to 47/3 inside the first seven overs. Travis Head and Marnus Labuschagne then added 192 for the fourth wicket. Head finished with 137 from 120 balls, Labuschagne with an unbeaten 58, and Glenn Maxwell struck the winning runs after Head's dismissal. [Cricket Australia's report](https://www.cricket.com.au/news/3789549/australia-india-2023-world-cup-final-ahmedabad-narendra-modi-stadium-match-report-video-hig) documents that recovery and the finish.

At 47/3, Australia still needed 194. Losing three wickets so early had exposed more of the batting order before the pursuit had properly settled. A substantial partnership would need to preserve wickets as well as make progress toward 241.

The fourth-wicket partnership supplied 192 of Australia’s 241 runs, or about 79.7%. This measures the stand’s share of the team total, including any extras during it; it does not mean both batters scored all their personal runs together.

## What the winning margin does and does not show

Head fell at 239/4, leaving two runs to reach the target of 241. Australia finished with 42 balls unused. The six-wicket margin describes the end state, not how secure the chase looked at 47/3.

The most useful comparison is between the two innings' changing demands. India needed to turn an aggressive opening into a larger completed total; Australia needed to keep the chase intact after early damage. Head and Labuschagne's stand did both jobs required of a recovery: it protected wickets and completed most of the scoring still required. That recovery was the decisive contrast between the two innings.
