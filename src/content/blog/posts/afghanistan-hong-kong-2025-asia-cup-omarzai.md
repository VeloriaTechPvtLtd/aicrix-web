Afghanistan beat Hong Kong by 94 runs in the Asia Cup opener at Abu Dhabi on 9 September 2025. Afghanistan made 188/6; Hong Kong finished on 94/9. Hong Kong were **not all out**. [Afghanistan Cricket Board fixture and report](https://acb.af/en-us/matches/fixture/129)

## Atal and Nabi provided the bridge

Early wickets removed Rahmanullah Gurbaz for eight and Ibrahim Zadran for one. Sediqullah Atal and Mohammad Nabi then added 51 for the third wicket. Atal finished unbeaten on 73, while Nabi made 33. [ACB match report](https://acb.af/en-US/post/all-round-afghanistan-off-the-mark-in-acc-mens-t20-asia-cup-2025)

That stand gave Afghanistan a middle-innings recovery before Azmatullah Omarzai’s acceleration. It should not be confused with the sum of Atal’s and Nabi’s complete innings: their 106 combined runs include scoring outside that partnership.

## Omarzai accelerated, then struck

Omarzai scored 53 from 21 balls, reaching fifty in 20. The ACB reported the milestone as Afghanistan’s fastest men’s T20I half-century at the time. He later dismissed Zeeshan Ali as Hong Kong fell to 12/2. [ACB contemporary account](https://acb.af/en-US/post/all-round-afghanistan-off-the-mark-in-acc-mens-t20-asia-cup-2025)

His complete innings had a strike rate of **252.38**: 53 ÷ 21 × 100. That is separate from the 20-ball milestone. The former uses every ball he faced; the latter stops when he first reached fifty.

## Why the two totals produce a 94-run margin

The winning margin is 188 − 94 = **94 runs**. The target Hong Kong needed to win was 189, so they finished 95 short of that target. Those statements use different reference points and do not contradict each other.

The result therefore contains three distinct contributions: a rebuilding partnership, rapid late scoring and wickets during the defence. Retaining those stages explains more than describing the match simply as a large win.
