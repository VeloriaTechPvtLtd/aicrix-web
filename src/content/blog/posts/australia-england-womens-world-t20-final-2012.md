Australia retained the Women’s World T20 title in Colombo on 7 October 2012, beating England by four runs. Australia made 142/4 and England finished on 138/9, both from 20 overs. England still had a chance to win from the final delivery, but the required six became a single.

## Cameron accelerated the middle overs

Australia reached 47 during the powerplay. Meg Lanning made 25 and Alyssa Healy 26 before Jess Cameron contributed 45 from 34 balls. Cameron and Lisa Sthalekar added 51 in six overs, a partnership rate of 8.50 runs per over.

England restricted the final four overs to 23 runs, or 5.75 per over. Holly Colvin finished with 2/21. The innings therefore contained both an Australian acceleration and a successful English restriction, rather than one continuous surge.

## Sthalekar’s contribution crossed innings

Sthalekar made 23 from 26 balls and then took 2/16 from four overs. Her batting supported the middle-innings partnership; her bowling helped make the resulting target defendable. These were two different jobs within the same victory.

The close result makes England’s late restriction worth retaining in the account. Australia had built a useful total, but the bowlers still had to defend it through to the final ball.

## A win despite late mistakes

Australia’s post-match account acknowledged missed chances and fielding errors late in England’s chase. England’s lower order kept the contest alive despite the wickets already lost. It was a successful defence, not a flawless one.

England needed 143 to win. Their eventual 138 was five short of that target, but the official margin was four because it compares the two teams’ completed scores: 142 minus 138. Before the last delivery, a six could still have won the match. The single that followed left Australia ahead.

The final is best understood through those connected phases: Australia’s opening runs, Cameron and Sthalekar’s partnership, England’s restrictive finish, and a chase that remained unresolved until the last ball.
