**Desert Vipers topped the ILT20 Season 4 league table with eight wins and 16 points.** MI Emirates finished second, Dubai Capitals third and Abu Dhabi Knight Riders fourth. Gulf Giants and Sharjah Warriorz missed the playoffs. [Season 4 table](https://www.cricbuzz.com/cricket-series/10884/international-league-t20-2025-26/points-table)

The edition ran from **2 December 2025 to 4 January 2026**. Its league phase ended on 28 December; the matches after that were playoffs. This distinction prevents a common calendar mistake: Season 4 finished in early 2026, while Season 5 starts later in that same year. This is not the table for the forthcoming fifth edition. ## Points and NRR answer different questions

In this completed table, points are twice the displayed win total. Vipers’ eight wins produce 16 points, MI Emirates’ seven produce 14, and the remaining rows follow the same arithmetic. Across all six teams there are 30 wins, 30 losses and 60 points, matching 30 league matches with a winner assigned to each.

That does not mean every game had an outright winner before a tie-break. Vipers’ 8 December meeting with Gulf Giants was tied before Vipers won the Super Over. The league ledger counts the resolved result in the win and loss columns; it is not a no result. [Match-result ledger](https://www.cricbuzz.com/cricket-series/10884/international-league-t20-2025-26/points-table) · [Contemporary account of the Super Over](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/sports/cricket/cricket-stakes-rise-as-dp-world-ilt20season-4enters-the-playoffs)

NRR is not a winning percentage or a replacement points total. MI Emirates and Dubai Capitals both had higher published NRRs than Vipers, but neither had as many points. Sorting the teams solely by NRR would therefore give the wrong order.

For a separate descriptive calculation, Vipers won **8 of 10 league matches, or 80%**, while MI Emirates won 70%. Those percentages describe this completed sample. They are not probabilities of winning a future game or the title.

## The last available playoff place

Abu Dhabi Knight Riders secured the final berth by beating Gulf Giants by 32 runs on 28 December. The result left Knight Riders on eight points and Giants on six. The contemporary playoff report identifies that match as the final league-stage fixture and confirms all four qualifiers. [29 December match report](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/sports/cricket/news/pepper-salt-fire-abu-dhabi-knight-riders-into-ilt20-playoffs/articleshow/126224983.cms)

Knight Riders qualified despite a negative NRR. Gulf Giants’ less negative NRR did not overcome the two-point deficit. This is a useful example of why “better NRR” is not enough when teams are not level on points.

The report’s description of the match as must-win is historical context for that completed contest. It should not be copied into a current playoff forecast without the new season’s table and remaining fixtures.

## The playoff route and the trophy are separate

The top two, Vipers and MI Emirates, met in Qualifier 1 on 30 December. Dubai Capitals and Knight Riders met in the Eliminator on 1 January, followed by Qualifier 2 on 2 January and the final on 4 January. [Contemporary playoff schedule](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/sports/cricket/cricket-stakes-rise-as-dp-world-ilt20season-4enters-the-playoffs)

Vipers eventually won the title, beating MI Emirates by 46 runs in Dubai. The official Vipers report identifies that match explicitly as the Season 4 final. That championship did not change the league table to 11 or 12 played; it belongs to the separate knockout phase. [Official final report](https://www.thedesertvipers.com/news/desert-vipers-v-mi-emirates-final-ilt20)

When comparing season records, label the denominator. “Eight league wins” and “wins across the whole tournament” are different statistics, even when they describe the same team. A table row can stay fixed while a match list beneath it includes later playoff results.
