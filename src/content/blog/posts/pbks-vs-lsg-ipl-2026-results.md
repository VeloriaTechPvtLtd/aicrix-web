Punjab Kings won both of their IPL 2026 meetings with Lucknow Super Giants. They defended 254/7 in New Chandigarh on 19 April, then chased 197 in Lucknow on 23 May.

The two results are worth reading together because different players supplied the decisive partnerships. The first was a rapid rebuild after an opening wicket; the second was a chase recovered from two early wickets.

## 19 April: Arya and Connolly built the large total

Lucknow chose to field. Priyansh Arya’s 93 from 37 balls and Cooper Connolly’s 87 from 46 powered Punjab’s 254/7. Arya was Player of the Match.

The second-wicket stand added 182 after Punjab had lost their first wicket at three. Lucknow’s 200/5 was a substantial score in isolation, but the winning target was 255. The eventual 54-run margin is the difference between the two completed innings totals, not between Lucknow’s score and the target. ## 23 May: Iyer and Prabhsimran repaired the chase

Punjab chose to field in the return fixture. Lucknow made 196/6, including Josh Inglis’s 72 from 44 balls. Punjab then slipped to 22/2 before a 140-run third-wicket partnership between Prabhsimran Singh and Shreyas Iyer changed the chase.

Prabhsimran finished with 69 from 39 and Iyer with an unbeaten 101 from 51. Punjab reached 200/3 with 12 legal balls unused; Iyer was Player of the Match. This was a successful chase, not another defence of a 250-plus total. ## Comparing the two partnerships properly

The scorecards record 80 legal balls for the April stand and 76 for the May stand. Converting those to six-ball-over rates gives:

| Partnership | Runs and legal balls | Runs per six legal balls | Share of Punjab’s final total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Arya–Connolly, 19 April | 182 from 80 | 13.65 | 71.7% |
| Prabhsimran–Iyer, 23 May | 140 from 76 | 11.05 | 70.0% |

The calculations are runs multiplied by six, divided by legal balls; and partnership runs divided by team runs. Partnership totals include extras scored during the stand, so they should not be treated as the two batters’ combined individual runs.

Both stands supplied about seven-tenths of Punjab’s eventual score. However, the April innings could use its entire allocation to build a target, while the May innings ended when the chase was won. Their scoring rates describe different situations, not a controlled test of which pair was better. ## Why a team score of 200 meant different things

Lucknow scored 200 in April and lost. Punjab scored 200 in May and won. There is no contradiction: the targets were 255 and 197.

A round-number team score is not a universal measure of a winning innings. The opposition’s total, the balls available and the match state decide whether it is enough. Similarly, the toss winner lost the first meeting but won the second, offering no basis for an automatic “choose to chase” rule.

## What to carry into a future preview

These archives establish two Punjab victories in one season. They do not establish permanent superiority or guarantee that the same batters will occupy the same roles next time.

For a future match, refresh squad availability, likely batting positions, venue and current conditions. Keep the historical ledger intact, but separate it from the new evidence. A result page answers what happened; a preview must still establish what has changed.
