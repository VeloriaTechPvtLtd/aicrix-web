Kumble’s total comes from 132 Tests; Ashwin finished with 106. ## Career wickets and a ten wicket haul mean different things

A career total adds the bowler’s credited wickets across Tests. An innings figure describes one opposition innings; a match figure combines both innings when the opposition bats twice.

Kumble’s Delhi Test against Pakistan in 1999 provides a clear example:

- First innings: **4/75**.

- Second innings: **10/74**.

- Combined match figures: **14/149**, adding four and ten wickets, then 75 and 74 runs.

The famous ten refers to the second innings. Fourteen wickets from that match contributed to his career total, not ten. The innings figures are documented in [ICC’s account of the ten wicket performances](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/laker-kumble-patel-how-the-trio-claimed-all-ten).

A ten wicket **match** haul does not require ten wickets in one innings. Hypothetical figures of 6/50 and 4/60 produce 10/110 for the match. Conversely, the rare feat of taking all ten in one innings belongs in an innings-record table.

Ashwin finished with **37 five wicket innings** and **eight ten wicket matches**, according to the [BCCI retirement release](https://www.bcci.tv/news/article/bcci-congratulates-ravichandran-ashwin-on-a-phenomenal-career). These are counts of qualifying performances. They are not extra wickets to add to his career tally.

## Why the team’s wickets can exceed the bowlers’ total

A run out is a team dismissal but is not credited to a bowler. MCC’s **Law 38.6** makes that distinction explicit. This also applies when the bowler personally carries out the run out. [MCC’s 2026 Laws of Cricket](https://www.lords.org/getmedia/a4ef9f77-2a25-4f5b-a286-4601f08e6334/Laws-of-Cricket-2017-Code-4th-Edition-%282026%29_5.pdf).

For example, if an innings ends with ten batters dismissed and two were run out, the bowlers’ wicket columns can total eight. That is consistent scoring, not two missing bowling wickets.

## How to compare bowlers beyond the total

Career wickets answer who accumulated the most. For a comparison of performance, also consider bowling average, strike rate, workload, opposition and home or away conditions.

Average measures runs conceded per wicket; bowling strike rate measures balls per wicket. Neither is the same as wickets per match. Compare the same format and period, and retain the number of matches or balls behind the rate.

For a forthcoming match, add recent fitness, selection and likely bowling workload to that analysis. A career record is useful context, but it does not establish how many wickets a player will take next.
