Chris Gayle’s unbeaten 133 carried West Indies past South Africa in the Champions Trophy semifinal at Jaipur on 2 November 2006. South Africa had made 258/8, with Herschelle Gibbs scoring 77; West Indies reached another final. [ICC retrospective](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/top-five-the-best-of-chris-gayle-in-odis)

## The change at 154 was not a wicket

Gayle and Shivnarine Chanderpaul put on 154 before Chanderpaul retired hurt on 57. The first wicket fell later at 196, when Ramnaresh Sarwan was dismissed. [Contemporary scorecard](https://i.imgci.com/db/ARCHIVE/2006-07/OD_TOURNEYS/ICCCT/SCORECARDS/KNOCKOUTS/RSA_WI_ICCCT_ODI-SEMI2_02NOV2006.html)

Retirement changed the pair at the crease without adding a wicket to the score. Treating 154 as 154/1 would misrepresent both the opening partnership and the later dismissals.

Against a target of 259, the opening pair had removed about 59.5% of the requirement before Chanderpaul left. Another 105 runs were needed, with no wickets yet lost. This was a strong platform, not a completed chase.

## Gayle stayed through later dismissals

West Indies finished on 262/4 in 44 overs. Sarwan made 27, Dwayne Bravo 15 and Brian Lara 9; Runako Morton was dismissed without scoring. Marlon Samuels remained unbeaten alongside Gayle. [Batting record](https://i.imgci.com/db/ARCHIVE/2006-07/OD_TOURNEYS/ICCCT/SCORECARDS/KNOCKOUTS/RSA_WI_ICCCT_ODI-SEMI2_02NOV2006.html)

Gayle’s 133 represented about 50.8% of the final total. The opening stand and his innings are overlapping contributions, not separate amounts to add together. Chanderpaul helped create the initial advantage; Gayle connected it to the finish while partners changed.

## A scoring detail worth preserving

The scorecard notes a five-ball eighth over. Its recorded 44 overs should not be multiplied by six and presented as an exact count of legal deliveries. [Scoring notes](https://i.imgci.com/db/ARCHIVE/2006-07/OD_TOURNEYS/ICCCT/SCORECARDS/KNOCKOUTS/RSA_WI_ICCCT_ODI-SEMI2_02NOV2006.html)

The result was a six-wicket victory. Explaining it accurately requires three distinct events: the long opening partnership, an injury retirement that was not a dismissal, and Gayle’s unbeaten continuation through the wickets that followed.
