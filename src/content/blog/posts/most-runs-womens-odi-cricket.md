Mithali Raj leads women’s ODI career run-scoring with 7,805 runs. The complete 6,000-run group in the all-time ledger checked on 14 November 2026 contains two players: Raj and West Indies’ Stafanie Taylor. This is an ODI-only record, not a combined international tally or a Women’s World Cup leaderboard. [ACS career list](https://stats.acscricket.com/Records/Womens_ODI/Overall/Players/Most_Career_Runs.html).

Taylor’s official Cricket West Indies record lists 6,398 runs. That leaves a 1,407-run gap to Raj. Charlotte Edwards, next on the overall list at 5,992, sits eight runs below the threshold; Suzie Bates follows with 5,982. Being close to a round number does not qualify a career total for inclusion above it.

## The complete 6000 run group

| 1 | Mithali Raj | India | 7,805 | 232 | 211 | 57 |

Raj’s figures agree between the [ACS list](https://stats.acscricket.com/Records/Womens_ODI/Overall/Players/Most_Career_Runs.html), her [individual record](https://www.cricbuzz.com/profiles/9072/mithali-c) and the ICC’s retirement total. Taylor’s row follows [Cricket West Indies](https://www.windiescricket.com/players/stafanie-taylor-2091/), which agrees with ACS. Cricbuzz instead displays 6,396 runs, 176 innings and 26 not outs. That unresolved difference does not change the two qualifiers or their order, but the figures should not be mixed.

## Cutoffs belong to the format

Raj’s final ODI was against South Africa at Christchurch on 27 March 2022. The ICC announced her international retirement on 8 June 2022, confirming 7,805 ODI runs from 232 matches. The retirement announcement and the final match are different dates. [Career timeline](https://www.cricbuzz.com/profiles/9072/mithali-c) and [ICC retirement report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/india-legend-mithali-raj-announces-retirement).

Taylor’s latest ODI entry is 30 September 2026 against Zimbabwe in Harare, when she made eight from ten balls. Her October appearances against Zimbabwe were T20Is and cannot increase this ODI total. The [individual match ledger](https://www.cricbuzz.com/profiles/9095/stafanie-taylor/all-matches/batting) separates the formats, while [CWI’s results](https://www.windiescricket.com/results/team_id/1949/) confirm the completed September ODI series.

Edwards’ 5,992 is corroborated by her [ICC Hall of Fame profile](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/charlotte-edwards-record-run-scorer-inspirational-captain-and-cricketing-trailblazer). Bates’ [individual record](https://www.cricbuzz.com/profiles/9163/suzie-bates) also shows 5,982 and identifies 16 May 2026 against England as her last ODI. These are actual career totals, not totals rounded up to make a larger milestone club.

The boundary demonstrates why an all-time list must be inspected beyond its headline. Raj’s lead over Edwards is 1,813 runs, the margin quoted when Raj retired. Her lead over the current second-placed Taylor is smaller. An old article can preserve the correct leading total while giving the wrong present-day runner-up and gap.

## Runs per innings and batting average answer different questions

Raj scored 7,805 runs in 211 batting innings, or 36.99 per innings. Her batting average is 50.68 because the denominator is 154 dismissals: 211 innings minus 57 not outs. Neither number is a replacement for the other.

Using Taylor’s official ledger consistently gives 36.56 runs per innings: 6,398 divided by 175. Removing 27 not outs leaves 148 dismissals and an average of 43.23 when rounded to two decimals; the source displays 43.22. The two players’ runs per batting innings are therefore much closer than their career totals alone suggest.

That comparison does not adjust for batting position, opposition or era, and it cannot predict future innings. Raj had 36 more batting innings in this ledger. Career volume reflects both the opportunities accumulated and the runs produced in them; a leaderboard is most useful when those two ideas remain visible.
