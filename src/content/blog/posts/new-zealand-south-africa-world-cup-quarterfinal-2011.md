New Zealand beat South Africa by 49 runs in the men’s World Cup quarterfinal at Shere Bangla National Stadium, Dhaka, on 25 March 2011. New Zealand made 221/8 in 50 overs; South Africa were dismissed for 172 in 43.2. Jacob Oram was Player of the Match. This was a defence built around wickets and fielding, not an unusually large first-innings total. [Full match record](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/2211/nz-vs-rsa-3rd-quarter-final-icc-world-cup-2011)

## The two innings

| Team | Score | Overs | Largest individual innings |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| New Zealand | 221/8 | 50 | Jesse Ryder 83 from 121 |
| South Africa | 172 all out | 43.2 | Jacques Kallis 47 from 75 |

South Africa’s target was 222. Their innings ended with 40 scheduled legal balls unused, because 43.2 overs means 260 deliveries rather than 43.2 multiplied by six. Losing all ten wickets prevented those remaining balls from being used. The completed totals differ by 49, which is the winning margin. [Scorecard](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/2211/nz-vs-rsa-3rd-quarter-final-icc-world-cup-2011)

## Ryder and Taylor repaired an early setback

New Zealand were 16/2 when Ross Taylor joined Jesse Ryder. Their 114-run partnership carried the score to 130 before Taylor fell for 43. Ryder ultimately made 83, while Kane Williamson’s unbeaten 38 helped New Zealand complete their allocation. [NZC contemporary report](https://www.nzc.nz/match-reports/archive/blackcaps-through-to-the-semis/)

The third-wicket stand occupied 27 overs, from the end of the sixth through the end of the 33rd. Its calculated scoring rate was 4.22 per over. That is not a rapid partnership, but it replaced the immediate danger of an early collapse with a platform from which the remaining batters could work. The full innings still required contributions after both partners had gone. [Wicket timings](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/2211/nz-vs-rsa-3rd-quarter-final-icc-world-cup-2011)

South Africa kept taking wickets after the stand ended. Morne Morkel finished with three, while Dale Steyn and Imran Tahir took two each. Williamson’s unbeaten innings is therefore better understood as sustaining the total through those losses than as an isolated finishing cameo. [Complete batting and bowling figures](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/2211/nz-vs-rsa-3rd-quarter-final-icc-world-cup-2011)

## New Zealand attacked with spin from the start

Nathan McCullum and Daniel Vettori opened the bowling. Hashim Amla was caught in the first over after the ball deflected from wicketkeeper Brendon McCullum’s boot. Graeme Smith and Kallis then added 61 before Oram removed Smith. Later, Oram caught Kallis near the boundary off Tim Southee. [NZC match account](https://www.nzc.nz/match-reports/archive/blackcaps-through-to-the-semis/)

That last dismissal was at 108/3, not 121/3. South Africa subsequently reached 121 before losing JP Duminy and AB de Villiers in the same over. Duminy was bowled by Nathan McCullum; de Villiers was run out. The sequence matters: the collapse was a succession of distinct events rather than a single wicket erasing the whole chase. [Wicket ledger](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/2211/nz-vs-rsa-3rd-quarter-final-icc-world-cup-2011)

## Wickets changed a manageable asking rate

| Checkpoint | Score | Runs needed | Balls available | Required runs per over |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| After de Villiers was run out at 28 overs | 121/5 | 101 | 132 | 4.59 |
| After Steyn fell at 37.4 | 146/8 | 76 | 74 | 6.16 |

These are calculated from the target of 222 and the recorded wicket checkpoints. Between them South Africa added only 25 in 58 legal balls while losing three more wickets. The rate requirement rose, but the more immediate limitation was the loss of batting partners. A rate below five at the earlier checkpoint did not mean the chase was secure.

Faf du Plessis continued to resist, making 36, but Oram dismissed him at 172. Luke Woodcock then took the last wicket without another run being added. The result advanced New Zealand to the semifinal, not to the final. [NZC account](https://www.nzc.nz/match-reports/archive/blackcaps-through-to-the-semis/)

## Oram and McCullum shared the decisive return

Oram finished with 4/39 and Nathan McCullum with 3/24. Together they took seven wickets while conceding 63 runs across 19 overs, a calculated economy of 3.32. Vettori’s ten overs cost 39 without a wicket, illustrating why wicket totals alone do not describe every useful spell. [ICC match report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/oram-stars-in-thrilling-new-zealand-win)

The record supports a concrete explanation: a rebuilding first innings, controlled bowling, catches and a run-out. It does not establish a permanent psychological trait of either team or forecast another knockout. This archive belongs to the specific players, sequence and result of 25 March 2011.
