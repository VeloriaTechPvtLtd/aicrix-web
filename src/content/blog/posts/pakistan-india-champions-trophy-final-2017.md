**Pakistan won the 2017 Champions Trophy final by 180 runs at The Oval on 18 June.** After India chose to field, Pakistan made 338/4 in 50 overs and dismissed India for 158 in 30.3. Fakhar Zaman’s 114 and Mohammad Amir’s early wickets shaped Pakistan’s first title in the competition. [ICC match report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/electric-pakistan-marches-to-maiden-title).

## Final score and awards

| Item | Recorded outcome |
| --- | --- |
| Pakistan | 338/4 in 50 overs |
| India | 158 all out in 30.3 overs |
| India’s target | 339 |
| Result | Pakistan won by 180 runs |
| Player of the Match | Fakhar Zaman, 114 from 106 balls |
| Player of the Tournament | Hasan Ali, 13 wickets across the competition |

The target and margin use different arithmetic. India needed one more than Pakistan’s 338 to win, hence 339. The winning margin compares the two completed totals: 338 minus 158 equals 180, not 181. Fakhar’s final award was separate from Hasan’s tournament award. [Completed scorecard](https://cricketdatabase.com/matches/2017-06-18-india-v-pakistan), [presentation record](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scores/16714/pak-vs-ind-final-icc-champions-trophy-2017).

## Fakhar’s reprieve became a century

Fakhar was on three when a catch behind off Jasprit Bumrah did not count because the delivery was a no-ball. He went on to make his first ODI hundred. Azhar Ali contributed 59 to their 128-run opening stand, and Pakistan had a foundation that survived Azhar’s run-out. [ICC first-innings report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/fakhar-ton-muscles-pakistan-to-338-for-4).

That reprieve mattered, but it is not a complete explanation of the result. Pakistan still had to convert its start and then defend a large total. Babar Azam made 46, Mohammad Hafeez finished unbeaten on 57 from 37 balls, and Imad Wasim added 25 not out. Their contributions prevented the innings from becoming only a story about one opener. [Two-innings ledger](https://cricketdatabase.com/matches/2017-06-18-india-v-pakistan).

## The finish added to the opening platform

Pakistan’s fourth wicket fell at 267 after 42.3 overs. Hafeez and Imad then added an unbeaten 71 to reach 338. From that wicket to the end, 45 legal balls remained: seven full overs plus three deliveries. The team therefore scored at 9.47 runs per six legal balls over that closing segment. [Fall of wickets](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/16714/pak-vs-ind-final-icc-champions-trophy-2017).

The 71-run stand is not Hafeez’s complete 57 plus Imad’s 25. Hafeez had already begun his innings before Babar was dismissed, and a team partnership also includes extras. Keeping the score checkpoints attached to the stand avoids overstating the contribution of that particular pairing.

## Amir removed the top three

Amir dismissed Rohit Sharma without scoring, Virat Kohli for five and Shikhar Dhawan for 21. India were 33/3 after nine overs. Kohli had been dropped immediately before his dismissal, but Amir created another chance rather than allowing the missed catch to become a lasting escape. [ICC account of the opening spell](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/electric-pakistan-marches-to-maiden-title).

India’s problem was not simply a climbing rate. At 33/3, 306 were still needed from 246 legal balls, an asking rate of 7.46 per over. Three established top-order batters were already out. The remaining runs and the remaining batting resources had to be considered together.

## Hardik’s counterattack did not restore the lost wickets

| Chase checkpoint | Runs still needed | Legal balls available |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 33/3 after 9 overs | 306 | 246 |
| 72/6 after 17 overs | 267 | 198 |
| 152/7 after 26.3 overs | 187 | 141 |

Hardik Pandya scored 76 from 43 balls, including six sixes. With Ravindra Jadeja he added 80 for the seventh wicket before being run out at 152. India then lost the final three wickets while adding six. [Batting and wicket ledger](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/16714/pak-vs-ind-final-icc-champions-trophy-2017).

The recovery illustrates why a falling required rate is not the same as a recovered position. It moved from 8.09 per over at 72/6 to 7.96 at 152/7. The scoring had improved, but Hardik was now out and only three wickets remained. Those calculations describe the task; they are not a reconstructed win-probability model.

## A final decided in both innings

Amir finished with 3/16 and Hasan with 3/19; Shadab Khan took two wickets. India’s innings ended after 183 legal balls, leaving 117 unused. That unused time did not represent a rain-shortened chase: the side had been bowled out. [Complete bowling figures and result](https://cricketdatabase.com/matches/2017-06-18-india-v-pakistan).

Pakistan combined an opening platform, a productive finish and early wickets in the reply. Hardik’s attacking innings was substantial in isolation but could not erase the earlier losses. This is a dated account of that final, not evidence that the same teams, players or venue will behave similarly in their next meeting.
