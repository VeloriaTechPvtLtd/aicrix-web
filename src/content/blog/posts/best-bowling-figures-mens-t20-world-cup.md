**Ajantha Mendis’s 6/8 against Zimbabwe remains the best bowling return in a men’s T20 World Cup innings through the completed 2026 tournament.** Rangana Herath’s 5/3 is the least expensive five-wicket haul, but six wickets place Mendis ahead when ranking innings figures. [ICC historical record](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/game-changers-and-record-makers-statistical-standouts-from-the-icc-men-s-t20-world-cups), [record review updated 20 August 2026](https://www.sportsadda.asia/cricket/features/most-wickets-in-t20-world-cup/).

The table below includes every innings with **at least five wickets** in the published tournament record through 2026: 14 performances by 14 bowlers. It covers the men’s tournament proper, not qualifying competitions, warm-ups, the women’s event or other T20 internationals. A six-wicket performance belongs inside a five-or-more threshold.

## Why 6/8 ranks above 5/3

Bowling figures are written as wickets taken and runs conceded. Mendis took one more wicket than Herath, so his return leads this ranking even though Herath conceded fewer runs.

This is not an economy-rate ranking. Gul’s 5/6 in three overs and Mendis’s 6/8 in four overs both cost exactly two runs per over, but their wicket totals differ. Likewise, Shepherd and Mujeeb share 5/20 despite bowling different numbers of overs. A separate comparison of economy could distinguish them; an innings-figures table should not quietly switch its sorting rule.

## Overs are not decimal fractions

Herath’s 3.3 means three complete overs and three legal balls: **21 balls**, not 3.3 decimal overs. His calculated economy is 3 × 6 ÷ 21 = approximately **0.86 runs per over** when conventionally rounded to two decimal places. Some record displays show 0.85 through truncation.

Curran’s 3.4 similarly means 22 legal balls. For any derived rate, convert the overs notation to balls before dividing. The original figures remain 5/3 and 5/10 regardless of how a website formats a rounded rate.

## The two 2026 additions

Shepherd entered this group with 5/20 against Scotland in Kolkata on 7 February. Siddique followed with 5/35 against Canada in Delhi on 13 February. Both joined the threshold list, but neither displaced Mendis’s six-wicket record. [Updated tournament record](https://www.sportsadda.asia/cricket/features/most-wickets-in-t20-world-cup/), [ICC’s UAE match report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/live-australia-look-to-stay-unbeaten-at-t20-world-cup).

A later addition can therefore change the number of qualifying performances without changing the record holder. That is why both the cutoff and the threshold matter.

## A great spell does not guarantee a win

Malik’s 5/19 and Mustafizur’s 5/22 came in defeats. Their figures still qualify. Team results also depend on the other bowlers, batting and the target; a record table should not exclude a performance because the bowler’s side lost. [Match outcomes in the ledger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Men%27s_T20_World_Cup_five-wicket_hauls).

Nor is a single spectacular spell the same as sustained tournament output. Career wicket totals and one-edition wicket leaders answer different questions. Use this page for the best individual innings, then consult the appropriate career or season table for accumulated wickets.

## Use the threshold without changing the question

A five-wicket-haul list includes five or more wickets, so Mendis’s six-wicket spell appears once. It is not counted as both a five-wicket and a six-wicket performance. The fourteen rows here also happen to represent fourteen different bowlers; those are separate counts that would diverge if a bowler added another qualifying innings.

The two shared ranks mean the next place advances by the number of performances already listed. After two entries at seventh, the next rank is ninth, not eighth. This preserves equal treatment of identical figures without implying that the spells took place in identical conditions. Opponent, date and overs remain useful context, but none changes the stated wickets-first sorting rule.
