## The current women’s ODI measurement

A new ball under the ICC women’s ODI conditions effective October 2026 must weigh 142–149 grams and measure 21.5–22.0 centimetres in circumference. Both requirements apply. A correct weight does not compensate for an incorrect circumference. [ICC women’s ODI conditions, clause 4.1](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/qunpw1kfh3ibxlz58jlw.pdf)

The circumference is the distance around the ball, not its width from one side to the other. Confusing it with diameter would make a normal cricket ball appear much larger than it is.

## Three sizes in the MCC Laws

The 2026 MCC Laws describe three new-ball sizes. They also make the governing body responsible for choosing the size for a match; if no choice has been made, the umpires determine it through the specified pre-match process. A size table is therefore not a substitute for the competition’s conditions. [MCC Laws 4.1 and 4.6](https://www.lords.org/getmedia/a4ef9f77-2a25-4f5b-a286-4601f08e6334/Laws-of-Cricket-2017-Code-4th-Edition-%282026%29_5.pdf)

| MCC size | New-ball weight | New-ball circumference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Size 1 | 156–163 g | 22.4–22.9 cm |
| Size 2 | 142–149 g | 21.5–22.0 cm |
| Size 3 | 133–140 g | 20.5–21.0 cm |

The women’s ODI range matches Size 2. Do not automatically apply that international specification to every school, junior, mixed or domestic match merely because women participate; identify the governing conditions first.

## Test both dimensions rather than a product label

These fictional measurements illustrate the Size 2 limits. Assume exact readings for this arithmetic exercise, not a real equipment inspection.

| Example new ball | Weight | Circumference | Comparison with the Size 2 limits |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| A | 145 g | 21.8 cm | Within both ranges |
| B | 145 g | 22.2 cm | Weight within range; circumference too large |
| C | 150 g | 21.8 cm | Circumference within range; weight too high |

For A, the weight is 3 g above the minimum and 4 g below the maximum. Its circumference is 0.3 cm above the minimum and 0.2 cm below the maximum. B cannot pass the two-limit comparison merely because its weight matches A. These checks illustrate the published measurements, not complete approval of a manufactured ball.

A retail description such as “142 g” gives only one nominal number. It does not establish the actual ball’s weight, its circumference or its suitability for the particular competition. Check the applicable specification rather than treating a familiar label as a certificate.

## Circumference is not diameter

For a simplified spherical model, diameter equals circumference divided by pi. The Size 2 circumference endpoints correspond to approximately 6.84–7.00 cm in diameter. This conversion explains the geometry; it is not an extra official diameter rule or a substitute measurement procedure.

Likewise, 22.0 − 21.5 = 0.5 cm is the permitted circumference span, not half a centimetre of diameter variation. The corresponding idealised diameter span is about 0.16 cm.

## Which matches use the October change

The ICC’s [1 October 2026 announcement](https://www.icc-cricket.com/media-releases/new-icc-playing-conditions-bring-key-changes-across-formats) applies the changes to international series starting on or after that date. Series already underway retain the previous regulations. The announcement describes tighter tolerances and distinct specifications rather than a rule that every older ball was identical.

A hypothetical series beginning on 29 September therefore does not switch specifications simply because its next match falls in October. A series beginning on 2 October falls on the new side of the announced date test. Series start and individual match date are different fields.

The measurements answer an equipment question. They do not, by themselves, establish how much a delivery will swing, how far a shot will travel or how scoring will change. Ball allocation and replacement are separate rules, not consequences to infer from the Size 2 label.
