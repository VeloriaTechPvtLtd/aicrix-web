India beat Australia by five wickets in the **2025 Women’s ODI World Cup semifinal** at Navi Mumbai on 30 October. Australia made 338 all out; India reached 341 for five in 48.3 overs, with nine balls remaining. [ICC match report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/media-releases/awesome-rodrigues-leads-india-to-icc-women-s-cricket-world-cup-2025-final)

## Two large partnerships, different outcomes

Phoebe Litchfield’s 119 and Ellyse Perry’s 77 included a second-wicket stand of 155 for Australia. Ashleigh Gardner contributed 63 before late wickets ended the innings with one ball unused.

India lost both openers at 59 for two. Jemimah Rodrigues and Harmanpreet Kaur then added 167. Harmanpreet made 89; Rodrigues stayed to finish on 127 not out. Amanjot Kaur, not Rodrigues, scored the winning runs.

## The target stayed the same; the equation changed

The calculations below use the target of 339 and the [scorecard’s wicket positions](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/121670/icc-womens-world-cup-2025-2nd-semi-final).

| Checkpoint | Score | Needed | Balls left | Required runs per over |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Start | 0/0 | 339 | 300 | 6.78 |
| Mandhana out, 9.2 overs | 59/2 | 280 | 244 | 6.89 |
| Harmanpreet out, 35.2 | 226/3 | 113 | 88 | 7.70 |
| Deepti out, 40.5 | 264/4 | 75 | 55 | 8.18 |
| Richa out, end of 46th | 310/5 | 29 | 24 | 7.25 |

Overs notation is not decimal notation: 40.5 means 245 legal balls, leaving 55 in a 50-over chase. The required rate is therefore 75 ÷ 55 × 6, rounded to 8.18.

Between the fourth and fifth wickets India added 46 in 31 legal balls. The rate needed fell despite another dismissal because scoring had outpaced the requirement during that passage.

## A semifinal win, not the trophy itself

The ICC described the pursuit of 339 as the women’s ODI record chase at the time. That historical claim refers to the target, not India’s finishing total of 341. The extra runs came when the winning boundary took the score beyond the target.

Rodrigues faced 134 balls for her unbeaten innings. Her continuity was central, but the chase also depended on Harmanpreet’s partnership and the later contributions around her. The result secured a place in the final; it was not itself the championship match.
