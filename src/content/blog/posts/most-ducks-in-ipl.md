*Completed IPL seasons through 31 May 2026; sources rechecked 14 November 2026.*

Rohit Sharma and Glenn Maxwell jointly held the IPL career record with **19 ducks each** at the end of the 2026 season. Dinesh Karthik and Sunil Narine followed on 18. A duck is a dismissal without scoring, so the list counts zero-score dismissals rather than matches, balls faced or all innings ending on zero. [CricPrism’s record table, updated 31 May 2026](https://www.cricprism.com/cricket-records/ipl/most-ducks)

The joint lead matters. A website may put Maxwell first or display Rohit above him, but 19 is the same total. Row order cannot turn a shared record into a one-player lead. This is an IPL career comparison through the completed 2026 season, not the number of ducks in that season alone.

## The two record equalling innings were different

Maxwell reached 19 against Gujarat Titans at Ahmedabad on 25 March 2025. He was dismissed lbw by Sai Kishore for zero from one ball, a golden duck. [The scorecard](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/114987/indian-premier-league-2025-5th-match) establishes the dismissal; [Wisden’s contemporary report](https://www.wisden.com/series/ipl-2025/cricket-news/most-ipl-ducks-full-list-glenn-maxwell-overtakes-rohit-sharma-to-set-tournament-record) records him taking the outright career lead at the time.

Rohit joined him on 24 May 2026 against Rajasthan Royals at Wankhede. Jofra Archer had him caught by Dhruv Jurel for zero from four balls. That innings was a duck but not a golden duck. The [official IPL report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/tata-ipl-2026-match-69-mi-v-rr-match-report) confirms the early dismissal, the [scorecard](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/152252/indian-premier-league-2026-69th-match) supplies the four balls, and [the contemporary record report](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/sports/cricket/ipl/ipl-2026/rohit-sharma-equals-unwanted-all-time-ipl-record-after-horror-duck-vs-rr/articleshow/131294365.cms) confirms his nineteenth.

These examples explain why the most-ducks list is not automatically the most-golden-ducks list. One includes every zero-score dismissal; the other selects the first-ball subset. The career totals cannot reveal that subset on their own.

## Equal totals do not mean equal frequency

The end-of-season tables list Maxwell’s 19 ducks across 135 batting innings and Rohit’s 19 across 276. Dividing ducks by batting innings gives a descriptive frequency: **14.07% for Maxwell and 6.88% for Rohit**. The numerator is identical, while Rohit’s innings denominator is a little more than twice as large. [Published innings and duck totals](https://www.crictracker.com/most-ducks-in-ipl-history/)

That calculation does not rank their complete batting careers or predict their next innings. It also does not prove that technique, aggression or batting position caused the difference. It simply answers a narrower question than the cumulative list: in what share of their recorded batting innings did a zero-score dismissal occur?

Use batting innings rather than player appearances for this comparison. Someone can play without batting. Equally, zero not out is not a duck because no dismissal occurred. A hypothetical batter with two ducks, one zero not out and seven other batting innings has two ducks in ten innings, or 20%, not three in ten. Keep the counting rule visible.

## Career ducks are not captain ducks

A player who has captained a side does not have every career dismissal attributed to that role. In Rohit’s 24 May match, the scorecard identifies Hardik Pandya as Mumbai’s captain. That duck contributes to Rohit’s career total, but not to a table restricted to innings played as the designated captain.

A captain-only comparison therefore needs each batting innings joined to that match’s captain identity. The career leaderboard above cannot supply the answer by relabelling its heading. Start with the competition, cutoff and exact dismissal filter, then compare totals or frequencies. That keeps an eye-catching record useful without turning it into an unsupported verdict on batting quality.

## Use the record without losing its context

A cumulative count grows only when another qualifying dismissal occurs. The innings denominator also grows when a player bats successfully, so the percentage can move even when the duck total remains unchanged. That is why the date belongs with both inputs. Comparing an older count with a newer innings total would no longer describe the same snapshot.

The table gives a complete answer to the stated threshold, not a full ranking of batting quality. Maxwell and Rohit can share the count record while differing in innings played, batting roles and many other aspects of their careers. None of those differences removes the tie. Use the count to answer the record question and the carefully labelled frequency to answer the exposure question; neither needs to become a prediction.
