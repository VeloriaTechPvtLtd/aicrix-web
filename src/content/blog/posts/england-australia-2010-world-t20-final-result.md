## England won with three overs remaining

England beat Australia by seven wickets in the men’s World T20 final at Kensington Oval, Bridgetown, on 16 May 2010. Australia made 147/6; England reached 148/3 in 17 overs. It was the England men’s team’s first ICC title, with a second-wicket partnership between Craig Kieswetter and Kevin Pietersen central to the chase. [ICC retrospective](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/major-rivalries-england-v-australia-at-icc-events-x7776).

That finish left 18 legal deliveries unused. The target was 148, rather than 147: matching Australia’s total would only have levelled the scores. England’s winning margin describes wickets remaining, not the one-run difference between the innings totals.

## The two innings

| Team | Total | Overs | Leading scorer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Australia | 147/6 | 20 | David Hussey 59 from 54 |
| England | 148/3 | 17 | Craig Kieswetter 63 from 49 |

Ryan Sidebottom dismissed Shane Watson and Brad Haddin, while David Warner was run out. Australia were 8/3 after 2.1 overs, so three of their first four batters had gone within 13 legal deliveries. [Scorecard and match details](https://sports.ndtv.com/cricket/eng-vs-aus-scorecard-live-cricket-score-icc-world-twenty20-2010-final-enau160520107620).

## Australia rebuilt but could not erase the opening damage

Michael Clarke made 27 and Cameron White 30. David Hussey remained until the final over, and Michael Hussey finished unbeaten on 17 from ten balls. The recovery took Australia from 8/3 to a completed 147/6: 139 additional runs for three more wickets, rather than a continuing collapse. [Batting ledger](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/3272/aus-vs-eng-final-icc-world-t20-2010).

The wicket checkpoints show that the recovery itself changed pace. From Clarke’s dismissal at 45/4 after 9.2 overs to White’s at 95/5 after 15.4, Australia added 50 in 38 legal balls, about 7.89 runs per six balls. They then added 52 in the remaining 26, a rate of 12.00. That late acceleration explains why the final total was much healthier than the early score, without proving that it was sufficient.

Graeme Swann’s four overs cost 17 and brought Clarke’s wicket. Sidebottom finished with 2/26; Luke Wright took 1/5 in his single over. Australia lost six wickets, but England’s bowlers received credit for four because two dismissals were run outs. [Bowling and dismissal ledger](https://sports.ndtv.com/cricket/eng-vs-aus-scorecard-live-cricket-score-icc-world-twenty20-2010-final-enau160520107620).

## The stand that reduced the chase

Michael Lumb fell with England at 7/1. Kieswetter and Pietersen then added 111 before Pietersen was dismissed for 47 at 118/2. Kieswetter followed for 63 at 121/3. The partnership, not simply either batter’s individual total, was the decisive bridge between the early wicket and the short finish. [ICC account](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/major-rivalries-england-v-australia-at-icc-events-x7776) and [fall of wickets](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/3272/aus-vs-eng-final-icc-world-t20-2010).

| England checkpoint | Runs still needed | Legal balls remaining | Required runs per six balls |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 7/1 after 1.5 overs | 141 | 109 | 7.76 |
| 118/2 after 13.1 overs | 30 | 41 | 4.39 |
| 121/3 after 14.1 overs | 27 | 35 | 4.63 |

These are recalculated requirements, not win probabilities. Losing both established batters increased the pressure on the new pair, but it did not restore the original scoring burden. At 121/3, England still had more legal balls available than runs required.

## A completed chase rather than a last over escape

Paul Collingwood finished on 12 not out and Eoin Morgan on 15 not out. Their unbeaten stand supplied the remaining 27 runs. Kieswetter received the player-of-the-match award; Pietersen was player of the series. Those awards refer to different scopes, even though both players shaped the same final. [Final scorecard](https://sports.ndtv.com/cricket/eng-vs-aus-scorecard-live-cricket-score-icc-world-twenty20-2010-final-enau160520107620).

The useful comparison is between Australia’s need to rebuild after losing three wickets and England’s ability to lower the required rate through one long stand. It is not evidence that batting second always wins at Barbados, nor that a future target of 148 would present the same conditions or difficulty.
