New Zealand beat Pakistan by five wickets at the Wanderers on 3 October 2009. Grant Elliott finished the chase unbeaten, while Daniel Vettori earned the player-of-the-match award for contributing in both innings.

## Pakistan’s two recovery stands

Pakistan reached 233 for nine. Mohammad Yousuf and Umar Akmal added 80 after the fourth wicket fell at 86. Later, Mohammad Aamer and Saeed Ajmal added an unbeaten 35 for the last wicket, extending the total from 198. NZC’s contemporary report describes both recoveries. [NZC match report](https://www.nzc.nz/match-reports/archive/blackcaps-through-to-final/)

Ian Butler took four for 44 and Vettori three for 43. Pakistan’s late runs meant the chase was for 234, not the much smaller total that had seemed possible when the ninth wicket fell.

## The fifth wicket joined the middle to the finish

New Zealand lost three wickets for 71. Ross Taylor and Elliott added 55 before Taylor departed for 38 at 126 for four. Vettori then joined Elliott for a stand of 104.

Vettori was stumped for 41 with the total at 230. Elliott remained on 75 not out as New Zealand reached 234 for five in 47.5 overs. [NZC archive scorecard](https://archive.nzc.nz/Scorecards/251/251357.html)

The arithmetic makes the partnership’s role clear. It began with 108 still required and ended with four needed. It was the bridge to victory, even though it was not the final partnership.

## Why Vettori’s award fits the whole match

The award need not diminish Elliott’s unbeaten innings or Butler’s four wickets. Vettori supplied a different combination: three wickets in Pakistan’s innings and 41 runs during New Zealand’s decisive stand.

NZC’s report says Vettori was trying to finish with a boundary when he was dismissed. That reported intention belongs to the contemporary account; the scorecard alone establishes only the stumping and the remaining target.

Reading both innings together gives the fuller explanation. New Zealand first restricted Pakistan, then rebuilt through consecutive partnerships, and finally completed the small task left after Vettori’s dismissal.
