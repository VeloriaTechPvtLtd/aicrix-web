## Women’s Ashes 2027 schedule and points

The women’s Ashes in England is scheduled to run from **24 June to 20 July 2027**. It combines one Test, three T20Is and three ODIs into a single points contest. A Test win is worth four points; a win in each white-ball match is worth two. [Cricket Australia’s series announcement](https://www.cricket.com.au/news/4541972)

The Headingley Test is scheduled for four days, 24–27 June. That is different from the five-day women’s Ashes Test played in England in 2023. The number of scheduled days and the number of points are separate features of the competition.

## Why one Test changes the arithmetic

In a scenario where every match produces a winner and the announced win awards apply, the series distributes **16 points**: four for the Test and twelve across the six white-ball games. The Test therefore represents a quarter of that total and the same points as two white-ball wins.

Consider a hypothetical team that wins the Test. Its final score depends on how many of the remaining six matches it wins:

| White-ball wins by the Test winner | Its total points | Opponent’s total points |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| One | 6 | 10 |
| Two | 8 | 8 |
| Three | 10 | 6 |
| Four | 12 | 4 |

The team winning two white-ball games after the Test would win three of the seven fixtures but finish level on points with an opponent winning four. Counting matches won without their weights gives the wrong series picture.

Conversely, losing the Test would not end a side’s chance of finishing ahead. In this decisive-results scenario, it would need five of the six white-ball wins to reach ten points, while the Test winner would finish on six. The example compares points only; it does not settle trophy retention or rules for a drawn, tied or abandoned match.

## England’s preparation has changed

England’s newly announced plan includes three intra-squad red-ball matches and a three-day match against Australia A before Headingley. Its April West Indies tour is now planned as three T20Is and three ODIs only, rather than including the previously anticipated Test. [ECB red-ball preparation update](https://www.ecb.co.uk/news/4607824/england-women-announce-redball-plans-ahead-of-2027-metro-bank-womens-ashes)

These preparation fixtures are not additional Ashes Tests and do not add points to the seven-match contest. Their practical relevance is that an older calendar containing a Caribbean Test no longer describes the announced preparation programme. The revised plan does not identify a final Ashes XI or guarantee that a particular player will appear.

## Follow the whole contest rather than one format

A team can lose a format-level series yet remain competitive in the combined points race. Keep a running total with the correct award attached to each result instead of restarting the score when the T20Is or ODIs begin.

The fixtures above are the women’s competition, not the concurrent men’s five-Test Ashes. Refresh official dates, playing conditions and any changed start before using the schedule. It is checked through 14 November 2026; ticket availability and India broadcast access require their own current confirmation.

## Keep a separate result and points ledger

For each fixture, preserve the format, scheduled date, actual result and applicable points award in separate columns. Before any games have been played, leave the result and points-earned fields empty rather than filling them with the decisive-result examples above. An announced fixture is not a completed match, and a planning scenario is not a prediction.

If a match does not produce a winner, do not force it into the two-point or four-point win calculation. Check the edition-specific conditions first and then update the ledger. The same caution applies to deciding who keeps the trophy after a level points total: the worked 8–8 row establishes equality in that scenario, not the separate retention rule.
