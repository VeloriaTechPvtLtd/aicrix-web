## Rohit made 283 runs in nine IPL 2026 innings

Rohit Sharma finished IPL 2026 with **283 runs from 180 balls across nine batting innings** for Mumbai Indians. His highest score was 84 against Lucknow Super Giants, and his other fifty was 78 against Kolkata Knight Riders. Together, those two innings supplied more than half his season runs. [Season ledger](https://iplrecords.com/players/ipl-2026/rohit-sharma) · [Cricbuzz team statistics](https://m.cricbuzz.com/cricket-team/mumbai-indians/62/stats).

The nine innings contain **eight dismissals**, not nine. The 12 April scorecard records 19 from 13 balls as retired hurt. Keeping that scoring status intact gives an average of **35.38**, calculated as 283 divided by eight. The aggregate strike rate is **157.22**, using all 180 balls faced. This is a scoring-status distinction, not a medical assessment. [12 April batting card](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/149812/mi-vs-rcb-20th-match-ipl-2026).

## Two innings carried most of the scoring

The 78 against KKR and 84 against LSG total **162 runs from 82 balls**. That is **57.24% of Rohit’s runs from 45.56% of his balls faced**. Their combined strike rate was 197.56. The other seven innings supplied 121 from 98 balls, at 123.47.

It is deliberately selected after the results, so it is not a method for predicting which future innings will be productive. Removing the low scores would overstate the record just as surely as ignoring the two fifties would understate it.

Both fifties contributed to successful chases at Wankhede. The 78 from 38 against KKR was part of a 148-run opening stand with Ryan Rickelton. Rohit’s individual 78 and the partnership’s 148 are different quantities; the latter includes his partner’s contribution and any partnership extras. ## Average and runs per innings are different here

Dividing 283 by all nine innings gives **31.44 runs per innings**. Dividing by the eight dismissals gives the **35.38 batting average**. Neither calculation changes the run total; they answer different questions. The retirement entry is the reason their denominators differ in this season.

The final duck illustrates another distinction. Before the 24 May innings, the same 283 runs came from 176 balls and seven dismissals. Four additional balls and one dismissal changed the strike rate from 160.80 to 157.22 and the average from 40.43 to 35.38, even though the runs stayed unchanged. These are calculations using consistent rounding, not copied interim headline rates. [Final-match entry](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/152252/mi-vs-rr-69th-match-indian-premier-league-2026).

## Keep the opportunities in context

The 7 April game against Rajasthan was limited to 11 overs per side. Rohit’s six-ball innings therefore occurred in a different maximum team allocation from an ordinary 20-over match. That does not excuse or revalue the score; it prevents a false assumption that every fixture offered the same potential innings length. [Official MI fixture record](https://www.mumbaiindians.com/fixtures).

The nine scorecards record Rohit among the opening pair, but an opening position is not a promise of a long stay. His recorded innings lasted between four and 44 balls. Those are the actual balls faced, not a forecast based on his place in the order.
