South Africa beat Australia by one wicket at Johannesburg on 12 March 2006, reaching 438/9 with one ball remaining after Australia made 434/4. The fifth ODI decided the series 3–2 in South Africa’s favour. The match remembered as the 438 chase actually began with a winning target of 435. [Contemporary Reuters report carried by ABC](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2006-03-13/proteas-claim-series-with-stunning-chase/816426)

The [historical scorecard](https://img.cricinfo.com/db/ARCHIVE/2005-06/AUS_IN_RSA/SCORECARDS/AUS_RSA_ODI5_12MAR2006.html) distinguishes the target from the final score. South Africa did not need 438: Mark Boucher’s winning four carried the total beyond the minimum required. Nor was this a nine-wicket win. Nine wickets had fallen, leaving one wicket in hand.

## Ponting and Hussey made the last twenty overs expensive

Ricky Ponting scored 164 from 105 balls, Michael Hussey 81 from 51 and Simon Katich 79 from 90. Adam Gilchrist’s 55 completed four scores above fifty. Ponting and Hussey added 158 for the third wicket, between 216/2 and 374/3. [Full batting and wicket ledger](https://img.cricinfo.com/db/ARCHIVE/2005-06/AUS_IN_RSA/SCORECARDS/AUS_RSA_ODI5_12MAR2006.html)

Australia were 209/1 after 30 overs and finished on 434/4. That is 225 in the final twenty overs, at 11.25 runs per over, compared with 6.97 across the first thirty. The acceleration mattered because the chase had to match the completed total, not Australia’s earlier pace. [Contemporary delivery record](https://img.cricinfo.com/db/ARCHIVE/2005-06/AUS_IN_RSA/SCORECARDS/AUS_RSA_ODI5_12MAR2006_BBB-COMMS.html)

That starting requirement was 8.70 per over. It was a scoring equation, not a forecast: South Africa could remain ahead of Australia’s score at an intermediate point and still face a demanding finish.

## Gibbs and Smith reduced the rate but did not finish the chase

After Boeta Dippenaar fell at 3/1, Graeme Smith and Herschelle Gibbs added 187. Smith made 90 from 55 balls; Gibbs continued to 175 from 111 before falling at 299/4 in the 32nd over. [Complete South African innings](https://img.cricinfo.com/db/ARCHIVE/2005-06/AUS_IN_RSA/SCORECARDS/AUS_RSA_ODI5_12MAR2006.html)

Gibbs’s dismissal at 31.5 left 136 required from 109 scheduled balls, or 7.49 per over. The original asking rate had fallen, but six wickets still had to carry a large remaining target. Gibbs’s 175 was almost 40% of the eventual team total, not the whole chase.

By Justin Kemp’s dismissal at 355/6 after 42.1, the requirement had climbed to 80 from 47 balls, or 10.21 per over. Between those two wickets, South Africa added 56 while using 62 legal balls. The changing equation captures the interruption more precisely than describing the innings as one uninterrupted charge. [Reconciled wicket checkpoints](https://img.cricinfo.com/db/ARCHIVE/2005-06/AUS_IN_RSA/SCORECARDS/AUS_RSA_ODI5_12MAR2006.html)

## The lower order supplied more than survival

Johan van der Wath made 35 from 18 balls and Roger Telemachus 12 from six. Their partnerships with Boucher added 44 and 24 respectively. Together those stands moved the total from 355/6 to 423/8, cutting 68 runs from the remaining chase. [Batting records](https://img.cricinfo.com/db/ARCHIVE/2005-06/AUS_IN_RSA/SCORECARDS/AUS_RSA_ODI5_12MAR2006.html)

Nathan Bracken nevertheless finished with 5/67 from ten overs. Mick Lewis conceded 113 without a wicket in ten; Brett Lee took 1/68 from 7.5. These are separate bowling returns, not evidence that every Australian spell had the same outcome. Lee’s 7.5 means 47 legal balls, giving an economy rate of 8.68, not a calculation using 7.5 decimal overs. [Bowling ledger](https://img.cricinfo.com/db/ARCHIVE/2005-06/AUS_IN_RSA/SCORECARDS/AUS_RSA_ODI5_12MAR2006.html)

## How seven needed became a winning boundary

The final over began at 428/8, seven short of the target. Cricket Australia’s retrospective includes Boucher’s and Makhaya Ntini’s recollections of the finish: Hall’s boundary, his dismissal, Ntini getting the strike back to Boucher, and the winning shot. The [archived final-over sequence](https://img.cricinfo.com/db/ARCHIVE/2005-06/AUS_IN_RSA/SCORECARDS/AUS_RSA_ODI5_12MAR2006_BBB-COMMS.html) records ten runs from five balls. Ntini’s single levelled the scores but did not win the game; Boucher’s boundary did. Boucher finished on 50 not out from 43 balls and Ntini on one not out from one.

Australia’s total and South Africa’s response produced 872 runs. Yet the finish still depended on one remaining wicket and one unused delivery. The useful distinction is between setting up the chase, maintaining its required pace and completing it: the centuries shaped the match, while the last pair settled its result.
