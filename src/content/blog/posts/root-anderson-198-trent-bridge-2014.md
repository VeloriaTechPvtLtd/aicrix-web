Joe Root and James Anderson added 198 for England’s tenth wicket against India at Trent Bridge in July 2014. Root finished unbeaten on 154 and Anderson made 81. The ICC described the partnership as a world record at the time. England nevertheless did not win the Test: it ended in a draw. [ICC’s fourth-day report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/england-ahead-after-root-anderson-heroics), [BCCI’s final-day report](https://www.bcci.tv/news/article/report-england-vs-india-1st-test-day-5)

## From a deficit to a lead

India had made 457. England eventually reached 496, a lead of 39. Subtracting the last-wicket partnership places England at 298/9 when it began: 496 − 198 = 298.

That means the partnership first erased a **159-run deficit**, then created the 39-run lead. The two parts reconcile exactly: 159 + 39 = 198. [Contemporary innings totals](https://www.bcci.tv/news/article/report-england-vs-india-1st-test-day-5)

## The overnight score is not the partnership’s starting point

England resumed the fourth day at 352/9. They added 144 that day before being dismissed, but the partnership was already underway the previous evening. Confusing the overnight total with the fall of the ninth wicket would incorrectly reduce the stand from 198 to 144. [Day-by-day scores](https://www.bcci.tv/news/article/report-england-vs-india-1st-test-day-5)

Anderson’s 81 also was not the whole partnership. Runs added by both partners, plus any extras during their time together, contribute to a stand. Root’s final 154 likewise includes runs scored before Anderson arrived.

## Why England’s advantage did not become a victory

India slipped to 184/6 in the second innings before Stuart Binny made 78. His stands of 65 with Ravindra Jadeja and 91 with Bhuvneshwar Kumar helped them recover to 391/9 declared. Bhuvneshwar finished unbeaten on 63, and the teams ended the match without an England chase. [BCCI final-day report](https://www.bcci.tv/news/article/report-england-vs-india-1st-test-day-5)

India’s final lead was 352: 457 + 391 − 496. That is an aggregate lead at the close, not a victory margin. The distinction preserves both parts of the match: England’s extraordinary last-wicket recovery and India’s subsequent escape from a dangerous position.
