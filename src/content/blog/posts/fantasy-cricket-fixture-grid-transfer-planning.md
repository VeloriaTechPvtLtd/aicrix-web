A fantasy cricket fixture grid answers a narrower question than a player ranking: which teams have scheduled matches inside the period your game counts? That is useful for season-long squad management, but it does not tell you who will play every match or how many points they will score.

Read the grid in three steps: identify its time boundary, count the actual fixtures, then apply your game’s transfer and scoring rules. Skipping the first step can make a perfectly accurate schedule misleading.

## A rolling window is not a fantasy round

A seven-day view moves with its starting date. A fantasy round is a set of fixtures defined by the game operator. It may cover an irregular period rather than Monday through Sunday. A real-world double-header describes two matches on one day; it does not necessarily mean your selected team has two games in the same fantasy round.

A seven-day view, a fourteen-day view and a next-three-fixtures view are different filters. A player can have one fixture inside a chosen window while their next three fixtures extend beyond it.

For any real game, use the operator’s dated round allocation and lock rules. Do not reconstruct them merely by dividing the number of league matches by the number of rounds.

## Work through a complete miniature grid

This is an invented three-team competition. Round 1 contains A versus B and A versus C. Round 2 contains B versus C. Assume the fictional game counts both fixtures in a round and allows a transfer between rounds.

| Team | Round 1 fixtures | Round 2 fixtures | Total across both rounds |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| A | 2 | 0 | 2 |
| B | 1 | 1 | 2 |
| C | 1 | 1 | 2 |

A has a double followed by a bye. B and C have one match in each round. All three have two matches overall, so the busiest team in the first round is not the busiest across the whole horizon.

A reader considering a player from A should therefore check the next round before using a limited transfer. The grid exposes a scheduling trade-off; it does not settle the selection. Role, eligibility, budget and the cost of changing the squad still matter.

There is also a useful consistency check: the column totals are four and two team appearances, six altogether. Each match contains two teams, so six appearances correspond to three fixtures. This catches a missing or duplicated team entry without pretending to validate the fantasy game’s rules.

## Keep the underlying dates visible

A dated real example shows why the filter matters. [Adelaide Strikers’ official schedule announcement of 2 July 2026](https://www.adelaidestrikers.com.au/news/4529687/adelaide-oval-to-headline-adelaide-strikers-wbbl12-campaign) listed matches against Hobart on 10 November, Perth on 14 November, the Sixers on 17 November, the Renegades on 20 November and Brisbane on 22 November.

Using that announcement as a fixed schedule example, the inclusive date window 9–15 November contains two listed fixtures. The inclusive window 9–22 November contains five. These counts describe the announced dates, not a fresh claim that every game was completed or that every player appeared. A current planner must reconcile postponements and results before reusing them.

The same announcement labels Adelaide’s 5 November match at Drummoyne Oval in Sydney as a home fixture. Home designation and ground location therefore need separate fields; a home colour does not prove the match is at the club’s usual venue.

## Team opportunities are not player guarantees

Two scheduled games offer two possible appearances, not two confirmed selections. A squad member can miss either XI; a selected batter may not bat; a bowler may not complete a full allocation. Multiplying a historical points average by the fixture count creates an assumption, not a verified forecast.

Finals need similar care. Show an unassigned qualifying slot as conditional until the participant is known. Do not give every club the same guaranteed extra game, or count both an unresolved placeholder and the fixture that later replaces it.

## Check the game before making a change

Before using the grid, verify the season and timezone, exact lock time, which fixtures belong to each scoring period, and what happens to postponed games. Then check transfer allowances, reversals, squad restrictions and whether multiple-match points are added or treated differently.

Keep the fixture ledger and the game’s rules beside each other. An answer from a different game or an earlier season may use different round boundaries or transfer allowances.
