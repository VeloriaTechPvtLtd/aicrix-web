**India beat New Zealand by 96 runs in the men’s T20 World Cup 2026 final on 8 March at Narendra Modi Stadium, Ahmedabad.** India made **255/5 in 20 overs**, and New Zealand were dismissed for **159 in 19 overs**. This is the completed World Cup final, not a match from the teams’ later bilateral series. [Official result](https://archive.blackcaps.co.nz/Scorecards/1427/1427211.html).

## Final score and leading performances

| Team | Score | Leading batting contributions |
|---|---|---|
| India | 255/5 in 20 overs | Sanju Samson 89 from 46; Ishan Kishan 54 from 25; Abhishek Sharma 52 from 21 |
| New Zealand | 159 all out in 19 overs | Tim Seifert 52 from 26; Mitchell Santner 43 from 35 |

Jasprit Bumrah took **4/15** and was Player of the Match. Axar Patel added **3/27**. James Neesham was New Zealand’s leading wicket-taker with **3/46**. The [NZC-hosted scorecard](https://archive.blackcaps.co.nz/Scorecards/1427/1427211.html) records the individual innings and bowling figures.

New Zealand’s target was **256**, one more than India’s total. The 96-run winning margin is 255 minus 159; subtracting New Zealand’s score from the target instead would wrongly produce 97.

## India’s top three supplied most of the runs

Samson, Abhishek and Kishan contributed **195 runs between them**, or **76.5% of India’s total**. All three passed fifty, so the innings did not depend on one batter carrying a slow-scoring top order.

Samson and Abhishek added 98 for the first wicket. After Abhishek’s dismissal, Samson and Kishan added another 105. The two partnerships took India beyond 200 before they lost a second wicket. Their different tempos mattered: Abhishek’s 52 used 21 balls, Kishan’s 54 used 25, and Samson’s longer innings supplied 89 from 46. [Batting and partnership scorecard](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/139489/ind-vs-nz-final-icc-mens-t20-world-cup-2026).

India hit **19 fours and 18 sixes**. That is 184 boundary runs, calculated as 19 × 4 + 18 × 6, or **72.2% of 255**. Boundaries explain a large part of the scoring without needing to assume that every delivery was attacked successfully. [ICC match report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/mens-t20-world-cup-2026/news/live-india-new-zealand-chase-world-cup-glory).

## The powerplay comparison shows the early gap

After six overs, India were **92/0**, while New Zealand reached the same point at **52/3**. The difference was therefore 40 runs and three wickets, not just a slower scoring rate.

New Zealand still needed **204 from 84 balls**, an asking rate of **14.57 runs per over**. Losing early wickets while that rate climbed reduced the room for a rebuilding partnership. [Over-by-over comparison](https://sports.ndtv.com/cricket/ind-nz-over-comparison/ind-vs-nz-final-ahmedabad-innz03082026268129).

The comparison is more useful than looking at 52 runs in isolation. In another match, 52 after six overs could leave a manageable chase. Here, the target and wickets already lost made the same score a much more difficult position.

## Neesham’s three wickets slowed India briefly

India were **203/1 after 15 overs**. Neesham then dismissed Samson on the first ball of the 16th over, Kishan on the fifth and Suryakumar Yadav on the sixth. India finished that over at **204/4**: three wickets for one run. [Wicket sequence](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/139489/ind-vs-nz-final-icc-mens-t20-world-cup-2026).

That passage interrupted the acceleration, but India still had four overs remaining. They added 51 more runs, including **24 in the last over**, to move from 204/4 to 255/5. The late recovery is why the three-wicket over and the eventual total tell different parts of the innings. [Innings progression](https://sports.ndtv.com/cricket/ind-nz-over-comparison/ind-vs-nz-final-ahmedabad-innz03082026268129).

Shivam Dube finished unbeaten on **26 from eight balls**, with three fours and two sixes. His brief innings supplied a further burst after the top-order partnership had ended. [ICC account of the finish](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/mens-t20-world-cup-2026/news/live-india-new-zealand-chase-world-cup-glory).

## Bumrah and Axar took seven wickets between them

Bumrah and Axar combined for **seven wickets for 42 runs in seven overs**. Their combined economy was **6.00**, calculated from the total runs conceded divided by their combined overs. Bumrah’s individual economy was **3.75**, from 15 runs in four overs. [Bowling figures](https://archive.blackcaps.co.nz/Scorecards/1427/1427211.html).

Bumrah removed Rachin Ravindra early, then bowled Neesham, Matt Henry and Santner later in the chase. Axar dismissed Finn Allen, Glenn Phillips and Daryl Mitchell. Seifert’s fifty provided New Zealand’s strongest top-order resistance, but his dismissal left the chase without its fastest substantial innings. [Match account and dismissals](https://www.cricket.com.au/news/4464600/india-new-zealand-mens-t20-world-cup-final-2026-report-scores-highlights).

The contrast with India’s innings is clear: India’s first two partnerships both approached or exceeded 100, while New Zealand had already lost five wickets at 72. The latter innings required both rapid scoring and wickets in hand; it could not sustain both.

## Player of the Match and Player of the Tournament

**Bumrah won the final’s Player of the Match award. Samson won Player of the Tournament.** They are separate awards covering different performances.

Samson finished the competition with **321 runs from five innings**, including his 89 in the final. His tournament award should not be confused with the award for the title match. [ICC tournament-award announcement](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/mens-t20-world-cup-2026/news/sanju-samson-adjudged-t20-world-cup-player-of-the-tournament).

The ICC also recognised Samson’s 89 as the highest individual score in a men’s T20 World Cup final, and confirmed Bumrah’s final award in its tournament-team announcement. [ICC awards review](https://www.icc-cricket.com/media-releases/samson-and-bumrah-headline-icc-men-s-t20-world-cup-2026-team-of-the-tournament).

## What the result meant

India became the first men’s team to win three T20 World Cup titles, retain the trophy and win the competition at home. Their **255/5 was the highest total in a men’s T20 World Cup final**. That final-specific record should not be described as the highest score in every T20 World Cup match. [ICC confirmation](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/mens-t20-world-cup-2026/news/live-india-new-zealand-chase-world-cup-glory).

For match analysis, the decisive combination was the top order’s sustained scoring and the wickets taken during the chase. The 96-run margin summarises the result; the partnerships, powerplay positions and bowling figures explain how the two innings diverged.
