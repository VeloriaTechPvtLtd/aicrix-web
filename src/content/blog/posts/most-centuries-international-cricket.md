Sachin Tendulkar leads men’s international cricket with 100 centuries: 51 in Tests and 49 in ODIs. Six batters have reached at least 60 hundreds across recognised Tests, ODIs and T20Is as checked on 14 November 2026.

Virat Kohli is second with 86, followed by Ricky Ponting on 71, Kumar Sangakkara on 63, Jacques Kallis on 62 and Joe Root on 61. The format split matters: these are not six versions of the same batting career.

Each total is the sum of the three format columns. Hashim Amla, next in the overall list, has 55, so he does not qualify for this threshold. The [complete international-century list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cricketers_by_number_of_international_centuries_scored) provides the ranking boundary; individual career records corroborate the six splits.

Recognised international appearances for representative teams are included where applicable. This is not a national-team-only filter, nor a count of all senior cricket hundreds.

## Why Kohli can lead ODIs but remain second overall

Kohli has six more ODI hundreds than Tendulkar, 55 against 49. Tendulkar has 21 more Test hundreds, 51 against 30. Kohli’s single T20I hundred reduces the resulting combined gap to 14.

That arithmetic explains the apparently conflicting headlines: the ODI leader and combined international leader are different players. Kohli needs 14 further international centuries to equal 100 and 15 to pass it. Those are numerical targets, not a prediction that he will reach either.

The [BCCI’s historical Tendulkar record](https://www.bcci.tv/news/article/sachin-tendulkar-in-numbers) confirms his 100 and its 51-plus-49 composition. Kohli’s [current career ledger](https://www.cricbuzz.com/profiles/1413/virat-kohli) supplies the updated 30-plus-55-plus-one total.

## Similar totals can have different format foundations

Tests account for 45 of Kallis’s 62 hundreds, or 72.58%. For Root, the Test share is 41 of 61, or 67.21%. Kohli’s largest contribution instead comes from ODIs: 55 of 86, or 63.95%.

These percentages describe the composition of each total, not the chance of making a century in an innings. A century frequency requires an innings denominator, and a fair cross-format comparison would also need to account for different opportunities and match lengths.

Root and Ponting illustrate the distinction clearly. They have the same 41 Test centuries, yet Ponting’s combined total is ten higher because he has 30 ODI hundreds to Root’s 20. Neither has a T20I century. The combined table is therefore useful alongside, rather than instead of, separate format records.

## Do not add overlapping cricket categories

An ODI century also belongs in a player’s List A record. Adding List A hundreds to ODI hundreds would count some innings twice. Similarly, a Test hundred is already within the broader first-class category.

Kohli’s September 2026 hundred was reported as both his 55th ODI century and his 60th List A century. Those are two classifications of the same innings, not two additions to the international total. [Wisden’s explanation and List A table](https://www.wisden.com/series/india-vs-west-indies-m-2026/cricket-news/catching-up-to-sachin-virat-kohli-equals-tendulkars-world-record-after-breathtaking-ton-in-west-indies-series-opener).

Franchise T20 hundreds are excluded, as are domestic first-class and domestic one-day hundreds. A double-century counts as one century-scoring innings, not two.

## Current totals need a completed-innings cutoff

The career ledgers were rechecked on **14 November 2026**. Kohli’s latest completed international batting innings was his duck against New Zealand on 13 November. Root made 17 from 36 balls against Australia in Perth on the same date. Neither innings added a hundred, so their totals remain 86 and 61 respectively. [ICC India–New Zealand report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/young-henry-star-as-new-zealand-keep-india-odi-series-alive), [Root’s dated ledger](https://www.cricbuzz.com/profiles/8019/joe-root/all-matches/batting).

Update the relevant format column first when a new hundred occurs, then sum the total again. A match that is merely scheduled cannot add a century, and an appearance without batting does not establish a new scoring opportunity. The check date is not a prediction of either player’s eventual career total.
