## Mary Duggan leads the women’s Test wicket list

Mary Duggan has the most wickets in women’s Test cricket, with 77. Betty Wilson follows on 68 and Diana Edulji on 63. The career records checked on 14 November 2026 contain ten bowlers with at least 50 wickets.

This is a Test-only career list. It adds wickets across a player’s Test appearances; it does not combine ODI and T20I wickets, include domestic matches or rank bowlers by their best single innings. The retrieval date is not an independently established last-match cutoff for the ACS table.

The ranking here uses wickets alone. No hidden tie-breaker is applied to Maclagan, Kulkarni or Fitzpatrick. All three have 60 and therefore share fourth place; the next position is seventh. Different display orders among those three do not establish that one took more wickets.

## Why the 50-wicket boundary matters

“At least 50” includes Bakewell’s exact total of 50. A table labelled “more than 50” would exclude her and contain only nine players. The headline and the selection rule must agree.

The next two entries in both checked ledgers are Debbie Wilson and Sophie Ecclestone, each on 48. Neither meets this page’s threshold. Listing the boundary is a check against accidentally presenting ten selected famous names as an exhaustive record table.

For an active player on 48, two further wickets would reach 50 and one would reach 49. That arithmetic does not predict when, or whether, the milestone will occur. The table must be refreshed after subsequent Tests before being described as current at a later date.

## The India record is Edulji’s 63

Edulji is the highest Indian entry in this Test-specific list, ahead of Kulkarni’s 60. The ICC’s Hall of Fame account independently records Edulji’s 20 Tests and 63 wickets, with 6/64 as her best innings. [ICC profile and career summary](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/icc-hall-of-fame-welcomes-de-silva-edulji-and-sehwag-as-class-of-2023-inductees)

A familiar name from an ODI record should not automatically be placed at the top of a Test table. The format filter changes the matches included. Searching for “most wickets in women’s cricket” without specifying the format can therefore return several different, individually valid leaders.

## Career wickets and best innings answer different questions

Duggan’s 77 is her cumulative career total. Her 7/6 means seven wickets for six runs in one innings. Those figures belong in separate columns because they describe different achievements.

The table shows why: Fitzpatrick and Maclagan both have 60 career wickets, but their best innings are 5/29 and 7/10 respectively. Choosing an innings-performance ranking would produce a different comparison. It would not change their tie in total career wickets.

Likewise, a ten-wicket match aggregate can combine wickets from two innings. Do not substitute that aggregate into the best-innings column. For a player comparison, decide first whether the question concerns longevity, frequency, cost per wicket or a peak performance.

## What the record can and cannot establish

The ten qualifying bowlers represent four teams: England has four entries, Australia three, India two and New Zealand one. This is a count of players meeting this particular threshold, not a ranking of national teams or a measurement of their current attacks.

Bakewell is also a useful reminder that bowling records and all-round contributions can coexist. The ICC records her 50 wickets alongside 1,078 runs from 12 Tests. That does not require mixing her runs into the wicket ranking; it supplies context for the player at the inclusive boundary. [ICC Hall of Fame biography](https://www.icc-cricket.com/hall-of-fame/hall-of-famers/hall-of-famer-enid-bakewell?appview=true)

Use this list to answer a historical career question. For a future match or fantasy selection, current eligibility, expected workload and the applicable scoring system are separate questions that a career total alone cannot settle.
