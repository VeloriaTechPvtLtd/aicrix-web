---
title: "RCB 2 for 0 versus 49 all out and the lowest total question"
excerpt: "Compare RCB\u2019s rain-stopped 2/0 in 2015 with its 49 all out in 2017, and learn why low-total tables need an innings-completion rule."
status: draft
---

RCB really did finish a match on **2/0** against Delhi Daredevils on **17 May 2015**. But rain stopped that chase after seven balls: it was not an all-out innings. RCB’s **49 all out against Kolkata Knight Riders on 23 April 2017** describes a different event, in which the batting side was dismissed.

Sorting those scores as bare numbers makes two appear to be the more extreme collapse. Reading the innings status changes the answer. The smaller score was an unfinished chase with no wickets lost; the larger was a completed all-out innings.

## Two matches with different endings

| Match | First innings | RCB reply | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| RCB vs Delhi Daredevils, 17 May 2015 | Delhi 187/5 in 20 overs | 2/0 in 1.1 overs | No result because of rain |
| KKR vs RCB, 23 April 2017 | KKR 131 all out in 19.3 overs | 49 all out in 9.4 overs | KKR won by 82 runs |

Sources: the official IPL reports for [2015](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/report-m55-rcb-vs-dd) and [2017](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/match-report-m27-kkr-vs-rcb).

## The 2015 chase never produced a batting defeat

Delhi had completed its twenty overs before the weather ended the contest. RCB’s target was 188, but its reply stopped at two without loss. The teams received a point each. [IPL’s dated report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/report-m55-rcb-vs-dd).

The difference 187 − 2 = 185 is therefore not a winning margin. It compares scores at different stages of two innings in a match that had no result. Nor can the seven-ball start establish what RCB would have scored if play had continued.

## The 2017 innings ended through dismissals

At Eden Gardens, RCB had bowled KKR out for 131 and needed 132 to win. Instead, all ten RCB wickets fell for 49. [IPL report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/match-report-m27-kkr-vs-rcb).

The result is verifiable subtraction: 131 − 49 = **82 runs**, matching the [official match report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/match-report-m27-kkr-vs-rcb). Unlike the 2015 comparison, these were completed innings in a decided match.

The contemporary report called 49 the lowest IPL total at that time. That is a historical record statement. It should not be silently extended into a claim about a complete current leaderboard without checking subsequent matches.

## Apply eligibility to each innings

| Innings in these two matches | Retain in the match archive | Completed innings | All-out innings |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Delhi 187/5 in 2015 | Yes | Yes, allocated overs finished | No |
| RCB 2/0 in 2015 | Yes | No, chase stopped by rain | No |
| KKR 131 in 2017 | Yes | Yes, all out | Yes |
| RCB 49 in 2017 | Yes | Yes, all out | Yes |

This table reveals why filtering only by match result is inadequate. Removing every no-result match would discard Delhi’s completed 187/5 along with RCB’s unfinished chase. Keeping every score without checking its state would let 2/0 enter a table intended for completed or all-out innings.

An all-out table is narrower than a completed-innings table: Delhi’s 187/5 belongs in the latter but not the former. A full historical archive is broader still and should retain all four rows, with their labels intact. These are different selections from the same facts, not competing versions of the score.

## What a useful lowest-total entry should show

Keep the score, wickets, overs, opponent, date and innings-ending reason together. State whether the table admits unfinished scores, completed innings or only all-out innings before sorting by runs. Match outcome should remain a separate field.

For this comparison, the answer is straightforward: **2/0 is an authentic partial score, not a two-run dismissal; 49 is the authentic all-out innings.** The appropriate correction is to fix the record category, not delete the rain-affected match from history.
