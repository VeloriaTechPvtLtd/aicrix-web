Shreyanka Patil’s WPL 2026 bowling record contains one outstanding performance that deserves to be read alongside the rest of her season. She took 11 wickets in nine matches for Royal Challengers Bengaluru, with 5/23 against Gujarat Giants her best return. Her 183 legal balls cost 275 runs: a bowling average of 25.00 and a recalculated economy rate of 9.02. [WPL season row in the Cricket West Indies player record](https://www.windiescricket.com/players/shreyanka-patil-19300/)

Nearly half those wickets arrived in one match. That does not reduce the value of the five-wicket haul, which helped RCB defend a total, but it changes how the season should be described. A season total is not evidence that similar returns were spread evenly across every appearance.

## The five wicket match in context

On 16 January, RCB recovered to 182/7 and dismissed Gujarat for 150, winning by 32 runs. Shreyanka’s five wickets helped finish that defence, while Radha Yadav received Player of the Match for her contribution to the recovery. A bowling landmark and the match award are different records. [Official WPL Match 9 report](https://www.wplt20.com/news/article/match-report-tata-wpl-2026-match-9)

The scorecard records the innings ending at 18.5 overs, the same point at which Shreyanka completed her 3.5-over allocation. Her one unused legal delivery was therefore not evidence of a withdrawal from the attack: the opposition innings had ended. That is a concrete example of why a less-than-four-over allocation needs match context. [Gujarat scorecard](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/139648/rcbw-vs-ggtw)

The five-wicket match supplied 45.45% of her wickets from 12.57% of her legal-ball workload. Removing it from a comparison is a way to show concentration, not a reason to erase it from her real record. Both views belong together: an important high-impact performance, and a wider season in which wickets were less frequent and runs more expensive.

This is also why the best-figures column should not stand in for a typical-match forecast. A single repeat of 5/23 would be an assumption, not a conclusion supported by these nine observations.

## Selection did not mean four overs every time

Shreyanka bowled in all nine appearances but completed four overs in only four of them. Her final allocation was two overs, while Delhi’s chase on 24 January ended after 15.4 overs with her having bowled 2.4. Those different innings circumstances should not be collapsed into a claim that every short allocation had the same cause. Nor do total overs reveal their phase. A wicket in the first six overs and a wicket late in an innings count equally in the season tally, but require delivery-level evidence to separate. Her right-arm off-break classification identifies bowling type, not a permanent powerplay, middle-over or death-over assignment. For readers comparing players, the useful starting point is the complete row of appearances, legal balls, runs and wickets, followed by the match distribution. Shreyanka’s 2026 story is not simply “11 wickets” or “one five-for”: it is a major match-winning contribution within a season of varied allocations and uneven returns.
