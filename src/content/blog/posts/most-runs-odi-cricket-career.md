Sachin Tendulkar leads men’s ODI career run scoring with 18,426 runs. As checked on 14 November 2026, seven batters have reached 12,000: Tendulkar, Virat Kohli, Kumar Sangakkara, Ricky Ponting, Sanath Jayasuriya, Mahela Jayawardene and Rohit Sharma.

Kohli’s 15,190 and Rohit’s 12,143 include their latest completed batting innings against New Zealand on **13 November 2026**. Both were dismissed for ducks, so their career runs stayed unchanged while their batting-innings counts rose. The Hamilton ODI on 10 November was abandoned after the toss without a batting innings. [ICC report of the 13 November match](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/young-henry-star-as-new-zealand-keep-india-odi-series-alive). These are ODI career totals, not World Cup-only, List A or combined international figures.

## The gaps are different from the targets

Tendulkar’s lead over Kohli is 3,236 runs. Kohli would need 3,236 additional runs to draw level and 3,237 to move ahead, assuming the recorded totals remain unchanged. That is arithmetic, not a forecast about his remaining career.

Kohli is already 956 runs ahead of Sangakkara. At the other end of this group, Rohit trails Jayawardene by 507, meaning 508 further runs would take him past sixth place. Being seventh in career runs therefore says nothing about how close each adjacent position is.

Tendulkar’s 18,426 is independently documented by the BCCI. The board’s historical article also contains records that applied in 2013; those older surrounding claims should not be treated as current rankings. [BCCI record](https://www.bcci.tv/news/article/sachin-tendulkar-in-numbers).

## Opportunity and scoring rate answer different questions

Kohli has scored 49.32 runs per batting innings, compared with Tendulkar’s 40.77 and Rohit’s 42.46. These calculations divide career runs by batting innings, not matches. Tendulkar has had 144 more batting innings than Kohli, an important part of the context behind their different career totals.

Runs per innings is not batting average. Kohli’s 48 not-outs leave 260 dismissals, producing an average of 58.42. Dividing by all 308 innings gives the lower 49.32 figure. Neither denominator should be silently substituted for the other.

Scoring speed uses a third denominator: balls faced. Kohli’s 15,190 runs from 16,073 balls yield a strike rate of 94.51. Rohit’s 12,143 from 13,014 yield 93.31. Both are recomputed from the underlying counts, rather than copied from a rounded display. [Kohli ledger](https://www.cricbuzz.com/profiles/1413/virat-kohli) and [Rohit ledger](https://www.cricbuzz.com/profiles/576/rohit-sharma).

These figures describe output and opportunity; they do not adjust for era, batting position, opposition or conditions. A career-runs list cannot, by itself, settle a comparison of batting quality.

## The cutoff that keeps the list current

For Kohli and Rohit, **13 November is the batting cutoff and 14 November is the research date**. The [10 November match record](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/161260/nz-vs-ind-3rd-odi-india-tour-of-new-zealand-2026) confirms the intervening abandonment.

The [13 November scorecard](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/161271/nz-vs-ind-4th-odi-india-tour-of-new-zealand-2026) records Kohli’s zero from four balls and Rohit’s zero from one. Those dismissals increase the denominators used for average and strike rate even though neither adds a run.

A scheduled match contributes nothing to batting-innings statistics until the batter actually bats. When updating this table, check completed batting records first, then recompute the gaps. Do not add projected runs or treat a washed-out appearance as a zero-run dismissal.
