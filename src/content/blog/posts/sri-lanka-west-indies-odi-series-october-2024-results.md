Sri Lanka won the three-match men's ODI series against West Indies **2–1 in October 2024**. The hosts secured the series by winning the first two games at Pallekele. West Indies then won the third, with Evin Lewis's century completing a rain-adjusted chase.

All three matches had reduced playing time, but their targets did not work in the same way. In particular, West Indies's 185/4 in the opener and Sri Lanka's 156/3 in the finale were not totals the chasing team could simply beat by adding one. The revised targets were 232 and 195 respectively. ## First ODI and the recovery after early wickets

Sri Lanka's opening chase needed rebuilding after three early wickets. Debutant Nishan Madushka made **69 from 54 balls**, while captain Charith Asalanka contributed **77 from 71** and received the Player of the Match award. Their scores supplied 146 runs between them, but that sum is not their partnership: each batter also scored while other partners were at the crease.

The revised requirement was 232 in 37 overs. Sri Lanka's finish at 31.5 means 191 legal balls were used, not 31.5 decimal overs. With 222 balls available, the chase ended with **31 balls remaining**. ## Second ODI and the difference between a rescue and a winning total

West Indies recovered from **58/8** through a **119-run ninth-wicket partnership** between Sherfane Rutherford and Gudakesh Motie. Rutherford made 80 and Motie finished unbeaten on 50, lifting the innings to 189. The recovery created a contest, but did not reverse the damage from the early wickets. [Second-ODI scorecard](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/108267/sl-vs-wi-2nd-odi-west-indies-tour-of-sri-lanka-2024)

Wanindu Hasaranga took **4/40**, Maheesh Theekshana **3/25** and Asitha Fernando **3/35**. Those three bowlers accounted for all ten wickets. Asalanka then finished unbeaten on **62**, taking Sri Lanka to 190/5 and an unbeatable 2–0 lead. The 44-over allocation was already the match's shortened limit; the winning target was the ordinary 190, rather than an added DLS uplift. [Second-ODI scorecard](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/108267/sl-vs-wi-2nd-odi-west-indies-tour-of-sri-lanka-2024)

## Third ODI and Lewis's successful return

The final match produced a different challenge: West Indies needed **195 in 23 overs** despite Sri Lanka's recorded 156/3. Kusal Mendis had accelerated with **56 not out from 22 balls**. Lewis answered with **102 not out from 61**, while Rutherford supplied **50 not out from 26**.

West Indies reached 196/2 after 22 overs, leaving one over unused. Lewis and Rutherford's unbroken third-wicket stand added **88**, from 108/2 to the winning total. This was a consolation victory after the series had already been decided, not a series-clinching result for West Indies. ## What these results can and cannot tell an analyst

The three games are a useful example of why a series table needs targets and overs alongside scores. Comparing 234, 190 and 196 without their available balls would hide very different demands on the chasing sides. Likewise, combining these rain-affected totals into an unqualified Pallekele “par score” would remove the circumstances that shaped them.

For player comparisons, keep the ODI filter. Adding the three ODI scorecards gives Rutherford **204 runs** (74, 80 and 50) and Hasaranga **six wickets** (two, four and none). Those are ODI-series totals, not combined-tour figures that include the T20Is. Historical form from this series should not be presented as a current lineup or a forecast for the next meeting. [First ODI](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/108265/sl-vs-wi-1st-odi-west-indies-tour-of-sri-lanka-2024), [second ODI](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/108267/sl-vs-wi-2nd-odi-west-indies-tour-of-sri-lanka-2024) and [third ODI](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/108275/sl-vs-wi-3rd-odi-west-indies-tour-of-sri-lanka-2024).

The revised targets are recorded outcomes of the match calculations, not numbers that can be reconstructed just by comparing the final scores. A reader can independently check the unused balls and winning margins from the ledger without claiming to reproduce the DLS calculation. Keeping that distinction visible prevents a historical score table from turning into a misleading explanation of the rain rule.
