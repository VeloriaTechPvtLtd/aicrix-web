Rajasthan Royals beat Mumbai Indians in **the two IPL 2026 matches compared here**: by 27 runs in Guwahati on 7 April, then by 30 runs at Wankhede on 24 May. The margins look similar, but the matches were very different. The first was reduced to 11 overs per side; the second was a full-length match that secured Rajasthan's playoff place.

Mumbai won the toss and chose to field in both games.

This comparison keeps the shortened Match 13 separate from the full-length Match 69; it is not an all-time head-to-head ledger.

## Jaiswal set the pace in the shortened Guwahati contest

Rain shortened the first meeting, making immediate scoring especially important. Yashasvi Jaiswal finished **77 not out from 32 balls**, while Vaibhav Sooryavanshi contributed **39 from 14**. Rajasthan reached 150/3, leaving Mumbai a target of 151 from 66 legal deliveries.

Mumbai's reply finished at 123/9. Jaiswal was Player of the Match. The [official Match 13 report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/tata-ipl-2026-match-13-rr-vs-mi-match-report) documents the reduced allocation and the opening batters' contributions.

The important detail is the innings length. A total of 150 might look modest when read without context, but here Rajasthan made it in only 11 overs. Reading this as an ordinary 20-over score would badly understate the pressure on Mumbai's chase.

## Archer contributed with bat and ball at Wankhede

The return fixture did not begin as smoothly for Rajasthan's batting. Dhruv Jurel made **38 from 26 balls**, and Jofra Archer's **32 from 15** helped the visitors finish strongly. Rajasthan added 73 runs in its final five overs and reached 205/8.

Archer then struck against Mumbai's batting, and Suryakumar Yadav's **60 from 42 balls** could not rescue the chase. The win secured Rajasthan's playoff berth. [Official Match 69 report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/tata-ipl-2026-match-69-mi-v-rr-match-report)

Archer's Player of the Match award reflected an all-round contribution: the [official match centre](https://www.iplt20.com/matches/e84565a5-4651-474c-b58c-fbbeb9bf1644/mumbai-indians-vs-rajasthan-royals/match-details) records his 32 runs and three wickets. Unlike the first meeting, Rajasthan's standout contribution was not confined to a top-order innings.

## Why the raw totals are not a fair comparison

Rajasthan scored 55 more runs in Mumbai than in Guwahati, but also had nine additional overs. Its actual scoring rate was **13.64 runs per over in the shortened game**, compared with **10.25 at Wankhede**.

That does not mean one performance was automatically better. A shortened innings changes the available time and the risks batters can take. It does show why comparing only 150 with 205 would tell an incomplete story.

Mumbai lost nine wickets in each chase, although those wickets fell across very different innings lengths. Again, the same number does not describe the same match situation.

## What the season double tells us

Rajasthan successfully defended a total twice, at two grounds and under different innings limits. The first victory was driven by rapid top-order scoring; the second included an important late batting contribution from Archer before his work with the ball.

Those are conclusions about the completed 2026 fixtures, not a guarantee for another season. Winning both tosses did not produce a Mumbai victory, but two games cannot establish a universal rule about choosing to field.
