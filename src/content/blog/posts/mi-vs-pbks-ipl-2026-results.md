Mumbai Indians and Punjab Kings split the two IPL 2026 games compared here, on 16 April and 14 May, with the chasing team winning each time. Punjab reached 198/3 in 16.3 overs at Wankhede; Mumbai needed until 19.5 overs to reach 205/4 in Dharamshala. The targets differed by only five runs, but the chases had very different shapes. ## De Kock's hundred was not enough at Wankhede

Quinton de Kock finished unbeaten on 112 from 60 deliveries. Naman Dhir's 50 from 31 helped repair Mumbai's early losses, but Arshdeep Singh's three for 22 prevented an unrestricted finish. Mumbai ended on 195/6. [Official match report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/tata-ipl-2026-match-24-mi-vs-pbks-match-report).

Punjab also lost two early wickets, reaching 45/2 at 4.3 overs. Prabhsimran Singh and Shreyas Iyer then added 139 before Iyer departed at 184/3. Prabhsimran remained unbeaten on 80 from 39 balls, Iyer made 66 from 35, and Marcus Stoinis finished on ten not out. [Detailed scorecard](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/151785/mi-vs-pbks-24th-match-ipl-2026).

That third-wicket stand covered 66 legal deliveries, from 4.3 to 15.3 overs. Its scoring rate was therefore 139 ÷ 11, or 12.64 per over. It supplied the acceleration after the early wickets rather than leaving the entire target to the last few overs.

De Kock had the highest individual score, yet Punjab had the more comfortable team finish. Comparing only the leading batters would miss the value of Punjab's partnership and the time left at the end.

## Dharamshala required a much later acceleration

Punjab's 200/8 included Prabhsimran's 57 from 32 balls and Azmatullah Omarzai's 38 from 17. Shardul Thakur took four for 39, but Punjab still found enough lower-order runs to reach 200. [Official report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/tata-ipl-2026-match-58-pbks-vs-mi-match-report).

The scorecard records Punjab at 166/8 after 18 overs. Vishnu Vinod and Xavier Bartlett then added 34 without being separated in the final two overs. That is a rate of 17 per over for the last 12 legal deliveries, a useful reminder that a late wicket count alone does not determine the final total. [Dharamshala innings ledger](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/152141/pbks-vs-mi).

Ryan Rickelton gave Mumbai 48 from 23 balls, but the decisive finishing innings came from Tilak Varma: 75 not out from 33. Will Jacks contributed an unbeaten 25 from ten. Their unbroken partnership added 56 from 20 balls. The official report records Mumbai needing 51 from the last three overs before the pair completed the chase with one ball left. [IPL account of the finish](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/tata-ipl-2026-match-58-pbks-vs-mi-match-report).

These are calculations from the final scorecards, not estimates of pitch difficulty. In cricket notation, 16.3 means 16 overs and three balls, or 99 deliveries. Dividing 198 by 16.3 as a decimal would give the wrong rate.

Likewise, a successful chase can finish beyond the exact target. Punjab needed 196 but scored 198; Mumbai needed 201 but finished on 205. Use the target to calculate the requirement and the actual final total to calculate the recorded innings rate.

Two successful chases do not establish that fielding first is always preferable at either venue. The archive supports a more specific comparison: Punjab's major partnership made the first chase comfortable, while Mumbai's late unbeaten stand rescued the second from a demanding finish.

## A partnership is not the sum of two full innings

Prabhsimran’s 80 and Iyer’s 66 add to 146, not their 139-run stand. Prabhsimran had already been batting before Iyer arrived and remained after he departed. The partnership belongs to the time they were together, including any extras during that interval. The full innings totals answer a different question.

The same principle applies to Tilak and Jacks. Their full scores total 100, while the unbroken stand contributed 56. Tilak had scored before Jacks joined him. For comparing the two finishes, use the partnership ledger and legal balls rather than assigning the entire individual score to the final phase.
