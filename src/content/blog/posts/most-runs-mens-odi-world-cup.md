**Sachin Tendulkar has the most career runs in men’s ODI World Cups: 2,278. Virat Kohli holds the single-edition record with 765 runs in 2023.** These answer different questions: one adds a player’s runs across World Cups, while the other covers one tournament.

The records below cover the men’s main World Cup competition through the completed 2023 edition, checked on 14 November 2026. They exclude qualifiers, warm-ups, the Champions Trophy and T20 World Cups.

The career order and totals agree across the [Times of India tournament records](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/sports/cricket/icc-cricket-world-cup-2023/stats/batsman-with-highest-individual-runs) and [myKhel career table](https://www.mykhel.com/cricket/most-runs-in-odi-world-cup-s1rs35/). ICC reports separately confirm [Tendulkar’s total](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/thank-you-sachin) and [Warner’s 1,527](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/warner-confirms-odi-retirement-to-add-to-test-farewell).

India occupies three of the first four positions, but the list is a cross-country career ranking. The 2023 tournament’s own run chart has a different order because it includes only that edition.

## Tendulkar built his total across six editions

Tendulkar appeared in the 1992, 1996, 1999, 2003, 2007 and 2011 tournaments. His 2,278 runs came from 44 batting innings in 45 matches, with six centuries and 15 half-centuries.

His biggest tournament aggregate was 673 in 2003. He also led the competition’s run chart in 1996 with 523, then scored 482 in India’s title-winning 2011 campaign. His highest individual World Cup innings was 152 against Namibia in 2003. [ICC’s completed-career tribute](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/thank-you-sachin).

Those figures show why a career total cannot be substituted for a single-season record. Tendulkar’s six-tournament aggregate includes many separate campaigns; his 2003 tally is only one component.

## How far Kohli is behind Tendulkar

Kohli’s 1,795 leaves a gap of **483 runs** to Tendulkar. From that recorded total, he would need 483 more World Cup runs to draw level and **484 to move ahead**.

The arithmetic is straightforward:

- 2,278 − 1,795 = 483 to equal the existing total.

- 1,795 + 484 = 2,279, which would exceed it by one.

Only runs in the relevant World Cup competition would close this gap. Runs in bilateral ODIs, another ICC tournament or a T20 World Cup would not count toward this record.

Kohli’s 1,795 runs and 37 appearances are recorded in the linked career tables. His overall ODI total belongs to a separate leaderboard and must not be substituted for his World Cup total.

## Most runs in one men’s ODI World Cup edition

| Rank | Player | Edition | Runs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Virat Kohli | 2023 | 765 |
| 2 | Sachin Tendulkar | 2003 | 673 |
| 3 | Matthew Hayden | 2007 | 659 |
| 4 | Rohit Sharma | 2019 | 648 |
| 5 | David Warner | 2019 | 647 |

Kohli’s 765 exceeded the previous record of 673 by **92 runs**. That is about 13.7% above Tendulkar’s 2003 total. The [ICC’s review after the 2023 final](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/cricketworldcup/news/a-tournament-of-wonders-all-the-records-that-broke-at-the-cricket-world-cup-2023) records this five-player order.

Kohli’s 2023 campaign accounts for about **42.6%** of his 1,795 career World Cup runs. It was enough to establish the single-edition record, while Tendulkar retained the career aggregate record. Both statements can be true at the same time.

## Matches and batting average use different denominators

A match appearance does not always include a batting innings. A batting innings also does not always end in dismissal.

Tendulkar’s record contains 44 innings and four not-outs. His batting average is therefore 2,278 divided by 40 dismissals, which gives **56.95**. Dividing by his 45 matches would instead give about 50.62 runs per match.

Kohli has 37 innings and seven not-outs in the same competition record. His 1,795 divided by 30 dismissals gives **59.83** to two decimal places. The [innings and not-out columns](https://www.mykhel.com/cricket/most-runs-in-odi-world-cup-s1rs35/) make those calculations reproducible.

Average and aggregate describe different aspects of a record. A higher average does not change which player has accumulated the most runs.

## Highest individual innings is another record

Martin Guptill’s **237 not out against West Indies in 2015** is the highest individual innings in the men’s ODI World Cup. It is a single innings, rather than a player’s career aggregate or an edition total. [ICC’s innings record reference](https://www.icc-cricket.com/media-releases/icc-mens-cricket-world-cup-top-fives) and [2025 Guptill tribute](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/new-zealand-batting-great-retires-from-international-cricket?appview=true).

For a quick record lookup, use Tendulkar’s 2,278 for career runs, Kohli’s 765 for one edition and Guptill’s 237 not out for one innings. Keep the tournament and time period attached to each number.
