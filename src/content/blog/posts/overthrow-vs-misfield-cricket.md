A fielding mistake that sends the ball over the rope does not always produce “runs already taken plus four.” Under the MCC’s 2026 Laws, an overthrow boundary and a misfield boundary follow different calculations. First classify the incident, then count the runs.

The edition effective from 1 October 2026 makes that distinction explicit. A throw directed towards the wickets to attempt a run-out or prevent more scoring can produce an overthrow boundary. A failed stop, or a short pass to another fielder near the boundary, is not treated as an overthrow simply because the ball escapes. The umpire judges the incident. [MCC explanation of the change](https://www.lords.org/getmedia/86ac7ad2-1031-4e2b-bdea-ff407e7b0db0/Ten-major-changes-to-Laws-for-2026-edition_6.pdf) · [Implementation date](https://www.lords.org/lords/news-stories/mcc-prepares-for-new-edition-of-laws-from-1-october-2026)

## Ordinary misfield boundaries use a comparison

For a boundary that is not an overthrow or a wilful act, compare the boundary allowance with the qualifying runs taken. The latter includes completed runs and a run in progress if the batters had crossed when the boundary was scored. Award the greater amount, not their sum. Penalty runs are a separate consideration. [MCC Law 19.7](https://www.lords.org/getattachment/e8b12929-1e84-4927-af4e-2a5875b53589/Laws-of-Cricket-2017-Code-4th-Edition-%282026%29.pdf)

Suppose a batter hits a grounded shot, completes two runs and watches a fielder’s attempted stop send the ball over the rope. With the usual four-run allowance and no other relevant event, the score is four, not six. The two are not added to an ordinary boundary.

Now imagine an unusually large ground. Five runs have already been completed when a rolling ball finally reaches the boundary after a misfield. The comparison is five against four, so five stands. “A misfield boundary is always exactly four” is therefore also too broad.

## An overthrow boundary uses the throw as the cutoff

For an overthrow boundary, add the boundary allowance to the runs completed at the instant of the throw, plus the run in progress only if the batters had already crossed at that instant. Add any applicable penalties separately. Crossing later, while the throw travels towards the boundary, does not retrospectively qualify that run. [MCC Law 19.8](https://www.lords.org/getattachment/e8b12929-1e84-4927-af4e-2a5875b53589/Laws-of-Cricket-2017-Code-4th-Edition-%282026%29.pdf)

The following examples assume a grounded shot, a four-run boundary allowance, no short running, no penalties and a throw that qualifies as an overthrow.

| Position when the fielder releases the throw | Calculation | Runs awarded |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One completed run, batters have not crossed on the second | 1 + 4 | 5 |
| One completed run, batters have crossed on the second | 1 + 1 + 4 | 6 |
| Two completed runs, batters have not crossed on the third | 2 + 4 | 6 |

In the first row, the batters might cross a moment after release and reach their ends before the ball reaches the rope. That does not turn five into six. In the second row, the crossing had already happened, so the additional run qualifies even though the physical completion comes later.

## Why the direction and purpose matter

Compare two otherwise similar errors near the rope. In one, the fielder throws towards the wickets in an attempt to stop further runs, and the ball continues to the boundary. In the other, a fielder tries to shovel the ball a short distance to a nearby teammate and misses. The first can fall under the overthrow calculation; the second is treated as a misfield for this distinction.

The word “deliberate” is not a shortcut either. A purposeful attempt to save runs is not automatically the separate category of a wilful act under Law 19.8.2.3. Record what the fielder was doing rather than inferring the scoring category from whether the movement was intentional.

## Read the whole scoring sequence

For an ordinary boundary, the relevant crossing check is when the boundary is scored. For an overthrow boundary, it is when the throw is made. Those different timestamps explain why two similar-looking clips can yield different totals.

This guide concerns boundary awards. It does not add an automatic four when a wayward throw remains in play. Nor does the word “overthrow” itself identify who receives the runs on the scorecard: batting and extras attribution is a separate scoring question.
