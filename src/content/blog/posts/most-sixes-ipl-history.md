## Most sixes in IPL history

Chris Gayle leads the IPL career sixes list with 357. The 250-six club contains five batters through the completed 2026 season: Gayle, Rohit Sharma, Virat Kohli, MS Dhoni and AB de Villiers. This is an IPL-only career comparison across franchises, not an international record or a list of the biggest single-season totals.

The table uses the IPL columns in the linked Cricbuzz player ledgers. Rohit’s 323 and Kohli’s 316 also match IPL Records, but another published career table differs by one for each. Those two totals are therefore attributed figures, not a claim that every historical delivery has been independently reconciled.

The threshold is therefore a complete group, not five names selected from a longer qualifying list.

## Why career totals and frequency answer different questions

A career count rewards both six-hitting and the opportunity to keep batting. Balls per six is calculated by dividing recorded balls faced by sixes. Lower is more frequent. It does not use matches, team deliveries or dismissals as the denominator, and it does not predict when the next six will arrive.

Gayle and de Villiers make a useful comparison because their recorded exposure is close: 3,333 balls against 3,403. Gayle hit 106 more sixes while facing 70 fewer balls. His advantage here is not simply the result of a much larger batting sample. The calculation describes their historical IPL output; it does not adjust for ground dimensions, opponents or match situations.

Dhoni sits above de Villiers in total sixes, 264 against 251, but below him in frequency: one every 14.99 balls rather than 13.56. He faced 554 more balls. Reversing their order for the frequency column is not a contradiction. Total output and output per opportunity are different measures.

Kohli’s 6,926 balls are more than twice Gayle’s 3,333. That helps explain why an aggregate leaderboard should not be read as a pure power ranking. It also says nothing by itself about who contributed more runs, preserved wickets better or adapted more effectively to a chase.

## Keep the record category consistent

Career IPL sixes include a player’s IPL contributions for different franchises. A team-only page can legitimately show a smaller number. International matches, other domestic leagues and non-IPL competitions cannot be added to this table merely because the same player or franchise appears in them.

Likewise, a season leader and a career leader answer different questions. A new season record does not automatically replace the career record. For a fair comparison, retain the same competition and cutoff before examining either total sixes or balls per six.

## The unresolved one six differences

[CricketTaken’s August 2026 table](https://crickettaken.in/indian-premier-league/records/most-sixes) lists Rohit at 324 and Kohli at 317, rather than 323 and 316. The difference does not change their positions or membership of the 250-six club, but it does affect exact gaps and rates. No specific disputed delivery has been identified here, so neither Super Overs nor missing matches should be offered as an explanation.

Until that reconciliation is complete, use the linked figures with their attribution rather than treating the larger or more frequently repeated number as automatically correct. A reliable record needs consistent underlying entries, not just a convincing-looking leaderboard.
