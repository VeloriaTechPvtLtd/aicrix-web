**A wicketkeeper can cause a no-ball even when the bowler’s delivery is otherwise legal.** If its extra supplies the winning run, the chase can finish without another delivery. The apparent contradiction disappears when the keeper’s position, the score and the legal-ball count are considered separately.

## The keeper has three ways to become free of the position restriction

Under MCC’s 2026 Law 27.3, the keeper must stay entirely behind the striker’s wicket from the instant of delivery until one of these happens:

- The ball contacts the striker’s bat or body.

- The ball travels past the striker’s wicket.

- The striker attempts a run.

These are alternatives, not three requirements that must all occur. Reaching forward too soon breaches the rule; the striker’s-end umpire signals a no-ball. The relevant line is the wicket, not the batting crease. [MCC 2026 Laws](https://www.lords.org/getattachment/e8b12929-1e84-4927-af4e-2a5875b53589/Laws-of-Cricket-2017-Code-4th-Edition-%282026%29.pdf), Law 27.3.

## Why the bowler receives the extra

The one-run no-ball penalty enters extras and is charged to the bowler, rather than added to the batter’s score. The delivery does not consume a legal ball. A stumping cannot stand from a no-ball. Those are scoring consequences, not a finding that the bowler personally caused the infringement. [MCC Laws](https://www.lords.org/getattachment/e8b12929-1e84-4927-af4e-2a5875b53589/Laws-of-Cricket-2017-Code-4th-Edition-%282026%29.pdf), 21.14–21.17.

## A winning extra: a worked example

Imagine a fictional match in which Team A makes 154 and Team B reaches 154/6 after 19.5 overs. Assume an ordinary chase, no target adjustment and no other penalty or dismissal. On the next delivery, the wicketkeeper collects the ball in front of the stumps before any of the three permitted release events. The umpire calls no-ball.

The one-run extra takes Team B to 155/6 and completes the chase. The batter does not gain a personal run simply because the keeper caused the extra. The score remains 19.5 overs because the no-ball does not add a legal delivery: 19 × 6 + 5 equals 119 legal balls.

| Record | Before | After the winning no-ball |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Team score | 154/6 | 155/6 |
| Legal deliveries | 119 | 119 |
| Batter runs from this event | 0 | 0 |
| No-ball extras added | 0 | 1 |

These numbers illustrate the rule; they are not a report of a named match.

## Why a replacement ball is not required after victory

Under MCC Law 16.6, reaching the result ends the match, subject to the Law's specified exceptions. In this example, there is no remaining target to pursue. The usual need to replace an invalid delivery does not require play after the victory. [MCC Laws 16.6 and 21](https://www.lords.org/getattachment/e8b12929-1e84-4927-af4e-2a5875b53589/Laws-of-Cricket-2017-Code-4th-Edition-%282026%29.pdf)

The lesson is to separate runs, deliveries attempted and valid balls. An extra can change the result while leaving the legal-ball count unchanged.

## Does a free hit always follow?

Free-hit provisions belong to the relevant competition's playing conditions. They are not an unconditional promise derived from the general MCC Laws. For a continuing match, check those conditions before deciding what the next delivery permits. For the fictional completed chase above, there is no next delivery.

Keep the umpire's keeper-position ruling distinct from other penalties such as fielding restrictions. They can arise in the same passage of play without being the same offence. A useful explanation identifies the infringement, the awarded extra, the valid-ball count and whether the match has already ended.
