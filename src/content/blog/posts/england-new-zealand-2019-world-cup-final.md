England won the 2019 men’s Cricket World Cup at Lord’s on 14 July after the final and its Super Over both finished level. New Zealand made 241/8 and England 241 all out. Each side then scored 15 in the tie-break; England’s larger boundary count decided the title under that tournament’s rules. [ICC’s match-day report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/cricketworldcup/news/england-win-maiden-mens-world-cup-title-at-lords)

The distinction matters when reading the archive. England did not win the main innings by runs or wickets, and did not outscore New Zealand in the Super Over. The score remained tied; the competition’s prescribed tie-break identified the champion.

## The scores belong in separate panels

| Stage | New Zealand | England | What it establishes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Main match | 241/8 in 50 overs | 241 all out in 50 overs | Scores tied |
| Super Over | 15/1, batting second | 15/0, batting first | Scores tied again |
| Boundaries hit across both stages | 17 | 26 | England won the title on boundary count |

New Zealand chose to bat. Ben Stokes was Player of the Match after his unbeaten 84 in the main chase. Keep the Super Over separate rather than adding it to create a fictitious 256-run ODI innings. The [complete four-innings scorecard](https://www.mykhel.com/cricket/new-zealand-vs-england-icc-cricket-world-cup-2019-final-scorecard-43691/) shows Stokes’s additional eight and Jos Buttler’s seven in their own tie-break panel.

## How the target became difficult

Henry Nicholls made 55 and Tom Latham 47 in New Zealand’s innings. Liam Plunkett and Chris Woakes took three wickets apiece. The target was 242, but England were 86/4 when Eoin Morgan fell. Stokes and Buttler then added 110 before Buttler was dismissed for 59. [ICC match report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/cricketworldcup/news/england-win-maiden-mens-world-cup-title-at-lords)

The partnership restored wickets-in-hand stability without eliminating the required-rate pressure. At Morgan’s dismissal, England needed 156 from 161 legal balls. At Buttler’s dismissal, they needed 46 from 31. Those are different tasks even though 110 runs had been added between them.

| Checkpoint | Score | Legal balls used | Runs needed to win | Balls left | Required runs per over |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Morgan dismissed at 23.1 | 86/4 | 139 | 156 | 161 | 5.81 |
| Buttler dismissed at 44.5 | 196/5 | 269 | 46 | 31 | 8.90 |
| End of 49 overs | 227/8 | 294 | 15 | 6 | 15.00 |

These are calculations from the [completed fall-of-wickets ledger](https://www.mykhel.com/cricket/new-zealand-vs-england-icc-cricket-world-cup-2019-final-scorecard-43691/), not modelled win probabilities. The 110-run stand occupied 130 legal balls, a scoring rate of 5.08 per over. That helped prevent an early collapse, but it left a faster finish necessary. It does not prove a different tempo would have been safer or more successful.

## Two run outs completed the first tie

England entered the last over needing 15. After two scoreless deliveries, Stokes hit six. The following delivery produced six awarded runs after a return throw deflected from his bat to the boundary. That left three needed from two balls. Rashid and then Wood were run out attempting second runs; one run was completed on each delivery, leaving England on 241. [Contemporary explanation of the finish](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2019-07-15/15-minutes-of-madness-that-decided-the-cricket-world-cup-final/11308738)

The recorded last-over addition was therefore 14, one short of the winning requirement but enough to tie. This describes the official score as awarded. It is not a retrospective ruling on how the disputed overthrow should have been judged, nor an assumption that the later deliveries would have been identical under a different decision.

## Why another 15 was not enough for New Zealand

England batted first in the Super Over against Trent Boult. Stokes and Buttler made 15 without losing a wicket. Jofra Archer then bowled to New Zealand: an opening wide contributed one, Jimmy Neesham made 13 from five legal deliveries, and Martin Guptill completed one before being run out attempting the winning second. [Super Over batting and bowling panels](https://www.mykhel.com/cricket/new-zealand-vs-england-icc-cricket-world-cup-2019-final-scorecard-43691/)

At the last delivery, New Zealand needed two to exceed England’s total. Completing one made the tie-break scores equal. England’s zero wickets lost versus New Zealand’s one was not the deciding comparison. Nor were New Zealand’s two remaining wickets in the main innings a winning advantage.

## The boundary count was 26 to 17

The 2019 procedure counted boundaries hit by batters across both the main innings and the Super Over. A four and a six each counted as one boundary, not four and six units. [Contemporary explanation of the boundary-count rule](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2019-07-15/15-minutes-of-madness-that-decided-the-cricket-world-cup-final/11308738).

| Boundaries hit by batters | England | New Zealand |
|---|---:|---:|
| Main-innings fours | 22 | 14 |
| Main-innings sixes | 2 | 2 |
| Super Over fours | 2 | 0 |
| Super Over sixes | 0 | 1 |
| Combined count | 26 | 17 |

The four-innings scorecard supplies the components; [ABC’s contemporary account](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2019-07-15/15-minutes-of-madness-that-decided-the-cricket-world-cup-final/11308738) independently reports the combined 24 fours and two sixes for England, against 14 fours and three sixes for New Zealand. This was a count of boundary hits, not a nine-run victory.

That historical method should not be carried forward as a timeless rule. In October 2019, the ICC changed its event procedure so tied Super Overs in semifinals and finals would be repeated until one team scored more. [Cricket Australia’s dated report of the announcement](https://www.cricket.com.au/news/3302689/super-over-rule-change-after-world-cup-drama)

For this final, retain all three facts together: the main match tied, the Super Over tied, and England won the trophy on the boundary rule then in force. Removing any one makes the result harder, not easier, to understand.

For a different historical progression rule after a tie, compare the [Australia–South Africa 1999 semifinal](/blog/australia-south-africa-1999-world-cup-semifinal).
