Babar Azam leads men’s T20I career runs with 4,596. Four batters have reached 4,000 in the complete all-international ledger checked on 14 November 2026: Babar, Jos Buttler, Rohit Sharma and Virat Kohli. The next player is Paul Stirling on 3,895, so there is no fifth qualifier at this cutoff. [ACS career leaderboard](https://stats.acscricket.com/Records/International_Twenty20/Overall/Players/Most_Career_Runs.html).

This table counts recognized men’s Twenty20 Internationals, not every T20 match a player has played. IPL runs and other domestic or franchise runs do not enter it. Nor is it restricted to World Cups: bilateral internationals and other qualifying T20Is belong in the same career total.

## The gaps and the boundary

Babar’s lead over Buttler is 212 runs. Buttler is 153 ahead of Rohit, while only 43 separate Rohit and Kohli. Those gaps describe the current order; they are not forecasts about how many matches a player needs to change position.

Stirling is 105 short of the threshold. His [individual record](https://www.cricbuzz.com/profiles/1114/stirling-c) confirms 3,895 in the international T20 column. A later ODI appearance cannot move him into this club. Likewise, all-format news about a batter’s run tally should not be used to update this table.

## What the batting opportunities add

Dividing career runs by batting innings gives Babar 33.79, Buttler 29.23, Rohit 28.02 and Kohli 35.79 runs per innings. These calculated figures include not-out innings in the denominator. They are not batting averages, which use dismissals, or strike rates, which use balls faced.

The ordering changes: fourth-placed Kohli has the highest runs-per-innings figure within these four, while Babar retains the largest accumulated total. This is a useful distinction between output per batting opportunity and output across a career, not a verdict on overall batting quality.

Innings totals do not equalize roles, opponents, conditions or scoring tempo. A comparison of pace needs reliable balls-faced data; a prediction needs more than career aggregates. For a run leaderboard, preserve the simple question first: who has accumulated the most in the specified format, and what completed matches does the record include?

## Why this table omits match counts

Batting innings are the agreed denominator used here. ACS and Guinness list Babar with 145 appearances, while his Cricbuzz career panel lists 144; both statistical tables give 136 batting innings and 4,596 runs. Adding an appearances column without resolving that difference would introduce unnecessary ambiguity. The comparison therefore keeps only the agreed figures. A reader comparing another table should first check whether its column means appearances, innings or dismissals. None of those labels is interchangeable.
