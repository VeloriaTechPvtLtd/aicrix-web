India dismissed United Arab Emirates for 57 and reached 60/1 in 4.3 overs in Dubai on 10 September 2025. The nine-wicket win combined a collapse in UAE’s innings with a chase completed inside five overs. [Mumbai Indians’ match report](https://www.mumbaiindians.com/news/asia-cup-2025-india-vs-uae-report-mumbai-indians-cricket), [Scorecard](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/130019/uae-vs-ind-2nd-match-group-a-asia-cup-2025)

## Seven wickets from two spells

Kuldeep Yadav took 4/7 in 2.1 overs and Shivam Dube 3/4 in two. Together, that was seven wickets for 11 runs from **25 legal balls**. [Bowling figures](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/130019/uae-vs-ind-2nd-match-group-a-asia-cup-2025)

The ball count matters: 2.1 overs means 13 balls, not 2.1 decimal overs. Add Dube’s 12 and their combined economy rate becomes 11 × 6 ÷ 25 = **2.64 runs per over**. This is a combined calculation, not either bowler’s individual figure.

UAE’s third wicket fell at 47, and the innings ended at 57. From the position immediately before that third dismissal, the final eight wickets therefore added only ten runs. [Fall of wickets](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/130019/uae-vs-ind-2nd-match-group-a-asia-cup-2025)

## Why the final score exceeded the target

India needed 58 to win. Abhishek Sharma made 30; Shubman Gill finished unbeaten on 20 and Suryakumar Yadav on seven. The innings ended at 60/1. [Chase scorecard](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/130019/uae-vs-ind-2nd-match-group-a-asia-cup-2025)

The target and the final total are different numbers: reaching or passing 58 wins the chase, so finishing on 60 does not imply a target of 60. Likewise, 4.3 overs equals **27 legal balls**. Against the scheduled 120-ball allocation, India had 93 balls unused.

The result’s two margins describe separate resources: nine wickets remained, and 93 legal balls were unused. Neither should be mistaken for a runs margin.
