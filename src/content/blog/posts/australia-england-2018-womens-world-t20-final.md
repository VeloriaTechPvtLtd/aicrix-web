Australia beat England by eight wickets in the 2018 Women's World T20 final at the Sir Vivian Richards Stadium in Antigua. Played on 24 November locally, the match ended with Australia on 106/2 after England had been dismissed for 105 in 19.4 overs. Ashleigh Gardner was Player of the Match, and Australia regained the title with their fourth tournament victory. [ICC final account](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/young-guns-shine-as-australia-ease-to-fourth-wt20-title)

The defining contrast was not simply between a poor score and a successful chase. England could not build a substantial partnership, whereas Australia's middle order prevented two early dismissals from becoming a collapse.

## Wyatt's start did not become a team platform

Danni Wyatt scored 43 from 37 balls and Heather Knight made 25. No other England batter reached double figures, and no partnership exceeded 23. England had chosen to bat, but wickets repeatedly interrupted their progress. [ICC innings account](https://www.icc-cricket.com/media-releases/world-title-returns-down-under-as-australia-beat-england-in-style)

Wyatt and Knight together scored 68 of the team's 105 runs. That leaves 37 from everyone else and extras combined, not 37 credited solely to the remaining batters. Keeping that distinction avoids a common error when summarising a scorecard.

## Australia kept creating chances despite mistakes

Australia's fielding was not flawless. Wyatt survived several chances before being dismissed, while Georgia Wareham contributed a direct-hit run-out and two wickets in consecutive balls. Ellyse Perry dismissed Nat Sciver for her 100th T20I wicket. [ICC match report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/young-guns-shine-as-australia-ease-to-fourth-wt20-title)

That combination is worth retaining in the story. Describing a decisive win as a perfect performance can erase the actual difficulties the winners overcame. Missed chances and sustained wicket-taking can coexist in the same innings.

## Gardner influenced both halves of the final

Gardner took 3/22 and then finished unbeaten on 33 from 26 deliveries. Meg Lanning remained with her on 28 not out. Australia's chase ended on the first ball of the sixteenth over. Alyssa Healy, who made 22 in the final, received the Player of the Tournament award. [ICC awards and chase summary](https://www.icc-cricket.com/media-releases/world-title-returns-down-under-as-australia-beat-england-in-style)

## The chase had two setbacks, not a sustained crisis

Sophie Ecclestone bowled Healy, and Danielle Hazell dismissed Beth Mooney. Gardner and Lanning then finished the chase without another wicket falling. [ICC account of the reply](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/young-guns-shine-as-australia-ease-to-fourth-wt20-title)

Their unbroken third-wicket stand added 62 after Australia had reached 44/2. England’s highest partnership was only 23, so the winning pair alone added more than twice that amount. These are team partnership runs, including extras, rather than the sum of the batters’ entire innings. [Full scorecard](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/20580/ausw-vs-engw-final-icc-womens-world-t20-2018)

Australia's 15.1 overs mean 91 legal deliveries. Twenty-nine therefore remained in the scheduled allocation of 120. The eight-wicket margin and the unused balls both indicate resources left at the finish, but they measure different things.

The useful historical lesson is not that small targets always produce straightforward wins. It is that Australia answered their early losses with a stable partnership after restricting England's opportunities to do the same. Gardner's all-round performance sits at the centre of that explanation, supported by bowling and fielding contributions that prevented England from turning individual resistance into a competitive total.
