Australia beat Pakistan by eight wickets at Lord's on 20 June 1999, winning their second men's ODI World Cup. Pakistan had beaten them earlier in the tournament, but the final produced a very different contest. Both Pakistan openers were dismissed within 5.1 overs, and only Ijaz Ahmed went beyond 20. [ICC's anniversary account](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/cricketworldcup/news/on-this-day-australia-win-cwc99)

## Pakistan never built a substantial platform

Shane Warne took four for 33 as Pakistan were bowled out for 132. Australia completed the chase in 20.1 overs, with Adam Gilchrist's 54 from 36 balls providing the early acceleration. These are the core figures in the ICC's tournament overview. [ICC's 1999 overview](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/cricketworldcup/news/mens-cricket-world-cup-1999-overview)

Warne dismissed Ijaz, Moin Khan, Shahid Afridi and Wasim Akram. Glenn McGrath supported the attack with two for 13, and Pakistan's innings ended after 39 overs. In reply, Mark Waugh finished unbeaten on 37 and Darren Lehmann on 13, while Ricky Ponting contributed 24. [ICC's anniversary account](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/cricketworldcup/news/on-this-day-australia-win-cwc99)

## Gilchrist's innings did not require a century

The ICC's overview records a 50-over competition. Finishing after 20 overs and one ball means Australia used 121 legal deliveries of a possible 300, leaving 179. This arithmetic helps explain the scale of the victory without importing the misleading idea that “20.1” is a decimal number of overs. [ICC's 1999 overview](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/cricketworldcup/news/mens-cricket-world-cup-1999-overview)

## A dominant final was not an effortless tournament

In a later ICC interview, Gilchrist recalled Australia's uncertainty earlier in the competition and the emotional release of reaching the final. Australia had advanced from a [tied semi-final against South Africa](/blog/australia-south-africa-1999-world-cup-semifinal) because of their higher Super Six placing. His retrospective account describes the contrast between that narrow route and the far more straightforward final. [ICC interview with Gilchrist](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/warnies-magic-perfect-ponting-and-a-squash-ball-gilly-on-aussie-cwc-hat-trick)

## Separate the match award from the tournament award

Warne was Player of the Match in both the semi-final and final, taking four wickets in each. Lance Klusener, not Warne, was Player of the Tournament. Warne and Geoff Allott finished level on 20 wickets across the competition.
