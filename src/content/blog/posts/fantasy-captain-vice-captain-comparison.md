Choosing a fantasy captain is a multiplier decision built on a player-performance estimate. Start by identifying which players are eligible, what points system applies and what you want to compare. A larger projected average and a greater chance of exceeding a particular score are not automatically the same objective.

The examples below use a captain multiplier of **2×** and a vice-captain multiplier of **1.5×**, as displayed in the [official Dream11 T20 rules](https://www.dream11.com/features/how-to-play), checked on 14 November 2026. All player scores and probabilities in the examples are invented teaching inputs, not current picks or AiCrix forecasts.

### Compare the extra points rather than counting them twice

Suppose A is projected to score 50 base points and B 40, while the other nine players total 300. Both A and B are already in the same eligible roster.

| Assignment | A contribution | B contribution | Other players | Projected total |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| A captain and B vice-captain | 2 × 50 = 100 | 1.5 × 40 = 60 | 300 | 460 |
| B captain and A vice-captain | 1.5 × 50 = 75 | 2 × 40 = 80 | 300 | 455 |

The difference is five points, not 50. Swapping the roles changes each player's multiplier by only 0.5. Under these rules, the expected advantage of captaining A rather than B is **0.5 × (A's expected base points − B's expected base points)**.

There is another way to check the same total. The unmultiplied roster is 300 + 50 + 40 = 390. Add one extra copy of the captain's 50 and half of the vice-captain's 40: 390 + 50 + 20 = 460. Adding a further 100 and 60 to 390 would count the original player points twice.

For the sole objective of maximizing average points, the higher expected scorer should receive the larger multiplier when the roster, eligibility and other rules stay fixed. That conclusion depends on the estimates; it does not make them accurate.

### An expected score is not a promised score

Imagine a player with a 50% chance of scoring 20 and a 50% chance of scoring 80. The expected base score is 0.5 × 20 + 0.5 × 80 = **50**. The player never scores 50 in this invented two-outcome model.

An expected value combines possible outcomes with their probabilities. It is not a prediction that the average must occur in the next game. [OpenStax's explanation of expected value](https://openstax.org/books/statistics/pages/4-2-mean-or-expected-value-and-standard-deviation) gives the underlying weighted-average calculation.

This matters when two estimates are close. A displayed 50 versus 49 is not evidence of a decisive selection advantage unless the estimation uncertainty is also understood. A decimal point in a projection does not supply extra knowledge about tomorrow's batting position, bowling workload or fielding chances.

### The same averages can produce different ranges

Now suppose both captain A and vice-captain B individually have the same 20-or-80 distribution, each averaging 50. Their combined expected contribution is 2 × 50 + 1.5 × 50 = **175**, but the way their performances move together changes the possible outcomes.

If they are both low together or both high together, their combined scores are **70 or 280**, each with probability 50%. If one is high whenever the other is low, the combined scores are **160 or 190**, again equally likely. Both pairs average 175; the first is much more variable.

A target illustrates the difference. The first pair exceeds 200 in half its outcomes; the second never does. But the second exceeds 150 every time, while the first does so only half the time. Neither pair is universally better. These are deliberately simplified relationships, not measured correlations between actual cricketers.

### Check opportunity before trusting the projection

Before comparing multipliers, inspect what the underlying estimate assumes. Is the player expected to open or bat lower down? Does the bowling estimate depend on a full spell? Does it allow for the player not appearing? Is it based on this format and the current scoring version?

Keep the final check practical: confirm the eligible player pool, announced XI, mode, lock time and applicable replacement rules in the actual contest. An all-rounder label does not guarantee both batting and bowling opportunities, and a selection percentage does not measure expected points.

This is a points-comparison method, not a promise of prizes or returns. Check the current product and selected mode; do not assume an old cash-contest article describes what is available now.
