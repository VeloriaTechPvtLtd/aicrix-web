*Men’s T20I single-innings records: retained 1 November 2026 snapshot, rechecked 14 November 2026.*

**Sonam Yeshey’s 8/7 against Myanmar is the leading men’s T20I innings return in the checked records.** The Bhutan bowler took eight wickets in four overs on 26 December 2025. The ICC’s report appeared on 29 December; that publication date is not the date of the spell. [ICC record report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/bhutan-s-soman-yeshey-sets-new-t20i-bowling-record).

The inclusive seven-wicket threshold contains four performances in the records checked here. It includes an important 2026 addition: Kenya’s Sachin Gill took 7/7 against Cameroon in May, moving above Syazrul Idrus’s 7/8 without displacing Yeshey’s eight-wicket record.

[Crickrida’s innings record](https://crickrida.com/records/men/t20i/best-bowling-innings/) and [CricPrism’s 1 November snapshot](https://www.cricprism.com/cricket-records/t20i/best-bowling-figures-in-a-match) agree on these four rows. Both next list Nabil Master’s 6/2, below the seven-wicket boundary. There are no identical figures requiring a shared rank among the four qualifiers.

This is a wicket-first ranking: more wickets comes before fewer runs conceded. Yeshey’s eight wickets therefore lead Gill’s seven even though both conceded seven runs. Gill ranks ahead of Idrus because their wicket totals are equal and he conceded one fewer run.

## How the record changed

Idrus became the first man to take seven wickets in a T20I on 26 July 2023. His seven wickets against China were all bowled, and Malaysia won by eight wickets. The [ICC’s contemporary report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/history-made-at-t20-world-cup-qualifier-as-seamer-claims-world-record-haul) identifies the achievement as a world record at that time.

Dawood joined the seven-wicket group on 11 December 2025. His 7/19 helped Bahrain beat Bhutan by 35 runs. The [ICC report published the following day](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/bahrain-pacer-etches-name-in-history-books-with-record-spell) called it the second-best men’s T20I return. That historical statement was correct for its snapshot; it is not his position in the later table above.

Yeshey then moved the benchmark to eight wickets later that month. Gill’s May 2026 spell added another seven-wicket innings. Reading the articles in date order explains why several perfectly legitimate old headlines can give different “second-best” answers.

## Gill’s 2026 performance belongs in the list

The completed Cameroon–Kenya scorecard records Cameroon all out for 42, Kenya 43/0 in 2.2 overs and Gill as player of the match for 7/7 in four overs. The match was played on 28 May at Botswana Cricket Association Oval 1 in Gaborone. [NDTV scorecard](https://sports.ndtv.com/cricket/cmr-vs-ken-scorecard-live-cricket-score-icc-men-s-t20-world-cup-africa-sub-regional-qualifier-a-2026-match-15-camke05282026271203).

Kenya’s chase of 2.2 overs means 14 legal balls, not 2.2 decimal overs. It is separate from Gill’s four-over bowling spell. Mixing the chase length with his bowling figures would produce a meaningless economy calculation.

## The same economy can produce a different record rank

The innings ledger lists 24 balls for both Yeshey and Gill. Seven runs over four overs gives each an economy of 1.75. Their wicket totals are different, so they are not tied in the figures ranking.

A different calculation, balls per wicket for the spell, gives 24÷8 = 3 for Yeshey and 24÷7 = approximately 3.43 for Gill. Those are descriptions of two completed innings, not estimates that either bowler will take a wicket every three or four balls in the future.

Likewise, the table does not adjust for opposition strength, match conditions or career consistency. It answers a record question, not an all-purpose “best bowler” question.

## T20I is not the same filter as T20 World Cup

A World Cup qualifier can have T20I status without belonging to the final tournament’s record table. Idrus’s match is one such example. Franchise T20 matches also remain outside this international list.

For tournament-only figures, use the separate men’s T20 World Cup innings guide. For accumulated international wickets, use a T20I career table. Keeping the competition, sex category and innings scope in the title prevents an impressive number from being attached to the wrong record.

## Why the record date belongs beside the figures

An older article calling a spell the second-best can be accurate without matching a later table. Dawood’s position changed when Yeshey and Gill added their performances; his own seven wickets for 19 did not change. The ranking changed around that fixed historical result. Reading the match date and the article date separately avoids treating that movement as a scoring correction.

The threshold is also more durable than a fixed top-four heading. These four innings qualify because each contains at least seven wickets, not because four is a preferred list length. A future qualifying spell would require checking its status, figures and date before inserting it in the wicket-first order. Until that broader coverage check is done, the retained snapshot is the appropriate label for this reference.
