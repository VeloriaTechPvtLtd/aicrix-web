New Zealand beat India by eight wickets in Southampton on **23 June 2021**, winning the inaugural World Test Championship. Kane Williamson and Ross Taylor completed the chase on the scheduled reserve day, finishing unbeaten on 52 and 47 respectively. Kyle Jamieson was Player of the Match. [ICC final report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/new-zealand-crowned-world-test-champions-after-thrilling-final-day).

## Why the match reached a sixth calendar day

The final’s regular dates were 18–22 June, with 23 June set aside in advance. The reserve day was designed to recover lost playing time, not to guarantee a winner whenever a five-day final remained drawn. ICC’s [28 May conditions announcement](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/world-test-championship-final-playing-conditions-announced) explicitly distinguished those situations.

Rain removed the first and fourth days entirely. The [22 June reserve-day guide](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/guide-to-the-icc-world-test-championship-finals-reserve-day) confirmed play would continue the next day. It also retained the possibility of joint champions if the match finished drawn or tied. New Zealand won through the subsequent play; they were not awarded the trophy on a points-table or boundary-count tie-break.

## The first-innings lead was built from more than one score

India resumed their first innings at 146/3 and finished on 217. Jamieson’s 5/31 was central to that restriction. New Zealand’s reply also encountered trouble: they were 135/5 at lunch on the fifth calendar day. Williamson’s 49, followed by contributions of 21 from Jamieson and 30 from Tim Southee, helped turn that position into 249. [ICC fifth-day report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/media-releases/southee-stars-as-new-zealand-eye-victory-on-reserve-day).

From 135/5, New Zealand’s remaining five wickets added 114 team runs. That recovery mattered because it did more than close the deficit: it put India behind before their second innings began. A first-innings comparison based only on the highest individual score would miss that lower-order contribution.

## Two different 32-run leads

New Zealand’s first-innings advantage was 249 minus 217, or 32. India later closed the fifth day on 64/2, which put India 32 ahead overall. The identical number belonged to opposite teams at different stages. [End-of-day record](https://archive.nzc.nz/Scorecards/1171/1171545.html).

On the reserve day, Jamieson removed Kohli and Pujara early. Pant’s 41 helped extend the innings, but India lost their remaining eight wickets for 106 that day and finished on 170. With the earlier deficit deducted, their overall advantage was 138, leaving New Zealand a target of 139. [ICC completion report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/media-releases/williamson-and-taylor-steer-new-zealand-to-historic-victory).

## The chase still had a turning point

Ashwin removed both openers, leaving New Zealand 44/2. Williamson and Taylor then added an unbeaten 96 to end the match. Their final individual totals should not be added together to reconstruct that stand: Williamson had already scored before the second wicket fell. The partnership is the change in the team total during their time together. [Scorecard](https://archive.nzc.nz/Scorecards/1171/1171545.html).

Jamieson’s influence extended across both innings: seven wickets for 61 from 46 overs, including 22 maidens. Alongside those wickets, the low run concession limited India’s ability to enlarge the target while consuming time. This was one match’s workload and outcome, not a prediction about his future economy. [ICC bowling assessment](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/new-zealand-crowned-world-test-champions-after-thrilling-final-day).

The result is best understood as a sequence: India’s first innings was contained, New Zealand recovered enough to take a lead, the reserve day supplied lost time, and two experienced batters completed the chase after early wickets. None of those stages alone tells the whole story.
