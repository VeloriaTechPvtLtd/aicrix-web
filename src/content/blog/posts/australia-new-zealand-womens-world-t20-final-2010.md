Australia beat New Zealand by three runs at Kensington Oval on 16 May 2010 to win the Women’s World T20 title. Australia made 106/8 and New Zealand replied with 103/6, both from 20 overs. Ellyse Perry took 3/18 and bowled the final over.

## Two recoveries before the finish

Australia were 20/3 and had reached only 38/3 after 10 overs. New Zealand’s chase was in deeper trouble at 36/5 after 11 overs. Sophie Devine and Nicola Browne rebuilt the reply before Perry dismissed Browne for 20. Devine finished unbeaten on 38 from 35 balls.

At 36/5, New Zealand still needed 71 from 54 legal deliveries. Their eventual 103/6 shows how much ground the recovery made up: 67 runs arrived for one further wicket, but the innings ended before the winning target of 107 was reached.

## The last ball required five to win

New Zealand needed 14 from Perry’s final over. By the last delivery, the equation was five to win or four to tie. Devine drove straight, Perry deflected the ball with her right foot towards mid-on, and the batters completed only a single.

The observed deflection and single are enough to explain the finish. It is not necessary to claim the shot would certainly have reached the boundary without Perry’s intervention. Even a boundary four would have tied the scores rather than won the match outright.

## Three-run margin, four short of the target

Australia’s 106 minus New Zealand’s 103 gives the official three-run margin. The target was 107, so New Zealand finished four short of the score needed to win. Those are different calculations.

The final also separates wickets from remaining time. New Zealand finished with four wickets unused, but no deliveries left. Their recovery brought a difficult chase to the last ball; it did not erase the earlier loss of five wickets.
