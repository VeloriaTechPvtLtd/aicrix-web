**Kumar Sangakkara leads the checked men’s ODI wicketkeeping dismissal table with 482, ahead of Adam Gilchrist on 472.** MS Dhoni and Mark Boucher complete the four-player group with at least 400. These totals count catches made as wicketkeeper plus stumpings, not every dismissal in which the player was involved. [ACS career record](https://stats.acscricket.com/Records/ODI/Overall/Players/Most_Dismissals_by_Wicketkeeper_in_a_Career.html).

The distinction matters for players who sometimes fielded without the gloves. A career catches field and a wicketkeeping catches field can describe different sets of events. Adding stumpings to the wrong one can produce a plausible-looking but incorrect keeper total.

Sangakkara’s lead over Gilchrist is 10 dismissals. Gilchrist is 28 ahead of Dhoni, and Dhoni is 20 ahead of Boucher. The first-to-fourth difference is 58. Those are differences in accumulated career events, not percentages of missed chances or measures of reaction speed.

## Why Sangakkara’s other catches do not belong

[Guinness World Records’ 2015 certification](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/most-dismissals-by-a-wicket-keeper-in-a-one-day-international-career-%28male%29) records Sangakkara’s 482 keeper dismissals and separately identifies 19 catches taken when he was not keeping. Combining the two produces 501, but that larger figure is not his wicketkeeping dismissal record.

The classification follows the role in the particular innings, not the player’s usual job. A wicketkeeper-batter playing as an ordinary fielder does not turn an outfield catch into a keeping catch by virtue of their name or career reputation.

The same safeguard applies when comparing providers. First establish whether a catches column is restricted to keeping. Then add the stumpings belonging to the same format and career population. Do not import Test catches, franchise dismissals or run-out involvement into a men’s ODI keeper table.

## Dhoni and Gilchrist reached their totals differently

The [ICC’s June 2025 Hall of Fame release](https://www.icc-cricket.com/media-releases/mega-induction-sees-seven-new-names-added-in-icc-hall-of-fame) lists Dhoni’s ODI record as 321 catches and 123 stumpings. Their sum is **321+123=444**. The Guinness record gives Gilchrist 417 catches and 55 stumpings, which reconcile as **417+55=472**.

Stumpings therefore make up about **27.70%** of Dhoni’s recorded keeper dismissals, calculated as 123÷444×100. Gilchrist’s corresponding share is about **11.65%**, or 55÷472×100. These are composition percentages: they describe how the credited dismissals are divided.

They are not stumping success rates. To calculate success, a researcher would need a consistent definition and count of stumping opportunities, including unsuccessful ones. Neither 444 nor 472 supplies that denominator. Different bowling attacks and situations also prevent the composition difference alone from establishing who was the better keeper.

## A stable total can hide a component disagreement

Sangakkara’s headline total agrees, but the catch/stumping split does not match across every source. Guinness gives **383 catches and 99 stumpings**; ACS gives **384 and 98**. Both sum to 482. This table therefore reports the agreed total without presenting a falsely reconciled component breakdown.

That is a useful check when using any leaderboard: agreement on the last column does not prove agreement on every underlying classification. If a comparison specifically concerns catches or stumpings, resolve that component before drawing a conclusion. A total-only ranking cannot settle the discrepancy.

## Career appearances are not keeping opportunities

A dismissal-per-match calculation needs the right exposure measure. A player can appear in an ODI without keeping, and an appearance does not guarantee a completed opposition innings. Even keeping innings are not equal numbers of catching or stumping chances.

For a transparent comparison, show the numerator, identify the denominator and explain exclusions. Do not label total dismissals divided by career matches as a catch-conversion percentage. A career volume table, a rate per keeping innings and an opportunity-based success rate answer three different questions.

The four-player table locates the leading accumulated ODI keeping records. Keep a single-match performance separate from a career total, and keep credited dismissals separate from a judgement about keeping quality.
