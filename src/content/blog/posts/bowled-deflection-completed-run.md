An inside edge onto the stumps can be Bowled. A ball returning to those stumps after touching a fielder cannot produce the same dismissal. In both cases the ball breaks the wicket, but the intervening contact changes the legal route.

Even a completed run does not necessarily end the striker’s exposure to being bowled from that delivery. To explain an unusual incident, follow the ball from delivery to wicket rather than deciding from where the two batters finally stand.

This guide uses the [MCC 2026 Laws](https://www.lords.org/getattachment/e8b12929-1e84-4927-af4e-2a5875b53589/Laws-of-Cricket-2017-Code-4th-Edition-%282026%29.pdf) and [ICC men’s Test conditions effective October 2026](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/a8114zro5tkioifz0jqg.pdf). Its fictional cases assume the ball remains live, the wicket is fairly broken, an appeal is upheld where stated, and no competing earlier dismissal or match-ending event has occurred. They do not settle local tape-ball adaptations or historical incidents.

## The striker can deflect the ball onto the wicket

MCC Law 32.1.1 permits Bowled after the delivered ball touches the striker’s bat or person, provided it is not a no-ball. The striker need not miss completely. [MCC Law 32](https://www.lords.org/getattachment/e8b12929-1e84-4927-af4e-2a5875b53589/Laws-of-Cricket-2017-Code-4th-Edition-%282026%29.pdf)

Imagine an ordinary delivery hitting the inside edge, then the striker’s pad, then removing a bail. Those two contacts do not themselves prevent Bowled. Calling it hit wicket simply because the striker’s equipment participated would confuse the ball breaking the wicket with the batter’s bat or body doing so.

That distinction is useful when writing commentary: “played on” describes a path to the wicket, not a separate dismissal category. Describe the edge or rebound, but record the actual dismissal rather than inventing a new category for an untidy-looking result.

## Other contacts interrupt the Bowled route

Under Law 32.1.2, prior contact with another player, an umpire or the non-striker’s wicket rules out Bowled. Other dismissal provisions can still apply; the exception is not a universal not-out decision. [MCC Law 32](https://www.lords.org/getattachment/e8b12929-1e84-4927-af4e-2a5875b53589/Laws-of-Cricket-2017-Code-4th-Edition-%282026%29.pdf)

| Fictional path before the striker’s wicket breaks | Bowled classification |
| --- | --- |
| Delivery → striker’s bat → striker’s pad → wicket | Those contacts do not prevent Bowled |
| Delivery → striker’s bat → wicketkeeper’s pad → wicket | Not Bowled after another player’s contact |
| Delivery → striker’s bat → umpire → wicket | Not Bowled after umpire contact |
| Delivery → striker’s bat → non-striker’s wicket → striker’s wicket | Not Bowled after the other wicket’s contact |

The second row may require a stumping or run-out assessment, but the table does not supply the facts needed for either. Was a run attempted? Was the relevant batter out of their ground? Those questions cannot be answered merely by changing the dismissal label after Bowled is excluded.

## What if both batters have already changed ends

Consider an invented first-innings situation at 80/2. A lofts a legal delivery; neither a fielder, umpire nor the opposite wicket touches it. A and B complete one run. The ball lands, returns towards A’s original wicket and fairly breaks it while still live. Assume no boundary, safety stoppage or other event has ended play.

A is the striker from that delivery. The exchange of ends does not turn the untouched returning ball into a fielding run-out. Under the stated facts, A is Bowled even though A has reached the other end.

For a Bowled dismissal, the ordinary running runs are not credited. ICC clause 18.8 preserves penalties but does not give Bowled the completed-run allowance available for an ordinary run-out. Clause 18.11 returns the surviving batter to the original end. [ICC clauses 18.8 and 18.11](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/a8114zro5tkioifz0jqg.pdf)

With no penalties in this example, the score becomes 80/3, not 81/3. The physical single happened, but its scorebook credit does not survive that dismissal. A live feed briefly showing an extra run is not the final scoring decision.

## Change one fact and reassess

Now add a wicketkeeper’s touch before the ball reaches the wicket. Bowled is no longer available. Do not keep the 80/3 conclusion automatically: a different dismissal would need its own conditions, and its run treatment may differ.

A no-ball also excludes Bowled. And when Bowled’s conditions are fulfilled, clause 32.2 gives it precedence over another otherwise justified dismissal. These are reasons to establish the delivery and contact sequence before assigning a scorecard label. [ICC clause 32](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/a8114zro5tkioifz0jqg.pdf)

Keep the explanation focused on three questions: did the ball remain live, what did it touch, and which wicket did it break? Only then apply the relevant dismissal’s scoring rule. That method explains an unusual return to the stumps without assuming that every broken wicket is a run-out or every completed run must stand.
