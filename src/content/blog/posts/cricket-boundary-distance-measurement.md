A cricket ground does not have one boundary distance. The answer depends on the direction, the pitch being used and the boundary marked for that match. A stadium’s outside fence and a batter’s position are different reference points again.

For the ICC T20I measurements discussed here, the starting point is the centre of the selected pitch. These are not measurements from the striker’s stumps or a stadium’s geometric centre. A quoted distance therefore needs both a direction and a reference point. [Women’s T20I conditions, clause 19.1.3](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/o3vhm0zw7pppoxpgopke.pdf).

Under the [October 2026 men’s T20I conditions, clauses 19.1–19.2](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/bsxcho7ufe2pjban0wjp.pdf), umpires determine the boundary before the toss and it remains fixed for the match. A grounded boundary marker’s edge nearest the pitch defines the boundary. The ground’s perimeter fence need not be that edge.

The men’s conditions require three yards of safety run-off to the first solid object. Where the boundary is below 90 yards from pitch centre, its rope may be no more than ten yards from the perimeter fence, including that run-off. These are separate placement checks, not extra metres to add to the playing distance.

## Men and women have different T20I dimensions

The following table summarizes clause 19.1.3 in the respective October 2026 documents. The lower figure is worded as a recommended minimum, not an exception-free universal limit.

| Conditions | Maximum | Recommended minimum |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Men’s T20I | 90 yards, stated as 82.29 m | 65 yards, stated as 59.43 m |
| Women’s T20I | 70 yards, stated as 64 m | 60 yards, stated as 54.86 m |

Men’s clause 19.1.6 exempts previously approved international grounds unable to meet the minimum, requiring the playing area to be maximized. The aim generally is also to maximize the playing area. [Men’s T20I conditions](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/bsxcho7ufe2pjban0wjp.pdf).

For women, clause 19.1.5 covers grounds with an international match before November 2020, or under design or construction at that date, which cannot meet the minimum. A 55-yard minimum applies to that exception; new grounds must meet clause 19.1.3. The women’s conditions also require three yards of run-off. [Women’s T20I conditions](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/o3vhm0zw7pppoxpgopke.pdf).

These are international T20 conditions, not proof of the applicable IPL, WPL, junior or local-club specification. The [ICC’s 1 October announcement](https://www.icc-cricket.com/media-releases/new-icc-playing-conditions-bring-key-changes-across-formats) applies the updated conditions to international series starting on or after that date; ongoing series retain the previous version.

## Why the two square boundaries can differ

Consider an invented geometric example, not a ground survey. Along one straight line across a circular rope, the playing area spans 130 metres. A pitch centred on the circle would have 65 metres to either side along that line.

Now put the selected pitch centre five metres towards one side, leaving the rope unchanged. The two distances become 60 and 70 metres. Their sum remains 130 and their average remains 65, but the short side is now ten metres shorter than the long side.

An average of 65 would conceal the very asymmetry a viewer wants to understand. This example does not establish that real grounds are circular or that any particular rope is approved; it isolates the effect of moving the measurement origin.

Straight and square are also different directions relative to the pitch. A figure for one should not be relabelled as the other. Changing batting ends or the batter’s handedness changes the names of fielding positions, not the physical location of the rope.

## What to keep beside a boundary number

A useful record identifies the venue, fixture date, selected pitch if known, direction, units, measurement origin and source. Distinguish a reported match measurement from a map estimate or an undated stadium description.

A satellite view of a fence cannot establish where that match’s rope was placed. Nor does a boundary distance alone give a ball’s flight length, a guaranteed six or a fixed fantasy-points advantage. The right conclusion is more precise: this was the reported distance in this direction for this configuration.
