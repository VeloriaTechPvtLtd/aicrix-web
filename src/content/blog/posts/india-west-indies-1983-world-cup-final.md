India beat West Indies by 43 runs in the men’s World Cup final at Lord’s on 25 June 1983. Bowled out for 183, India dismissed the defending champions for 140 and won the tournament for the first time. Mohinder Amarnath was Player of the Match. [Wisden’s contemporary Almanack report](https://www.wisden.com/wisden-cricketers-almanack/1983-world-cup-final-india-v-west-indies-almanack-report)

## The scorecard belongs to a 60-over match

| Item | Completed record |
|---|---|
| Match | World Cup final, 25 June 1983 |
| Ground | Lord’s, London |
| Toss | West Indies chose to field |
| Allocation | 60 overs per side, six balls per over |
| India | 183 all out in 54.4 overs |
| West Indies | 140 all out in 52 overs |
| Target and margin | Target 184; India won by 43 runs |

The [ICC tournament overview](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/cricketworldcup/news/mens-cricket-world-cup-1983-overview) confirms the 60-over allocation; the [match scorecard](https://www.mykhel.com/cricket/india-vs-windies-scorecard-world-cup-s9992-43681/) records both innings lengths. This was not a modern 50-over innings extended by a delay. India used 328 legal balls and West Indies 312; both innings ended through dismissals before their scheduled 360-ball limits.

The initial requirement was 184 from 60 overs, approximately 3.07 runs per over. That describes the starting arithmetic, not the difficulty of facing the attack or a probability of winning. A low required rate cannot protect a chasing side from losing all ten wickets.

## India’s total came from several contributions

Kris Srikkanth top-scored with 38, Sandeep Patil made 27 and Amarnath 26. No Indian batter reached fifty. Andy Roberts took 3/32, while Malcolm Marshall, Michael Holding and Larry Gomes took two wickets each. [Complete batting and bowling ledger](https://www.mykhel.com/cricket/india-vs-windies-scorecard-world-cup-s9992-43681/)

At 111/6, India had lost much of its specialist batting. It nevertheless added another 72, including a last-wicket stand of 22 between Syed Kirmani and Balwinder Sandhu. Those last 22 represented just over 12% of the eventual total. Sandhu finished unbeaten on 11; Kirmani’s complete innings was 14. [Fall-of-wickets and innings record](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/7582/ind-vs-wi-final-prudential-world-cup-1983)

The stand should not be calculated by adding their final scores. Kirmani had already scored with earlier partners, while partnership totals also include extras. More broadly, the lower-order runs increased the target India could defend without making the final total large. They were part of the eventual winning score, not proof that India was already in control.

## Kapil’s catch was part of a sequence of wickets

Sandhu bowled Gordon Greenidge early. Viv Richards then made 33 before pulling Madan Lal towards the leg side, where Kapil Dev completed the running catch remembered as a turning point. The [ICC’s tournament overview](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/cricketworldcup/news/mens-cricket-world-cup-1983-overview) identifies the catch’s significance.

The wicket ledger gives a clearer sequence than treating that catch as the whole defence. Desmond Haynes fell at 50/2, Richards at 57/3, Larry Gomes at 66/4 and Clive Lloyd at the same score. Faoud Bacchus’s dismissal left West Indies 76/6. Madan Lal dismissed Haynes, Richards and Gomes; Roger Binny accounted for Lloyd. [Dismissal ledger](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/7582/ind-vs-wi-final-prudential-world-cup-1983)

Between 50/2 and 76/6, four wickets fell for 26 runs. The chase still had time available, but far fewer batters remained to score the 108 needed from that position. This is why the score and wickets must be read together rather than judging the chase only by its original asking rate.

## Dujon and Marshall made India work for the finish

Jeff Dujon and Malcolm Marshall added 43 for the seventh wicket, carrying West Indies from 76/6 to 119 before Amarnath bowled Dujon. The [Wisden Almanack report](https://www.wisden.com/wisden-cricketers-almanack/1983-world-cup-final-india-v-west-indies-almanack-report) highlights that recovery and Amarnath’s role in ending it.

At 119, another 65 were still required. The partnership had removed almost 40% of the 108-run deficit it inherited, but it had not completed the chase. After Dujon’s dismissal, the last three wickets fell for only 21 more runs. Marshall was caught off Amarnath, Roberts was lbw to Kapil, and Holding was lbw to Amarnath to end the match.

Amarnath finished with 3/12 in seven overs and Madan Lal with 3/31 in twelve. Their combined return was six wickets for 43 runs across 19 overs, or 2.26 runs per over. [Full bowling figures](https://www.mykhel.com/cricket/india-vs-windies-scorecard-world-cup-s9992-43681/). Equal wicket counts did not mean equal workloads; both the balls bowled and runs conceded belong beside the wickets.

West Indies ended 44 short of the winning target, but the recorded defeat was by 43 runs: the margin compares 183 with 140, not 184 with 140. Eight overs were unused because the side was all out. The final’s outcome rested on India taking ten wickets, not on running the scheduled clock down.
