**New Zealand beat South Africa by four wickets under the Duckworth/Lewis method at Eden Park on 24 March 2015**, reaching their first men's Cricket World Cup final. Grant Elliott hit the winning six with one ball remaining and finished unbeaten on 84. [NZC's contemporary report](https://www.nzc.nz/match-reports/archive/blackcaps-book-historic-first-cwc-final/).

The scorecard needs one important qualification: South Africa made 281/5, but New Zealand's winning target was **298 from 43 overs**. Comparing only the two final totals misses the central feature of this semifinal.

The [NZC archive scorecard](https://archive.nzc.nz/Scorecards/560/560900.html) preserves both the original 50-over match identity and the reduction to 43 overs per side. South Africa's actual runs remained 281; the revised target did not change any batter's individual score.

New Zealand used 257 of the available 258 legal balls. The final six took them beyond the target, which explains the 299 finish. The result was a four-wicket win, not an 18-run victory obtained by subtracting South Africa's actual total.

## Rain interrupted South Africa after they had rebuilt

Trent Boult removed both openers, leaving South Africa 31/2. Faf du Plessis and Rilee Rossouw added 83; du Plessis and AB de Villiers then put on 103. The rain interruption arrived with South Africa at 216/3. [NZC match account](https://www.nzc.nz/match-reports/archive/blackcaps-book-historic-first-cwc-final/).

After resumption, only five overs remained. South Africa added 65 runs in that passage, with David Miller making 49 from 18 balls. Du Plessis finished with 82 and de Villiers with an unbeaten 65. [Cricket Australia match report](https://www.cricket.com.au/news/3264888/ice-cool-kiwis-into-final-after-classic).

That closing burst ran at 13 per over, but it was not a forecast of what seven additional overs would have produced. Nor is the published target reproduced by simply adding 17 to every rain-interrupted first innings. The authoritative target for this specific match was 298; reconstructing the historical adjustment requires more than the final score.

## McCullum accelerated but the chase still needed rebuilding

Brendon McCullum made 59 from 26 balls before being caught off Morne Morkel. Kane Williamson, Martin Guptill and Ross Taylor also departed, and New Zealand were 149/4 when Taylor fell at 21.4 overs. [NZC archive scorecard](https://archive.nzc.nz/Scorecards/560/560900.html).

At that point they still needed 149 from 128 legal balls. Exactly half the winning target remained, but fewer than half the allotted deliveries: the early boundaries had not removed the need for a sustained middle-order partnership.

Elliott and Corey Anderson supplied it, adding 103 for the fifth wicket. Anderson's 58 ended at 252/5 after 38 overs, leaving 46 needed from 30 balls. [NZC innings ledger](https://archive.nzc.nz/Scorecards/560/560900.html).

The partnership mattered for its length as well as its scoring. It took the chase from four wickets down near halfway into a finish within reach of the remaining batters. It should not be confused with the sum of Elliott's and Anderson's full innings: Elliott also scored before and after their stand.

## The last over was not a one shot chase

New Zealand needed 12 from the final over. Daniel Vettori's boundary and subsequent single helped reduce the requirement to five from two balls. Elliott then struck Dale Steyn for six to complete the win. Vettori finished unbeaten on seven alongside Elliott. [Cricket Australia report](https://www.cricket.com.au/news/3264888/ice-cool-kiwis-into-final-after-classic).

Fielding chances had also mattered. Cricket Australia's report records Anderson surviving a missed run-out opportunity and Elliott surviving a late catching chance when two fielders collided. These were specific events in this match, not a reason to assign a general psychological label to the losing team.

The final shot is the lasting image, but the result depended on several contributions: the opening acceleration, the fifth-wicket stand and Vettori's support at the finish. Keep the event's ODI format, 43-over allocation and revised target together when reading or sharing this scorecard.
