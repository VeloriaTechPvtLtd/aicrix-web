West Indies won the 2004 Champions Trophy final by two wickets after an unbeaten ninth-wicket partnership of 71 between Courtney Browne and Ian Bradshaw. The most revealing detail is that they had time available even when almost all their wickets were gone.

## England’s 217 gave its bowlers a chance

Marcus Trescothick scored 104 in England’s 217 all out at The Oval on 25 September. Wavell Hinds took three for 24. West Indies then lost Shivnarine Chanderpaul for 47, leaving the chase at 147 for eight after 33.4 overs. [Contemporary scorecard](https://img.cricinfo.com/db/ARCHIVE/2004/OD_TOURNEYS/ICCCT/SCORECARDS/KNOCKOUTS/ENG_WI_ICCCT_ODI-FINAL_25SEP2004.html)

The remaining equation was 71 from 98 legal balls, approximately 4.35 runs per over. The immediate danger was running out of batters rather than an extreme scoring requirement. That does not make the chase easy; it identifies the particular problem the last recognised partnership had to solve.

## Familiar partners, not a desperate last over

In Cricket West Indies’ retrospective, Bradshaw recalls Browne calming him and emphasising that enough overs remained. Their familiarity as Barbados teammates is part of his account, rather than a mental state inferred from the scorecard. [CWI anniversary account](https://www.windiescricket.com/news/players-and-coach-recall-famous-victory-champions-trophy-2004/)

Browne finished on 35 not out and Bradshaw on 34 not out. West Indies reached 218 for eight after 48.5 overs, with seven balls unused. Their stand had lasted 91 legal deliveries, calculated from the eighth wicket and the finish.

## Why the 71 matters more than the margin

The two-wicket result compresses a substantial recovery into a single number. From 147 for eight, the partnership supplied nearly a third of the winning total without another dismissal. Bradshaw, who also took two wickets, received the player-of-the-match award.

This was not a last-ball escape or a chase won by one spectacular over. It was an extended ninth-wicket partnership that used the overs still available after England had taken control of the wicket column.
