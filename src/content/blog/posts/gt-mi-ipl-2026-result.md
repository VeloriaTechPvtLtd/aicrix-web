Tilak Varma's unbeaten 101 and Ashwani Kumar's four wickets supplied the standout individual performances. [Official IPL report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/tata-ipl-2026-match-30-gt-vs-mi-match-report).

## Tilak and Naman restored the innings before the late surge

Kagiso Rabada removed Danish Malewar, Quinton de Kock and Suryakumar Yadav, leaving MI 44/3. Naman Dhir made 45 from 32 and added 52 with Tilak before the fourth wicket fell at 96. Tilak then shared an 81-run stand with Hardik Pandya. [GT's match account](https://www.gujarattitansipl.com/news/gujarat-titans-vs-mumbai-indians-tata-ipl-2026-match-report).

The fourth wicket fell after 12.3 overs, which is 75 legal deliveries. From that point MI added **103 runs from the remaining 45 balls**, losing just one more wicket. That segment produced 13.73 runs per over. It includes runs from the whole partnership sequence and extras; it is not Tilak's personal strike rate.

Tilak finished on 101 from 45 balls, with eight fours and seven sixes. Those boundaries contributed 74 runs, leaving 27 from other scoring strokes. His 101 represented 50.75% of MI's team total. The calculation illustrates the concentration of scoring without implying that the other batters' partnerships or running were incidental.

MI's recovery therefore had two components: enough stability to reach the closing overs with a set batter, followed by much faster scoring. Describing the innings only as a hundred misses the earlier rebuilding work; describing it only as late hitting misses how the final platform was established.

## GT lost its route back into the chase

Jasprit Bumrah dismissed Sai Sudharsan with the first ball. Washington Sundar's 26 was GT's highest score; Shubman Gill made 14. Ashwani returned 4/24, while Mitchell Santner and AM Ghazanfar took two wickets each. Bumrah and Hardik supplied the other two wickets. [IPL innings account](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/tata-ipl-2026-match-30-gt-vs-mi-match-report).

The ten dismissals were thus distributed across five bowlers, not produced by the four-wicket spell alone. After the six-over mark GT added 55 runs in 59 legal balls while losing its final seven wickets. That is about 5.59 runs per over across the remainder actually played, well below the ten-per-over rate required at the start.

These are retrospective descriptions, not an estimate of the probability of recovery at any particular ball. They explain the recorded separation between the innings without inventing a model, a predicted total or a claim about which dismissal made defeat inevitable.

## What this archive can and cannot tell you

The useful historical answer is the sequence: Rabada created an early opening, MI rebuilt through Tilak and Naman, the closing 45 balls added 103, and GT could not sustain its chase after a similarly damaged powerplay. The final margin combines both innings rather than measuring Tilak or Ashwani in isolation.

Keep the date attached when using the result in a player or venue comparison. One April match does not establish today's pitch behavior, squad, bowling allocation or fantasy value. A later GT–MI result belongs in its own dated record, not silently inside this 2026 score.
