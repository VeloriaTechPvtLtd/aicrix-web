New Zealand beat India by 18 runs in the 2019 men’s ODI World Cup semifinal at Old Trafford, Manchester. Play began on 9 July and finished on the reserve day, 10 July. New Zealand made 239/8; India were dismissed for 221 with three balls unused. Matt Henry was Player of the Match. [NZC match archive](https://archive.nzc.nz/Scorecards/1065/1065913.html)

## The innings at a glance

| Team | Total | Overs | Leading scorers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| New Zealand | 239/8 | 50 | Ross Taylor 74, Kane Williamson 67 |
| India | 221 all out | 49.3 | Ravindra Jadeja 77, MS Dhoni 50 |

New Zealand chose to bat. The target was 240, not 239: India needed to exceed their opponent’s total to win. The final margin was 18 runs, calculated as 239 minus 221, rather than the 19 runs separating India’s score from the winning target. [Complete NZC-hosted scorecard](https://archive.nzc.nz/Scorecards/1065/1065913.html)

## Why the reserve day did not change the target

Rain stopped the first day with New Zealand 211/5 after 46.1 overs. The ICC announced that play would resume from that position, with New Zealand completing their innings and India retaining a full 50-over allocation. This was a continuation, not a replay or a fresh start. [ICC media advisory, 9 July 2019](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/media-advisory-india-v-new-zealand-old-trafford)

The notation matters: 46.1 means 46 completed six-ball overs plus one delivery, leaving 23 legal balls. New Zealand added 28 runs from those remaining balls and lost three more wickets. Their completed 239/8 therefore set an ordinary target of 240; the overnight interruption did not produce a reduced-over target.

## New Zealand built a total before defending it

New Zealand had lost Martin Guptill at 1/1. Williamson and Henry Nicholls then took the score to 69 before Nicholls fell for 28. Taylor’s 74 and Williamson’s 67 supplied the two largest contributions, but neither innings was a rapid burst: they occupied 90 and 95 balls respectively. Bhuvneshwar Kumar finished with 3/43. These are the components of the actual innings, not a claim that 239 was a generally sufficient score at Manchester. [Scorecard](https://archive.nzc.nz/Scorecards/1065/1065913.html)

## India lost three wickets before the chase could settle

Henry removed Rohit Sharma and KL Rahul, while Trent Boult dismissed Virat Kohli. All three scored one. India were 5/3 within 19 legal deliveries and 24/4 at the end of the tenth over. Mitchell Santner subsequently dismissed Rishabh Pant and Hardik Pandya, who each made 32. [ICC match report, 10 July 2019](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/cricketworldcup/news/henry-boult-take-new-zealand-to-world-cup-final)

At 92/6 after 30.3 overs, India required 148 from 117 legal balls. Describing that as “the last 20 overs” is a useful approximation for conversation, but not an exact chase equation: three balls of the 31st over had already been delivered.

## The recovery and the final two overs

Jadeja and Dhoni added 116 for the seventh wicket, taking India from 92/6 to 208/7. That partnership is not the sum of their final individual scores: Dhoni had already been batting before Jadeja arrived. Jadeja’s 77 ended when Williamson caught him off Boult at 47.5 overs. India then needed 32 from 13 balls. [NZC scorecard](https://archive.nzc.nz/Scorecards/1065/1065913.html)

India entered the final two overs needing 31. Dhoni hit a six but was run out attempting a second run when Martin Guptill’s direct hit broke the wicket. His dismissal left India 216/8 at 48.3: 24 still needed from nine legal balls. The remaining wickets fell before the allocation expired. [ICC report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/cricketworldcup/news/henry-boult-take-new-zealand-to-world-cup-final)

The result combines three distinct passages: New Zealand’s completed innings across two dates, India’s early loss of wickets and a seventh-wicket recovery that brought the target back within reach. The reserve day explains the calendar; it does not explain away the full-length chase or replace the closing sequence with a revised-target calculation.
