South Africa beat Sri Lanka in the rain-affected semifinal at Dhaka on 30 October 1998. The contemporary report records 240/7 from 39 overs, a revised target of 224 from 34 overs, Sri Lanka’s 132, and a published winning margin of 92 runs under Duckworth/Lewis. [Contemporary on-site report](https://img.cricinfo.com/link_to_database/ARCHIVE/1998-99/OD_TOURNEYS/WIC/SCORECARDS/RSA_SL_WIC_ODI-SF1_30OCT1998_CI365_MR.html)

## Preserve the labels, not just the numbers

Subtracting 132 from South Africa’s unadjusted 240 gives 108. That is the raw totals gap, not the published D/L result. The historical record explicitly labels 224 as the winning target and 92 as the result margin. These are retained as reported rather than used to reverse-engineer a par score or a historical resource calculation.

A modern calculator is not a substitute for the missing match-specific calculation inputs. This account explains what the surviving record says; it does not claim to reproduce the officials’ method or resolve its reporting convention.

## Kallis gave the shortened innings substance

Jacques Kallis made 113 not out from 100 balls and was player of the match. South Africa had been 57/3 before reaching 240/7. Steve Elworthy and Pat Symcox subsequently took three wickets each. [Full scorecard](https://i.imgci.com/link_to_database/ARCHIVE/1998-99/OD_TOURNEYS/WIC/SCORECARDS/RSA_SL_WIC_ODI-SF1_30OCT1998.html)

Kallis contributed about 47.1% of the total, with a personal strike rate of 113 runs per 100 balls. Those calculations describe his batting without attributing an undocumented tactical plan to him.

## Two details prevent false precision

Sri Lanka’s innings is recorded as all out at 23.4 overs, with Muttiah Muralitharan absent hurt. The notes also identify a five-ball tenth over. [Scoring notes](https://i.imgci.com/link_to_database/ARCHIVE/1998-99/OD_TOURNEYS/WIC/SCORECARDS/RSA_SL_WIC_ODI-SF1_30OCT1998.html)

An all-out entry here does not mean ten recorded dismissals. Nor does multiplying the displayed overs by six give an unqualified exact delivery count. Keeping those qualifications makes the archived result understandable without manufacturing a wicket, a delivery or a revised-target formula.
