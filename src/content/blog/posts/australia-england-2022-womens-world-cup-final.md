Australia won the Women's ODI World Cup final at Hagley Oval in Christchurch on 3 April 2022, defeating England by 71 runs. Alyssa Healy's 170 helped Australia reach 356/5. England were dismissed for 285 despite Nat Sciver remaining unbeaten on 148. Australia completed the tournament with nine wins from nine matches and claimed their seventh title. [Cricket Australia match report](https://www.cricket.com.au/news/3315925)

The final offers an unusually clear comparison between an outstanding innings supported by substantial partnerships and an outstanding innings that could not find enough support. Neither individual performance needs to be diminished to explain why the teams finished so far apart.

## Australia's acceleration had a foundation

Australia made 37 during the opening powerplay before expanding their scoring. Healy and Rachael Haynes put on 160, with Haynes contributing 68. Beth Mooney then joined Healy after being promoted in the batting order and made 62 from 47 balls. [ICC final report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/media-releases/australia-win-icc-womens-cricket-world-cup-2022-with-special-victory-over-england)

## Healy's 170 combined volume and acceleration

Healy faced 138 deliveries and struck 26 fours. Her first fifty took 62 balls, and her second required another 38. She was eventually stumped off Anya Shrubsole in the 46th over. [Cricket Australia account of Healy's innings](https://www.cricket.com.au/news/3315925)

Her fours alone contributed 104 runs. That arithmetic leaves 66 runs from other scoring outcomes; it should not be expanded into a claim about the exact distribution of singles and twos without checking the full delivery record.

The contrast between the two fifty-run intervals is also revealing. Scoring 50 from 62 balls and the next 50 from 38 is not a contradiction. It is evidence of a changing innings. An overall strike rate compresses those separate phases into one number and can conceal the timing of the acceleration.

Healy's score was a World Cup final record for either men or women when she made it. That is a historical milestone, not an unchecked assertion about every subsequent final. [ICC contemporary record account](https://www.icc-cricket.com/media-releases/australia-win-icc-womens-cricket-world-cup-2022-with-special-victory-over-england)

## England had a major innings but not an equivalent set of partnerships

Sciver's unbeaten 148 came from 121 balls. England's next-highest individual contribution was Tammy Beaumont's 27. Charlie Dean helped Sciver add 65 for the ninth wicket, but England's innings ended before they could use their full allocation. [Cricket Australia chase report](https://www.cricket.com.au/news/3315925)

## Why the unfinished overs matter

England were dismissed after 43 overs and four balls, with 38 legal deliveries unused. That means the chase ended through lost wickets rather than through the expiry of the innings allocation. [Full scorecard](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/32542/final-icc-womens-world-cup-2022)

In six-ball overs, 43.4 means 262 legal balls, not 43.4 decimal overs. Subtracting 262 from the scheduled 300 gives the unused 38. The calculation explains the available time; it does not assume England would have scored the remaining runs.

The target was 357, so they still needed 72 to win; the official defeat margin was 71 because Australia's total was 356.

The sound explanation is simpler: Australia built two major scoring partnerships around Healy, while Sciver carried a much larger share of England's reply. The final showcased two remarkable batting performances, but only one was embedded in an innings with enough collective support to win the trophy.
