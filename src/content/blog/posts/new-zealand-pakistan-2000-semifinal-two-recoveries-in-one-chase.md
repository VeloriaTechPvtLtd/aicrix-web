New Zealand beat Pakistan by four wickets at Nairobi on 11 October 2000. Pakistan made 252; New Zealand reached 255/6 in 49 overs. Shayne O’Connor’s 5/46 earned the player-of-the-match award. [New Zealand Cricket archive](https://archive.nzc.nz/Scorecards/70/70705.html)

## A century and a five-wicket haul

Saeed Anwar made 104. O’Connor removed Imran Nazir and then took Pakistan’s last four wickets, ending the innings at 252 in 49.2 overs. [Full scorecard](https://archive.nzc.nz/Scorecards/70/70705.html)

The last four wickets fell after the score reached 237, adding only 15. O’Connor’s five wickets were thus distributed between an early breakthrough and the closing phase, rather than one uninterrupted collapse.

## The first rebuilding stand

New Zealand slipped to 15/2. Nathan Astle and Roger Twose added 135 before Astle fell for 49. Twose made 87 and departed at 169. [Chase record](https://archive.nzc.nz/Scorecards/70/70705.html)

The 135-run stand covered approximately 53.4% of the winning target of 253. It transformed the chase, but neither participant was present to complete it. Their runs remained useful after their dismissals; their time at the crease could not be transferred to the next pair.

## A second repair became necessary

Adam Parore and Chris Harris fell on consecutive balls at 187. Craig McMillan, unbeaten on 51, and Scott Styris, unbeaten on 28, then added 68 to finish. [Dismissal sequence](https://archive.nzc.nz/Scorecards/70/70705.html)

At 187/6, the requirement was 66, with four wickets left. The final partnership reached 68 as New Zealand passed the target. It was smaller than the earlier stand but directly completed the remaining job.

The finish at 49 overs left six legal balls unused. This was not a chase solved once by a major partnership. New Zealand needed a substantial first recovery and then a separate response to consecutive dismissals. O’Connor’s earlier wickets helped contain the target those pairs had to pursue.
