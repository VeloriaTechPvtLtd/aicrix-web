New Zealand beat India by four wickets with two balls remaining in the 2000 ICC KnockOut final in Nairobi. The chase was built around Chris Cairns, but its decisive partnership did not last all the way to the winning run.

## Ganguly’s hundred set the target

Sourav Ganguly’s 117 and Sachin Tendulkar’s 69 helped India reach 264 for six on 15 October. Their opening stand was 141. New Zealand later slipped to 132 for five, leaving Cairns and Chris Harris to repair the chase. The ICC identifies their 122-run partnership as the central recovery. [ICC retrospective](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/10-classic-matches-at-icc-champions-trophy-x4450)

## The equation at five wickets down

The fifth wicket fell after 23.2 overs, leaving 133 runs needed from 160 legal balls: just under five runs per over. That calculation explains the opportunity. The target did not yet require a succession of boundaries, but another wicket would expose the lower order with much of the chase unfinished.

Harris made 46 from 72 balls before falling at 254 for six after 48.3 overs. The stand had reduced the remaining task to 11 runs from nine balls. Adam Parore joined Cairns for the finish. [Contemporary scorecard](https://img.cricinfo.com/db/ARCHIVE/2000-01/OD_TOURNEYS/ICCKO/SCORECARDS/IND_NZ_ICCKO_ODI-FINAL_15OCT2000.html)

## A hundred that survived the changing task

Cairns finished on 102 not out from 113 balls as New Zealand reached 265 for six in 49.4 overs. Harris had helped carry the innings from a vulnerable middle-order position to the final few runs, but he was not unbeaten at the end.

That distinction is worth preserving: the winning partnership and the partnership that made victory possible were different stands. The final margin records the finish; the movement from 132 for five to 254 for six explains how New Zealand got there.
