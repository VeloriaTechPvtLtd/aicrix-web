Virat Kohli scored 558 runs in six ODIs against South Africa in February 2018. Fakhar Zaman followed with 515 in five matches against Zimbabwe that July. This is a historical comparison of the records reported in 2018, not a live ranking. [ICC’s July 2018 table](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/fakhar-zaman-imam-ul-haq-march-into-the-record-books)

## The table as it stood in July 2018

| Batter | Opponent and year | Matches | Runs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Virat Kohli | South Africa, 2018 | 6 | 558 |
| Fakhar Zaman | Zimbabwe, 2018 | 5 | 515 |
| Rohit Sharma | Australia, 2013 | 6 | 491 |
| George Bailey | India, 2013 | 6 | 478 |
| Hamilton Masakadza | Kenya, 2009 | 5 | 467 |

The ICC listed these as the leading bilateral ODI series aggregates at the time. Bilateral means a series between two sides; a multi-team World Cup is a different comparison. [ICC record context](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/virat-kohli-master-of-the-50-over-game)

## Why 558 produced an average of 186

Kohli’s six scores were 112, 46 not out, 160 not out, 75, 36 and 129 not out. They sum to 558, with three dismissals. [ICC innings sequence](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/virat-kohli-master-of-the-50-over-game)

Dividing by six innings gives **93 runs per innings**. Dividing by three dismissals gives the batting average of **186**. These are not interchangeable. His three centuries supplied 401 runs, or approximately 71.9% of the aggregate; the other innings still added 157.

Fakhar’s reported average was 257.50, but his runs per innings were 515 ÷ 5 = **103**. Thus he had the higher average and runs per innings, while Kohli had the larger aggregate. The extra match and different dismissal counts matter when reading the table. [ICC’s Fakhar review](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/fakhar-zaman-imam-ul-haq-march-into-the-record-books)

Neither calculation measures opposition strength or adjusts for match conditions. It simply makes clear which achievement each number records.
