Rajasthan Royals beat Punjab Kings by six wickets in their only completed meeting of IPL 2026, at New Chandigarh on 28 April. Punjab made 222/4 in 20 overs; Rajasthan reached 228/4 in 19.2, with Donovan Ferreira finishing on 52 not out and Shubham Dubey on 31 not out.

The [official Rajasthan season schedule](https://www.rajasthanroyals.com/schedule-fixtures-results) contains this one league meeting, not a home-and-away pair. Its complete 16-match record also includes Rajasthan’s Eliminator against Hyderabad and Qualifier 2 against Gujarat, so there was no Punjab rematch in the playoffs.

## Punjab’s acceleration left a substantial target

Prabhsimran Singh made 59 from 44 balls, while Priyansh Arya’s 29 from 11 and Cooper Connolly’s 30 from 14 provided faster contributions. Marcus Stoinis then finished unbeaten on 62 from 22, taking Punjab to 222. These innings are documented in the [official IPL report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/tata-ipl-2026-match-40-pbks-vs-rr-match-report).

Punjab were 181/4 at the completion of 18 overs. The last two overs added 41 runs without another dismissal. That late segment matters because it raised the target from a strong platform to a chase requiring more than 11 runs per over from the outset. It does not mean Stoinis personally scored all 41: Suryansh Shedge also contributed in their final partnership. ## Punjab’s five bowling spells were very different

Rajasthan’s opening batters supplied 51 from 27 balls through Yashasvi Jaiswal and 43 from 16 through Vaibhav Sooryavanshi. But Yuzvendra Chahal removed Jurel, Jaiswal and Parag, while Harpreet Brar conceded only 25 from his four overs. The chase was not uniformly easy against every bowler.

Together, Brar and Chahal conceded 61 from 48 legal balls, or 7.63 runs per over. The three seamers conceded 166 from 68 legal balls, or 14.65. Their combined concessions total 227; the remaining run in Rajasthan’s 228 was a bye, not a bowler’s charge.

That comparison helps explain how a chase can contain economical spells and still finish at a high overall rate. It is an aggregate by bowling group, not a middle-overs-versus-death-overs split. Different batters, match situations and delivery choices were involved. These figures alone cannot isolate a pitch effect or prove that replacing a particular over with spin would have changed the result.

## Ferreira and Dubey finished from 151/4

Parag’s dismissal ended the 14th over at 151/4. Rajasthan still needed 72 from 36 legal balls. Ferreira and Dubey then added an unbeaten 77 across 32 balls to complete the chase. The [Cricbuzz partnership ledger](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/151943/pbks-vs-rr-40th-match-indian-premier-league-2026) separates that stand from their full individual scores.

The distinction is useful: Ferreira’s final 52 and Dubey’s 31 add to 83, not the partnership’s 77. Ferreira already had eight runs when Dubey arrived; the stand then contained 44 from Ferreira, 31 from Dubey and two extras. Reading the partnership rather than adding final scores preserves the actual finishing contribution.

By the start of the last over, RR were 221/4 and needed two. Dubey took a single from Jansen’s first delivery, then Ferreira hit the next for six. That sequence explains both the five-run overshoot beyond the target and the four balls remaining.
