Nine batters have reached at least 25 ODI hundreds in the career records checked on 14 November 2026. The threshold includes exactly 25, so all three players tied on that figure belong in the table. ## The recent hundreds behind the leading figures

Kohli’s 55th ODI century came against West Indies on 27 September 2026, an unbeaten 139 from 88 balls. Rohit reached his 35th three days later, scoring 101 from 75 in Guwahati. These are ODI milestones, not totals across Tests and T20 internationals. [ICC on Kohli](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/kohli-savours-challenge-of-playing-with-a-free-mind) and [ICC on Rohit](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/records-galore-as-india-west-indies-vie-for-the-odi-series?appview=true).

Their subsequent batting records through **13 November 2026** retain those hundred counts. Both were dismissed for ducks in that day’s ODI against New Zealand. That adds a batting innings to each career denominator without adding a century. [ICC match report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/young-henry-star-as-new-zealand-keep-india-odi-series-alive). A player can add appearances, innings and runs without changing the century column. That is why a recently updated run total should not be mistaken for a newly achieved hundred. ## The same century total can represent different careers

De Villiers, Gayle and Sangakkara are tied on 25 hundreds, but reached their final totals across 218, 294 and 380 batting innings respectively. That gives one hundred per 8.72, 11.76 and 15.20 innings. There is no reason to invent seventh, eighth and ninth place by using the order in which a website displays equal totals.

The innings measure adds context without erasing the tie. It says how frequently each career contained a hundred, not which player contributed more in every other kind of innings. A valuable 80 in a small chase remains outside the hundred count. [De Villiers](https://www.cricbuzz.com/profiles/370/de-villiers), [Gayle](https://www.cricbuzz.com/profiles/247/ChrisGayle) and [Sangakkara](https://www.cricbuzz.com/profiles/104/kumar-sangakkara).

Amla offers another useful contrast: 27 hundreds in 178 innings, or 6.59 innings per hundred. Jayasuriya made one more hundred but batted in 433 innings. The higher aggregate and the more frequent hundreds therefore belong to different players in this comparison. [Amla’s record](https://www.cricbuzz.com/profiles/314/amla) and [Jayasuriya’s record](https://www.cricbuzz.com/profiles/102/sanath-jayasuriya).

## What the frequency measure does not mean

Divide batting innings by hundreds, not matches by hundreds. A match in which the batter never batted contributes no batting innings. Conversely, an unbeaten innings still belongs in the denominator; removing not-outs would turn the calculation into a different and misleading measure.

A career ratio is not a countdown to the next century. It pools changing roles, opponents, venues, scoring environments and chase targets. Amla’s 6.59 does not mean that his centuries arrived at regular intervals, and Kohli’s 5.60 does not supply a probability for his next appearance.

## Keep the statistical categories separate

World Cup hundreds are already included in a player’s ODI career total; adding them again double-counts them. Test and T20I hundreds belong to other formats. A double-century is one innings of at least 100, not two separate centuries. The table answers how often a batter reached the milestone in ODIs, not how many blocks of 100 runs he accumulated.

The denominator is important even when the headline record stays unchanged. Kohli’s 308 innings divided by 55 hundreds gives exactly 5.60; Rohit’s 286 divided by 35 gives 8.17 after rounding. Neither figure should be carried forward unchanged after another innings simply because the century count has not moved. The retired-career figures for [Tendulkar](https://www.cricbuzz.com/profiles/25/sachin-tendulkar) and [Ponting](https://www.cricbuzz.com/profiles/38/ricky-ponting) provide stable comparisons, while active players require a fresh cutoff.
