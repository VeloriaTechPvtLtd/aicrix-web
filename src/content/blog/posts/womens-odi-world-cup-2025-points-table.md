**Australia finished first in the Women’s ODI World Cup 2025 league table with 13 points.** England, South Africa and India completed the top four and qualified for the semi-finals. India then won the tournament, defeating South Africa by 52 runs in the final on 2 November 2025. [ICC semi-final confirmation](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/womens-cricket-worldcup-2025/news/cwc25-semi-final-matchups-confirmed) · [ICC final report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/media-releases/india-clinch-historic-first-icc-women-s-cricket-world-cup-title).

This is the completed **2025 women’s 50-over competition**, separate from the Women’s T20 World Cup. League positions and knockout results are recorded separately because finishing first in the table did not decide the champion.

## Which teams qualified

Australia, England, South Africa and India qualified. Sri Lanka, New Zealand, Bangladesh and Pakistan were eliminated after the league stage.

All eight teams competed in one round-robin league, with each team playing the other seven. There were no separate groups or Super Six stage. The four highest-placed teams advanced, with first facing fourth and second facing third. [ICC tournament format](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/all-you-need-to-know-for-icc-women-s-cricket-world-cup-2025).

The resulting semi-finals were:

- **England versus South Africa:** Second against third, played in Guwahati on 29 October.

- **Australia versus India:** First against fourth, played in Navi Mumbai on 30 October.

England moved above South Africa by beating New Zealand on the last day of the league stage. Australia had secured first place with their victory over South Africa. [ICC semi-final matchups](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/womens-cricket-worldcup-2025/news/cwc25-semi-final-matchups-confirmed).

## How points and tiebreakers worked

A league win earned two points, a defeat earned none, and a no-result or abandoned match gave each team one point. Australia’s six wins and one abandoned fixture therefore produced 13 points, rather than the maximum 14. [NZC points key](https://archive.nzc.nz/Events/Tables/ICC_Womens_World_Cup_2025-26.html).

For teams level on points, the first tiebreaker was **number of wins**. NRR came next when both points and wins were equal. ICC’s published explanation of the tournament rules makes that order explicit. [ICC qualification rules](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/womens-cricket-worldcup-2025/news/remaining-scenarios-for-teams-still-in-cwc25-semi-final-race).

Bangladesh and Pakistan illustrate the distinction. Both finished with three points, but Bangladesh had one win while Pakistan had none. Bangladesh therefore ranked higher without needing NRR to decide the position.

NRR also did not override points. South Africa qualified comfortably with 10 points despite a negative NRR, while India finished below them with seven points and a positive NRR. Sorting the table by NRR alone would give the wrong qualification order.

## How India secured fourth place

India confirmed their semi-final place by beating New Zealand on 23 October, before their last league match against Bangladesh.

That victory took India to six points from three wins. Even if another contender had drawn level on six points, India’s number of wins secured the advantage under the first tiebreaker. Their qualification did not depend on maintaining a particular NRR in the Bangladesh match. [ICC confirmation of India’s qualification](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/womens-cricket-worldcup-2025/news/four-semi-finalists-confirmed-for-cwc25).

The Bangladesh fixture subsequently ended without a result because of rain. India received one further point and completed the league stage in fourth place with seven. [ICC final league-day report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/womens-cricket-worldcup-2025/news/live-two-semi-finalists-in-action-as-double-header-hits-cwc25).

## Semi-final and final results

**South Africa beat England by 125 runs.** South Africa made 319/7 in the first semi-final before dismissing England for 194. Laura Wolvaardt scored 169 and Marizanne Kapp took 5/20, sending South Africa into the final. [ICC match report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/media-releases/wolvaardt-and-kapp-inspire-south-africa-to-famous-victory).

**India beat Australia by five wickets.** Australia scored 338 in the second semi-final. India reached 341/5 in 48.3 overs, led by Jemimah Rodrigues’ unbeaten 127 and Harmanpreet Kaur’s 89. Australia’s unbeaten league campaign ended with elimination in the knockout round. [ICC match report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/media-releases/awesome-rodrigues-leads-india-to-icc-women-s-cricket-world-cup-2025-final).

**India beat South Africa by 52 runs.** In the final at Navi Mumbai, India scored 298/7 and bowled South Africa out for 246 to claim their first Women’s Cricket World Cup title. Deepti Sharma contributed 58 and took 5/39. [ICC final report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/media-releases/india-clinch-historic-first-icc-women-s-cricket-world-cup-title) · [BCCI final scorecard](https://www.bcci.tv/matches/2b893690-23b7-40c7-b3b5-fce9834d336f/india-women-vs-south-africa-women/scorecard).

India’s fourth-place league finish and their championship win describe different stages. The table decided who reached the semi-finals; victories in the semi-final and final decided the champion.
