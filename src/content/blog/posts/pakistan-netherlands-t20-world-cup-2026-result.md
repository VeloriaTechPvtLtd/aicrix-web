Pakistan beat the Netherlands by three wickets in the opening match of the 2026 men's T20 World Cup at the Sinhalese Sports Club Ground in Colombo on 7 February. The Netherlands were dismissed for 147 in 19.5 overs; Pakistan reached 148/7 in 19.3. Faheem Ashraf was Player of the Match for an unbeaten 29 from 11 balls. [BCCI match record](https://www.bcci.tv/matches/3fcda428-d2d2-4b8e-859b-ebe32b249acf/pakistan-vs-netherlands/match-details).

The winning margin conceals a chase that changed character twice. Pakistan initially scored quickly enough to reduce the asking rate, then lost wickets in the middle overs, and finally needed a concentrated burst of boundary hitting to avoid defeat.

## The Netherlands lost wickets at the finish

Pakistan won the toss and fielded. Scott Edwards led the Netherlands with 37 from 29 balls. At the end of 16 overs, his dismissal left the side 127/5. The innings ended 23 legal balls later with only another 20 runs added. [Full scorecard](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/138953/ned-vs-pak-1st-match-group-a-icc-mens-t20-world-cup-2026).

That closing segment produced runs at 5.22 per over. It does not mean every delivery was defensive or that a particular final total was inevitable; it quantifies how much the scoring slowed while the remaining wickets disappeared. The last wicket fell with one ball unused in the original allocation.

## The middle overs reversed the chase

Sahibzada Farhan made 47 from 31 deliveries, but his dismissal was followed by further damage. Pakistan went from 98/2 to 114/7, losing five wickets while adding 16. The ICC report identifies that spell as the passage that brought the Netherlands back into the contest. [Official opening-day report, Pakistan section](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/mens-t20-world-cup-2026/news/pakistan-v-netherlands-t20-world-cup-opener).

After Shadab Khan fell at 16.1 overs, Pakistan needed 34 from 23 legal balls. That is a required rate of 8.87 per over. The number was manageable in isolation, but only three wickets remained. A required rate without a wicket count would miss the central pressure in the chase.

By the end of the eighteenth over, the requirement had tightened to 29 from 12. It was no longer a question of gradually working through the target: Pakistan needed 14.50 runs per over across the remaining two.

## The nineteenth over changed the result

Faheem hit three sixes in the penultimate over, leaving five needed from the last. His final boundary completed the chase with three balls remaining. The ICC's account documents that decisive late acceleration rather than a comfortable finish from the position Pakistan had held earlier.

The equation changed by 24 runs in six legal balls, removing 82.76% of the 29-run requirement. That is a team-over calculation, not a claim that every run in the over came from Faheem's bat. His complete innings contained two fours and three sixes: 26 boundary runs out of 29, or 89.66%.

Faheem and Shaheen Afridi's unbeaten partnership added 34 in 20 legal balls. Its scoring rate was 10.20 per over, but the stand was not uniform: the late burst compensated for the quieter deliveries before it.

Pakistan ultimately used 117 of its 120 available legal balls. Three wickets in hand therefore did not signal an easy chase. The archive's defining contrast is between the middle-order collapse and the brief, boundary-heavy recovery that finished the match, not a prediction about either team's next meeting.
