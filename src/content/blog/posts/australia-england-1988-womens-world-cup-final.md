Australia beat England by eight wickets at the Melbourne Cricket Ground on 18 December 1988. It completed Australia’s third consecutive Women’s World Cup title, following seven wins in eight preliminary matches. [ICC tournament history](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/womens-world-cup-a-trip-down-memory-lane)

## Five bowlers shared the full allocation

England made 127/7 from 60 overs. Lyn Fullston took 3/29 and Karen Brown conceded only 15 runs without taking a wicket. Each of Australia’s five bowlers delivered 12 overs. Janette Brittin finished unbeaten on 46. [Full scorecard](https://i.imgci.com/link_to_database/ARCHIVE/WORLD_CUPS/WWC88/AUS-WOMEN_ENG-WOMEN_WWC88_WODI-FINAL_18DEC1988.html)

Brown’s figures illustrate a contribution that wicket rankings can miss. Fifteen runs from 12 overs is an economy rate of 1.25, compared with England’s overall scoring rate of approximately 2.12. The team rate includes extras that are not all charged to bowlers, so the two figures are not interchangeable accounting measures. They nevertheless show how restricted the innings was.

Five spells of 12 overs add to the full 60-over allocation. This was not a modern 50-over final; describing those spells without the match length could make them look anomalous.

## Two early wickets did not decide the chase

Australia slipped to 3/1 and then 14/2. Lindsay Reeler finished unbeaten on 59 and Denise Annetts on 48 as Australia reached 129/2 in 44.5 overs. [Scorecard](https://i.imgci.com/link_to_database/ARCHIVE/WORLD_CUPS/WWC88/AUS-WOMEN_ENG-WOMEN_WWC88_WODI-FINAL_18DEC1988.html)

The unbroken stand was 115: the final total of 129 minus 14 at the second wicket. It should not be calculated simply by adding the two batters’ final scores, because those figures include different starting points and exclude extras.

## Time and wickets tell the fuller story

Australia needed 128 to win and finished on 129. The eight-wicket margin records wickets remaining, while the finish at 44.5 overs left 91 legal balls unused in a 60-over chase: 360 minus 269.

The result therefore reflects both recovery and control. England created an early opening with the ball, but Australia’s third-wicket stand completed the chase without another dismissal. The modest target allowed the surviving batters to rebuild without needing an immediate high scoring rate.
