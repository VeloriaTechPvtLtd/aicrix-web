Delhi Capitals and Royal Challengers Bengaluru split their **two men's IPL 2026 meetings 1–1**. Delhi won in Bengaluru on 18 April; RCB won the return fixture in Delhi on 27 April. Both victories came while chasing, but the similarity ends there: one finished with a ball remaining, while the other left 81 balls unused.

This archive covers the men's IPL teams, not their Women's Premier League sides. It is also a single-season comparison, not an all-time head-to-head record.

## Delhi recover to win in Bengaluru

Phil Salt's 63 from 38 balls led RCB's batting, but Delhi prevented that start from becoming a larger total. Axar Patel took 2/18 in three overs and Kuldeep Yadav 2/32 in four. Together, the spinners returned four wickets for 50 runs across seven overs.

Bhuvneshwar Kumar's 3/26 then threatened Delhi's chase. KL Rahul made 57 from 34 deliveries and Tristan Stubbs stayed unbeaten on 60 from 47, giving Delhi a route back into the contest. David Miller provided the finish with 22 not out from ten balls.

Delhi needed 15 from the last over. Miller's late boundaries helped complete the chase with one delivery left, and Stubbs was named Player of the Match. Those performances explain why this was a recovery followed by a pressured finish, rather than a comfortable pursuit from the outset. ## RCB opening bowlers dominate in Delhi

The return match was decided much earlier. Delhi were 13/6 after the powerplay. Josh Hazlewood finished with 4/12 in 3.3 overs and Bhuvneshwar with 3/5 in three overs: seven wickets for just 17 runs between them.

Abishek Porel's 30 from 33 balls and Miller's 19 helped Delhi reach 75, but the target remained small. RCB finished at 77/1, with Devdutt Padikkal unbeaten on 34 from 13 balls and Virat Kohli unbeaten on 23 from 15. Hazlewood received the Player of the Match award.

The [official Match 39 report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/tata-ipl-2026-match-39-dc-vs-rcb-match-report) records the nine-wicket result. It was not merely a faster version of the Bengaluru chase: Delhi's early loss of wickets had radically reduced the target.

## What the numbers show

The scorelines make the difference in chase duration precise. Delhi used **119 legal balls** in Bengaluru: 19 × 6 + 5. RCB used **39 legal balls** in Delhi: 6 × 6 + 3. The difference was 80 balls, with one and 81 balls remaining respectively. Cricket notation matters here: 6.3 overs means six overs and three balls, not 6.3 decimal overs.

A second comparison is less obvious. Delhi scored 254 runs across the two matches; RCB scored 252. That two-run aggregate edge did not make Delhi the winner of a two-leg contest. Each match was decided separately, and the successful chasing side stopped once it passed its target. Adding the innings cannot produce a fair measure of unused batting capacity.

The useful summary is therefore **one win each, with sharply different match situations**. Two successful chases do not establish a universal venue or toss advantage. For a future meeting, these results are historical context, not a substitute for the current XI, conditions or available players.

## Two different bowling comparisons

Axar and Kuldeep’s combined seven overs in Bengaluru cost 50 runs, an economy rate of about 7.14. Hazlewood and Bhuvneshwar’s combined return in Delhi was 17 runs from 39 legal balls, about 2.62 per over. The second calculation uses 3.3 overs as 21 balls, then adds Bhuvneshwar’s 18. It does not treat 3.3 as a decimal.

These are descriptions of different attacks in different matches, not a controlled comparison of player ability. Keep the venue, opposition, innings position and wickets alongside the rate. The more informative distinction is that Delhi’s spinners helped contain a substantial Bengaluru innings, whereas RCB’s opening bowlers removed six batters within the Delhi powerplay.
