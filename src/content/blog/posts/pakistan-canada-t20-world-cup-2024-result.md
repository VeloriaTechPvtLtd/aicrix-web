Pakistan beat Canada by seven wickets at Nassau County International Cricket Stadium in New York on 11 June 2024. Canada made 106/7 in 20 overs; Pakistan reached 107/3 in 17.3 overs. The victory kept Pakistan in contention for the men's T20 World Cup Super Eight, but it did not secure qualification. [ICC match report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/t20cricketworldcup/news/live-pakistan-win-toss-and-field-against-canada-in-crucial-group-a-clash).

That distinction is the key to this match archive. Pakistan needed the points after opening defeats against the USA and India. Winning the Canada game answered the immediate question; the remaining Group A results still determined whether Pakistan could advance.

## Match details

- Date: 11 June 2024.

- Competition: men's T20 World Cup, Match 22, Group A.

- Venue: Nassau County International Cricket Stadium, New York.

- Toss: Pakistan elected to field.

- Result: Pakistan won by seven wickets, with 15 balls remaining.

The [full scorecard](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/87647/pak-vs-can-22nd-match-group-a-icc-mens-t20-world-cup-2024) records both innings, dismissals and bowling figures.

## Canada's innings depended heavily on Aaron Johnson

Johnson scored 52 from 44 balls, accounting for 49.06% of Canada's total. His four fours and four sixes produced 40 runs, or 76.92% of his own score. Those are different denominators: one measures his share of the team's innings, the other how his runs were accumulated.

Mohammad Amir returned 2/13 from four overs, while Haris Rauf took 2/26. Amir's exact economy was 3.25 runs per over. The five Pakistan bowlers each completed four overs, so the comparison is not distorted by differing workloads. Amir conceded half as many runs as Rauf over the same number of deliveries, although economy alone does not measure the value of particular wickets.

Canada's 106 was a team total, not the sum of batter runs alone. Thirteen extras were included. That matters when comparing Johnson with his teammates or reconciling the innings with the bowling card.

## Pakistan used most of its allotted deliveries

Mohammad Rizwan finished unbeaten on 53 from 53 balls, and Babar Azam made 33 from 33. Their second-wicket partnership added 63 from 62 deliveries. Pakistan preserved enough batting resources to get home, but the chase occupied most of its allocation. [ICC's account of the chase](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/t20cricketworldcup/news/live-pakistan-win-toss-and-field-against-canada-in-crucial-group-a-clash).

Seventeen overs and three balls equals 105 legal balls, not 17.3 decimal overs. Pakistan therefore used 87.5% of the available 120 deliveries and left 15 unused. Its innings scoring rate was 107 divided by 17.5 actual overs, or 6.11 runs per over.

That is an innings rate, not Pakistan's tournament net run rate. A tournament calculation combines the relevant innings across matches and applies competition rules. Subtracting Canada's rate from this chase's rate would not reproduce Pakistan's cumulative group NRR.

## What happened to qualification

The decisive later event was the USA–Ireland washout on 14 June. The teams shared points, taking the USA to an unassailable five. Pakistan was eliminated before playing its final group fixture against Ireland. [ICC qualification report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/live-usa-v-ireland-delayed-as-outcome-of-group-a-hangs-in-the-balance).

With only two points from the Canada win and one match left, Pakistan could reach a maximum of four. No improvement in net run rate could overcome that points gap. The accurate conclusion is that Canada was Pakistan's first victory of the campaign, not the match that booked a Super Eight place.
