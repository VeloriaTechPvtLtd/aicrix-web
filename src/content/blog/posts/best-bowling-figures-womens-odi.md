**Sajjida Shah’s 7/4 against Japan leads the checked women’s ODI bowling-figures record.** Seven innings have reached seven wickets in the 28 October 2026 source snapshot used here. The most recent addition is Melie Kerr’s 7/34 against Zimbabwe in March 2026. [ACS women’s ODI record](https://stats.acscricket.com/Records/Womens_ODI/Overall/Bowling/Best_Bowling_in_an_Innings_by_BB.html), [ICC seven-wicket table](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/new-zealand-star-breaks-44-year-record-with-seven-wicket-haul).

All seven bowlers took the same number of wickets, so fewer runs conceded determines their order. This is an innings-figures ranking, not an economy-rate table or a ranking of complete careers.

## A map for finding the right match

The seven performances were recorded at seven different grounds. Shah’s came at Sportpark Drieburg in Amsterdam; Chamberlain’s at Sportpark Koninklijke HFC in Haarlem. Mohammed’s was at Shere Bangla National Stadium in Mirpur, King’s at Holkar Stadium in Indore, Perry’s at St Lawrence in Canterbury, Nitschke’s at Chester Road in Kidderminster and Kerr’s at University Oval in Dunedin. [ACS venue ledger](https://stats.acscricket.com/Records/Womens_ODI/Overall/Bowling/Best_Bowling_in_an_Innings_by_BB.html).

Those ground names help distinguish a specific innings from another meeting between the same countries. They also prevent the season labels used by some records sites, such as 2025/26, from being mistaken for an uncertain calendar date.

## What Kerr’s record actually changed

Kerr’s 7/34 displaced Jackie Lord’s 6/10 as New Zealand’s best women’s ODI return. Lord’s figures had been recorded against India in the 1982 World Cup. Kerr took her seven against Zimbabwe in the second ODI in Dunedin on 8 March 2026, when Zimbabwe were dismissed for 102. [ICC’s contemporary report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/new-zealand-star-breaks-44-year-record-with-seven-wicket-haul).

That was a national record, not a new global best. Kerr became the seventh seven-wicket performer and entered seventh in the figures order shown here. A headline can correctly say that she broke a 44-year record without implying that she overtook Shah’s 7/4.

The distinction is useful whenever a milestone story omits its filter. Ask whether the record is national, global, tournament-specific, on debut or tied to a particular ground before comparing it with another headline.

## Same wickets does not mean the same spell

Shah conceded four runs and Kerr 34 while each took seven wickets. The difference is 30 runs, but the table alone does not explain why. Opposition, conditions, match length and the circumstances of each wicket are not captured by those two numbers.

A simple spell average illustrates a separate measure: 4÷7 is about 0.57 runs per wicket, while 34÷7 is about 4.86. Neither is an economy rate. Economy requires balls bowled; it cannot be reconstructed from wickets and runs alone.

Nor should the lower-concession innings automatically be described as the more difficult or valuable performance. The figures record uses a transparent sorting rule. A match-impact comparison would require a different question and additional evidence.

## Chronology is different from the ranking

Chamberlain’s 1991 innings is the earliest performance in this group. Shah’s later 2003 return leads the figures order. King’s 2025 and Kerr’s 2026 performances are the two latest additions in the checked list, but their places are determined by runs conceded, not recency.

Three of the seven qualifying innings belong to Australian bowlers. That is a count of these record performances, not a share of all ODI wickets or a measure of the current national attack. The group covers five national teams and should not be reduced to whichever names are most familiar to a particular audience.

## Use the correct record for your question

For a single remarkable ODI spell, use this innings table. For career accumulation, use the separate women’s ODI wicket-leaders resource. World Cup-only figures are another subset; not every ODI in this table was played in the World Cup proper.

Keep the snapshot date attached when reusing the list. A later seven-wicket return should be inserted at its correct place, and a future eight-wicket return would lead the wicket-first ranking regardless of conceding more than four runs. The threshold and sorting rule, rather than a fixed number of rows, determine what belongs.
