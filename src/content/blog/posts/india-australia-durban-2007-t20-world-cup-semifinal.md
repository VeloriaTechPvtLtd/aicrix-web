India beat Australia by 15 runs at Kingsmead, Durban, on 22 September 2007 to reach the inaugural men’s World Twenty20 final. India made 188/5 and restricted Australia to 173/7. Yuvraj Singh’s 70 from 30 balls earned the match award, but the defence still required India to break a dangerous Australian recovery. [Contemporary PTI report](https://www.rediff.com/cricket/report/india1/20070922.htm)

## Yuvraj changed the pace after eight overs

India were only 41/2 after eight overs. Yuvraj then attacked immediately, hitting his second ball for six, and ultimately struck five sixes during his 30-ball innings. This was a different match from his six-sixes over against England three days earlier, even though both were played at Kingsmead. [ICC retrospective](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/yuvraj-singhs-greatest-hits)

From 41 after eight overs to 188 after 20, India added 147 in 12 overs: a calculated 12.25 per over. The opening eight had produced 5.13 per over. That change is more informative than simply quoting the final average of 9.40, which smooths two very different scoring periods into one number.

Robin Uthappa made 34 and MS Dhoni 36 from 18 balls. Yuvraj’s 70 was the largest contribution, but it was not the entire recovery. The 84-run third-wicket stand ended at 125 when Uthappa was run out; Yuvraj later fell at 155. [Batting and wicket ledger](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/3686/aus-vs-ind-2nd-semi-final-icc-world-t20-2007)

Those 84 partnership runs were added in 39 legal balls, from the end of the eighth over to 14.3. Their calculated rate was 12.92 per six balls. A partnership includes both batters’ runs during that stand and any extras; adding their complete individual scores would measure something different.

## Hayden and Symonds kept Australia in contention

Matthew Hayden and Andrew Symonds added 66 for the third wicket in six overs. Sreesanth bowled Hayden in the 15th over, and Irfan Pathan later bowled Symonds. These were separate breaks in the chase, not two wickets in the same over. [BCCI semifinal recap](https://www.bcci.tv/news/article/icc-world-t20-rewind-india-win)

The required rate actually fell slightly between those dismissals, from a calculated 10.31 to 9.90 per over. Yet both established scorers were now gone. The numerical task had improved while the batting personnel had changed, illustrating why an asking rate alone cannot describe the position. ## Sreesanth supplied control as well as wickets

Sreesanth conceded 12 in four overs, including a maiden, and dismissed both openers: Gilchrist for 22 and Hayden for 62. His economy was 3.00, compared with the team’s final concession of 173 across 20 overs. The latter includes extras not charged to bowlers, so it is a team scoring rate, not an individual bowling economy. [Full bowling figures](https://www.mykhel.com/cricket/india-vs-australia-scorecard-icc-world-t20-s9920-122505/)

Harbhajan Singh finished with 1/24 and removed Michael Clarke. Pathan and Joginder Sharma each took two wickets, although their spells cost 44 and 37 respectively. The defence combined an economical spell with wickets from several bowlers; its success cannot be reduced to one player’s average rate. [Bowling ledger](https://www.mykhel.com/cricket/india-vs-australia-scorecard-icc-world-t20-s9920-122505/)

## Joginder closed the match

Australia entered the final over needing 22. Joginder began with two dots, dismissed Michael Hussey, conceded two, then bowled Brett Lee. Mitchell Johnson hit the last ball for four, completing a six-run over rather than a successful chase. [PTI’s closing sequence](https://www.rediff.com/cricket/report/india1/20070922.htm)

The final over began at 167/5 and ended at 173/7. India had secured a place in the final against Pakistan, not yet the trophy itself. The [separate title match against Pakistan](/blog/india-pakistan-2007-t20-world-cup-final) followed in Johannesburg on 24 September. Keep those two results apart when looking up India’s 2007 knockout run.
