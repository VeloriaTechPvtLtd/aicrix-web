title: "How WTC points percentage is calculated"
excerpt: "Calculate net points and percentage with worked examples, deductions and the right denominator."
status: draft
---

**WTC points percentage is a team’s net competition points divided by the maximum available from its counted Tests, multiplied by 100.** It is not win percentage, net run rate or the share of points held by all teams combined.

Under the [October 2026 WTC playing conditions](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/jrwhpcwk8mmpv0fx3lnd.pdf), a win earns 12 points, a tie six, a draw four and a loss zero. Slow-over-rate deductions reduce the numerator. Every counted Test contributes 12 to the denominator, regardless of the result.

## A worked example

Imagine a team with eight completed, counted Tests: four wins, two draws and two defeats, with no ties. This is a hypothetical record, not a current national team’s standings.

| Calculation step | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Four wins | 4 × 12 = 48 points |
| Two draws | 2 × 4 = 8 points |
| Two defeats | 0 points |
| Gross points | 56 |
| Hypothetical deduction | 3 points |
| Net points | 53 |
| Maximum available | 8 × 12 = 96 |
| Points percentage | 53 ÷ 96 × 100 = 55.21% |

Before the deduction, the same record produces 58.33%. Losing three competition points reduces it by 3.125 percentage points, or 3.13 after rounding. It does not produce 55.33%, which would be the result of simply subtracting three from the displayed percentage.

The denominator explains the difference. Three deducted points across eight Tests have a larger percentage effect than three across 12 Tests: 3/96 × 100 is 3.125, while 3/144 × 100 is about 2.083. A sanction’s raw points and its percentage impact are different quantities.

## More points do not always mean a higher position

Suppose Team A has 72 points from ten Tests and Team B has 60 from eight. A has more points, but its percentage is 60.00%; B’s is 62.50%. The comparison divides by 120 and 96 respectively. It does not award Team A a higher place merely for having played more matches.

Nor is the percentage a forecast. It summarizes the credited record at a cutoff. It does not estimate the strength of future opponents, the chance of winning a final or the probability of finishing in the top two.

## Count the right matches and use the right tiebreak

The [competition regulations](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/doztximb3sxhwcc4oo3f.pdf) exclude matches that do not take place because of acceptable non-compliance from this calculation. Do not automatically treat every unplayed fixture as a draw. The rules also make a specific provision for an abandoned match with an officially unfit pitch or outfield, so the official points allocation matters.

At the end of the round stage, equal points percentages are separated by series wins, then away points percentage, then the specified ICC Test ranking criterion. Net run rate is not the tiebreak. Keep the unrounded fraction for calculations and round only for display; two displayed values can look identical without being exactly equal.

## What a useful standings record should retain

A reproducible table needs the cycle, counted matches, result points, deductions and the effective date of each change. Keep the match-completion timestamp separate from a later sanction announcement. A correction should explain what changed, not silently replace a historical snapshot with today’s figure.
