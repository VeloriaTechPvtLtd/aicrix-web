## Pakistan recovered from 61/5 to beat India U19

Pakistan beat India by **three wickets** in their men’s U19 Asia Cup group match at Al Amerat on **8 November 2026**. India were dismissed for 208 in 46.3 overs; Pakistan reached 209/7 in 48.1. The winning margin was wickets remaining, not the one-run difference between the final totals. [BCCI completed match record](https://www.bcci.tv/matches/88a0848a-2f7c-4a65-9c53-5aff839b969c/india-under-19s-vs-pakistan-under-19s)

Two centuries shaped the game. Arjun Rajput made 107 from 85 balls for India, while Hamza Zahoor finished unbeaten on 100 from 108 for Pakistan. Both sides had to recover from losing early wickets, but Pakistan’s lower-order partnership survived long enough to complete the chase. ## Rajput’s century rebuilt India’s innings

India were 42/4 when Aaryavir Sehwag was dismissed, and 86/5 after Anvay Dravid fell. Rajput made 107 with eleven fours and five sixes before becoming the seventh wicket at 170. Kavya Patel’s 23 was the next-highest individual score, narrowly above Sehwag’s 22. [India innings and dismissals](https://cricketaddictor.com/livescore/india-under-19s-vs-pakistan-under-19s-group-a-acc-mens-2026/scorecard/)

Rajput supplied **51.4% of the team total**, calculated as 107 divided by 208. That denominator includes extras. It describes how much of this innings came from one batter, not a batting average or a prediction of his next score.

India added another 38 after his dismissal but were all out with 21 scheduled legal balls unused. Those unused deliveries show the innings ended early; they do not establish how many additional runs India would have scored. Ali Raza, Rizwanullah and Niqab Shafiq each took three wickets. ## A 107-run stand changed the chase

At 61/5 after 14.4 overs, Pakistan still needed 148 to win. Hamza and Hasnain Dar then added 107 for the sixth wicket, taking the score to 168 before Dar was dismissed for 38. This was a partnership total, not 107 individual runs for either batter. [Pakistan innings record](https://www.cricnepal.com/match/india-under-19s-vs-pakistan-under-19s-match-8-mens-under-19-asia-cup-2026/scorecard)

Pakistan still needed 41 after that stand. Mohammad Sayyam’s dismissal left them 183/7 at 42.5 overs, with 26 required from 43 legal balls. Hamza and Niqab Shafiq completed the remaining 26 without another wicket falling. Niqab finished on two not out, while Hamza remained on 100.

Manal Chauhan’s 3/32 from ten overs was India’s best wicket return; Mohit Ulva took 2/45. Those figures explain the pressure Pakistan had to absorb, but neither an early five-wicket position nor an economical spell is itself a completed match result.

## Why 48.1 overs leaves eleven balls

A 50-over innings permits 300 legal deliveries. Pakistan used 48×6+1, or **289**, leaving **11**. Reading 48.1 as decimal overs would give the wrong answer. Likewise, India’s 46.3 represents 279 legal balls, not 46.3 multiplied by six.

The scorecard therefore records two different unused-ball counts: India lost its last wicket with 21 left, while Pakistan reached the target with 11 left. Only the latter belongs in the winning result. The teams’ final batting totals, wickets remaining and balls remaining answer different questions.

This archive is specifically the 8 November group meeting. It is not a score for a later India–Pakistan fixture or a claim about the tournament winner. Keep the date attached when sharing the result, especially while another match between the same teams is being discussed.

## Compare the recoveries without merging them

India’s 42/4 and Pakistan’s 61/5 were different situations, even though both describe early trouble. India were setting a total; Pakistan knew that 209 would win. Rajput’s century ended before India’s innings was complete, while Zahoor was still there when Pakistan reached the target. A century in each innings therefore did not mean the two innings followed the same path.

The 107-run sixth-wicket stand also should not be added to Zahoor’s individual hundred as a separate batting contribution. A partnership already includes the runs made by its two batters while together, plus any extras during that period. Keeping team totals, partnerships and individual scores separate avoids double counting the recovery.
