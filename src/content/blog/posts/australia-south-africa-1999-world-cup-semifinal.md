Australia and South Africa tied their 1999 World Cup semifinal at Edgbaston on 17 June. Both were dismissed for 213, but Australia advanced to the final because they had finished higher in the preceding Super Six stage. The result was a tie, not an Australian victory by one run. [ICC tournament history](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/cricketworldcup/news/mens-cricket-world-cup-1999-overview)

The last run-out explains how the scores stayed level; the tournament progression rule explains which side qualified. Those are separate questions. No Super Over or boundary-count comparison settled this match.

The [contemporary archived scorecard](https://i.imgci.com/db/ARCHIVE/WORLD_CUPS/WC99/SCORECARDS/FINALS/AUS_RSA_WC99_ODI-SEMI2_17JUN1999.html) records the match and progression separately. This prevents a common misreading: South Africa reaching 213 did not complete a winning chase. They needed one more run.

## Australia recovered twice before reaching 213

Shaun Pollock and Allan Donald took nine wickets between them. Steve Waugh’s 56 and Michael Bevan’s 65 were central to Australia reaching 213 after early trouble. Those innings and the main bowling figures are summarized in [Cricket Australia’s historical review](https://www.cricket.com.au/news/3271270/five-classic-australia-south-africa-odis-one-day-international-history).

The fall-of-wickets ledger shows the shape more clearly. Waugh and Bevan took Australia from 68/4 to 158/5, a 90-run stand. Tom Moody then fell without the total changing. Bevan and Warne added 49 before Warne’s dismissal at 207; Reiffel and Fleming also fell on 207. The final pair added six. [Complete innings ledger](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/5650/aus-vs-rsa-2nd-sf-icc-world-cup-1999)

That makes the final total more than a story about two fifties. The fifth-wicket recovery supplied 90 runs, the seventh-wicket stand another 49, and the last pair preserved six that mattered in a tied finish. Their combined individual scores should not be substituted for those partnership totals.

## Warne interrupted the opening and the recovery

South Africa reached 48 without loss before Warne dismissed Herschelle Gibbs, Gary Kirsten and Hansie Cronje. Cullinan’s run-out left them 61/4. Kallis and Rhodes rebuilt, making 53 and 43 respectively, but Warne later removed Kallis as well and finished with 4/29. [ICC semifinal account](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/cricketworldcup/news/mens-cricket-world-cup-1999-overview)

The [scorecard](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/5650/aus-vs-rsa-2nd-sf-icc-world-cup-1999) credits seven South African dismissals to bowlers and three to run-outs. Warne’s four wickets were therefore four of the seven bowler wickets, not four of all ten dismissals attributed to the bowling unit. The distinction is particularly relevant when the deciding wicket was a run-out.

At 198/9 after 48.4 overs, the chase needed 16 from eight scheduled balls. Klusener hit six and then took a single, retaining strike and leaving nine required from the last over. [Contemporary closing-over commentary](https://i.imgci.com/link_to_database/ARCHIVE/WORLD_CUPS/WC99/SCORECARDS/FINALS/AUS_RSA_WC99_ODI-SEMI2_17JUN1999_BBB-COMMS.html)

## Four balls that left the scores level

Fleming began the final over with South Africa 205/9. Klusener hit its first two balls for four, tying the scores with four balls left. A scoreless third delivery included a missed run-out opportunity. On the fourth, Klusener set off but Donald did not initially respond; the eventual run-out ended the innings without a completed run. [Cricket Australia’s account of the last over](https://www.cricket.com.au/news/3271270/five-classic-australia-south-africa-odis-one-day-international-history)

| Stage | South Africa | Runs needed to win | Scheduled balls left |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Start of final over | 205/9 | 9 | 6 |
| After first four | 209/9 | 5 | 5 |
| After second four | 213/9 | 1 | 4 |
| After the dot ball | 213/9 | 1 | 3 |
| Donald run out | 213 all out | Winning target not reached | 2 unused |

The table follows the [archived delivery sequence](https://i.imgci.com/link_to_database/ARCHIVE/WORLD_CUPS/WC99/SCORECARDS/FINALS/AUS_RSA_WC99_ODI-SEMI2_17JUN1999_BBB-COMMS.html). Nine were needed, eight were scored, and the last wicket fell. Two scheduled balls were unused, but an all-out batting side could not use them.

Klusener remained unbeaten on 31 from 16 balls. Donald was run out for zero without facing a delivery. Those facts can coexist: a non-striker can be dismissed while the other batter faces every ball of their partnership. The last stand added 15, not Klusener’s entire 31. ## Why Australia went through

The ICC’s historical account explicitly identifies Australia’s higher Super Six finish as the progression basis. The semifinal’s equal totals were not recalculated using boundaries, wickets remaining or a hypothetical extra delivery. [ICC overview](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/cricketworldcup/news/mens-cricket-world-cup-1999-overview)

This is also why the 1999 semifinal and the 2019 England–New Zealand final require separate explanations. Both are remembered for tied scores, but their tournament procedures were different. A modern Super Over guide should not be applied retrospectively to supply a missing finish in 1999.

The precise summary is short: South Africa needed 214, finished level at 213, and Australia qualified through their prior tournament position. The final run-out was decisive for progression without changing the match’s recorded result from a tie.
