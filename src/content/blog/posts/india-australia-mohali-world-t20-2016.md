India beat Australia by six wickets at Mohali’s Punjab Cricket Association IS Bindra Stadium on 27 March 2016, reaching 161/4 with five balls remaining. Australia had made 160/6. Virat Kohli finished unbeaten on 82, but the revealing part of the chase is not just his final score: India needed 39 from the last three overs and completed the job in 13 deliveries. [Full scorecard](https://m.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/15800/ind-vs-aus-31st-match-super-10-group-2-icc-world-t20-2016)

## Result and innings summary

| Innings | Total | Overs | Leading score |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Australia | 160/6 | 20 | Aaron Finch 43 from 34 |
| India | 161/4 | 19.1 | Virat Kohli 82 not out from 51 |

The target was 161, not Australia’s total of 160. The notation 19.1 means 19 completed overs and one delivery, or 115 legal balls. India therefore won with five of its scheduled 120 balls unused. That distinction matters when comparing a result margin with the amount of batting time left. [Scorecard](https://m.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/15800/ind-vs-aus-31st-match-super-10-group-2-icc-world-t20-2016)

## A group match with knockout consequences

This was the final Super 10 meeting between the sides, with a semifinal place at stake. It is often described as a virtual quarterfinal, but it was not a formally scheduled quarterfinal. Both teams had lost to New Zealand and beaten Pakistan and Bangladesh before this meeting. Winning here advanced India; it did not settle the tournament title. [ICC historical account](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/too-good-for-you-what-its-like-on-the-receiving-end-of-a-kohli-masterclass)

Australia chose to bat and India’s reply later slipped to 49/3. Kohli was not beginning a short, uncomplicated finishing assignment: the innings first needed rebuilding. Yuvraj Singh’s 21 from 18 helped bridge that period before MS Dhoni joined Kohli. Treating the whole chase as a sequence of boundary hits misses the earlier work required to keep the target within reach. [ICC historical account](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/too-good-for-you-what-its-like-on-the-receiving-end-of-a-kohli-masterclass)

## Australia could not sustain its opening speed

Australia raced to 53 in the first four overs but finished on 160. The remaining 16 overs therefore produced 107, a calculated rate of 6.69 per over against 13.25 in the opening four. This is an innings-level contrast, not evidence that every later over was economical. Peter Nevill struck a four and a six from the last two deliveries, preserving a demanding target. [Contemporaneous ABC coverage](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2016-03-28/india-knocks-australia-out-of-world-twenty20-as-it-happened/7278710)

India’s bowling return was shared rather than dominated by a single large wicket haul. Hardik Pandya took two wickets; Ashish Nehra, Ravichandran Ashwin, Yuvraj and Jasprit Bumrah took one apiece. Six bowlers contributed to the completed 20-over innings. [Scorecard](https://m.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/15800/ind-vs-aus-31st-match-super-10-group-2-icc-world-t20-2016)

## How 39 from 18 became four from six

| Checkpoint | India | Needed | Required runs per over |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| After 17 overs | 122/4 | 39 from 18 balls | 13.00 |
| After 18 overs | 141/4 | 20 from 12 balls | 10.00 |
| After 19 overs | 157/4 | 4 from 6 balls | 4.00 |
| After 19.1 overs | 161/4 | Target reached | Not applicable |

These requirements are calculated from a target of 161 and ABC’s contemporaneous checkpoints. The 18th over brought 19 runs and the 19th another 16: together, two overs removed 35 of the 39 runs still needed. Dhoni then hit the winning four from the first delivery of the final over. A required rate describes the arithmetic at that moment, not a measured probability of winning. ## The final partnership changed the finish

At the fourth wicket India needed 67 from six overs. Kohli and Dhoni completed that unbeaten partnership in 31 deliveries, equivalent to a calculated 12.97 runs per six balls. This does not mean the stand progressed at a constant speed: the checkpoint table shows how much of the acceleration arrived near its end. [innings and fall-of-wickets ledger](https://m.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/15800/ind-vs-aus-31st-match-super-10-group-2-icc-world-t20-2016)

Kohli’s innings and Dhoni’s finish belong together in the result. The former carried the chase through its changing demands; the latter was there when the target was reached. Neither this partnership rate nor one successful chase should be turned into a forecast for a later India–Australia match.
