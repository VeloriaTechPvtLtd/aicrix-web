Virat Kohli scored **540 runs from 435 balls in his last ten completed ODI batting innings through 7 November 2026**. He was dismissed nine times, giving a batting average of **60.00** and a combined strike rate of **124.14**. The window contains two centuries, three other fifties and one duck.

This is a dated form window, not his career record or his complete 2026 ODI record. It runs from **14 January to 7 November 2026**. The India–New Zealand match scheduled for 10 November was abandoned without a ball, so it does not replace a batting innings with a zero. ## Average is not runs per innings

The 540 runs divided by **nine dismissals** give the official-style batting average of 60.00. The same runs divided by **ten batting innings** give a mean score of 54.00.

Those figures answer different questions. Average describes runs per dismissal and therefore accounts for the unbeaten 139. Mean score describes the arithmetic runs per appearance at the crease. Neither says that the next score should be 54 or 60.

The median score in the window was **47**. Sorting the ten scores places 29 and 65 in the middle; their mean is 47. This is lower than the mean of 54 because the two centuries pull the arithmetic mean upward. It also shows why “averaging 60” is not the same as regularly making a score near 60.

The strike rate uses the combined denominators:

**540 ÷ 435 × 100 = 124.14 runs per 100 balls.**

A simple average of ten individual innings strike rates would give every innings the same weight. A one-ball duck and a 108-ball hundred would then contribute equally to that average, even though they represent very different amounts of batting.

## Where the runs came from

Kohli reached fifty in **five of the ten innings**. Two of those became hundreds: 124 against New Zealand in January and 139 not out against West Indies in September.

Together, those two innings supplied **263 of the 540 runs, or 48.7%**. The remaining eight supplied 277. That concentration is not a criticism of the centuries; substantial innings are precisely what a team wants. It is a reminder that a short-window total can be dominated by a few excellent performances.

At the other end, three innings ended below ten: five against England, the first-ball duck against West Indies and seven against New Zealand. A form summary that lists only hundreds and the overall average misses that variation.

Even an individual hundred does not establish the team result. In the January Indore match, New Zealand defended 337/8 and won by 41 runs despite Kohli's century. His personal contribution and India's match outcome are different pieces of information. ## The same ten innings span several different settings

“Last ten” can sound like a compact burst of recent cricket. Here it spans almost ten months, with innings in India, England and New Zealand.

That matters when using the table for an upcoming match. The September century, the July England innings and the November New Zealand innings did not occur in identical conditions or against identical attacks. Pooling them is a clear way to summarize recent ODI batting, but not a substitute for considering the next opponent and venue.

The latest two entries are 74 from 51 in Auckland and seven from seven in Wellington. Their combined 81 from 58 balls describe those two innings only. They should neither erase the longer sample nor be presented as proof of a permanent change in form.

Nor should IPL scores be inserted to make the timeline look more current. They belong to a different format and competition. A player can have many franchise appearances between ODI innings without changing this ODI-only table.

## What this means for fantasy research

The ledger is useful for comparing scoring volume, innings length and the frequency of large scores. It does not by itself produce fantasy points.

Boundary bonuses, dismissal penalties, fielding contributions and captain multipliers depend on the contest's actual rules. A fantasy comparison should apply one verified scoring contract to the relevant match data, rather than treating 540 batting runs as a universal points total.

The more immediate checks are whether Kohli is in the announced XI, what role he is expected to play and how much batting opportunity the match is likely to provide. Historical not-outs and centuries do not guarantee those opportunities.

## Keeping the window honest

When a new ODI batting innings is completed, add it at the top and remove the oldest included innings. Recalculate runs, balls and dismissals together. If the new appearance is a did-not-bat, or a game is abandoned without play, there is no new batting innings to add.

That rule preserves what “last ten innings” means. It also prevents a common error: replacing a real batting performance with a zero merely because a new fixture appeared on a schedule.
