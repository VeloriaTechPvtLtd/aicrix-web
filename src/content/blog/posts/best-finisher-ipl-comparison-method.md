---
title: "Best finisher in IPL and how to compare the evidence"
excerpt: "Compare IPL finishers using consistent phase windows, sample sizes and match situations, rather than treating not-outs or strike rate as a universal ranking."
status: draft
---

There is no single statistic that settles who is the best finisher in the IPL. A batter can lead a selected death-over strike-rate table, accumulate more late-innings runs or complete more successful chases. Those are different achievements, and the answer changes when the comparison quietly switches between them.

The useful starting point is to define the job: accelerating at the end of a first innings, completing a chase, or doing both across a stated period. Then keep the phase, sample and match context consistent.

## First check which overs are included

Imagine one comparison uses overs 17–20 and a 100-ball minimum, while another uses overs 16–20 and a 12-ball minimum and sorts by runs. These hypothetical filters answer different questions before any player records are examined.

In over notation, the 16th over begins at 15.1; the 17th begins at 16.1. Writing down that boundary prevents a hidden one-over difference when extracting deliveries.

Consider this invented example. The rows are aggregate batter runs and balls across a hypothetical sample, not records for actual players.

| Batter | 16th-over runs and balls | Overs 17–20 runs and balls | Strike rate in 17–20 | Strike rate in 16–20 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| A | 6 from 6 | 60 from 30 | 200.00 | 183.33 |
| B | 24 from 6 | 54 from 30 | 180.00 | 216.67 |

A leads when only the last four overs count. Include the 16th over and B leads. Neither calculation is wrong: A has 66 from 36 across the wider window, while B has 78 from 36. The mistake would be presenting the two rankings as answers to an identical question.

## A qualification threshold is not proof of reliability

Show balls faced beside the rate. Both invented players above would be excluded by a 100-ball threshold, despite producing calculable strike rates. A threshold decides eligibility; it does not establish that every eligible player has an equally reliable estimate.

Small samples are especially sensitive to individual deliveries. Replacing one dot ball with a six in a 20-ball sample changes strike rate by 30 points. The same replacement in a 200-ball sample changes it by three. This is a sensitivity demonstration, not a confidence interval or a prediction of either batter’s ability.

Compare the same seasons and competition, and show dismissals and innings alongside runs and balls. Do not average separately rounded innings strike rates: calculate the combined rate from combined runs and balls.

## An unbeaten innings does not necessarily finish a chase

MS Dhoni made 84 not out from 48 balls against RCB on 21 April 2019, yet CSK lost by one run, finishing on 160/8 against 161/7. His survival and the team’s result were different facts. [Official IPL match report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/match-report-m39-rcb-vs-csk).

Kieron Pollard’s 87 not out from 34 balls against CSK on 1 May 2021 ended differently: Mumbai reached 219/6 to win by four wickets. The [official report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/vivo-ipl-2021-match-27-mi-vs-csk-match-report) places his acceleration well before the last four overs, illustrating why a finishing role is not identical to a fixed phase window.

These examples do not rank Dhoni against Pollard. They show why a comparison should record entry point, target, balls remaining, wickets available and result rather than equating every not-out with a successful finish.

## Make the comparison reproducible

Keep first-innings acceleration separate from chase completion. A batter adding runs before a target exists faces a different task from someone needing a specified number from the remaining balls. Similarly, an opener still batting late and a newly arrived lower-order hitter have reached the same phase through different routes.

For a useful comparison, publish the cutoff date, phase boundaries, eligibility threshold and sorting measure beside the table. Retain the underlying innings list, including failures, and identify shortened matches separately. A limited sample can support a precise statement such as “highest observed strike rate under these filters.” It cannot, by itself, establish the best finisher in every situation.
