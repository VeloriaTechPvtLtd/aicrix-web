## Identify the dismissal before following the crossing

After a catch, crossing does not allow the surviving batter to take over the dismissed striker’s end. After an ordinary run-out, crossing during the unfinished run can affect where the survivor belongs. Then a separate question remains: has the over ended?

Keeping those decisions separate avoids the misleading shortcut that the new batter always faces after every wicket. This guide uses ordinary six-valid-ball overs, assumes the innings continues, and excludes runners and special penalty rulings.

## The catch rule changed in October 2022

The [MCC’s announcement](https://www.lords.org/lords/news-stories/mcc-announces-new-code-of-laws-from-1-october-2022) says the incoming batter takes the dismissed striker’s end after a catch, with an end-of-over exception to facing immediately. The change took effect on 1 October 2022. [ICC’s announcement of 20 September 2022](https://www.icc-cricket.com/media-releases/icc-announces-changes-to-playing-conditions) confirmed the same effective date for its playing-condition change, irrespective of whether the batters crossed.

This is a dated change, not a new 2026 exception. Older clips and explanations can therefore show a different outcome without describing the current rule. MCC Law 18.11.1.7 still includes Caught among dismissals that send the survivor back to the original end. [2026 Laws](https://www.lords.org/getattachment/e8b12929-1e84-4927-af4e-2a5875b53589/Laws-of-Cricket-2017-Code-4th-Edition-%282026%29.pdf)

## Sequence one a catch during the over

Start with A facing at the South end and B at the North end. On the fourth valid ball, A hits the ball into the air. A and B cross, but the fielder completes a fair catch. A is dismissed.

B returns to North. Incoming batter C takes South and faces the fifth ball. No completed running runs from the caught delivery count; crossing does not create a single. The over has not ended. [MCC Laws 18.11 and 33.4](https://www.lords.org/getattachment/e8b12929-1e84-4927-af4e-2a5875b53589/Laws-of-Cricket-2017-Code-4th-Edition-%282026%29.pdf)

## Sequence two a catch on the last valid ball

Reset the same starting positions: A at South, B at North. This time A is caught from the sixth valid ball. Whether they cross makes no difference to the catch allocation: B belongs at North and C enters at South.

The next over is bowled from the opposite end, so B faces its first ball at North. C has entered at the dismissed striker’s end but is now the non-striker. This is the distinction between occupying an end and facing the next delivery. [MCC Laws 17.1 and 18.11](https://www.lords.org/getattachment/e8b12929-1e84-4927-af4e-2a5875b53589/Laws-of-Cricket-2017-Code-4th-Edition-%282026%29.pdf)

## Sequence three a run out attempting a second

Reset again: A faces at South and B starts at North. On the fourth valid ball, they complete one run: A reaches North and B reaches South. They attempt a second and cross. A is then run out short of South; B survives.

B belongs at North, the end towards which B was running when they crossed. C enters at South and faces ball five. Only the completed first run scores, not the unfinished second. The over has not ended. [ICC ODI clauses 18.8.2 and 18.12.1](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/bxkzkcyvfn6tvi70zh0w.pdf)

Here crossing matters, but it is the crossing during the second run that matters. Saying only “they crossed” leaves out which run, which batter was dismissed and which end the survivor should occupy. Those missing details can change the answer.

## A short checklist for any replay

1. Name the dismissed batter and the dismissal mode.

2. Mark both ends before the delivery.

3. For a run-out, separate completed runs from the run in progress, then establish the crossing position at the dismissal.

4. Place the survivor under the applicable rule and put the incoming batter at the other end.

5. Check whether another delivery remains in the over.

In an ordinary run-out, if they have not crossed on the run in progress, the survivor returns to the wicket just left. Disallowed runs can require a different return. Also, a wide or no-ball does not complete a six-valid-ball over merely because it was the sixth delivery attempted. [ICC ODI clauses 18.12 and 17.3](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/bxkzkcyvfn6tvi70zh0w.pdf)

The useful question is therefore not simply “did they cross?” Ask “what dismissal, which run, which survivor and is the over finished?” For a real match, use its applicable playing conditions rather than transferring these six-ball examples to every competition.
