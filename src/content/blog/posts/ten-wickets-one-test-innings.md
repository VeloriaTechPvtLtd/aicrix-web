**Jim Laker, Anil Kumble and Ajaz Patel are the three bowlers to have taken all ten wickets in a men’s Test innings.** Laker’s 10/53 is the best return by runs conceded, followed by Kumble’s 10/74 and Ajaz’s 10/119. The [ACS innings-record table](https://stats.acscricket.com/Records/Test/Overall/Bowling/Most_Wickets_in_Innings_by_BB.html), checked on 12 November 2026, contains these three before the nine-wicket entries.

The shared achievement did not produce identical matches. England and India won Laker’s and Kumble’s Tests, while New Zealand lost the match in which Ajaz took his ten. An individual bowling record and a team result answer different questions.

## The complete ten wicket innings group

| Bowler | Team and opponent | Ground | Match began | Innings figures | Overs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Jim Laker | England vs Australia | Old Trafford, Manchester | 26 July 1956 | 10/53 | 51.2 |
| Anil Kumble | India vs Pakistan | Feroz Shah Kotla, Delhi | 4 February 1999 | 10/74 | 26.3 |
| Ajaz Patel | New Zealand vs India | Wankhede Stadium, Mumbai | 3 December 2021 | 10/119 | 47.5 |

The date column is the **start of the Test**, not the day on which the tenth wicket fell. The [dated record ledger](https://crickrida.com/records/men/test/best-bowling-innings/) corroborates the match starts, while [Guinness’ record summary](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/655369-most-wickets-taken-by-a-player-in-a-test-match-innings-male) gives the complete bowling figures. Delhi’s ground is now commonly listed as Arun Jaitley Stadium; Feroz Shah Kotla identifies its historical name.

## Laker took nineteen wickets across the match

Laker’s ten came in Australia’s second innings, after he had already taken nine in the first. England made 459, Australia replied with 84 and then made 205 following on. England therefore won by an innings and 170 runs: **459 − 84 − 205 = 170**. The [scorecard](https://crex.com/cricket-live-score/aus-vs-eng-4th-test-the-ashes-1956-ZQD/match-scorecard) preserves the second-innings ten and the final totals.

His first-innings 9/37 and second-innings 10/53 combine to **19/90**, not 20 wickets. That means Laker took 95% of Australia’s 20 wickets in the match. The distinction is striking: the innings feat was complete, but one Australian dismissal across the whole Test still belonged to someone else. [ICC historical account](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/laker-kumble-patel-how-the-trio-claimed-all-ten).

Australia’s second innings lasted 150.2 overs, of which Laker delivered 51.2. Taking every wicket did not mean bowling every over. His share was 308 of the innings’ 902 legal balls, or about **34.1%**. The other bowlers’ work remained part of the innings even though none took a wicket.

## Kumble ended a chase that had started with a century stand

India’s 252 and Pakistan’s 172 gave India an 80-run first-innings lead in Delhi. India then made 339, leaving Pakistan a target of **420**. Pakistan’s openers put on 101 before Kumble took the first wicket; the innings ended at 207. India’s winning margin was 212, the difference between its 419-run aggregate lead and Pakistan’s reply. [Full contemporary scorecard](https://www.rediff.com/sports/1999/feb/07score.htm).

The change from 101 without loss to 207 all out means Pakistan lost all ten wickets while adding 106 runs. That 106 is a **team scoring interval**, not Kumble’s bowling concession. His 10/74 covers the runs charged to him throughout his own spells, including before the first dismissal. Runs scored from other bowlers and some extras belong to different accounting columns.

The [second scorecard](https://www.cricketsky.com/india-vs-pakistan-2nd-test-india-vs-pakistan-1999-scorecard-54189/) agrees on the innings totals, target and Kumble’s 26.3-over ten-wicket return. The century opening stand explains why a final 212-run margin should not be read as a chase that was hopeless from its first ball.

## Ajaz took all ten away from home but New Zealand lost

Ajaz’s 10/119 came in India’s first innings at Mumbai. He is the only member of this three-player group to achieve the feat away from home and in the match’s opening innings. Laker and Kumble were bowling at home later in their Tests. [ICC comparison](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/laker-kumble-patel-how-the-trio-claimed-all-ten).

India still reached **325**. Ajaz’s 119 conceded is not the team total: other bowlers conceded runs, and extras also contributed. New Zealand then made 62, leaving a first-innings deficit of 263. India’s second-innings 276/7 declared set a target of 540; New Zealand’s 167 left India winners by 372 runs. [Mumbai scorecard](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/38587/ind-vs-nz-2nd-test-new-zealand-tour-of-india-2021).

Ajaz added 4/106 in India’s second innings, giving him **14/225 for the match**. India lost only 17 wickets before declaring, so his share was 14 of 17, not 14 of an assumed 20. The declaration changed the number of available dismissals; it did not diminish the completeness of his first-innings ten.

## Comparing workloads without turning them into a ranking of skill

The overs translate to **308 legal balls for Laker, 159 for Kumble and 287 for Ajaz**. Cricket notation is not decimal notation: 26.3 means 26 six-ball overs plus three balls, not 26.3 multiplied by six.

Kumble used the fewest legal deliveries of the three, while Laker conceded the fewest runs. Those are different descriptive comparisons. Conditions, opposition and match situations were different, so neither number alone produces an era-adjusted ranking of bowling skill.

The all-ten list is an innings record, not the career Test wicket leaderboard. Keeping that label attached preserves what makes the achievement exceptional: every dismissal in one opposition innings was credited to the same bowler.
