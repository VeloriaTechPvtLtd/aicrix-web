*Seven selected ODI meetings, 17 March 2023 to 25 October 2025; rechecked 14 November 2026.*

Across the seven ODI meetings listed below, Travis Head scored **111 runs from 81 balls against Mohammed Siraj**, who dismissed him **three times**. That gives Head a matchup strike rate of **137.04** and **37 runs per dismissal**. The figures describe these dated encounters, ending on 25 October 2025, not their combined Test, ODI and IPL history.

A useful Head–Siraj comparison needs both the aggregate and the match ledger. A hundred against India is not a hundred against every Indian bowler, and a dismissal late in a chase does not explain the entire contest.

## The World Cup final was 30 against Siraj rather than 137

Head’s complete innings in the 19 November 2023 final was **137 from 120 balls**. Against Siraj specifically, he made **30 from 22**, before being caught by Shubman Gill off Siraj. Assigning all 137 to this head-to-head would add 107 runs that were scored against other bowlers. The same distinction works in the other direction. At Sydney in October 2025, Head’s overall score was 29, but his Siraj component was 17 from 11 balls. The wicket belongs in the matchup; the other 12 runs do not. [Sydney breakdown](https://www.cricbuzz.com/player-match-performance/match/116927/player/8497/batting).

## Three different denominators

Strike rate uses balls faced: 111 ÷ 81 × 100 = **137.04 runs per 100 balls**. Matchup average uses dismissals by the bowler: 111 ÷ 3 = **37**. Dividing by seven instead gives **15.86 runs per meeting**, which is neither of those statistics.

Similarly, 81 ÷ 3 = **27 balls per dismissal** describes the observed sample. It does not mean the next wicket is due on the 27th ball, or establish a constant wicket probability. Each meeting had different innings conditions and amounts of exposure.

## Two meetings account for more than half the runs

The 30-run entries at Visakhapatnam and Ahmedabad supply **60 of the 111 runs**, or **54.05%**, from 36 of the 81 balls. That concentration matters when interpreting a single aggregate.

As a sensitivity check, leaving those two entries aside would produce 51 runs from 45 balls, a strike rate of **113.33**. That is not a replacement record: removing strong innings selectively would distort the history. It simply shows how much the summary depends on two performances in a small sample.

## Not every India match belongs in this comparison

Head’s 39 in the March 2025 Champions Trophy semifinal should not be added. Siraj did not bowl in that match; the scorecard lists India’s six other bowlers. BCCI had named him among the tournament’s non-travelling substitutes. [Semifinal scorecard](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/112462/aus-vs-ind-1st-semi-final-a1-v-b2-icc-champions-trophy-2025), [BCCI squad update](https://www.bcci.tv/news/article/icc-champions-trophy-2025-team-india-squad-update).

Keep the format and dated rows attached when reusing the numbers. A Test matchup, an IPL matchup and these ODIs answer different questions. For a future meeting, recent role and conditions need separate assessment; seven historical encounters cannot establish who will win the next duel.

## Read the exposure as well as the result

The three dismissals did not come after identical amounts of contact. The Mumbai entry contains six balls, Ahmedabad 22 and Sydney 11. A wicket flag records who ended the innings, while the ball count tells you how much of that innings the bowler actually controlled. Neither column should be discarded when comparing the encounters.

At Visakhapatnam, for example, the table records 30 runs from 14 Siraj deliveries without a dismissal. That row can coexist with a different complete-innings score, because Head also faced other bowlers. At Perth the corresponding sample is only four balls. Treating those two meetings as equally large observations would hide the unequal exposure. The aggregate remains a useful description of this ledger, but not a prediction or a complete explanation of either player’s ability.
