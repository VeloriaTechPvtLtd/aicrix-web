Australia beat India by 98 runs at Centurion on 10 April 2005 in the Women’s World Cup final. Karen Rolton finished unbeaten on 107 from 128 balls as Australia made 215/4 and won their fifth title. [ICC final-centuries retrospective](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/world-test-championship/news/memory-lane-the-best-hundreds-seen-in-icc-tournament-finals)

## A partnership changed the innings

At halfway, Australia were 71/3. Rolton and Lisa Sthalekar added 139 for the fourth wicket; Sthalekar contributed 55. The partnership supplied both continuity and a platform for acceleration. [ICC Sthalekar profile](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/lisa-sthalekar-a-pioneering-all-rounder)

The two halves are revealing. Australia scored 71 in the first 25 overs and 144 in the last 25: scoring rates of 2.84 and 5.76 runs per over respectively. Those are calculations from the halfway and final totals, not estimates of how the pitch changed. The second half produced slightly more than twice the first half’s runs.

The 139-run stand represented about 64.7% of the completed total. That is a partnership contribution, including any extras during the stand, rather than the sum of two individual innings.

## India could not build a matching recovery

India were dismissed for 117 in 46 overs. Four batters were run out. Shelley Nitschke returned 2/14 and Cathryn Fitzpatrick 2/23, while India’s sixth wicket fell at 64. [Full scorecard](https://i.imgci.com/db/ARCHIVE/WORLD_CUPS/WWC2005/SCORECARDS/KNOCK-OUTS/AUS-WOMEN_IND-WOMEN_WWC2005_WODI-FINAL_10APR2005.html)

Four run-outs account for 40% of the wickets, so a summary based only on bowling figures would omit a substantial part of the dismissals. They should not be assigned to individual bowlers.

## Reading the result correctly

The winning margin is 215 minus 117, or 98 runs. India’s winning target was 216, making the distance from their final score to victory 99. Those numbers answer different questions.

The final was therefore not simply a century followed by economical bowling. Australia repaired an early setback through a long partnership, increased their scoring sharply after halfway and then prevented India from constructing a comparable recovery.
