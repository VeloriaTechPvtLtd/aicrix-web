Muttiah Muralitharan leads men’s ODI cricket with **534 career wickets**. Wasim Akram follows with 502, Waqar Younis with 416 and Chaminda Vaas with exactly 400. These are the four members of the 400-wicket club in the career tables checked on 14 November 2026. The ICC independently confirms Muralitharan’s 534 wickets across 350 appearances. [ICC career summary](https://www.icc-cricket.com/media-releases/muralidaran-formally-inducted-into-icc-cricket-hall-of-fame).

The cutoff is inclusive: Vaas belongs because 400 qualifies, not only 401 or more. Shahid Afridi is immediately outside it on 395. The broader [myKhel career leaderboard](https://www.mykhel.com/cricket/x-rs25/) supplies that boundary check; the individual Cricbuzz bowling tables supply the innings and ball counts.

Matches are appearances, while bowling innings count occasions on which a player bowled. Substituting one for the other changes the denominator. The differences here are nine for Muralitharan, five for Wasim, four for Waqar and two for Vaas. Those gaps alone do not identify why a player did not bowl; they should not be labelled missed opportunities caused by injury or poor selection.

## Which bowler took wickets most frequently

Among these four, Waqar required the fewest balls per wicket: **30.52**. The corresponding figures are 35.23 for Muralitharan, 36.23 for Wasim and 39.44 for Vaas, calculated as legal balls divided by wickets and rounded to two decimals. These are bowling strike rates, not batting strike rates. Lower means wickets arrived more frequently per legal delivery.

Waqar’s 416 wickets therefore came from a materially smaller workload than the others: 12,698 balls, compared with Vaas’s 15,775. He took 16 more wickets while bowling 3,077 fewer balls. That comparison describes their recorded careers, not a forecast of what either would have achieved with the other’s fixtures. [Waqar’s full bowling record](https://www.cricbuzz.com/profiles/3706/waqar-younis) and [Vaas’s record](https://www.cricbuzz.com/profiles/108/chaminda-vaas).

Muralitharan and Wasim offer a different comparison. Muralitharan took 32 more wickets despite bowling in ten fewer innings, but delivered 625 more balls overall. He averaged 55.16 balls per bowling innings against Wasim’s 51.81. An innings count measures participation; balls capture how much bowling actually occurred. Neither measure, on its own, explains the entire wicket gap. ## Wickets and economy answer different questions

Wasim conceded 11,812 runs from 18,186 balls: 3.90 runs per six-ball over when rounded to two decimals. Waqar’s 9,919 runs from 12,698 balls produce 4.69. Within this pair, Waqar took wickets more frequently, while Wasim conceded fewer runs per over. Calling either rate the only measure of effectiveness discards the other result.

Career totals also combine different opposition, venues and stages of matches. These aggregates do not isolate powerplay bowling, death overs or a particular opponent. A phase-specific comparison needs a phase-specific delivery ledger rather than a role inferred from someone’s reputation.

## All ODIs are not the ODI World Cup

This table includes a player’s recognized ODI career, including tournament and bilateral matches, rather than only World Cup fixtures. World Cup wickets are a subset, so adding them to these totals would double-count them. A country-only filter can also differ from an all-ODI filter when representative teams are included.

Finally, the biggest career total and the best individual spell are separate records. Vaas’s career best is 8/19, but his cumulative tally is 400. One exceptional innings does not replace the full career denominator. [Cricket Australia’s Vaas record](https://www.cricket.com.au/news/3271766/odi-goat-malinga-v-vaas).
