## Rabada finished one wicket ahead

Kagiso Rabada won the IPL 2026 Purple Cap with **29 wickets**, one ahead of Bhuvneshwar Kumar’s **28**. Jofra Archer finished third with **25**. This is the final season list, including the playoffs and the 31 May final, rather than a league-stage or mid-season snapshot.

[Gujarat Titans’ season review](https://www.gujarattitansipl.com/news/gujarat-titans-tata-ipl-2026-season-review-kagiso-rabada-shubman-gill-sai-sudharsan), published on 1 June, confirms Rabada’s award and total. [RCB’s completed-season bowling review](https://www.royalchallengers.com/rcb-cricket-news/news/from-bhuvneshwar-kumar-to-josh-hazlewood-rcbs-top-five-bowlers-in-ipl-2026) independently records Bhuvneshwar’s 28 and the one-wicket gap.

## The final changed the race

The [official final report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/tata-ipl-2026-final-rcb-v-gt-match-report) identifies two relevant wickets. Bhuvneshwar dismissed Jason Holder to reach 28 in Gujarat’s innings. Rabada then dismissed Devdutt Padikkal during RCB’s chase, securing the Purple Cap. Stopping the count before the final would miss the match that settled the award.

The individual award and the team championship went to different sides: Rabada represented Gujarat, while RCB won the final. A bowler’s wicket tally answers an individual season question; it is not a substitute for the competition’s match results.

## A one wicket gap can hide a close delivery rate

The [Cricbuzz final bowling ledger](https://www.cricbuzz.com/cricket-series/9241/indian-premier-league-2026/stats?matchFormat=3&seasonSeriesId=9241&statsType=mostWickets) records Rabada delivering 388 legal balls and Bhuvneshwar 378. Dividing by their wickets gives **13.38 balls per wicket** for Rabada and **13.50** for Bhuvneshwar. Their wicket-taking intervals were close, although Rabada finished with the larger total.

That comparison does not erase the award result. It shows why a season count and a delivery-normalized rate answer different questions. Neither number establishes which bowler would take more wickets in a hypothetical equal-opportunity season.

The same issue appears inside a tie. Siraj and Rasikh both took 19 wickets, but their recorded workloads were 372 and 257 balls respectively. That produces **19.58 versus 13.53 balls per wicket**. Calling their wicket totals equal is correct; calling their bowling opportunities identical is not. The calculation does not adjust for opponents, phases, conditions or fielding support.

## Read the list with the right scope

This table is neither the IPL career wicket ranking nor the record for most wickets in any single edition. It counts one completed tournament. Likewise, a best bowling spell is an innings record, not a replacement for a season total.

When comparing two dated lists, check their last included match before assuming a numerical disagreement. A qualifier-stage table and a post-final table can legitimately answer different cutoffs.
