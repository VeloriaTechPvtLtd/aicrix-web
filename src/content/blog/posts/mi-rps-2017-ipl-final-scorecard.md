**Mumbai Indians beat Rising Pune Supergiant by one run in the IPL final at Hyderabad on 21 May 2017.** Mumbai made 129/8 and Pune finished on 128/6, both from 20 overs. Krunal Pandya was player of the match, while Mitchell Johnson took 3/26 and delivered the last over. [Official match centre](https://www.mumbaiindians.com/scores/rps-vs-mi-match-rpmi05212017183437) · [IPL final report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/match-report-final-rps-vs-mi).

The finish mattered, but the defence began with a recovery. Mumbai had been 79/7. Krunal and Johnson then added 50, giving the bowlers a total they eventually protected by the smallest possible run margin.

## How 79 for seven became 129

Jaydev Unadkat removed both openers, leaving Mumbai 8/2. Rohit’s 24 helped rebuild, but he and Kieron Pollard fell in Adam Zampa’s eleventh over. After Hardik Pandya and Karn Sharma also departed, the score was 79/7 at 14.1 overs. [IPL report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/match-report-final-rps-vs-mi).

Krunal and Johnson’s stand occupied the remaining **35 legal balls**: 120 − (14 × 6 + 1) = 35. Their 50 runs therefore came at a combined team rate of **8.57 per six balls**, compared with 79 from the preceding 85 legal balls, or 5.58. These are rates for two innings segments, including extras, not individual strike rates.

The partnership supplied **38.8% of Mumbai’s total**. Krunal finished with 47 and Johnson with 13 not out, but adding those individual innings gives 60, not the stand of 50: Krunal had already scored before Johnson arrived. The wicket sequence, rather than the sum of two full innings, fixes the partnership boundary. [Scorecard and fall of wickets](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/18180/rps-vs-mi-final-indian-premier-league-2017).

## Pune kept wickets but still needed a finish

Rahane and Smith added 54 for the second wicket. Rahane made 44 before Johnson dismissed him; Smith eventually reached 51. Their combined 95 runs were **74.2% of Pune’s 128**, but concentration of runs does not by itself explain a chase: the time used and the runs still needed also matter.

At 71/2 after 11.5 overs, Pune needed **59 from 49 balls**. Dhoni’s departure at 98/3 after 16.2 left **32 from 22**. The required pace had moved from about 7.22 to 8.73 runs per six balls. Those calculations use the fall-of-wicket positions, not rounded over labels. [Complete innings ledger](https://cricketdatabase.com/matches/2017-05-21-mumbai-indians-v-rising-pune-supergiant).

Karn Sharma and Lasith Malinga took no wickets but conceded only 18 and 21 respectively in their four-over spells. Together that was **39 from eight overs**, or 4.88 per over. Johnson and Bumrah supplied five bowler-credited wickets; the sixth dismissal was a run-out. Wicket totals alone would miss an important part of the defence.

## The last over in team runs

Pune began the twentieth over on 119/3, needing 11. Tiwary hit the first ball for four, then Tiwary and Smith were caught from consecutive deliveries. That left two new batters to find seven from three balls. [IPL’s post-match account](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/mitchell-johnsons-experience-came-in-to-play-parthiv-patel).

| Point in the over | Pune score | Runs still needed to win |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before the over | 119/3 | 11 from 6 balls |
| After the opening four | 123/3 | 7 from 5 |
| After two successive wickets | 123/5 | 7 from 3 |
| Before the final two balls | 124/5 | 6 from 2 |
| Before the final ball | 126/5 | 4 from 1 |
| Innings complete | 128/6 | Target not reached |

Christian collected two runs from each of the last two deliveries, with a run-out ending the innings on the final attempt. Pune added nine team runs in the over, not the eleven needed. [Contemporary finish report](https://www.cricket.com.au/news/3281470/final-ball-thriller-sees-mumbai-home).

That explains both numbers attached to the result: **129 − 128 = 1**, the winning margin; **130 − 128 = 2**, the shortfall from the winning target. A third completed run on the last ball would have brought Pune level with Mumbai’s score, not ahead. Mumbai instead secured their third IPL title, after 2013 and 2015.
