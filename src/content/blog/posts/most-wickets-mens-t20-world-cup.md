**Shakib Al Hasan leads the men’s T20 World Cup career wicket list with 50.** Adam Zampa and Adil Rashid share second place on 44 after the 2026 edition. This is the tournament’s career record, not the record for all T20 internationals or for one World Cup. [Career wicket table](https://www.mykhel.com/cricket/most-wickets-in-t20-world-cup-s2rs20/)

This completed-tournament snapshot was rechecked on **14 November 2026**.

The distinction matters when a search result simply says “most wickets.” A league season, a national-team career and a global tournament all count different sets of matches. Mixing them can produce a plausible-looking number that answers the wrong question.

Equal wicket totals retain equal ranks. A website may sort Zampa ahead of Adil Rashid using another statistic, but that does not give him an extra wicket or sole possession of second place.

## Shakib reached fifty against India in 2024

Shakib entered the 22 June 2024 match against India on 49 World Cup wickets. Dismissing Rohit Sharma at the Sir Vivian Richards Stadium in Antigua took him to fifty, the first bowler to reach that milestone. [Contemporary report from Antigua](https://www.dhakatribune.com/sport/cricket/349966/shakib-first-bowler-to-take-50-wickets-in-t20)

A milestone date and a career cutoff are different fields. The date tells you when wicket number fifty arrived. The career table tells you the total at the end of the period being compared. Neither should be replaced by the date on which an article was most recently opened.

## How large is the gap to the record

Against a fixed benchmark of 50, Zampa and Adil Rashid would each need six more wickets to draw level and seven to move ahead. Rashid Khan would need seven to tie and eight to pass it. Bumrah and Hasaranga would each need ten to tie and eleven to pass.

Those are arithmetic gaps, not predictions about selection, fitness or the next tournament. Converting a gap into “matches needed” assumes a future wicket-taking rate that the record table cannot promise.

## Career totals and one edition answer different questions

Bumrah took **14 wickets in eight matches in 2026**, finishing joint-leading wicket-taker with Varun Chakaravarthy. Adil Rashid took **13 in eight**. Bumrah also returned 4/15 in the final against New Zealand and received the match award. [ICC’s completed-tournament review](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/team-of-the-tournament-announced-for-t20-world-cup-2026)

For Bumrah, 14 of his 40 career World Cup wickets came in that edition: **35%**. That calculation describes how much the latest tournament contributed to his accumulated record. It does not turn a career total of 40 into a one-edition total.

Hasaranga’s 2026 tournament was much shorter. He took three wickets against Ireland, then a hamstring injury ruled him out of the remainder, with Dushan Hemantha approved as his replacement. His place on the career list therefore should not be mistaken for evidence that he played a full 2026 campaign. [ICC injury and replacement report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/mens-t20-world-cup-2026/news/sri-lanka-s-star-bowler-ruled-out-of-t20-world-cup)

## Match count is not bowling workload

Two bowlers with the same wickets may have very different opportunities. A career total rewards repeated selection and sustained contribution as well as wicket-taking rate. An appearance alone does not tell you whether a bowler delivered four overs, one over or none.

For a meaningful comparison, retain separate columns for wickets, innings bowled, legal balls and runs conceded. Average measures runs per wicket; bowling strike rate measures balls per wicket; economy measures runs per over. None is interchangeable with wickets per match. Start with the record that answers your question, then use workload and match context to explain it.
