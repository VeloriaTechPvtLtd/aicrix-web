A tied score does not always settle a T20 international. Under the October 2026 ICC men's T20I conditions, the teams use a Super Over: each gets up to six valid balls, and losing two wickets ends that side's innings. More runs wins; losing fewer wickets does not break another tie. The original chasing side bats first in the first Super Over. [ICC conditions, clause 16.3 and Appendix F](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/bsxcho7ufe2pjban0wjp.pdf).

## What changes if the Super Over also finishes level?

Another Super Over follows, subject to time and conditions. Batting order reverses. A batter dismissed in any earlier Super Over cannot return; a bowler cannot bowl consecutive Super Overs. The fielding sides switch their bowling ends. If the required tie-break cannot be completed, these standard conditions leave the match tied. The referee can announce a limit beforehand when unavoidable time constraints require it. [ICC Appendix F, paragraphs 17–26](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/bsxcho7ufe2pjban0wjp.pdf).

## A small scorecard that makes the sequence clear

Here is an invented example, not a record of a real match:

| Stage | Team Amber | Team Blue | Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Main match | 168 | 168 | Level |
| First Super Over | 12/1, batting second | 12/0, batting first | Still level |
| Second Super Over | 15/0, batting first | 11/2, batting second | Amber wins |

The middle row is the common trap. Blue's extra wicket in hand is not a winning margin. Keep three separate score panels rather than merging 168, 12 and 15 into one innings total.

Before the second round, imagine Amber's dismissed batter was A1 and its bowler was A4. A1 is unavailable; the team must choose someone other than A4 to bowl next. Keeping a simple “dismissed batters” list and a separate “previous bowler” entry avoids treating the two restrictions as identical.

## Why can the same batter return but not the same bowler?

Imagine the first Super Over ends with A2 unbeaten and A4 having bowled. A2 remains eligible to bat in the next round, but A4 must wait while another eligible bowler delivers it. If A2 had been dismissed instead, the batter could not return in a later Super Over. This hypothetical example shows why a single “used players” list is insufficient.

## Check the competition, not just the format

For an IPL game, domestic final or older match, open that event's own dated conditions before applying this guide. ICC publishes separate standard and tournament documents in its [playing-conditions directory](https://www.icc-cricket.com/about/cricket/rules-and-regulations/playing-conditions). A screenshot of an old tie-break rule is not enough.

The useful viewing checklist is short: identify the rule edition, keep each round's score separate, track who was dismissed, and note who bowled last. Those four entries explain most of the apparent surprises without guessing at a boundary-count or wicket-count winner.
