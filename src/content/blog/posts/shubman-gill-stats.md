## ODI scoring frequency and the 223 not out

Gill reached at least fifty in **31 of 71 ODI innings**, or **43.7%**, calculated from 11 centuries plus 20 half-centuries. This measures the frequency of substantial scores rather than relying on his highest innings alone. [ODI figures](https://www.cricbuzz.com/profiles/11808/shubman-gill).

His highest score was **223 not out against West Indies at Guwahati on 30 September 2026**. He faced 133 balls, hit 26 fours and eight sixes, and helped India chase 406 with 39 balls remaining. ICC’s account records that he reached 200 in 117 balls. The distinction matters: 117 is the time taken to reach the double-century, while 133 is the length of the completed innings. [ICC performance review](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/icc-men-s-player-of-the-month-revealed-for-september-2026).

The latest two included ODI innings were 37 in Auckland and a first-ball duck in Wellington. Together they added 37 runs and two dismissals, illustrating why an average can fall even while the run total rises. ## Test hundreds and the Edgbaston contribution

Gill’s 11 Test centuries and nine fifties mean that **55% of his scores of fifty or more became hundreds**: 11 divided by 20. This is a conversion measure, not the percentage of all innings in which he made a century. [Test summary](https://www.crictracker.com/cricket-players/shubman-gill/stats/).

At Edgbaston in July 2025, he made **269 and 161**, totaling 430 runs. ICC recorded that match aggregate as the second-highest in men’s Test history at the time, behind Graham Gooch’s 456. Those two innings account for approximately **14.1%** of Gill’s 3,043 Test runs in this snapshot. [ICC’s Edgbaston report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/gill-s-marathon-knock-leaves-england-a-mountain-to-climb).

That concentration adds context. A career average describes the full record but does not show whether runs were spread evenly across opponents or tours. His most recent included Test began against Sri Lanka in Colombo on 23 August 2026, when he scored 50 in his only batting innings. ## Reading the averages correctly

Batting average means runs per dismissal, not runs per innings. Gill’s ODI calculation is **3,750 ÷ (71 − 11) = 62.50**. Dividing by all 71 innings instead gives 52.82, a different measure.

Compare each format separately and keep its date visible. Average, strike rate and substantial-score frequency describe different aspects of batting; none by itself captures every opponent, condition or match situation.

## Why a headline rate may differ

The underlying balls-faced totals used here are 4,956 in Tests, 3,651 in ODIs and 627 in T20Is. Dividing the runs by those balls and multiplying by 100 gives the rates in the table. A provider’s displayed rate may use a different rounding convention or a different update point. For example, CricTracker’s ODI aggregate still lists 3,713 runs, while its recent-form list includes the subsequent 37 and zero. Adding those completed innings gives the 3,750 used here; their 57 balls take its 3,594-ball baseline to 3,651. Do not mix an older run total with a newer denominator.
