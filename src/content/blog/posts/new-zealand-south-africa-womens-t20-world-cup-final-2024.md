New Zealand won its first Women’s T20 World Cup by beating South Africa by 32 runs in Dubai on **20 October 2024**. The White Ferns made 158 for five and held South Africa to 126 for nine. [ICC final report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/media-releases/kerr-shines-as-new-zealand-win-their-first-ever-icc-women-s-t20-world-cup-x7488)

## A powerplay lead was not the result

New Zealand reached 43 for one after six overs. South Africa were 47 without loss at the equivalent point, four runs ahead with an extra wicket in hand.

Against a target of 159, South Africa still needed 112 from 84 balls: exactly eight runs per over. Their favourable start did not remove the remaining scoring requirement.

## The middle overs changed the chase

Amelia Kerr removed Laura Wolvaardt and Anneke Bosch in the tenth over. Marizanne Kapp and Nadine de Klerk subsequently fell from consecutive deliveries across the end of one over and the beginning of the next.

Kerr finished with three for 24 and Rosemary Mair three for 25. These breakthroughs explain why describing the match solely through the powerplay comparison would be misleading.

## Kerr contributed before taking the ball

Kerr top-scored with 43 from 38 deliveries. Her fourth-wicket partnership with Brooke Halliday helped rebuild the innings, and New Zealand scored 48 in the final five overs. That closing rate was 9.60 runs per over.

Kerr received both Player of the Match and Player of the Tournament. Across the competition she scored 135 runs and took 15 wickets. Those tournament totals are separate from her final figures. [ICC award announcement](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/brilliant-all-rounder-melie-kerr-named-player-of-the-tournament?sf204890453=1)

## Why the margin was 32, not 33

New Zealand’s 158 exceeded South Africa’s 126 by 32. South Africa would have needed 33 more to reach the winning target of 159. The official run margin compares the innings totals; the runs needed to win include the extra run beyond equality.

This final is a useful example of reading a chase through wickets and remaining runs together. An early lead over the opponent’s corresponding score is a checkpoint, not a guarantee of victory.
