India beat South Africa by ten runs in the 2002 Champions Trophy semifinal in Colombo. The turning point is often linked to Herschelle Gibbs leaving the field, but his retirement and India’s subsequent wickets were separate events.

## The target was 262

India made 261 for nine on 25 September, led by Yuvraj Singh’s 62 and Virender Sehwag’s 59. South Africa reached 192 for one before Gibbs retired hurt on 116. The ICC’s retrospective identifies Sehwag and Harbhajan Singh as central to the recovery that followed. [ICC match retrospective](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/india-v-south-africa-icc-champions-trophy-2017-a-look-ahead)

Retired hurt is not an ordinary dismissal. Gibbs’s departure ended his participation in the partnership without adding a wicket to the team total or crediting a bowler with his wicket. No medical diagnosis is needed to explain that scorecard distinction.

## Harbhajan changed the wicket column

Jonty Rhodes and Boeta Dippenaar fell in the same Harbhajan over, both with the total at 194. Sehwag later removed Mark Boucher, then dismissed Jacques Kallis and Lance Klusener in the final over. Kallis finished on 97; South Africa ended on 251 for six. [Contemporary scorecard](https://img.cricinfo.com/db/ARCHIVE/2002-03/OD_TOURNEYS/ICCCT/SCORECARDS/KNOCKOUTS/IND_RSA_ICCCT_ODI-SEMI1_25SEP2002.html)

The final score was ten runs below India’s total, and eleven short of the winning target. That is why the official margin is ten runs rather than eleven.

## Sehwag contributed in both innings

Sehwag’s five overs brought three wickets for 25 runs, complementing his earlier half-century. Harbhajan took two for 37 from ten overs. Sehwag was player of the match.

The precise sequence matters more than a general claim about momentum: a settled batter retired, two new batters were dismissed in one over, and further wickets helped stop the chase. Gibbs leaving did not by itself win the match for India. The bowlers still had to convert that disruption into a successful defence.
