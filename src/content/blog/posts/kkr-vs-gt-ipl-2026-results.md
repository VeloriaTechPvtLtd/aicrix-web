Kolkata Knight Riders and Gujarat Titans took **one win each in the two IPL 2026 matches compared here**. Gujarat won the first match in Ahmedabad by five wickets; Kolkata won the return at Eden Gardens by 29 runs. The same side batted first both times, but KKR's second total was substantially harder to chase.

## Gujarat recover and complete the chase in Ahmedabad

KKR chose to bat, but Cameron Green's **79 off 55 balls** was needed to rebuild the innings. Kagiso Rabada took **3/29** and Mohammed Siraj **2/23**, helping Gujarat bowl Kolkata out for 180.

Shubman Gill supplied **86 off 50** in the chase. GT reached 181/5 with two deliveries remaining, so the five-wicket margin should not be mistaken for a finish with several overs unused. [Official Match 25 report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/tata-ipl-2026-match-25-gt-vs-kkr-match-report)

Gill was Player of the Match, confirmed by the [match centre](https://www.iplt20.com/matches/237289/More/match-details). The win combined a bowling effort that took all ten wickets with a major innings from the captain. Neither part alone fully describes how Gujarat secured the result.

## Kolkata set a much bigger target at Eden Gardens

In the return game, Gujarat chose to field. Finn Allen made **93 off 35 balls**, while Angkrish Raghuvanshi finished **82 not out** and Green **52 not out**. Their work lifted KKR to 247/2.

Gill again produced a substantial innings, this time **85 off 49**, but GT's 218/4 was not enough. Sunil Narine took **2/29**, and Saurabh Dubey's penultimate over conceded only four runs while removing Jos Buttler. [Official Match 60 report](https://www.iplt20.com/news/article/tata-ipl-2026-match-60-kkr-vs-gt-match-report)

Narine received Player of the Match, according to the [official match details](https://www.iplt20.com/matches/dfec94ad-9b8d-4951-82b7-c611db494112/kolkata-knight-riders-vs-gujarat-titans/match-details). That is worth noting in a game with so much batting: the award went to a bowler, not automatically to the highest scorer.

## Kolkata raise their first innings total

KKR added **67 runs** to its earlier total, moving from 180 to 247. Because both innings lasted 20 overs, that means its scoring rate rose from **9.00 to 12.35 runs per over**. It also lost only two wickets in Kolkata, compared with ten in Ahmedabad.

These are direct comparisons, not proof that one tactical adjustment caused the improvement. The matches took place at different grounds and nearly a month apart. The scorelines establish what changed; they do not isolate every reason why.

Gill's innings offer another useful contrast. He scored almost the same number of runs from almost the same number of balls in each game: 86 from 50, then 85 from 49. Yet one contributed to victory and the other to defeat. The target and the contributions around a batter matter as much as the headline individual score.

## Reading the two results fairly

Gujarat lost fewer wickets in the second match but still lost the game. Wickets preserved are a resource, not a substitute for reaching the target before the overs run out.

Similarly, the two winning margins cannot be combined into an aggregate “series margin”: five wickets and 29 runs measure different outcomes. The summary of these two games is simply **one win apiece, both at home**.

For anyone using this archive to study a future matchup, keep that limit in view. These historical games can explain the split between the two results; they cannot establish that the home side, the chasing side or a particular batter will dominate the next meeting.
