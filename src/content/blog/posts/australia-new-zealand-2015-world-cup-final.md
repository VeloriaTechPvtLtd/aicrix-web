---

Australia won the 2015 men's World Cup final by seven wickets at the Melbourne Cricket Ground on 29 March. New Zealand were dismissed for 183 in 45 overs; Australia reached 186 for three in 33.1. The scorecard gives the result plainly, but the decisive passage was the interruption of a recovery that had taken New Zealand to 150 for three. [New Zealand Cricket's archived scorecard](https://archive.blackcaps.co.nz/Scorecards/560/560902.html)

The final was not simply an early collapse followed by a chase. New Zealand rebuilt before losing their way again. That middle passage is essential to understanding James Faulkner's contribution and why his wickets mattered so much.

## The recovery after the first blow

Mitchell Starc dismissed Brendon McCullum for a duck in the first over. New Zealand were subsequently 39 for three, before Grant Elliott and Ross Taylor added 111. Faulkner then dismissed Taylor with a slower delivery, ending the stand. [ICC's Faulkner retrospective](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/unreal-awesome-james-faulkner-looks-back-on-australias-2015-world-cup-triumph)

The scoreboard moved from 150 for three to 150 for five, then 151 for six. Taylor, Corey Anderson and Luke Ronchi were dismissed in that sequence. The last seven wickets therefore fell for 33 runs, calculated from the score at three down and the final total. [NZC's fall-of-wickets record](https://archive.blackcaps.co.nz/Scorecards/560/560902.html)

## Why Faulkner received the match award

Faulkner finished with three for 36 and removed Taylor, Anderson and Elliott. Mitchell Johnson also took three wickets, conceding 30. The ICC's anniversary account identifies Faulkner as Player of the Match and records Elliott's 83 and Taylor's 40 as New Zealand's principal batting contributions. [ICC's anniversary report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/cricketworldcup/news/on-this-day-australia-win-icc-cwc-2015)

Faulkner's later account also resists an overly neat tactical story. He recalled that the delivery dismissing Taylor was not exactly where he had intended, and that his approach to Anderson departed from the original plan. Those are his retrospective explanations, not evidence that every successful delivery was executed precisely as designed. [ICC's Faulkner retrospective](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/unreal-awesome-james-faulkner-looks-back-on-australias-2015-world-cup-triumph)

## Australia also lost an opener cheaply

Aaron Finch was dismissed without scoring, but Australia's response developed differently. David Warner made 45, Michael Clarke 74 and Steve Smith an unbeaten 56. Clarke's innings came in his final ODI appearance, and Australia secured their fifth men's World Cup title. [ICC's anniversary report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/cricketworldcup/news/on-this-day-australia-win-icc-cwc-2015)

Australia's recorded innings length left 101 legal deliveries unused: 300 minus 199. That is 16 overs and five balls. It is not 16.5 decimal overs, even though cricket notation commonly writes it that way. [NZC's innings record](https://archive.blackcaps.co.nz/Scorecards/560/560902.html)

## The final is not the entire tournament

Starc was Player of the Series, and he finished level with Trent Boult on 22 wickets. Faulkner's final award is a separate honour. [ICC's 2015 overview](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/cricketworldcup/news/mens-cricket-world-cup-2015-overview)

Keeping those distinctions clear gives a more accurate account of the title. Starc delivered the memorable opening blow, Taylor and Elliott rebuilt, Faulkner interrupted the recovery, and Australia's batters completed the chase. The sequence explains more than the finishing margin alone, while allowing each performance to retain its proper place in the match.
