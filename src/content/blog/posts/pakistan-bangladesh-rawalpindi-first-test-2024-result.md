Bangladesh beat Pakistan by ten wickets in the first Rawalpindi Test, played from 21 to 25 August 2024. It was Bangladesh's first Test victory over Pakistan, arriving in their 14th meeting after twelve defeats and a draw. A large first-innings response gave Bangladesh a lead; their final-day bowling then made a small chase possible. [ICC report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/world-test-championship/news/bangladesh-script-history-with-momentous-test-victory-over-pakistan?sf198969327=1)

## The four innings at a glance

| Innings | Score | Overs |
|---|---:|---:|
| Pakistan first innings | 448/6 declared | 113 |
| Bangladesh first innings | 565 all out | 167.3 |
| Pakistan second innings | 146 all out | 55.5 |
| Bangladesh second innings | 30/0 | 6.3 |

The completed scorecard records a declaration in Pakistan's first innings, not an all-out total. Bangladesh's final 6.3 overs means six completed overs and three legal deliveries, or 39 balls, rather than 6.3 decimal overs. [Full scorecard](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/101027/pak-vs-ban-1st-test-bangladesh-tour-of-pakistan-2024)

## Pakistan recovered, but Bangladesh built the bigger innings

Pakistan had been 16/3. Saud Shakeel made 141 and Mohammad Rizwan finished unbeaten on 171, with their fifth-wicket partnership contributing 240. Bangladesh's reply included Shadman Islam's 93 and a seventh-wicket stand of 196 between Mushfiqur Rahim and Mehidy Hasan Miraz. That stand moved the score from 332/6 to 528/7. [Scorecard and partnership ledger](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/101027/pak-vs-ban-1st-test-bangladesh-tour-of-pakistan-2024)

Those two Bangladesh checkpoints show how the match position changed. At 332, Bangladesh were 116 behind Pakistan's first innings. By 528, they were 80 ahead. The same partnership both removed the deficit and built a lead; counting only the runs scored after passing 448 would understate its contribution.

Mushfiqur's 191 was the largest individual score of the match and earned him Player of the Match. Bangladesh eventually reached 565, taking their advantage to 117. [Reuters report](https://www.reuters.com/sports/cricket/bangladesh-ease-first-ever-test-victory-over-pakistan-2024-08-25/)

## The final day changed the purpose of Pakistan's innings

Pakistan resumed on 23/1 and were dismissed for 146. Mehidy took 4/21 and Shakib Al Hasan 3/44, combining for seven wickets. Rizwan's second-innings 51 was not enough to produce a substantial target. [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/sports/cricket/bangladesh-ease-first-ever-test-victory-over-pakistan-2024-08-25/)

A side beginning its second innings behind first has to erase that deficit. Pakistan's 146 did not become a 147-run target because Bangladesh already held 117 runs of credit from the first innings. Only the excess over that lead counted toward the final chase.

## Why Bangladesh needed 30, not 117 or 146

The calculation can be written in two equivalent ways:

- Pakistan's second innings minus Bangladesh's lead, plus one: 146 − 117 + 1 = 30.

- Pakistan's combined innings minus Bangladesh's first innings, plus one: 448 + 146 − 565 + 1 = 30.

The extra one is the run needed to move ahead, not merely level the aggregate scores. Bangladesh reached 30 without losing a wicket; Zakir Hasan and Shadman completed the chase. [ICC result report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/world-test-championship/news/bangladesh-script-history-with-momentous-test-victory-over-pakistan?sf198969327=1)

“Ten wickets” describes the wickets remaining in the successful final innings. It does not describe the first-innings advantage, nor imply that Pakistan lost twenty wickets: their declaration left four first-innings wickets unused.

This distinction also guards against an overly simple explanation of the defeat. A declaration preceded the loss, but the scorecard cannot prove that declaring alone caused it. Bangladesh still had to outscore 448, dismiss Pakistan cheaply and finish the chase. Those completed stages, rather than a hypothetical alternative declaration, explain the recorded result.
