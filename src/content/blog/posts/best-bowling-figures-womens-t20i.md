**Laura Cardoso's 9/4 against Lesotho on 9 April 2026 leads the checked women's T20I bowling-figures record.** The Brazilian took nine wickets in three overs, becoming the first player in men's or women's T20 internationals to take nine in an innings. [ICC record report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/brazil-s-laura-cardoso-makes-t20i-history-with-nine-wicket-haul).

The inclusive seven-wicket threshold contains **six performances** in the tables checked on 12 November. It includes Chamari Athapaththu's September 2026 addition and two shared 7/3 returns. This is an innings record, not a ranking of the best bowlers across complete careers.

## All six performances at seven wickets or more

| Rank | Bowler and team | Figures | Opponent | Match date |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Laura Cardoso, Brazil | 9/4 | Lesotho | 9 April 2026 |
| 2 | Rohmalia Rohmalia, Indonesia | 7/0 | Mongolia | 24 April 2024 |
| 3 equal | Frederique Overdijk, Netherlands | 7/3 | France | 26 August 2021 |
| 3 equal | Alison Stocks, Argentina | 7/3 | Peru | 14 October 2022 |
| 5 | Chamari Athapaththu, Sri Lanka | 7/5 | Indonesia | 2 September 2026 |
| 6 | Samanthi Dunukedeniya, Cyprus | 7/15 | Czech Republic | 2 May 2025 |

The complete group and dates agree between [Crickrida's women's T20I innings ledger](https://crickrida.com/records/women/t20i/best-bowling-innings/) and [CricPrism's 12 November table](https://cricprism.com/cricket-records/t20i/womens/best-bowling-figures-in-an-innings). Both next list Anjali Chand's **6/0**, below the threshold. The ICC's [September report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/athapaththu-s-majestic-spell-guides-sri-lanka-to-mammoth-win) independently describes Athapaththu's return as the sixth seven-plus instance.

More wickets come first; fewer runs conceded breaks a tie in wickets. Cardoso's nine therefore rank above Rohmalia's seven even though Cardoso conceded four runs and Rohmalia none. A zero-concession spell is not automatically first in a wicket-first record.

## Cardoso took nine of ten wickets

Brazil made 202 before dismissing Lesotho for 13. Cardoso took three wickets in her first over, four in her second and two in her third; Marianne Artur took the remaining wicket. Brazil won by 189 runs. [ICC's match account](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/brazil-s-laura-cardoso-makes-t20i-history-with-nine-wicket-haul).

The sequence reconciles as **3+4+2=9**, not three separate hat-tricks. Nine wickets also means 90% of the ten team wickets in that innings. It does not mean she conceded all 13 runs, or that her teammates bowled no deliveries.

Her spell lasted 18 legal balls. Four runs divided by three overs gives an economy of about **1.33**. Balls per wicket for that spell were **18÷9=2**. These describe one completed performance; they are not forecasts that she will take a wicket every second ball in another match.

## Rohmalia's record belongs to its date

Rohmalia took **7/0 from 20 legal deliveries** against Mongolia on 24 April 2024. The ICC report was published on 25 April, so its publication date should not replace the match date. Its account says all 20 deliveries were dots and all seven dismissed batters scored ducks. [Contemporary ICC report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/indonesia-s-rohmalia-rohmalia-breaks-record-for-best-spell-in-t20is).

That was the overall T20I best at the time. Later records changed the answer. Cardoso's nine-wicket performance now leads this women's table, while the intervening men's eight-wicket record belongs in the men's T20I record rather than this women's table.

Twenty legal deliveries are **3.2 overs**, not 3.2 decimal overs. For any calculation, convert the notation to balls before using it.

## Equal figures remain equal

Overdijk and Stocks each took **7/3**. Both occupy joint third place in this table; the next position is fifth. Their different spell lengths do not separate them in a ranking defined only by wickets and runs.

Crickrida lists 24 legal balls for Overdijk and 22 for Stocks. That produces different economy rates even though the figures match. It is a useful distinction: “best figures” and “lowest economy” are different questions, so a table should not silently switch its sorting rule.

The ICC's 2024 report already lists both 7/3 performances. A list that includes one and excludes the other at a tied cutoff would be incomplete.

## Athapaththu added a 2026 entry

On **2 September 2026**, Athapaththu took **7/5 in 3.3 overs** against Indonesia at the Women's Asia Cup in Dubai. Sri Lanka had made 124/7; Indonesia were dismissed for 49, giving Sri Lanka a 75-run win. [ICC match report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/athapaththu-s-majestic-spell-guides-sri-lanka-to-mammoth-win).

Her spell ranks fifth in the group, not first. The 21 legal balls in 3.3 overs are also separate from Indonesia's full 18.3-over innings. Mixing a bowler's spell length with the team's innings length would give a false economy rate.

This is why a record list frozen after Cardoso's April achievement still needs a later coverage check. A new seven-wicket performance may enter below the leader without changing the headline record.

## Cyprus supplies the sixth qualifying performance

Dunukedeniya took **7/15 in 3.5 overs** at Scott Page Field in Prague on 2 May 2025. The [ACS scorecard](https://archive.acscricket.com/records_and_stats/women_itts/witt_statistics_to_20251231/05%20m2336.htm) records Cyprus 151/1 and Czech Republic 37 all out, a 114-run result.

The bowling table credits one other wicket to D Koralalge, while two dismissals were run-outs. **Seven plus one plus two equals ten team wickets.** The run-outs do not increase either bowler's wickets.

Her 23 legal balls are not 3.5 decimal overs. The example also shows why a team all-out total and a bowler's credited wickets must remain separate fields when building records or fantasy calculations.

## Use the right population

These are senior women's T20 internationals. A domestic or franchise spell does not enter merely because the game had 20 overs. Nor does a women's ODI seven-wicket haul belong in this table.

The dates, opponents and format are essential parts of the record. Keep the full threshold, including ties, when updating it. A later seven-plus haul belongs at its correct place rather than being omitted to preserve a fixed “top five” layout.
