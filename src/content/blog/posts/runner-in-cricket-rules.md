Under the October 2026 ICC men’s Test conditions, a batter cannot use a runner. A local match depends on its own playing conditions: the MCC Laws still contain runner provisions. [ICC Test conditions, clause 25.5](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/a8114zro5tkioifz0jqg.pdf).

That is a difference between rulebooks, not a contradiction that can be settled by pointing to any cricket law page.

## Injury does not override a Test prohibition

The point is not that an injury must look severe enough on television. Severity does not erase a competition's prohibition. Equally, a video from another competition or another year is not proof that the current Test rule has been broken.

## Three questions before applying a rule

| Question | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Which competition and season? | The applicable conditions may modify the MCC baseline. |
| Is the player asking for someone to run, or to leave the innings? | A runner and a retirement are different requests. |
| What have the umpires actually authorised? | A teammate walking onto the field is not evidence of eligibility. |

For a club game, obtain the organiser's conditions and ask the umpires to apply them. Avoid using “runners are banned everywhere” as the answer. Also avoid promising a runner solely because a player was injured: the applicable conditions and eligibility requirements still need checking.

## What the MCC runner provision actually requires

The MCC baseline allows a runner when the umpires are satisfied that a match injury affects the batter’s ability to run, or for another wholly acceptable reason. The runner must be a nominated member of the batting side, have no unserved penalty time, carry a bat and wear equivalent external protective equipment. If possible, the runner should already have completed their innings. [MCC Law 25.5](https://www.lords.org/getmedia/a4ef9f77-2a25-4f5b-a286-4601f08e6334/Laws-of-Cricket-2017-Code-4th-Edition-%282026%29_5.pdf).

## Keep the roles separate in analysis

A runner assists with running; that is not the same analytical category as a new batter replacing a retired batter. Likewise, permission for a substitute to field does not establish permission to run for someone during the batting innings. Retirement is a separate procedure under Law 25.4; it is not permission for someone else to run while the injured batter continues facing.

When recording an incident, keep the injured player's identity, any runner's identity, the competition and the official decision in separate fields. Do not merge two people into one batting record or turn a runner into a claimed additional batter.

For the men’s Test conditions covered here, the immediate answer is no. For another level of cricket, resolve the governing conditions before answering.
