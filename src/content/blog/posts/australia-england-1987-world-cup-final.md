Australia won their first men's World Cup by defeating England by seven runs in Kolkata in 1987. David Boon's 75 helped set a target of 254, and Mike Gatting's dismissal attempting a reverse sweep against Allan Border became the final's defining image. But a seven-run result deserves a wider explanation than one unsuccessful shot. [ICC's 1987 overview](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/cricketworldcup/news/mens-cricket-world-cup-1987-overview)

## The contribution below the half-century line

Australia finished on 253 for five. Mike Veletta supplied an unbeaten 45 from 31 balls, including six fours, and Australia scored 65 in their last six overs. England eventually reached 246 for eight after using their full allocation. [ICC's final retrospective](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/cricketworldcup/news/cwc-memories-australia-win-the-1987-cricket-world-cup)

Veletta's innings is easy to overlook if a summary lists only fifties and centuries. A milestone is a convenient label, not a complete measure of contribution. A short innings near the end can change the target substantially even without reaching 50.

Boon's knock followed a 65 in Australia's semi-final victory over Pakistan. England reached the final by beating India, with Graham Gooch's century helping them advance. Both finalists had therefore overcome a host nation in the preceding round. [ICC's 1987 overview](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/cricketworldcup/news/mens-cricket-world-cup-1987-overview)

## The shot and the rest of the chase

Bill Athey made 58, Gatting 41 and Allan Lamb 45. England needed 17 from the last over but could not finish the chase. Border and Steve Waugh each took two wickets. These details place the celebrated reverse sweep within a broader batting and bowling contest. [ICC's final retrospective](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/cricketworldcup/news/cwc-memories-australia-win-the-1987-cricket-world-cup)

It is tempting to turn an unusual dismissal into a complete explanation of defeat. Yet identifying a consequential wicket is different from proving that an alternative shot would have won the match. That counterfactual would require the entire subsequent innings to be known, which it cannot be.

## Keep tournament achievements separate

Gooch finished as the competition's leading scorer with 471 runs. Craig McDermott led the wickets with 18; his five for 44 against Pakistan came in the semi-final, not the final. [ICC's 1987 star-performers review](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/cricketworldcup/news/1987-world-cup-star-performers)

## Reading seven runs correctly

The gap between 253 and 246 is seven, but England's winning target was 254. The result margin is not the same as the number of extra runs needed to win at the finish. This elementary distinction is especially helpful in close matches, where “short by seven” is often used loosely.
