**A standard cricket pitch is 22 yards long and 10 feet wide, conventionally written as 20.12 metres by 3.05 metres.** Its ends are the bowling creases. [MCC Laws 6 and 7](https://www.lords.org/getattachment/e8b12929-1e84-4927-af4e-2a5875b53589/Laws-of-Cricket-2017-Code-4th-Edition-%282026%29.pdf).

These measurements describe the central playing strip, rather than the whole ground inside the boundary. The other white markings around the stumps identify different positions, so measuring between whichever lines are most visible can give the wrong answer.

## Converting 22 yards to metres

The international yard equals exactly 0.9144 metres, and the foot equals exactly 0.3048 metres. [NIST conversion definitions](https://www.nist.gov/pml/special-publication-811/nist-guide-si-appendix-b-conversion-factors).

The length calculation is:

**22 × 0.9144 = 20.1168 metres**

Rounded to two decimal places, that becomes 20.12 metres. In feet, the calculation is 22 × 3 = 66 feet.

The same method converts the width:

**10 × 0.3048 = 3.048 metres**, or 3.05 metres to two decimal places.

This explains why a search result may show 20.12 metres while a calculator returns 20.1168. They describe the same conversion at different precision. An exact 20-metre measurement would be 0.1168 metres, or 11.68 centimetres, shorter than 22 yards. Keep the units and rounding visible when comparing diagrams.

## Bowling popping and return creases

| Marking | MCC baseline measurement |
| --- | --- |
| Bowling crease | 8 feet 8 inches long; defines the pitch end |
| Popping crease | 4 feet in front of the bowling crease |
| Return crease | 4 feet 4 inches either side of the middle-stump centreline |

The bowling and popping creases use the **back edges** of their painted lines; return creases use the **inside edges**. Popping and return creases extend indefinitely in law, although only part is painted. [MCC Law 7](https://www.lords.org/getattachment/e8b12929-1e84-4927-af4e-2a5875b53589/Laws-of-Cricket-2017-Code-4th-Edition-%282026%29.pdf).

The bowling-crease marking is therefore shorter than the pitch width. It is not a painted outline of the whole rectangle.

## Why the popping creases are closer together

Start with the 66-foot pitch length and subtract the four-foot offset at each end:

**66 − 4 − 4 = 58 feet**

Converting that result gives **58 × 0.3048 = 17.6784 metres**, or about 17.68 metres between the popping creases.

This is a geometric distance between lines. It does not establish exactly how far a batter’s body travels for every run. Starting positions, a stretched bat and the path taken all affect that movement. Likewise, pitch length alone does not give a delivered ball’s exact flight distance: the release point and where the batter meets it also matter.

## Junior cricket and artificial surfaces

MCC leaves junior pitch length to the national governing body. Its non-turf rule sets an artificial-surface minimum of 58 feet by 6 feet; that is a surfacing requirement, not a replacement for standard pitch length. Law 6 also allows a narrower side where a nearby artificial pitch meets the surface. [MCC Law 6](https://www.lords.org/getattachment/e8b12929-1e84-4927-af4e-2a5875b53589/Laws-of-Cricket-2017-Code-4th-Edition-%282026%29.pdf).

For an actual junior match or ground-marking job, use the governing body’s current competition requirements rather than scaling a television screenshot. The measurements above explain the baseline geometry.
