New Zealand beat South Africa by nine wickets in the men's T20 World Cup semifinal at Eden Gardens, Kolkata, on 4 March 2026. South Africa finished on 169/8; New Zealand reached 173/1 in 12.5 overs. Those totals, date and venue are confirmed by the BCCI match centre. [Official match header](https://www.bcci.tv/matches/3a4f725f-f333-4643-b686-57cac2395314/tbc-vs-tbc/scorecard)

Finn Allen's unbeaten century transformed a target of 170 into a chase completed with 43 balls remaining. But the match was not simply an isolated hundred. New Zealand first disrupted South Africa's top order, then built an opening stand that left little room for the bowling side to recover.

## Early wickets and South Africa's recovery

New Zealand elected to field and introduced Cole McConchie in the second over. He dismissed Quinton de Kock and Ryan Rickelton on successive deliveries, leaving South Africa 12/2. Further wickets reduced them to 77/5 before Tristan Stubbs and Marco Jansen added 73 runs. [ICC match report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/mens-t20-world-cup-2026/news/live-new-zealand-take-on-unbeaten-south-africa-in-semi-final)

The scorecard puts the fifth wicket at 77 and the sixth at 150, so the recovery supplied 73 runs. That is the partnership total, not the sum of both batters’ complete innings. Jansen continued after Stubbs departed, which is why his eventual 55 cannot simply be added to Stubbs’ 29 and labeled their stand. The partnership repaired much of the early damage without erasing the fact that five wickets had already fallen.

Jansen finished unbeaten on 55 from 30 deliveries. McConchie and Rachin Ravindra took two wickets each, as did Matt Henry. South Africa's recovery left New Zealand a target of 170. [ICC report](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/mens-t20-world-cup-2026/news/live-new-zealand-take-on-unbeaten-south-africa-in-semi-final)

## The opening stand changed the chase

New Zealand reached 84 without loss after six overs. Tim Seifert's dismissal came at 117/1 after 9.1 overs; he had made 58 from 33 balls. Allen and Rachin Ravindra then completed the chase, with Ravindra unbeaten on 13. [Full scorecard](https://www.cricbuzz.com/live-cricket-scorecard/139472/rsa-vs-nz)

At the end of the powerplay, the equation was 86 needed from 84 balls. The required rate had fallen from 8.50 at the start to approximately 6.14. That shift explains why the opening phase mattered beyond the attractive run rate: New Zealand had reduced the scoring pressure without spending a wicket.

After Seifert fell, 53 were required from 65 balls. Those figures use the winning target of 170 and convert 9.1 overs to 55 legal deliveries. They do not treat the decimal-looking over notation as an ordinary fraction. South Africa's first breakthrough therefore arrived with the chase already substantially advanced.

## What Allen's hundred contained

Allen ended on exactly 100 from 33 balls, including ten fours and eight sixes. The ICC identified it at the time as the fastest century in men's T20 World Cup history, improving on Chris Gayle's 47-ball hundred against England in 2016. This is a dated tournament record, not a claim about every form of cricket. [ICC's next-day record summary](https://www.icc-cricket.com/tournaments/mens-t20-world-cup-2026/news/allen-basks-in-glory-as-new-zealand-look-ahead-to-decider)

The boundary arithmetic is striking: ten fours supplied 40 runs and eight sixes another 48. Thus 88 of Allen's 100 came in boundaries. His other 12 runs still count toward the century, but the distribution shows why South Africa could not contain him merely by limiting singles.

The chase lasted 77 legal balls, leaving 120 minus 77, or 43, unused. New Zealand's final 173 exceeded the target because the winning boundary carried the score beyond 170. The result is correctly expressed as a nine-wicket victory, not a three-run victory or a win by the difference between the two totals.
