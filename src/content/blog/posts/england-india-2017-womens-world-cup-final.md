England beat India by nine runs in the Women's ODI World Cup final at Lord's on 23 July 2017. England made 228/7, and India were dismissed for 219 with eight balls unused. Anya Shrubsole took 6/46, while Punam Raut's 86 and Harmanpreet Kaur's 51 had earlier put India in a strong position. [BCCI match report](https://www.bcci.tv/news/article/england-beat-india-in-gripping-final-to-win-world-cup)

This was not a final in which a huge target steadily wore down the chasing team. Its distinctive feature was the reversal near the end: a manageable requirement became unreachable because wickets disappeared faster than the remaining runs.

## England's recovery gave the bowlers something to defend

England slipped to 63/3 before Sarah Taylor and Natalie Sciver added 83. Jhulan Goswami then removed Taylor and Fran Wilson in successive deliveries and later dismissed Sciver. She finished with 3/23 from ten overs. England still recovered sufficiently to set a target of 229. [BCCI match report](https://www.bcci.tv/news/article/england-beat-india-in-gripping-final-to-win-world-cup)

## Raut and Harmanpreet made the chase plausible

India were 43/2 when Raut and Harmanpreet established their partnership. They added 95 before Harmanpreet was caught off Alex Hartley for 51. Raut and Veda Krishnamurthy then added another 53. The requirement had fallen to 38 from 43 deliveries when Raut was dismissed for 86. [BCCI partnership account](https://www.bcci.tv/news/article/england-beat-india-in-gripping-final-to-win-world-cup), [ECB account of the late chase](https://www.ecb.co.uk/news/441642/england-women-seal-dramatic-world-cup-win)

Thirty-eight from 43 balls is less than one run per delivery. That arithmetic explains why the chase appeared manageable; it does not establish a probability of victory. Such a probability would require a specified model, assumptions about the batters and bowlers, and the match situation at that moment.

## Shrubsole changed the available batting resources

Shrubsole's late wickets included Raut, Krishnamurthy and Deepti Sharma. She ended the match by bowling Rajeshwari Gayakwad. Her six-wicket return helped England secure their fourth Women's World Cup title. [ICC final feature](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/shrubsoles-date-with-destiny)

There was also work beyond her bowling. Shrubsole ran out Shikha Pandey during the closing stages. The final wicket followed a dropped catch, so even the last moments required England to recover from a missed chance rather than simply coast home. [ECB closing sequence](https://www.ecb.co.uk/news/441642/england-women-seal-dramatic-world-cup-win)

## Why the margin was nine runs, not ten

India needed 229 to win but finished on 219. Ten more runs would have reached the target; the official margin was nine because England's completed total was 228. The margin compares the two totals, not the losing team's score with the winning target.

The unused deliveries are equally important. India did not merely run out of time. Their innings ended with wickets exhausted. For readers studying chases, that distinction is the lasting lesson of this final: runs, balls and wickets must be read together, especially when a low required rate makes the finish look simpler than it really is.
