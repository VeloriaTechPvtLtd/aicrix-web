## What the BBL rule allows

The men’s BBL introduces an optional designated batter and designated fielder for 2026–27. A club choosing this arrangement names the pair before the bat flip. It can instead use a traditional XI. The change does not apply to WBBL|12. [Cricket Australia’s announcement](https://www.cricket.com.au/news/4434311/bbl-wbbl-new-rule-changes-designated-batter-fielder-bbl16-wbbl12-cricket-australia).

| Nominated role | Bat | Bowl | Field | Keep wicket |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Designated batter | Yes | No | No | No |
| Designated fielder | No | No | Yes | Yes |

The designated fielder takes the batter’s place for the team’s fielding innings. This separates two jobs; it does not create an additional bowling option. The nomination deadline is before the bat flip, not a later tactical choice based on how the first innings unfolds.

## Three hypothetical selection decisions

Consider a batter who sometimes supplies two overs. If the club nominates that player as its designated batter, a match preview cannot carry those two overs into its bowling projection. The squad label “all-rounder” describes a general skill set, not permission to bowl in this nominated role.

Now consider a specialist wicketkeeper who offers less with the bat than another squad member. The club could use the specialist as its designated fielder and the other player as designated batter. A preview should attach keeping opportunity to the nominated keeper rather than automatically to the person who usually wears the gloves.

Finally, suppose a team wants a reserve pace bowler’s fielding ability but also hopes to use that player for an over. Naming the player designated fielder does not achieve both goals: bowling is prohibited. These examples illustrate the announced role restrictions, not predicted choices by any club.

## Why the team sheet matters more than the player label

A useful match record needs both a player identity and that match’s confirmed role. Store the nomination source and its timestamp alongside the lineup. A squad announcement or a coach’s suggestion that someone might suit the job is not a confirmed nomination.

Before nominations, an analyst can describe alternative lineup scenarios. Afterwards, those scenarios should be replaced with the actual roles rather than blended into an impossible workload. This is a data-quality decision: a database should not quietly retain a player’s usual responsibilities after the match-specific evidence changes.

Nor does a nomination establish how many balls a batter will face, whether a catch will come to a fielder, or which team will win. Removing an impossible activity from a projection is different from guaranteeing an opportunity in an allowed activity.

## Is this the IPL Impact Player rule

No. The BBL arrangement has its own role restrictions and nomination process. “Substitute” is not a sufficient description of a player’s permissions. The older BBL X-Factor experiment is also historical context, not the rule to apply in 2026–27.

## Does the nominated role determine fantasy points

Not by itself. A sporting rule determines permitted participation; a fantasy platform decides selection eligibility, player categories and scoring. A prediction should identify which platform and contest rules it uses before attaching points to an event.

A rule summary from one fantasy service is not the rulebook for another. Do not transfer credits, role limits or designated-player scoring assumptions between platforms.

For a match-day fantasy explanation, verify the platform’s current selection screen, nominated-player eligibility and scoring terms separately. Until those checks are complete, keep the answer about sporting opportunities rather than promise points. Injury replacements and unusual nomination changes also require the final competition conditions; they should not be improvised from the short announcement.
