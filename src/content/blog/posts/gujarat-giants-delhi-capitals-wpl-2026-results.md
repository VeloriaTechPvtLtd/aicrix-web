Gujarat Giants won two of their three meetings with Delhi Capitals in WPL 2026, but Delhi won the Eliminator. That distinction matters: a 2–1 season head-to-head describes the results, while the knockout result explains which team advanced to the final.

This archive covers all three completed meetings, not the franchises' all-time record. The two league games were followed by their 3 February knockout encounter; the season final featured Delhi and Royal Challengers Bengaluru, so it added no fourth meeting between these teams. ## Two league games decided in the last over

In Navi Mumbai, Sophie Devine's 95 from 42 balls helped Gujarat reach 209. Delhi still entered the final over needing seven, but Devine conceded only two and removed Jemimah Rodrigues and Laura Wolvaardt. Gujarat's four-run win was therefore much tighter than a 200-plus first innings might suggest. [Official Match 4 report](https://www.wplt20.com/news/article/match-report-tata-wpl-2026-match-4).

The return game produced another late defense. Niki Prasad and Sneh Rana revived Delhi from 100/6 with a 70-run partnership. Delhi needed nine from the last over; Devine allowed five and dismissed both batters. Gujarat completed the league double by three runs. [Official Match 17 report](https://www.wplt20.com/news/article/match-report-tata-wpl-2026-match-17).

The combined winning margin was seven runs, but that is not an aggregate-score contest. Each chase had its own target, wickets and remaining deliveries. Adding margins is useful for showing how close the finishes were, not for deciding the season's qualification outcome.

## Why the Eliminator was different

Delhi's chase did not reach the final-over contest that had twice favored Gujarat. Chasing 169, Delhi finished in 94 legal balls, leaving 26 unused. [Official Eliminator report](https://www.wplt20.com/news/article/match-report-tata-wpl-2026-eliminator).

The official report records an 89-run opening stand between Lizelle Lee and Shafali Verma. That partnership supplied more than half the target before the middle order completed the job. This does not prove that attacking batting always wins; it identifies one important contribution in this particular chase. [Eliminator report](https://www.wplt20.com/news/article/match-report-tata-wpl-2026-eliminator).

Gujarat's three first-innings scores fell from 209 to 174 to 168. Their mean was 183.67, yet that average hides the actual targets of 210, 175 and 169. For understanding the reversal, the individual targets and Delhi's chase progress are more useful than a single rivalry average.

The accurate season summary is therefore two narrow Gujarat league wins and one substantially earlier Delhi chase in the knockout. Neither the 2–1 record nor the Eliminator alone should replace the full three-match picture.
